# Tilemaps (Sub-project 6a): speed

Spec: `docs/specs/2026-10-29-tilemap-design.md` (Acceptance, Targets). The 6b feature list is `docs/backlog/tilemap-features.md`.

## 6a: measured

Measured on an Apple M5 Pro (18 cores, Metal, macOS 27.0.1), a shared machine. The record below is from 2026-10-31 between 00:35 and 00:41, after the review's fixes. By then the other work that ran through the evening had ended: three `BanjoRecompiled` processes (a game, rendering on the same GPU) and a Python job. Frames cost less than in the first record, at the same 1-minute load: an empty frame's step read 93 µs, against 147 to 175 µs earlier that night. So numbers from different sessions do not compare. Each fix's before and after numbers come from one session, and each is given with its load or time.

**Method.**
- `benches/tilemap.rs` (its module docs say what each guard does) ran three times on the branch, in the workspace's own `target/` (the bench profile: release with debug info). Each run waited until the 1-minute load was 4 or less. The loads at the runs' starts were 3.79, 3.56 and 3.12, and at their ends 4.03, 3.12 and 3.18.
- Engines run at 1920 x 1080 with 16 px tiles at zoom 1, headless (`HeadlessEngine`) on the GPU.
  - A frame's cost is the wall time of the whole step. The GPU finishes before each step, outside the measure (as `benches/ui_render.rs` does).
  - The static guard's engines step in turn, one frame each. The landing frames are paced at 60 Hz with a spin wait.
- Each verdict holds the median, with the 95th percentile and the maximum printed beside it.
  - The landing's verdict is its slowest frame over the run's median ordinary frame. A run over the limit is measured once more, and the better run is held.
- The one-off timings (ignored tests) ran once at a load of 3.91, in release.

### The static frame (`static_frame`)

The map: 1024 x 1024 cells of 16 px in four layers over one 256 x 256 atlas:
- a background on every cell;
- cave rock from value noise, with a full-cell collision shape, so it collides and casts shadows;
- props on about one open cell in 16;
- front details on one in 64.

It is built in code and landed, then given 120 frames to warm up. Each engine then runs 2,000 frames, the engines interleaved. The camera flies a long loop over the map at 444 to 690 px/s (561 on average). New chunks come into view every few dozen frames, and the frames that build them cost more; the 95th percentiles and maxima include them.

### The edit (`edit`)

The map: 1024 x 1024 cells in four layers.
- The ground holds 3 x 3 islands every 8 cells: about 16,000, packed into about 1,000 chunk colliders, each with one occluder chain or more.
- Two loops sit each in its own row of 4 x 1 chunks:
  - a jagged skyline, 128 columns up to 24 cells high, whose column 99 holds only its bottom cell (a neck);
  - a one-cell serpentine filling the four chunks.

Eight edits, each set and cleared again with an ordinary frame between edits, as a game edits:
- a bump on each loop: 400 edits at (64, 20) and (64, 64). The loop stays one.
- a split and a join on each loop, 200 edits each: the skyline's neck (99, 31), and the middle of the serpentine's first row (64, 65). Clearing the cell splits the loop in two, and setting it joins it again.
- the serpentine split and joined through its middle row (64, 79): halves of about 2,000 edges each, the worst split a loop of four chunks can have.

### The fixes made in this task

| Fix | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| **Outline splice** (`physics/tiles/outline.rs`). An edit's retrace copies the dropped loops' corners up to the points whose edges changed, and looks up only those points; each unchanged stretch is copied in one go. - **Every run:** 1,040 chunks landed and 1,046 unloaded, no frame doing both, two at most in one. No unfilled chunk in any of the 2,120 frames. ## Limits

- **Transform propagation still scans every entity for changes.** It walks only the subtrees that changed (`ecs/propagate.rs`), but finding them is a change-tick check per entity per run, about 0.7 ns: about 3 µs a run for the 1024² cave map's 4,096 entities, three runs a frame. Bigger maps, and streamed chunks, add to it.
  - **A run that changes many entities pays the scan on top.** Where the changed subtrees cover the hierarchy, the walk visits every entity as the full walk would, plus the scan: `benches/foundation.rs`'s 100 roots moved over 10,000 entities cost 77.4 µs against 73.5 µs before (+5.3%, the same session).
  - **A fifth changed walks everything.** Once the changed entities are a fifth of those with a `Transform` (and more than 64), a run walks every hierarchy, as before 6b, the map's children included: there that costs about what the changed subtrees would (2,000 leaves moved of 10,000 entities: 83 µs through the changed subtrees, about 81 through the full walk). The fifth was measured on leaves two deep; deeper changed entities cost the changed subtrees' walk more each, so for them the crossover comes earlier.
  - Proposed, if the scan shows in a profile: keep a static map's parts (collider parts, occluder chains) out of the scanned set, which changes what `TilePart` and `MapOccluder` carry.
- **Drawing a big map is most of its frame:** the map drawn only adds 34.1 to 35.2 µs, of the static map's 43.8 to 45.7 µs (three runs). Proposed, for the margin: **merged contiguous chunk draws.** Neighbouring chunks of a layer whose instances lie next to each other in the arena could share one draw, instead of one draw and its encoding a chunk.
- **The landing frame draws the map for the first time** (the landing miss). What is left after the fixes:
  - **The first landing of a tileset** creates its padded texture and writes its levels in that frame: about 0.35 to 0.58 ms more than a later landing.
  - **Every landing** builds and uploads its visible chunks: about +0.5 ms at 1080p (27,000 instances of 128 bytes). The busier machine's profile put about 0.2 ms in the drawer (0.1 ms of it building the instances), about 0.26 ms in copying them into the arena's buffer, and about 0.12 ms elsewhere.
  - **Instances built on the worker were weighed and not built.** They can save at most the 99 µs the build takes, which leaves the copy, the upload and the first texture: the guard would still miss.
  - **Proposals that keep the map whole on its landing frame (R16):**
    - Write the padded texture over the frames the landing job already takes (about 5 ms, several frames at 60 Hz), a level or a band of rows a frame, so it is whole before the map lands.
    - Give chunks a compact instance: a cell index and a tile slot, about 16 bytes, the shader working out the rest. That cuts the copy and the upload about eightfold. Deferred by the ruling.
    - Give the chunk arena a mapped staging buffer that the build writes straight into, instead of copying with `write_buffer`.
  - Each is a design change across the landing, the graphics upload and the tile shader; none is built.
- **A split relabels its smaller part edge by edge.** Each edge that moves to the other loop's id costs a lookup. The middle cut of a four-chunk serpentine (about 2,000 edges) adds about 8 µs, and its loop rebuild reads 60 to 65 µs, but a loop spanning many more chunks, split into long halves, pays in proportion.
  - An edit with doubled edges at a changed point (a broken shape's) still walks the whole of every loop it dropped.
  - Proposed: labels by range (each loop's edges as runs in a per-loop order, so a split moves whole runs), or relabelling lazily on the next trace that meets an edge.
- **On the web the landing job runs inline**, in the frame it is sent: about 3.8 ms for a 256² four-layer map (natively spawn to landed, frames stepped back to back; the landing guard's median of five, 2026-11-01), and about 60 ms for a 1024² one (56.7 to 60.4 ms). A web game should show a loading screen over a big map's landing.
- **Every occluding group keeps its chunks' shadow edges** (for a chunk rebuilt only at its borders when a neighbour changes, `occluders.rs`): 20 bytes an edge, shrunk to fit as kept, paid by bounded and static maps as well as streamed ones. The guard's cave rock holds at most 141 edges a chunk (about 3 KB), so a 1024 x 1024 map of it keeps up to about 3 MB; a chunk of scattered single tiles holds about 2,000 (about 40 KB), so a 1024 x 1024 map of them about 40 MB. Proposed, if it shows: keep the edges only for the chunks of unbounded groups, or only for chunks whose neighbours changed recently.
- **Lighting has no spatial index.** Every lit frame gathers every map occluder, and each shadowed light loops over all of them, however far away. One light over the 1024² cave map costs about 30 µs more than the unlit map. Proposed: index occluders by chunk (they are per chunk already), so a light reads only the chunks its reach meets.
- **A 1024² four-layer map's first build** takes about 60 ms on the worker (56.7 to 60.4 ms spawn to landed) and 2.4 to 2.7 ms in its landing frame, which spawns its 2,900 parts and chains and inserts the colliders. This is beyond the spec's 256² target, and a hitch a loading screen should cover.
- **The chunk arena holds at most the GPU's largest buffer:** 256 MiB under the engine's limits, 2,097,152 tile instances of 128 bytes, the chunks in view with those drawn in the last 120 frames. A view meeting more (a 1024² four-layer map seen whole at about zoom 0.12 at 1080p, or a minimap camera over a big map) first frees the chunks drawn least recently, then draws only the chunks it meets first, and the dev log warns once. Proposed: the compact instance of about 16 bytes (eight times the tiles in the same buffer), or several arena buffers.
- **A tileset's padded image must fit one texture:** 8,176 texels a side under the engine's limits (the GPU's 8,192, less the atlas's border). That is about 3,800 tiles of 64 px, 15,876 of 32 px, or a collection of 196 images up to 256 px (36 up to 512 px). A larger one draws nothing, warned. Proposed: cells per size class, or several padded pages per tileset.
- **Enabling a disabled map rebuilds its collision and shadows on the main thread, in that frame's fixed step and `PostUpdate`.** A disabled map takes away its colliders and empties its shadow chains, and enabling it builds every grid and every chunk's chains again from its tiles, as a first build, but not on the builder thread. Not measured on its own: the same work off the frame (a landing job) takes 3.8 ms spawn to landed for a 256² four-layer map, which bounds it, and about 60 ms for a 1024² one, as does `reload_timing`'s main-thread rebuild of every chunk (76.9 ms). So parking big levels with `Disabled` and enabling them costs a visible hitch. Proposed: keep a disabled map's grids, traces and chains and disable their parts (physics already skips a disabled collider), or send the rebuild to the builder thread as a landing is.
- **An edit walks every chunk of a layer whose version moved, twice.** `changed_chunks` compares each chunk's version with the last seen for a layer whose grid version moved, once for collision and once for shadows, so an edit costs a check of all the layer's chunks, not only the chunk it wrote. A 1024 x 1024 layer's 1,024 chunks make that part of the 17 to 66 µs above. The two walks cost about 3.5 ns a chunk together (release, measured once: 35 µs at 10,000 chunks, 200 µs at 50,000, 341 µs at 100,000), so on an unbounded map grown past about 30,000 chunks an edit passes the spec's 100 µs on the walks alone. Proposed: a list of the chunks written since the last look.
- **An unbounded layer's edges cost chains in the chunks past them.** An unbounded group has no right or bottom border, so the borders right of and below its last cells belong to the next chunk, which builds chains of its own: the 1024² four-layer map built infinite spawns 48 more children and chains than its bounded twin (2,930 and 2,426 against 2,882 and 2,378), 1.2 to 2.5 µs a frame. Proposed, if it matters: let a chunk own the right and bottom borders of its cells when the next chunk holds none.
- **An LDtk project of many tiny levels counts more than its cells against the project's cap.** Each level's grids count whole engine chunks of 32 x 32, so a 16 x 16 level counts 4 times its cells and an 8 x 8 one 16 times: a project of thousands of tiny rooms meets the 2^27 cells sooner than its cells suggest. Proposed: count a level's grids by the cells they hold once grids are allocated lazily by chunk.
- **Collision reaches 186,411 cells from cell (0, 0) on either axis.** There is no cap on a grid's size: a map's collision groups hold the chunks their layers have (a 10,000 x 64 runner level collides end to end, where 6a capped a group at 8,192 cells a side). Outline points stay `i32`, 11,520 a cell, and every point a cell within that reach works out (its edges and the borders past it) is under 2^31: about 3 million px of 16 px tiles, or 50 minutes of a runner at 1,000 px/s. A cell past it does not collide: `TileColliders::set` refuses it, a map's chunk build leaves it out (`put_chunk`), and the dev log warns once a grid. On an unbounded layer such a tile still draws and casts its shadow. Colliders are placed at their chunk's origin with chunk-relative points, so a collider's own points are as exact far out as near the origin; its position is Rapier's, in f32 metres, about 0.2 px apart at that reach with 100 px a metre. Proposed if a game needs more: i64 outline points.
- **Shadows reach all but the first and last chunk of `i32`'s cells on each axis.** A group of unbounded layers builds a chunk's chains only where its cells, the column left of it and the row above are cells `i32` holds (`SHADOW_CHUNKS` in `tilemap/occluders.rs`), so a tile within 32 cells of `i32::MIN` or `i32::MAX` casts none. Far before that a tile's place in f32 pixels loses whole pixels (past about 2^24 px).
- **A map's tile layers take at most 2^27 cells together** (512 MiB of cells), and an LDtk project's levels the same together (an IntGrid layer's values counting once more, empty layers counted), checked from the layers' sizes before any is decoded or allocated, a layer at most 2^26. Cells are counted as the engine allocates them, since a grid makes each 32-cell chunk whole where a tile goes: a bounded layer counts every chunk it spans (ceil(w / 32) x ceil(h / 32) x 1,024), and an infinite map's layer the chunks its chunks reach, or the cells they hold if more. So a thin 1 x 2^26 layer, which the sparse grids would have let a small zlib file fill to 8 GiB, is refused, as are scattered one-cell chunks, and a small hostile file cannot ask for gigabytes. The spec's maps still load: an 8192² layer and a 1024² map of 128 layers count as before, and the 10,000 x 64 runner counts 641,024 cells for its 640,000 (its last chunk column taken whole). A landing copies the grids once more, so a map at the cap peaks near 1 GiB. A project of many big levels, all held at once, meets it first. Proposed: LDtk levels loaded one at a time (a level asset each), and grids allocated lazily by chunk.
- **An infinite map's chunks lie within the pixels an `i32` holds.** A Tiled chunk whose cells' places in pixels (their outlines' box on the map's grid) pass what an `i32` holds, or whose cells do, fails its load, naming its layer and place: with 16 px square tiles, cells within about 134 million of (0, 0); on an isometric grid a cell's x is its column less its row in half cells, so the reach runs along the diagonals. Far before that a tile's place in f32 pixels loses whole pixels (past about 2^24 px), and collision stops at 186,411 cells (above).
- **A map in a Tiled world is placed by its tile origin**, the world's x and y being where its pixel (0, 0) sits and its rectangle its cells' box on its grid from there (`MapGrid::bounds_of`, the grid built from the file's header as the load builds it): a bounded map's every cell (an isometric map's diamond's box), an infinite map's tiles' bounds (the loaded map's `bounds()`, worked out by the same function), whatever width and height the world lists it with; one with no tile takes its origin cell. Tiled documents no rule for infinite or non-orthogonal maps in worlds, and its world loading does not run headless (checked again with 1.12.2), so this reading is the engine's, unmeasured. A hexagonal map whose side is longer than its tile has outlines the row step tall, past the tile box Tiled stands its tiles on and sizes the map by, so its rectangle reaches a few pixels below Tiled's map (4 px for side 40 on 32 px tiles). The world loader reads and decodes every listed map to tell an infinite one and find its tiles, so a world's load costs its maps' parses once more, and a save of any listed map loads the world again.
- **Art that fills its cell's box but not its outline draws chunk by chunk.** A plain layer keeps Tiled's order across tilesets and chunks only where a tile leaves its cell's box (its outline's bounding box). On isometric, staggered and hexagonal grids neighbouring cells' boxes overlap, so art that fills the box (a rectangle in a diamond's box) overlaps its neighbours without leaving its own, and where two such tiles meet across a chunk border, or are of two tilesets, the later in the engine's order draws over (chunk by chunk, tileset by tileset), not the later in Tiled's. Art shaped like its cells, the editor's usual, overlaps nothing there. Proposed, if a game needs it: take the in-order path for every layer on those grids, at its cost (below), or a tileset property saying its art fills its box.
- **Art that leaves its cells keeps Tiled's order with more draws.** Tall art of one tileset on an orthogonal or isometric map draws as art that fits, a draw a chunk; tall art of several tilesets a draw per tileset per row per chunk; art wider than its cell (or moved across by its tileset's offset, or a turned hexagonal tile), and tall art on staggered and hexagonal maps, a run of one tileset per row per chunk, each a draw of its own. At 1080p over four 256 x 256 layers of 32 x 16 isometric cells (one measurement, a busy machine, the camera panning): art that fits drew 96 tile draws a frame and its render step read about 250 µs; 32 x 48 art of one tileset 96 draws and about 370 µs; 48 x 48 art 4,080 draws and about 730 µs; 32 x 48 art on a staggered map 3,072 draws and about 580 µs; and with each diagonal or cell of a second tileset, 4,000 to 6,100 draws and 0.8 to 1.2 ms (5,120 draws on 16 px orthogonal cells of 16 x 32 art). Proposed: draws that interleave only the bands next to a chunk border, or chunks a camera sees whole merged into one list in paint order.
- **On a resolution-independent target an isometric map of odd sizes draws its tiles at the exact half pixels** its grid places them on, filtered; Tiled's raster rounds them, as the pixel-art target does.
- **An oblique layer skewed down its columns sorts cell by cell, a layer with `sort_offset` tiles item by item.** Cells that share no sort point (an oblique grid with `skew.y`) and tiles with a `sort_offset` are draw items of their own, so a dense y-sorted layer of either costs up to a draw a cell. Such layers are rare, and a game y-sorts only the layers characters walk among; batching items that share a sort point is not done.
- **Hexagonal maps neither collide nor cast shadows yet.** Their tile layers default `collides` to false, a Tiled hexagonal map with a tile layer whose `collides` or `occludes` property is true fails to load naming it, and a tile layer added to one in code starts not colliding. Collision and shadows trace a square lattice, which hexagons do not make.
- **An LDtk layer splits into 16 layers at most** for its stacked, offset and translucent tiles: each is a grid the size of the level and a draw a chunk. Past 16 the tiles left over draw in the nearest sub-layer, at a nearby offset or depth, warned once a layer. LDtk 1.5.3's samples ask for up to 44 in a rule-heavy layer, which then snaps 28 tiles. Proposed: sub-layers sized to their tiles' bounds, or chunks holding several tiles a cell.
- **Editor features drawn otherwise, warned once a file:** Tiled's tile render size "grid" and fill mode, collection tiles drawing part of their image, render orders other than right-down on orthogonal and oblique maps (Tiled draws the other grids in an order of their own whatever they say: rows, and on isometric maps diagonals), top-down object layers whose tile objects overlap out of order by y, polylines among collision shapes; LDtk's entity tile render modes other than stretching, and parallax scaling. Each could be read: sub-rectangles as a tile's source rectangle in the padding, render order and draw order as a sort, the render size as a scale in the chunk build.
- **Each chunk a stream lands or unloads builds its collision and shadows in that frame**, on the main thread: about 65 µs each in the guard after the perf round (its streaming systems' profile: the collision rebuild about a third, the shadows a quarter, Rapier's new polyline for each part reshaped a sixth, the stream's look a tenth), so the budgets (two a frame, landing and unloading rarely in one frame) bound what streaming adds to a frame: about 0.14 ms to the median frame landing chunks, over the frames doing nothing. A part holds the loops starting in its chunk, so a vast cave whose outline starts in a chunk next to a landing rebuilds that whole outline's polyline. Proposed, for the margin: build a landed chunk's interior shadow edges and its parts' shapes on the chunk builder thread (the shadows' `rebuilt_borders` can take a chunk's interior edges from anywhere, the classification would have to go with them), and keep the stream's look from walking the load areas in a frame whose areas and chunks did not change.
- **A chunk's first draw costs its frame.** A column or row of chunks coming into a 1080p view (three to five chunks of two layers) costs about 0.1 ms to build and upload on the busy machine, in a frame of its own or one that also lands chunks. It is the static map's first-draw cost (see the landing), streamed. Proposed: the compact instance, or building the chunks just outside the view in the frames before they show.
- **The guard's held 99th percentile is met on a quiet machine, over a floor that is the machine's.** The frames that stream nothing read +250 to +295 µs at their 99th percentile in the quiet record, mostly in the render step, and the empty frame's own 99th percentile sat 117 to 187 µs over its median; on the busy afternoon those frames read about +360 µs. The held figure (357 to 427 µs quiet) leaves 70 to 140 µs of room, so contention like the afternoon's (a virtual machine, a game on the GPU) can still miss it.
- **A teleport fills its view in about 11 frames and lets go of the old place over about 29** (two chunks of each a frame: more than eight chunks wait to unload, so the frames landing unload too), the chunks left behind resident, drawn and colliding meanwhile, so the resident chunks are past the radii for those frames and within them once they have gone. Hold a loading screen until `stream_ready`, as the skill says. A crossing's unloads wait out its landings, so up to nine chunks past the unload distance stay resident for up to nine frames (65 resident at most where 62 were), and while chunks keep landing at most eight do, whatever the unload budget.
- **A streamed Tiled infinite map keeps its hand-made chunks resident.** Every engine chunk any layer's Tiled chunk touches is the file's and never unloads, with its drawing, colliders and shadow chains, so a big hand-made area costs its memory however far the anchors go (the file's chunks count against the map's cells cap as it loads, which bounds it). Proposed, if a game needs it: unload the file's chunks too, bringing them back from the asset.
- **The file wins in its chunks, and a save game keeps a copy.** A hot reload brings a streamed map's file chunks to the file, dug tiles back, as for any map. Their changes go into `SavedChunks` only through `save_changed_chunks` and as the map's root goes (they never unload), counted from the stream's first frame, so a chunk a reload changed counts as changed too and goes in with the file's tiles; a stream starting with a save puts its copies back over the file, so a designer's later change to such a chunk does not show in that save game. A coin collected in a file chunk counts as a change until a reload, which forgets the collected class tiles the file left as they were, so a save written after the reload brings the coin back. A save that adds, removes or reshapes a tile layer unloads every generated chunk and streams it again, over a few frames.
- **Saved chunks keep what a chunk held, not every change.** A chunk a player changed goes into the stream's `SavedChunks` whole, its cells and its living class tiles, a few hundred bytes a layer once serialized, and the store never forgets a chunk by itself, so it grows with every chunk changed over a game (a runner that keeps one, with a coin collected in each chunk, keeps every chunk passed: such a game keeps no `SavedChunks`, or removes the chunks behind it). Not kept: IntGrid values (a game's on an unbounded layer; LDtk levels are bounded), and a change to a class tile's `MapTile` the game made in place (its tile changed, an opened chest): a chunk changed otherwise keeps the tile the `MapTile` names then, but that change alone does not count. A chunk goes in only as it unloads or as the map's root goes, so a save game written during play without `MapsView::save_changed_chunks` first leaves out the chunks changed round the player. A save is read back whole, each layer's cells inflated to at most a chunk's: a hostile save of many tiny layers holding a tile each costs about 4 KB of memory a layer, around 100 times its size. Proposed: refuse a saved chunk holding more layers than a map can have tile layers (or a fixed count), or keep a layer's cells packed until its chunk lands.
- **Hot-reload unloads skip the unload budget.** A save that reshapes a streamed map's tile layers, claims generated chunks for the file or drops file chunks unloads every chunk it touches in the frame the save lands, `unload_per_frame` aside: a hitch while developing, never in a release build.
- **A `ChunkOwned` entity goes only with the chunk it names.** One tagged with a chunk not resident, or spawned from a `ChunkEvent::Loaded` read after its chunk had unloaded again, stays until that chunk next lands and unloads, or the map's root goes. An entity that moves keeps the chunk it was tagged with, and goes when that chunk unloads, wherever it is. Proposed: a tag that follows the entity's position, or a dev-log warning for a tag naming a chunk not resident.
- **A chunk the file newly claims keeps its old `SavedChunks` entry.** When a save of the map makes a chunk hand-made where a player had changed the generated one, the store keeps the player's copy, and the next stream of the map (a new root, or a save game loaded) puts it back over the hand-made chunk. Proposed: forget the entries of the chunks a reload claims.
- **Streaming on the web is unmeasured.** The generator runs inline there, within `web_budget` a frame; neither its frames nor the landings and unloads have been timed on wasm.
- **On other grids a stream may take a chunk whose box, not its cells, reaches its area.** An isometric, staggered, hexagonal or oblique chunk's cells make a diamond, a strip or a parallelogram, so a stream takes the chunks of the chunk range over its areas whose boxes (the boxes round their cells) meet them: every chunk with a cell in them, and of the others those whose boxes reach in. On an isometric grid that is none in practice: a 1080p view grown by the default 512 px over 32 x 16 isometric cells takes 39 chunks on average (34 to 42 over 2,000 random places), each holding a cell in it, where the range holds 49 to 64; grown by the 1,536 px unload distance, 106 against 182 to 196. Staggered, hexagonal and oblique chunks' boxes reach further past their cells, unmeasured. What streaming costs on these grids is not measured yet.
- **The guard's world has no class tiles, owned entities or lights.** Its chunks are tiles and colliders alone, so what a game's class tiles, `ChunkOwned` entities and lit shadow chains add to a streaming frame is not measured.
- **Many anchors cost quadratically.** Each frame a wanted chunk is checked against every earlier anchor's area, and its nearness against every area, so the look grows with the anchors squared times their chunks (a camera's view counts once for the map, and once more for each placement of a layer with parallax or an offset). A few anchors cost nothing to speak of; hundreds would. Proposed: gather the areas into one set of chunks before the look.
- **A `NavigationGrid` over a streamed map treats unloaded chunks as open.** `rebuild_from_world` blocks the cells of the colliders there are, so a chunk not landed is walkable to it, and the grid does not follow the chunks as they land and unload. A game rebuilds it on `ChunkEvent`s and keeps paths within the resident chunks. Proposed: a grid that reads a streamed map's resident chunks, unloaded ones blocked.
- **Replacing a live stream's generator or store keeps the old chunks.** A `MapStream` put over another on a streaming root keeps the stream's state and the chunks the old generator made; only the chunks asked for from then come from the new generator, and the unloads from then go into the new store. The root's going saves its changed chunks too, into whatever store it holds then, so despawning the root or putting a `MapRoot` over it after the swap writes the old generator's changed chunks into the new store. To stream afresh, despawn the old root and spawn a new one with its `MapRoot` and `MapStream` together.
- **The keep window holds a padded tileset's memory** for 120 frames after the last map using it goes. A level change with a different tileset briefly holds both paddings, and their textures (see the tilemap module notes).
