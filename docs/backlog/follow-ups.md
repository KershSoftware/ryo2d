## Particles

- Owner decisions (2026-11-09):
  - The design was left to the planner ("skip" the questions; spec `docs/specs/2026-11-08-particles-design.md`). The spec and plan were approved together, and the plan ran subagent-driven.
  - Merged after the scripted tour (133 pages; every non-particles page matches main but the four that already differ run to run; all 14 particles pages identical between two runs). The owner ran `./dev.sh web particles` and said "all looks good".
  - The owner approved, one-time, deleting `target/debug` mid-branch when the disk neared the 40 GiB floor.
- Rulings the owner may revisit (the spec's "Details settled at planning" records the design ones):
  - **Particles freeze only when their scene is not updating**, whatever the audio flag says, and keep moving through scene fades, as physics, tweens, trails and audio do: a leaving scene's particles keep moving under its fade. Freezing through fades is a small change back in `SceneManager::sync_audio_holds`.
  - **`skip_interpolation` also marks a particle emitter**, so a teleport never streaks, interpolated or not; `ecs/world_ext.rs` depends on `particles::emitter` for it.
  - **A particle reaching its lifetime dies where it stood** at the step's start, as the old system did, so a death effect can't appear past a wall.
  - **The bench fails if any frame figure is slower than the scene-driven system's** (669.3, 704.8 and 285.3 µs, this machine's same-session medians), besides the spec's ceilings. A slower machine can fail them.
  - **`explosion` and `dust` drag at 1.0**, what they effectively did (the old `with_drag` clamped to 1). Effect drag itself is unbounded.
  - **The demo's repeating bursts fire at once**, not one interval in (`with_start_delay` gives the old timing).
  - **Prewarm** skips the start delay, runs on first play and for a new effect (not after `restart`), plays sub-effects without messages, and past 600 steps takes 600 longer steps.
  - **The particle `Align` is `ParticleAlign` in the prelude**, since the UI's `Align` holds the name.
  - **A reload away from `Stick` releases stuck particles**, and a reload never changes spawn values of live particles.
  - **The collision grid** adds a small query margin (0.5 plus 1/65,536 of the coordinates' size), doubles its cell past 256 once it would exceed 65,536 cells, and finds huge or non-finite items from every query, all to stay bit-identical to testing everything.
  - **A collision's sub-effect plays on the surface** (radius out) with the velocity before the response, and a `Die` contact plays both `on_death` and `on_collision`.
  - **A `stopped()` emitter driven only by `burst` never reports `EmitterFinished` nor despawns** (only `play` and `restart` arm it; documented on `burst`). Letting `burst` arm it is a small change.
  - Sequencing and test calls: draw order arrived with the sheet frames, a 1e-4 s tolerance makes burst and rate counts exact on step boundaries, and the before/after benches ran old and new alternately from a `git archive` export under the scratchpad (not a worktree), deleted after.
- Limits recorded (`docs/backlog/particles.md`, Limits):
  - CPU simulation on the main thread, bounded by the 20,000 budget; collisions in ground space on storeys; `Local` effects don't collide or feel attractors; sub-emitters go one level deep.
  - Whole runs are culled, not particles; the cull box ignores glow and outline padding, particles spawned larger under an earlier version, and a fast stretched (`Align::Velocity`) quad's length.
  - A `Local` emitter not stepped (held, or speed 0) whose entity moves can be culled on screen. A mirrored emitter that turns or scales between steps draws its `Local` particles unmirrored between steps.
  - Prewarm is paid at once in the emitter's first step. Web effect files must be embedded by a hand-kept list. One distant or huge collider coarsens the grid.
  - An unscaled emitter over several steps in one frame gets lumpy inherited velocity, can finish and despawn in one frame before any scene reads `EmitterFinished`, and its turbulence attractor time stops while the game is paused or no game-time emitter steps.
  - A `speed_over_life` curve starting at 0 leaves particles no speed of their own for life. A `play_at` whose effect never loads (a typo'd path) leaves its entity waiting, a slow leak in a game that plays it on every hit: finish an emitter whose handle's `LoadState` is `Failed`, or warn once. `alive` goes stale on held emitters after `ParticlesView::clear`, and `PreviousGlobalTransform.skip` on unscaled interpolated emitters while paused.
  - The Before figures and every After round ran at a load of 4 to 10: measure again on a quiet machine.
- Deferred findings:
  - **Numbers and safety.**

    - The step's finiteness guard checks the translation only (`step.rs` ~235): a NaN rotation or scale gives NaN positions.
    - A file's `SpriteEffect` skips its constructors' clamps (NaN intensity, `Pixelate` block size 0 load silently; no panic): add `effect:` to `problems()`.
    - `sanitized` leaves a lifetime max below a raised min (harmless). A negative burst count is neither reported nor refused (it spawns nothing). `problems` reports two entries for a sheet with no columns.
  - **The step and module shape.**
    - `step.rs` is about 1,100 lines: an `emit.rs` and a `compiled.rs` when it is next touched. The two passes repeat their setup; `unit_axes` and `rigid_axes` (`space.rs`) near-duplicate; `Own { centre, noise_seed }` is built twice (`step.rs`, `sub.rs`); `store.rs` has a local `count` shadowing the imported function.

    - `is_physics_paused`, audio holds and particle holds could share one "scenes not updating" computation.
    - `StepState`'s kept `EcsSystemState` carries a change tick `clamp_schedule_ticks` never clamps (harmless until a `Changed` filter reads through it).
    - `noise_seed` is taken at the first seeded step, so a later effect version changing `seed` keeps it (as `pool.rng` does).
  - **Drawing.**
    - The atlas slot lookup is repeated between the run and sprite arms (`render/batch/gpu.rs` ~461 and ~601).
    - Sub runs fall back to their own z when the main effect is no longer compiled.
    - Aligned `Local` particles take their heading through `frame.world_rotation`, which may ignore a mirrored emitter: check and test.
    - `draw.rs` `collect`'s doc comment has one over-long line.
  - **Speed.**
    - Criterion's GPU-paced `particles_10k` reads about 4% over the old system (~1,037 against 995 µs), unexplained; one `--profile-time` run would settle it.
    - The per-particle loop jumps from about 3 ns to 36 ns at 4,000 to 20,000 particles (fourteen equal-length arrays possibly aliasing in L1): look before more pool work. `pool.rs`'s comment ("a few microseconds at most" below 4,096) is contradicted by the 73 µs measured at 4,000. One `retain` losing a large share at 6,000 particles is slower than the old loop (33% scattered, 14 to 26 µs).

  - **Files and reloads.**
    - The loader turns `AssetError::Cycle` into `Decode` (`loaders/particles.rs` ~186; an effect's `on_death` can name itself): pass it through and add a self-reference test.
    - A sub pool keeps old particles through a switch to a loading sub-effect only while something else owns the old one (`AssetId`, not `Handle`): hold a `Handle`, or record it.
    - A nested sub-effect in a file warns at load and again at play. Malformed-range errors give line and column but not the field. The `ColorRange` visitor is unused in production and duplicates the loader's pair parsing.
    - `CurveKey` writes a 2- or 3-tuple and reads `deserialize_tuple(3)`, so it round-trips in RON only.
  - **Tests missing or loose.**
    - Presets: three are spot-checked; nothing asserts the converted size ratios, circle thickness, `Line.length` or angular velocity degrees.
    - Forces and space: `Local` forces with an emitter away from the world origin; one burst's particles getting different radial values; the drawn `Local` frame at alpha below 1 and with a `DrawLift`; attractors on the unscaled pass; a mask-0 attractor on a layer-0 effect; turbulence time on the engine step. Noise and turbulence smoothness tolerances are loose, and one noise test compares a single point.
    - Sub-effects: `sub_spawns_repeat_with_the_seed` doesn't prove the parent pool's stream; a sub-effect's reload changing its look; an unscaled emitter's sub-effects.
    - Drawing: half of `render_layers_choose_the_cameras` can't fail (the white emitter draws on top at equal z); the order test's ±3 red tolerance couples it to whole-frame birth ages; unscaled and held pools' fractions are unit-tested only; `draw.rs` ~299 asserts a Flash id that equals the emission.

## Floors (6b, stage 6)

- Owner decisions (2026-11-07):

- Rulings the owner may revisit (the spec's "Floors: details settled at planning" records the design ones):
  - **A climber changes floor halfway along a run**, not where it leaves the stairs; its drawn height eases by its progress from the run's foot (the side farthest from its top along the run) to its top. A run meets its upper floor only at its top.
  - **A stair link costs 1e-3 of the grid's shortest step**, not nothing, so a path crosses a run once.
  - **Two public navigation constructors beyond the plan:** `FloorNavigation::new` and `NavigationGrid::on_floor`, so a game can build a floor graph without a tile map.
  - **Stair runs live in a crate-internal `MapStairs`**, not in `MapFloors`, so `MapFloors`' derived `PartialEq` stays honest.
  - **A higher floor's objects and class tiles carry its `Floor`** on every spawn path (the plan named none). A game that carries one (a key picked up) sets or removes its `Floor`.
  - **A floor a game or a respawn sets wins over the stairs** that step; `climb_stairs` runs before `gameplay::apply_kill_zones`, ordered through a crate-only re-export.
  - **Plain tile draws take their layer's floor in the tie**, so a higher storey's plain layer draws after ground-floor draws at its z.
  - **Every skill the branch changed was bumped once**, ryo-particles included though the plan's list did not name it.
- Limits recorded (`docs/backlog/tilemap.md`, Floors):
  - **Sorting on a storey:** a character standing on a y-sorted storey in the back half of a cell is cut at the waist by its own cell's block (rows sort at cell middles). The demo sorts its knight half a cell down while lifted. An engine rule needs the entity's floor in the row comparison, or breaks bridges (a character under a bridge must stay behind its deck): a later lever.
  - **Physics pays about 1% in a scene without floors** (the A/B in the record). Take the floor read out of the per-collider resync, and skip the floor systems, while no `Floor` exists.
  - A light brightens every floor under it (one light image per camera); a light on every floor draws at ground height; shadows draw at the light's lift.
  - Lines and the F2 overlay stay in ground space; particles collide by layer, not floor.
  - Floor searches are synchronous only; a link ignores a stair cell's own cost, so differing `Difficult` costs per floor can still cross a run twice.
  - A cell whose only tile on a storey is a class tile is a hole in its edge; picking and the footprint disagree on hidden layers and class tiles.
  - Any stair cell change rebuilds every run of the map (14.7 µs at 64 runs, about 60 at 256): a run's ends read the storey above, so a per-layer rebuild would be wrong.

## Hexagonal maps at speed

- Owner decisions (2026-11-06):
  - Merged with the hex static frame and edit targets met. The owner chose this pass at the hexagonal collision merge, ahead of floors.
  - The three refactors below (under "Refactors") were held back from the branch and logged here at the owner's choice, not done before the merge.
- Rulings the owner may revisit:
  - **Physics' change scans stay as they were.** The design made them conditional on the hex static frame reading over 46 µs after the propagation marker. One run read 38.2 µs, and the final three read +44.4 to +47.8. In one session the head read 42.1 to 48.9 against the marker commit's 42.2 to 43.9 and main's 56.5 to 57.7, so they were left. On a busy machine the static frame has 2 to 8 µs of headroom under 50.
  - **Despawn order.** Only hexagonal chunks, and `Group::clear`, despawn their chains newest first. Square, lattice and staggered chunks keep spawn order: an A/B on those grids was inconclusive (62 to 81 µs either way).
  - **`HEX_WHOLE` = 64.** Above 64 changed cells a chunk reads (its own and those across its sides), a hexagonal chunk is chained again whole, as on side-0 grids and past twice the last whole chaining's count. This is a cost threshold, not a correctness one: it routes streamed landings to the whole path.
  - **The ring-heavy bench cases each hold one median over both their edits** (cleared and set back, turned whole and back). A regression in one direction could hide behind the other's median. Today the 95th percentiles show both directions close.
  - **One commit subject overstates a cause.** "the record says the busy runs were load" (ebb61fff) claims a cause that was never measured. The next commit's text corrects it, and the history was left as it is.

  - **No loops on hex.** Hex grids trace no outline loops, since a hex outline has nothing to merge. A hex chunk is one collider part while it holds at most B = 128 boundary edges. Past that it splits into 16 x 16 quarters, then into 8 x 8 blocks, and nothing merges back until the map reloads. An edit rebuilds at most four parts of sides, plus the part of rings holding a cell whose rings changed.

  - **Bounded borders.** On a bounded grid, a side between an inside cell and an outside cell belongs to the inside cell, so there is no part or shadow block outside the grid.
  - **`HexCells`.** It gives exact corners for every cell size. `traceable()` refuses collision for cells taller than 16,384 px, cells under 2 px across, and zero steps.
  - **Own shapes on hex.**
    - A hex tile's own shape collides as closed rings in its chunk's parts of rings, apart from the sides, not merged with its neighbours. Parts of rings split by their own count, a block's past B into its 4 x 4 sub-blocks.
    - Rings wind clockwise after placement.
    - A self-crossing polygon collides as its hull.
    - A polygon collides as authored wherever it lies relative to its box. Square grids clip it.
    - A polygon whose cleaned corners match the cell's hexagon within 0.5 px collides as the whole cell.
  - **The doubled shadow.** A shape tile's outline lying on a neighbour's side casts twice, darkening shadows with partial alpha. This is a recorded Limit.
  - **Step costs.** Navigation off the square grid costs a step by its world distance over the shortest step. The same measure feeds the heuristic and the tie-break.

## Transform propagation (6b, stage 1)

- **The full walk's threshold was measured on leaves two levels deep** (`src/core/ecs/propagate.rs`, `FULL_WALK_SHARE`). A run takes the full walk once a fifth of the entities with a `Transform` changed (and more than 64).
  - Root movers (bullets, enemies) cost less per changed entity, since there's no walk up. Their break-even is nearer 40%, so between a fifth and that, the full walk costs about 1.8 times what the changed-subtree walk would. It is never worse than before 6b, apart from the change scan.
  - Isolated deep leaves break even earlier than a fifth.
  - Proposed: budget the steps taken walking up the hierarchy instead of counting changed entities, and add a root-movers bench beside `benches/foundation.rs`'s propagation benches.
- **Re-enabled entities are no longer healed by unrelated changes.** The old walk visited every entity whenever anything moved, so it repaired state left while an entity was disabled. Now a pooled interpolated entity that is acquired but not moved gets no `PreviousGlobalTransform` until it moves (one unblended frame when it starts).
  - Proposed: a `RemovedComponents<Disabled>` reader that seeds re-enabled entities. It would restore the healing.
  - It would also refresh a re-enabled non-physics entity whose parent moved while it was disabled; that entity is stale on main too.
  - Adding it would break `a_fifth_of_the_entities_changed_takes_the_full_walk`, which tells the two walks apart by that healing; give the test another witness then.
- **A hierarchy cycle gets no warning.** It now costs up to the world's entity count in lookups for each changed entity under it, each run. The depth bound stops it hanging. Cycles are documented as invalid.

## Audio (5b)

- Owner decisions (2026-10-24 to 2026-10-29):
  - Virtual voices are for loops only. A loop goes virtual, keeping its place silently, when it is out of range, cut by a ranking rule (`StopQuietest`, `StopFarthest`, `StopLowestPriority`) or cut by the bus cap.
  - Settings saved before 5b are converted once to the new volume curve (v_new = √v_old, same dB), with a `format_version` in `system.json`. Files written while the branch was being hand-tested are converted too.
- Rulings the owner may revisit:
  - **I1:** `PreventNew` and `StopOldest` end loops as before: refused or stopped, never virtual. This keeps patterns like "one bed at a time" and "keep one alarm" working, and departs partly from the approved "a loop cut by a limit keeps its place". Making those two rules virtualize loops too is a single mapping (`ends_loops` in `engine.rs`).
  - A bus `limit` on sounds that have no limit of their own ranks like the cap, so their loops go virtual. "One bed at a time" therefore needs the beds to carry `StopOldest`.
  - Under `PreventNew` or `StopOldest`, a returning loop takes only free room, never another voice's place.
  - When a limit cuts a loop, the loop's own sound's rule decides whether it ends or goes virtual, not the rule of the play that cut it. A footstep with a default `StopOldest` limit therefore sends the fire loop on its bus virtual instead of ending it.
  - Under `PreventNew` and `StopOldest`, a sound's virtual loops count against its own limit; under the ranking rules they count against nothing.
  - **P11:** a scene's exit stops its loops with their fade and lets its one-shots finish. A long stream played as a one-shot outlives its scene until it ends.
  - A play from a system through `Audio` is deferred to the system's sync point, so a refusal shows one sync point later.
  - A streamed `play` costs about 24 µs against the spec's 5 µs (guarded at 100 µs). The extra time is the decoder's lazy open (minor 10, accepted as an exception).
- Limits:
  - **Virtual voices.**
    - Under `PreventNew` or `StopOldest`, a sound's virtual loops count against its own limit. So a `PreventNew` loop that is out of range refuses a new play of that sound elsewhere; `StopOldest` is the idiom for "the newest wins".
    - A streamed comeback waits paused for its decoder, so it lands a few ms late (a frame or two on the mock).
    - Comebacks bypass `per_frame`.
    - A louder returning loop can still displace a one-shot under `PreventNew` or `StopOldest`. Wave 4's design displaces one-shots of any rule, and the "outside the ranking" ruling names only loops.
    - A stale displacement mark can displace a victim that came back while its comeback waited paused. `virtual_count` is one over until the next recount.
    - A clip reloaded while a loop is virtual uses the old clip's length for that loop's place until it comes back.
  - **Reloads and rebuilds.**
    - A loop moved by a reload or rebuild skips the target bus's limits and the 96-voice cap. It is held only to kira's room.
    - Reloading a clip that very many loops on one bus play can fill kira's 128-sound track. The refused loops play on the old data, with one warning.
    - A reload's switch or a rebuild's move of a playing stream steps in once its decoder starts (dev-time). Closing the gap means keeping the old copy playing until the new stream has frames.
    - A failed music switch during a rebuild whose definition changed tries no plain restart. The voice stays on the retired tree, with a warning, until a retry.
  - **Mixer tails.**
    - Send room holds only for rebuilds about 50 ms apart. Three rebuilds inside 50 ms, with four trees ringing, can leave a send bus without a track (warned).
    - A retired tree is dropped 50 ms after its fade starts. That covers device callback periods under 50 ms; a longer one could cut the fade.
    - Ringing trees keep the route volumes they had when retired; sliders don't reach them (documented, B12).
    - A loop left on an older retired tree logs its Stop under the just-retired tree's bus name. The two differ only if the mixer renamed that bus.
    - A 10 s delay tail moves about 3.8 MB per chunk while it rings.
  - **Streams.**
    - The failure-fade cap assumes kira's decoder had a full 16,384 frames ahead. A stream that fails just after a partway start, or just after a loop's wrap, can still be cut.

## Asset registry (5a)

- Owner decisions (2026-10-24):
  - The reload-hitch target is met in 5a, through two changes: images are uploaded over frames (14b), and shaders are compiled off the main thread (14c).
  - The determinism fix was made in 5a: draw-order ties and physics registration follow entity index (`ecs::entity_order`). The tour's baseline is Task 1's commit with that fix.
- Rulings the owner may revisit:
  - **R27:** a large image's first upload borders on the main thread, about 4 ms at 2048². Only reloads are prepared off the main thread.
  - **R29:** an `Assets<T>` store comes only from a world, with no public constructor. Tests use `testing::asset_world()`.
  - **R32:** an image's texture is never dropped for going undrawn. GPU memory is bounded by count, not bytes: 64 standalone textures (up to about 2.9 GB at 4K) plus 8 atlas pages. A full atlas clears its least recently used page.
  - **R33:** a `.wgsl` file must define `fn sprite` or `fn effect` to load as a `Shader`.
- Limits:
  - **Large first loads.** A large image first loaded with `load` during play uploads in the frame it lands (2–4 ms at 2048²). A shader first drawn during play, or a blend mode first used, compiles on the main thread in that frame and draws plain for one frame.
  - **Resized reloads.** A large image reloaded at another size is drawn in the UI at its natural size from the landing frame, so its old pixels are stretched for the spread's frames. If a `Modified` is missed (frames not drawn), the spread starts from the next draw, which keeps the 17 MB bordered copy until then. `MessageCursor::missed_messages` could make that fallback exact.
  - **Spreads.** They are served lowest key first, with no fairness across several large reloads. Frames during a shader compile cost about +0.1 ms. An engine runs up to three shader threads.
  - **Mip chains.** Large images keep their mips on the CPU for life (+5.7 MB at 2048²), so an evicted texture re-uploads without remaking them.
  - **Paths.** Asset keys are case-sensitive, so on a case-insensitive file system a path typed in another case loads but never hot-reloads.
  - **Embedding.** An added asset has no path, so an embedded UI file cannot be imported by another; on the web, embedded imports need a `MemorySource`. `Theme::parse` is crate-only.
  - **Watching.** A folder created after startup is never watched. notify's dropped and rescan events are ignored. `Pending::record` is O(n²) for a large burst.
  - **Events.** A failed asset, or a load dropped before it lands, is freed with no `Removed`. A missing image warns again in every scene that asks for it (bullet-hell's backdrop gives 4 lines). A `NotFound` fallback on native warns once per file.
  - **UI files.**
    - A file that imports a cycle it is not part of reports only the cycle, not its own position.
    - A cached `parsed_check` error is permanent for that parsed document; key it on the imports' newest version.
    - Two brace cases are skipped at check time and load at build: a literal `{` inside a template body, and a brace path passed from the root as a template argument.
    - `check-ui` lists image issues after build issues, and files outside `assets/` fail as "not an asset path".
  - **Uploads.** Scenes have no query for a pending upload; only `HeadlessEngine::image_upload_pending` exists. A reloaded image that is not drawn keeps its old pixel `Arc` in the texture registry until it is drawn again or freed (check after 14b).
  - **Sibling query order.** It still follows query or command order, invisible in the tour, in composite tiles, world text, parallax, trails and lines, `push_changes`/`move_characters`, `track_disabled` re-enables and `forget_removed`.
