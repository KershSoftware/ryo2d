# AGENTS.md - Tilemap Module

Tile maps from Tiled and LDtk (Tiled's files of every grid, orthogonal, isometric, staggered, hexagonal and oblique, and maps built in code on any of them, `TileMap::with_shape`: a map and its layers carry their grid, and its drawing, class tiles, picking and the board tools (`MapsView::grid`), streaming and hot reload go through it, as do collision and shadows: isometric, staggered and oblique maps collide and cast shadows along their cells' outlines, traced in the grid's square lattice, and hexagonal layers collide and cast shadows along their hexagons' sides and their tiles' own shapes; sparse, signed grids, so Tiled's infinite maps load and maps built in code with `TileMap::infinite` grow wherever a tile is written: `docs/backlog/tilemap-features.md`). Design: `docs/specs/2026-10-29-tilemap-design.md`; using it from a game: the `ryo-tilemap` skill; what it costs: `docs/backlog/tilemap.md`. The module holds the data model, the Tiled (JSON and XML) and LDtk readers as registry loaders, maps spawned as entities (landing whole, worked out on a thread of their own), reading and editing them (`MapsView`, `ctx.maps()`) with Wang terrain, their collision and 2D shadows kept in step with their tiles, infinite maps streamed from a game's generator round the cameras and anchors (`stream.rs`), worlds of levels, hot reload, and the padded tilesets they draw from. Drawing is the render module's: tile layers from cached chunks (y-sorted ones row by row among the entities), image layers as one repeating quad per view, tile objects as sprites (`render/tiles.rs`; render module notes, Tile chunks, and Image layers and tile objects).

## Where it runs

- `PreUpdate`, chained after the asset pump (`register_engine_systems`): `spawn_maps` (world levels resolved; ready maps' landings sent to the `Worker`; landings that came back land whole, with `MapEvent::Spawned`; failures reported once) and `reload_maps` (spawned maps brought to saved files, `MapEvent::Reloaded`), then `stream_maps` (streamed maps unload, ask for and land chunks round their anchors: `stream.rs` below), then `follow_chunk_swaps` (class tiles follow the chunks loaded and unloaded whole on unbounded layers: `spawn.rs` below). So a map whose asset lands this frame starts landing this frame, and starts streaming once it has landed, a save is followed in the frame it lands, and the class tiles of the chunks a stream landed or unloaded follow in that frame, all before the scenes' update.
- `FixedSet::Movement`: `collision::sync_map_colliders`, before physics' `rebuild_tile_colliders`, so an edit made in `update` collides in that step. `MapsView::navigation` runs it too, ahead of the step, from wherever a game takes a grid (`view.rs` below). Then, chained, `stairs::sync_stair_runs` (each map with floors' runs of stairs brought up to its tiles) and `stairs::climb_stairs` (climbers change floor on them), both before gameplay's `apply_kill_zones`, so a respawn wins over the stairs in a step, and a floor changed on stairs is filtered by that step's physics (`stairs.rs` below). After `FixedSet::Transforms`, before `Trails`: `floors::lift_floored`, so a trail spawned on a floor records its first point lifted. `PostUpdate`, before `PostUpdateSet::Transforms`: `occluders::sync_map_occluders`, so an edit casts its shadow that frame; after it, before `PostUpdateSet::Camera`: `floors::lift_floored`, so an entity on a floor draws lifted where it stands that frame (`floors.rs` below). All from `register_tilemap_systems` (`mod.rs`).
- The render step: `render/tiles.rs`'s `TileDrawer` (engine-owned) pads each spawned map's tilesets (`PaddedCache`, a world resource the landings share), builds changed chunks into the batch's chunk store and gathers the visible ones. A layer above the ground floor draws lifted by its floor's height (`MapFloors::lift`, added to the layer's transform as it stands in ground space), its tiles, image and tile objects where Tiled draws them (`a_floor_s_tiles_draw_lifted_where_tiled_draws_them`, against Tiled's render of `floors.tmj`), culled where they draw; its rows and tile objects sort where they stand, and at one ground y the lower floor draws first. On a map with floors every y-sorted layer draws at the first one's z (`edit::y_sort_z`, shared with `MapsView::y_sort_z`); a map without floors draws as before (render module notes, Draw Order).
- The map builder thread (`jobs.rs`, `ryo2d map builder`): each landing's colliders, shadows and missing paddings. Where there is no thread (the web) a landing is worked out in the frame it is sent.
- The chunk builder thread (`jobs.rs`' `Worker` again, `ryo2d chunk builder`, kept by `stream_maps`): every streamed map's generator calls, so a chunk never waits behind a landing. Where there is no thread (the web) a chunk is generated in the frame it is asked for, within its stream's `web_budget`.
- The world holds no map state of its own beyond the assets' stores, `Messages<MapEvent>`, `Messages<ChunkEvent>`, `PaddedCache` and `WangWarned` (`wang.rs`): everything else is on the map's entities (a stream's chunks in `StreamState` on its root), and the chunk worker with the jobs out is `stream_maps`' own (a `Local`).

## What exists

- `tile.rs`: `Tile` (tileset index, id, `TileFlip`) and `TileFlip` (H, V, D bits as Tiled stores them; `rotated_cw` uses Tiled's table). On a hexagonal map D's bit is a 60° turn (`HEX_ROTATE_60`, an alias) and a fourth bit, 8, a 120° one (`HEX_ROTATE_120`), after the flips (measured, formats note §6). `rotated_hex_cw` writes a half turn as H | V, so the twelve states without both turn bits cycle back after six turns; the four with both (the same half turn written otherwise) come back as the same turn in the other spelling, since sixteen spellings name the dihedral group's twelve (`hex_turns_come_round_after_six`). `quad_turn` (crate) is the turn a tile's flips make of its quad, the 2x2 drawing turns a tile's quad by and collision a hexagonal tile's shapes by, so both turn alike.
- `property.rs`: `PropertyValue` and `Properties` (insertion-ordered, typed getters; `int` accepts a whole float, `float` accepts an int).
- `tileset.rs`: `Tileset` (an `Asset`; atlas or collection; per-tile `TileData` with shapes, animation, probability, `sort_offset` (the reserved tile property, read by the Tiled loaders: a y-sorted layer moves the tile's sort point by it, in pixels, positive down; LDtk has none) and `stairs` (the reserved tile property: the floor a stair or ramp tile leads up to from its layer's floor, read as the layer's `floor` is, `None` unless set; LDtk has none); `WangSet`s) and `URect`, the engine's own texel rectangle (glam has none).
- `grid.rs`: `TileGrid`, a layer of 4-byte cells (0 empty; the four flip bits in 28..=31, H, V, D and the hex 120° turn, tileset in 20..=27, id + 1 in 0..=19; `four_flip_bits_round_trip_through_a_cell`) in `CHUNK_SIZE` (32) chunks keyed by signed chunk coordinate (`chunk_of` is `div_euclid`, so cell -1 is in chunk -1 and cell -33 in chunk -2) in a `FastMap`, each chunk's cells a boxed array with its version and dirty mark, and a list of the chunk coordinates kept in row order as chunks are made (one moved into place, several sorted), so `chunk_coords` and `drain_dirty` give row order without sorting each call. A bounded grid (`new(size)`, every bounded map's) holds the cells from (0, 0) to its size and refuses the rest, as before; an unbounded one (`unbounded()`, every tile layer of an infinite map) holds any cell. Chunks persist (the `TileGrid` and `TileMap::new` docs say so for games): a spawned bounded layer has every chunk of its bounds and collision, shadows and `Tracked` keep an entry for each, so a huge bounded map built in code is slow to land and costly, where `TileMap::infinite` costs nothing until written. Both make a chunk only when a tile is first written in it (clearing an absent cell makes none, and a clearing `fill` reads only the chunks there are: those of its region or the list of chunks, whichever is shorter), and a chunk stays once made, its cells emptied. **The engine alone takes a chunk out:** `unload_chunk` (crate) takes a chunk of an unbounded grid out whole and returns its cells (`None` for a chunk the grid lacks, and from a bounded grid, which refuses: only unbounded layers stream), the chunk gone as if never made (out of the list of chunks, its dirty mark with it, the extent shrunk to the chunks left) and the grid's version moved on as for any change (the extent is worked out again only when the chunk was on its edge, and then lazily: `extent` works it out from the chunks while it is stale, and `settle_extent`, crate, keeps it once after a run of unloads, so a teleport unloading n chunks costs O(n), not O(n²); `a_run_of_unloads_keeps_the_extent_exact_and_works_it_out_once`); `load_chunk` (crate) puts a whole chunk's cells in at once as one change, the chunk made or its cells replaced whole, made even when every cell is empty (holding none then), refused by a bounded grid and past the chunks `i32` holds (`an_unloaded_chunk_leaves_the_grid_as_one_change`, `a_loaded_chunk_comes_in_whole_as_one_change`, `a_bounded_grid_refuses_to_unload_or_load_chunks`). The grid keeps no tombstone of an unloaded chunk: every consumer keeps something for each chunk it read, and the chunks it keeps that the grid lacks are the ones unloaded (`kinds.rs`, `changed_chunks`), so consumers reading at different times each find an unload, and an unloaded chunk costs the grid nothing. A load is noted on its chunk (`loaded_since(version)`, crate, which later edits leave) and loads and unloads on the grid (`swapped()`, the version of the last one, 0 for a grid that has had none), for class tiles, which follow those and not edits (`spawn.rs`). A chunk tells whether its cells changed since its last whole load and a version (`changed_since(chunk, version)`, crate: its version past both; with 0, since the load, and a chunk never loaded, a Tiled infinite map's own, is told from the version its stream started at), and `fold_into_load` (crate) counts what changed since as part of the load, for the class tiles taken out of a chunk just loaded, which are not an edit; together they tell a stream which chunks a player changed (`a_loaded_chunk_tells_whether_its_cells_changed_since`). A tile `fill` that would make more chunks than a map holds (`MAX_FILL_CHUNKS`, 131,072: `MAX_MAP_CELLS` over a chunk's cells; counted by `missing_chunks`, crate, without making or walking more than the region or the list) changes nothing and returns 0, where `IVec2::MIN..=IVec2::MAX` on an unbounded grid was an out-of-memory abort (`an_unbounded_fill_making_more_chunks_than_a_map_holds_is_refused`; a bounded grid's fill is clipped, so never refused). The cap is read through `fill_cap()`, which in test builds is the thread's `FILL_CAP`: the refusal tests lower it to 4 and ask for 5 chunks, so were the guard ever gone they would allocate 20 KB, not exhaust the machine (a test of a memory guard must stay bounded without it). `set` looks its chunk up once (clearing reads it, a tile makes it). `write_rows` (crate) writes a block of cells (rows of encoded cells, `TileGrid::encode`) from a cell, one lookup per chunk it reaches, as one change, making only the chunks it puts a tile in and leaving out what lies outside a bounded grid or `i32`: the Tiled reader writes a bounded layer's data and each of an infinite layer's chunks with it (a lookup and a version per loader cell cost the `load` guard 1.50 ms against 1.23 to 1.27 before the grids went sparse; written by rows it reads 1.22 ms). A chunk can exist with no cells (`Chunk::cells` `None`): `mark_all_dirty` makes every chunk of a bounded grid's bounds that way (each landing calls it, so an empty chunk is versioned without 4 KB of zeros; so `chunk_coords` on a spawned bounded layer lists every chunk of its bounds), `clear` drops every chunk's cells, and `touch` and `assign` make them for chunks they move with no tile. `bounds()` is `None` for an unbounded grid; `extent()` is the bounds, or for an unbounded grid the smallest rectangle holding all its chunks (kept as chunks are made; it also holds whatever lies between them, chunks or not, and the end of the last chunk `i32` reaches stops at `i32::MAX`): never size anything from an unbounded grid's extent. `tile_extent()` (crate) is the smallest rectangle holding every cell with a tile, found from the chunks along the edges of those holding tiles (a look at each chunk for a tile, then the edge chunks' cells): what `TileMap::bounds()` and `MapsView::extent` give. A chunk never changed has version 0, and versions come from one process-wide counter (`VERSIONS`), as before. Edits mark chunks dirty; `drain_dirty` hands each changed chunk out once in row order. A new grid has no dirty chunk. A tile that does not fit a cell (tileset over 255, id over 1,048,574) is refused by `set`. `assign` (crate) takes another grid's cells (same bounds, or both unbounded), moving on only the chunks whose cells differ (or that its caller names), as one change: what a reload uses, so only the chunks a save changed rebuild; a bounded grid compares every chunk of its bounds, an unbounded one the chunks either grid has, emptying (and keeping) a chunk the other lacks. `assign_where` (crate) does so over the chunks a filter takes alone, the others keeping their cells and versions whatever the other grid holds: a streamed map's reload, over the file's chunks (`assigning_within_some_chunks_leaves_the_others_as_they_are`). `touch` (crate) moves on the chunks it is given, the cells as they are, for IntGrid edits. `chunk_cells` (crate) hands a chunk's cells out row by row, read with `TileGrid::decode`, for a builder walking a chunk without a lookup per cell (the renderer's `chunk_instances`, the classification in `kinds.rs`, class tiles, `edit::clear_parts`). Also crate-private: `chunk_versions` (each chunk with its version, no lookup each), `chunks_within(first, last)` (the chunks there are in a chunk range, in row order, found by walking the range or the list of chunks, whichever is shorter), `chunk_count`, `has_chunk`, and `id`: a number no other grid has, a clone included (`Clone` gives the copy a new one), so a consumer that keeps the id and version it last read a grid at finds what changed since as the chunks of a later version, while the grid is the one it read (`kinds::changed_chunks`). Debug prints the bounds and the chunks holding tiles, so grids holding the same tiles print alike.
  - **Consumers walk the chunks there are**, signed: the renderer (culling floors the view to signed chunks), collision and shadows (a group of bounded layers is bounded by their largest bounds, one with an unbounded layer unbounded), class tiles (`take_class_tiles`, per chunk) and `edit::clear_parts`; edits, Wang terrain and the cell conversions take any cell of an unbounded layer. An unloaded chunk is gone for every one of them: not drawn (the renderer visits the chunks a layer has, and its store frees one as any chunk undrawn for 120 frames: `an_unloaded_chunk_draws_nothing_and_the_store_lets_it_go`, `testing/gpu_world.rs`), not collided, casting no shadow, its class tiles despawned. Nothing is sized from an unbounded grid's extent: an unbounded layer with tiles at (0, 0) and (1,000,000, 1,000,000) lands holding two chunks (`an_unbounded_layer_with_a_tile_a_million_cells_out_lands_holding_only_its_chunks`), and every consumer agrees on the cells -1, 0, -32 and -33, shadows by their geometry too, each solid cell's outline edge by edge in the chunk owning it (`negative_and_chunk_edge_cells_agree_in_every_consumer`; both in `spawn/signed.rs`). A bounded map builds what it built before. IntGrid values (`IntGrid`, LDtk's) stay dense and bounded: LDtk has no infinite maps. An IntGrid is read in any chunk its grid holds cells of (`chunk_kinds`), and a build of collision or shadows visits every chunk of its IntGrid's cells besides its grid's chunks (`kinds::build_chunks`): a game that swaps `TileLayerData::tiles` for a new grid holding a few tiles keeps its IntGrid colliding and casting shadows through a full rebuild (the map disabled and enabled, a layer flag, a tileset's shapes), where the build read only the new grid's chunks and the rest stopped (`an_int_grid_whose_grid_a_game_swapped_collides_and_casts_shadows_after_a_rebuild`).
- `grid_shape.rs`: `MapGrid` (prelude, with `GridShape`, `StaggerAxis`, `StaggerIndex` and `CellOutline`: a shape, a cell's size and an `origin` added to every position it gives), the one geometry for every grid Tiled saves (orthogonal, isometric, staggered, hexagonal, oblique), which every cell-and-pixel question is to go through: a cell's centre, outline (clockwise on screen from its top-most point, the left one of a level top; 4 points, or 6 on hex maps, where a side of length 0 repeats its point), box (`cell_bounds`, `bounds_of` a `CellRect`) and `tile_anchor` (where a tile image's bottom-left stands, before its tileset's offset: the box's bottom-left, on an oblique grid the skewed corner below the cell's top-left), `cell_at`, and the board tools `neighbours` (clockwise from the top: the cell across the top edge, or where the top is a vertex the one across the edge right of it; an oblique grid keeps the unskewed order), `distance`, `line`, `range` and `ring` (on hex grids Red Blob's cube maths through odd-r/even-r offsets for axis Y and odd-q/even-q for axis X, the line nudged off edges; on square lattices steps over the four neighbours, so a range is a diamond, and a line breaks a tie at a corner along x first; staggered grids through their lattice). Crate-side: `chunks_over` (the storage chunks, `CHUNK_SIZE` cells a side by cell index, whose cells may meet a rectangle: every chunk of a cell overlapping it and of the cell under each of its points, at most a chunk more a side, never one missing; on an orthogonal grid its ends are the chunks of `cell_at`'s cells at the rectangle's corners, floors at both ends as the renderer culls, where an orthogonal stream's area leaves out a chunk it only touches at its far edge), `chunk_boxes` (`ChunkBoxes`: chunk (0, 0)'s box in exact `f64` and the steps to the next chunk's along x and along y, every grid repeating by whole chunks (32 lines keep a staggered grid's phase), so any chunk's box comes without working out its cells, as a stream and the renderer's culling ask of many each frame; `chunk_boxes_give_each_chunk_s_exact_box`, against `exact_box` far and near and against `chunk_bounds`, the box worked out from a chunk's cells, kept for the tests, which `chunk_bounds_and_bounds_of_hold_their_cells_exactly` holds to the union of the cells' outlines), `lattice` (public, as `Lattice` is, in the prelude: the square lattice collision and shadows trace in and square generators place their cells through, `to_pixel` its affine to the layer's pixels; the index space of orthogonal, isometric and oblique grids, and on a staggered grid the isometric lattice of its diamonds, indexed through `lattice_cell` and `grid_cell` so that lattice cell centres are the cells' centres; none on hex grids; procgen's `to_tile_map` and `to_terrain_map` put each generated cell at `grid_cell` of it), `paint_key` (Tiled's order in a plain layer: rows, then columns, a row's unshifted columns before its shifted ones on axis-X staggered and hex grids; on isometric grids the diagonals, x + y, each left to right, as Tiled's renders of overlapping pairs show (`measured-order.json`, written by `make-fixtures.js`; `paint_keys_order_overlapping_tiles_as_tileds_renders_do`); oblique grids draw as orthogonal ones, whatever the skew; its first two parts are the paint band the renderer splits a chunk's tilesets into), `raster_offset` (where Tiled's raster draws a tile placed on a half pixel by an isometric grid of odd sizes, from its anchor, by its tileset's tile size: on an odd cell width x rounded up, but floor plus one for a tileset whose tile size is wider or taller than the cell is wide; on an odd cell height y one pixel lower where it is whole, whatever the tile's height; measured: `measured-raster.json`, `pixel_art_draws_tiles_where_tileds_raster_does_on_odd_isometric_grids`; pixel art draws tiles there), `sort_band` (and `sort_band_of`, for a paint band), `sorts_per_cell` and `band_y`, which y-sorted layers sort by (a band's cells share a sort point: the diagonal c + r on isometric grids, half a row on axis-X staggered and hex grids, the row elsewhere; it sorts at its cells' centre, an orthogonal row at its bottom edge as before; an oblique grid skewed down along its rows has no shared point, so its band sorts at its column 0 cell's centre). `MapGrid::for_map` gives Tiled's origin: a bounded isometric map's cell (0, 0) top vertex at its height times half a cell across, rounded down to a whole pixel; zero otherwise. Every tile layer carries one (`LayerKind::Tiles::grid`, copied from the map's `TileMap::grid()` as `add_tile_layer` adds the layer and by the Tiled reader, a square one of the layer's own cell size from the LDtk reader; then `TileLayerData::grid`): class tiles stand at its `cell_center` (`spawn_class_tile`, and a reload's re-placed ones), `MapsView::grid` (`edit::layer_grid`) gives a layer's grid with its place in the world (`LayerGrid`, `view.rs`), and `world_to_cell` and `cell_to_world` go through it: the point into the layer's pixels and `cell_at` (`None` for a layer scaled to nothing or of cells with no size), `cell_center` out through the layer's transform, and a stream's `ChunkRequest::grid` is its first unbounded layer's (`a_code_built_isometric_map_places_class_tiles_and_picks_through_its_grid`, `negative_and_chunk_edge_cells_of_an_isometric_layer_place_and_pick_through_its_grid`, `a_streamed_request_carries_the_grid`). Drawing goes through it (`render/tiles.rs`: each tile at its `tile_anchor`, hex turns about each tile's own middle, a chunk's instances in `paint_key` order split into its bands, culling by `chunks_over` and each chunk's box from `chunk_boxes`, the whole grid in the layer stamp, pixel art's `raster_offset`, and a y-sorted layer's rows sorting at `band_y` of their `sort_band`, a cell alone at its centre on an oblique grid whose `skew.y` isn't 0 (`sorts_per_cell`), plus its tile's `sort_offset`; render module notes, Tile chunks and Draw Order). Collision and occluders group layers by it whole (`kinds::GridKey`) and trace in its `lattice` (`collision.rs` and `occluders.rs` below, Other grids), and `kinds::Placement` takes a tile's shapes into its cell's square through it. A stream's areas go through it (`stream.rs`, `Chunking`). On an orthogonal grid, every one of these, and drawing, is what it was with a bare cell size, bit for bit.
  - **Placement is Tiled's, measured:** `tests/fixtures/tilemap/tiled/measured.json` (written by `scripts/make-fixtures.js`' measure section: `tileToScreen` of 49 cells on each of 31 sample grids, where `toImage()` drew a tile at four cells of each bounded one, `screenToTile` round a hexagon, and the oblique draw order), against which the tests check every cell (`tile_anchors_match_tileds_measurements`, `odd_and_degenerate_grids_place_as_tiled_measures`). The findings are the formats note's section 7 (`docs/research/2026-10-31-tile-shapes-formats.md`): staggered and hex grids step in whole pixels, half a cell rounded down and a hexagon's slant rounded toward zero, so a 33 x 17 staggered cell is a 32 x 16 diamond and its tile stands on that box, reaching a pixel above it (`Lines` keeps those steps: `half`, `step`, `slant`, `depth`); a hexagon's corners come from `physics::HexCells` (whole pixels, exact for every cell size and every cell, which the collision tracer shares; only collision refuses cells past 16,384 px), `Lines` keeping the staggered diamonds; isometric grids step in half pixels from a whole-pixel origin; a hex side longer than the tile spreads the rows apart, the outline then taking no slant (rectangles in brick rows, still six neighbours).
  - **Exact arithmetic:** positions are worked in `f64` on whole and half pixels and rounded to `f32` once, so a vertex two cells share is the same point from either outline, and a point on an edge picks the same cell all along it (`floor_div` rounds a quotient down exactly). An orthogonal grid keeps the engine's `f32` maths to the bit (`an_orthogonal_grid_is_6a_s_rectangles`: `cell_center`, `tile_anchor`, `cell_bounds`, `cell_at`, `band_y`).
  - **Picking follows the outlines, what draws there** (`cell_at_round_trips_and_splits_shared_edges_one_way`, over cells -40..40 a side on every grid): on staggered and hex grids the band of rows (columns on axis X) by division, then which side of the slanted sides the point is on; floor division in the lattice elsewhere. A shared edge goes wholly to one cell: a slanted side to the later row (column), any other side to the later cell along its row, and on the other grids a side to the later cell along the axis it crosses. Tiled picks a hexagon by the nearest centre instead, which on hexagons of other than the regular side hands a sliver by each vertex to a neighbour (measured: (31, 22.5) on 32 x 32 hexagons of side 12 is inside (0, 0), and Tiled picks (0, 1)).
  - Degenerate grids (cells of no size, sides past the tile, skews folding the grid flat or over, cells at the ends of `i32`, points that are not numbers) answer without panicking (`degenerate_grids_answer_without_panicking`): index arithmetic is `i64`, results clamp to `i32`, and a line, range or ring allocates no more than 4,096 cells ahead (a far line is still as long as it is).
- `map.rs`: `TileMap` (an `Asset`) of `MapLayer`s (`LayerInfo` plus a `LayerKind` of tiles, objects or an image) over `TilesetSource`s (asset handle or embedded `Arc`). `LayerInfo::floor` (a `u8`, 0 the ground floor) is the storey a layer is on (`floors.rs` below); `LayerInfo::new` puts a layer on the ground floor, and a map built in code sets it as a Tiled map's reserved property does (`a_code_built_map_has_floors_too`). A tile layer is `LayerKind::Tiles { tiles: TileGrid, intgrid, grid: MapGrid }`. `shape` (a `GridShape`, orthogonal unless set) and `grid()` (`MapGrid::for_map` of the shape, `tile_size`, `size` and `infinite`) are the map's grid, which `add_tile_layer` copies into each layer it adds; `with_shape` sets the shape and moves the tile layers whose grid was the map's onto the new one (setting the `shape` field moves none: `every_layer_copies_its_maps_grid`). `infinite` marks a map whose tile layers are unbounded (a Tiled infinite map; `add_tile_layer` adds an unbounded layer to one; `TileMap::infinite(tile_size)` builds an empty one in code, grown by `MapsView` writes: `a_map_built_infinite_in_code_grows_where_a_game_writes` in `spawn/signed.rs` and the GPU test `a_tile_painted_far_from_the_origin_on_an_infinite_map_draws_with_a_camera_there`): its `size` is zero, and `bounds()` is the smallest rectangle holding every cell of its tile layers with a tile (`tiles_bounds`, the union of the grids' `tile_extent`s; empty with no tile), where it was the engine's 32-cell chunks around them, up to 31 cells wider a side, for games to use for camera limits and world rectangles (`an_infinite_map_s_bounds_hold_its_tiles_exactly`). A bounded map's is (0, 0) to its size. It reads the asset: a spawned map's edits change its layers' copies (`MapsView::bounds`). `file_chunks` (crate) is, for a Tiled infinite map, the engine chunks any layer's Tiled chunks touch, by row (the Tiled reader's `Builder` notes each chunk's span as it decodes it, bounded by the cells cap the chunks were counted against), which a stream leaves to the file; empty for every other map. `ImageLayer::image` is `None` for an image layer given no picture (Tiled saves a new one as `"image": ""`, or an `<imagelayer>` with no `<image>`), which loads and draws nothing (`an_image_layer_tiled_left_without_an_image_loads_empty`; it failed the whole map). `IntGrid::get` returns `None` for 0 as well as outside.
- `spawn.rs`: maps as entities. `MapRoot` (a `TileMap` handle, or an LDtk project and a level by identifier or iid; class tiles, IntGrid collision overrides, collision off, base z) on an entity whose `Transform` places the map's cell (0, 0): its top-left corner, an infinite map's tile origin. `spawn_maps` (`PreUpdate`, chained after the asset pump) lands each ready map whole (below), or sends `MapEvent::Failed` once per failure. `map_asset` resolves a root to its `TileMap`, `root_map` the same from the stores a system holds.
- **A map lands whole.** When a root's asset (and its tilesets) is ready, `spawn_maps` copies the asset's layers (each Tiled tile object given what its tile carries, `inherit_tile_data`: with no class of its own it takes its tile's, and its properties lie over its tile's, which hold the tile class's defaults; an object with a class of its own keeps the tile class's defaults under its own, where Tiled keeps only what the tile wrote; LDtk entities take nothing; done here because a map's external tilesets are not loaded while it loads, `a_tile_object_takes_its_tile_s_class_and_properties`, asserted against Tiled's own resolution), the root's settings, the class-tile table and the tilesets' `ShapeSource`s and stamps into a `LandingJob` for its `Worker`, and marks the root `MapLanding { job }`. The job works out the map's floors (`MapFloors::of` its layers' infos, `None` with every layer on the ground floor), takes the class tiles out of the grids' copies (marking every chunk dirty), and works out the colliders (`collision::prepare`: every grid classified, traced and packed, the packs' Rapier shapes built) and the shadows (`occluders::prepare`: every chunk's chains) for the layers as they will spawn, layers named by their place (entities are stand-ins until they spawn). When it comes back (polled every `spawn_maps`), `land` spawns the layers (each at `floors::ground_offset`: its offset, less its floor's height above the ground floor) and the objects' entities, inserts `MapIds` and `MapLayers` (and `MapFloors` where the job found floors, taking away any other), puts the colliders (with their `Traced`) and the shadows on (`collision::land`, `occluders::land`: entities relinked by place; `collision::land` spawns each higher floor's edge, a `MapFloor` child of the map), then gives the objects their components (observers run), spawns the class tiles, and sends `MapEvent::Spawned`: all in one frame, and the fixed step that frame (or the next) builds the parts from the traces before physics steps, so a body an object observer or a `Spawned` reader puts on the map lands on its ground (`a_body_on_a_map_reported_ready_never_falls_through`). Until then nothing of the map is in the world but its root, and `MapsView` reads nothing. A root disabled, despawned or given another root while its landing is out drops the result (`root_discarded` takes `MapLanding` too, and `MapFloors` with the rest of a landed map's state, and despawns its floors' edges, `collision::drop_edges`, so a root landing at once in its place, before a fixed step takes the old collision down, leaves none behind: `a_discarded_map_takes_its_floor_edges_with_it`). The arrival looks roots up with `Allow<Disabled>`, so a disabled one loses its `MapLanding` and is worked out again once enabled (`a_root_disabled_while_its_landing_is_out_lands_once_enabled`; looked up with the default filter, its result was dropped with `MapLanding` left on, and it never landed). A job that panicked is logged and worked out on the main thread. If that panics too, the root gets `MapFailed` holding `landing_stamp` (the versions of its map or LDtk project and of each tileset asset the map uses) and `MapEvent::Failed` is sent once; it is not tried again until one of those versions changes (`a_landing_that_always_panics_is_reported_once_and_not_retried`; before, it was sent again every couple of frames). A test's map makes its landing panic with the `PANICKING_LANDING` property (test builds only). A landing records the loads of the files it was worked out from (`reload::content_stamp`); one that comes back after a load of one of them landed (a save while it was out) is dropped and worked out again from the files as they are, so the map lands as saved, with one `Spawned` (`a_save_while_a_map_lands_lands_the_saved_map_once`; before, it landed the old map and never followed the save). It lands with a `MapApplied` (below). On the web (no threads) the job runs where it is sent, so the map lands in the frame its asset is ready. Layer flags set on layer entities (`collides`, `occludes`, properties) can only change after the landing, through the systems' rebuild path.
- `floors.rs`: storeys (spec "Floors: details settled at planning" in `docs/specs/2026-10-31-tilemap-shapes-design.md`). `MapFloors` (prelude, a component on the map entity when some layer is above the ground floor; none otherwise, so a map without floors lands, draws and collides as before: `a_map_without_floors_lands_as_before`): each floor's height (its lift) by number, `lift(Floor)` (`GROUND` zero, a floor no layer is on and `Every` `None`), `floors()` (ascending, ground first) and `highest()`; `MapFloors::of` (crate) works it out from the layers' infos bottom first: a floor's height is `(0, offset.y)` of its bottom layer, the first in draw order on it, so a storey's other layers keep their offset from it (`a_floor_s_height_is_its_bottom_layer_s_vertical_offset`), and its x offset stays in ground space. `ground_offset(info, floors)` (crate) is where a layer stands: its offset less its floor's height on a floor above the ground, its offset otherwise. Layers above the ground floor therefore stand in ground space, and everything a layer owns (objects, class tiles, collider groups targeting it, shadow chains) lands there through the hierarchy with no further work (`a_higher_floor_s_layers_stand_in_ground_space`): it sits where characters stand on that storey, and the drawer adds the height back (the render step, above). Its runs of stairs are not in it but in `MapStairs` beside it (`stairs.rs` below), so its derived `PartialEq` is the whole of it: reload's `replace` puts a new one only when the heights changed. `MapsView::floors` reads it; `MapsView::lift(map, floor)` gives the height as a world vector through the map's `local_to_world` as it is now (`transform_vector2`: a moved map's place adds nothing, a turned or scaled one turns and scales it; `GROUND` zero on any landed map; `a_floor_s_world_lift_follows_the_map_s_transform`), worked out by `world_lift` (crate), which `lift_floored` shares. LDtk maps have no floors (no layer properties; its world depths are levels). `MapFloor` (prelude, `{ floor: u8 }`) marks the entity holding a higher floor's edge (`collision.rs` below).
  - **Entities on floors draw lifted** (`lift_floored`, after transforms propagate: in each fixed step, after `FixedSet::Transforms` and before `Trails`, so a trail spawned on a floor records its first point lifted (`a_trail_spawned_on_a_floor_records_its_first_point_lifted`) and an emitter emits lifted from its next step, and in `PostUpdate`, before the cameras; two instances, each with its own change ticks and lists). An entity with a `Floor` (not `ParentPlaced`, so a map's own collider parts and shadow chains stay out of the scan) stands in ground space, and draws lifted by its floor's height on the map under it: the first map with `MapFloors`, in entity order, whose box of tile layers' cells (`MapGrid::bounds_of` of each layer's `TileGrid::extent`, through its `Transform`, in the map's pixels: `MapArea`) holds its `GlobalTransform`, through that map's `GlobalTransform` (`world_lift`), so a turned map lifts along its own up (`a_turned_map_lifts_its_floor_entities_along_its_own_up`); zero off every map with floors, and for a floor the map lacks or `Every` (`an_entity_off_every_map_with_floors_draws_unlifted`, `an_entity_on_every_floor_draws_unlifted_and_sorts_with_the_ground`, `where_maps_with_floors_overlap_the_first_lifts`). A disabled map is under nothing. It writes `DrawLift { lift, floor }` (`ecs/floor.rs`, crate) with `set_if_neq` on the entity and every descendant, walking `Children` with a `Local` stack and stopping at a descendant with a `Floor` of its own, which its own walk lifts (`a_floor_entity_s_children_draw_lifted_with_it`); its queries see disabled entities, so a put-away child's lift is written once and goes with its parent's (`a_disabled_child_keeps_its_lift_in_step_with_its_parent`); on the ground floor, unlifted, it takes `DrawLift` away instead, so the ground floor has none. A run takes the entities whose `Floor`, `GlobalTransform` or `Children` changed, and every entity with a `Floor` once a map with floors' `GlobalTransform` or `MapFloors` changed, a `MapFloors` went, or a map with floors lost `Disabled` (`RemovedComponents<Disabled>` filtered to the maps with floors, so a still entity spawned or rescanned while its map was put away takes its lift when the map is enabled again: `a_still_entity_takes_its_lift_when_its_map_is_enabled_again`); an entity that lost its `Floor` (`RemovedComponents<Floor>`), and a lifted one without one whose `ChildOf` changed or went or that was given children, take the lift of their nearest ancestor with a `Floor` (its subtree walked again), or lose it with their descendants (`removing_a_floor_drops_the_lift_from_the_entity_and_its_children`, `a_child_moved_between_floored_parents_takes_its_new_parent_s_lift`). With no entity on a floor and none lifted it returns after two empty looks (the removal readers cleared), so maps and entities without floors pay nothing; its lists are `Local`s (`Lifting`), so a warm run allocates nothing (`a_frame_with_entities_moving_on_floors_allocates_nothing`, `tests/tilemap_alloc.rs`, against frames where they stand still). On a map with floors a higher floor's objects and class tiles keep that gate open, and each run is a change-tick check per entity with a `Floor`, every fixed step and every frame. Sorting keeps the ground: the draw sites add the lift, and `DrawOrder::floor` ties on it (`lifting_never_moves_an_entity_s_sort_point`; render module notes, Placement). **Limit:** a map's box changing with nothing else changing (chunks landing or unloading, an edit past it, a reload that moves a layer but keeps `MapFloors`, the map disabled) reaches an entity standing still only when it next moves (or its floor, children or the map's transform change, or the map is enabled again).
  - **Climbers ease on stairs** (`lift_floored`'s one extension, `Climbing`): while a map with floors is there, every `Climber` (not `ParentPlaced`, disabled ones too) is a seed on every run, since its eased lift moves with where it draws, which moves between steps with nothing changing; its `set_if_neq` writes nothing when the lift stands. A climber's lift is `lift_at` with the maps' `Stairs` (`MapStairs` by map entity): on a run of stairs one of whose two floors it is on (`MapStairs::climbing`, the run under its point in the map's pixels), the run's floors' world lifts eased by its progress, `lift(from).lerp(lift(to), t)`; elsewhere its floor's, as any entity. Its point is where it draws: in `PostUpdate`, `render_global(global, previous, overstep)` as the draw sites place it, so the lift blends as the movement does between fixed steps (`a_climber_s_lift_eases_along_the_stairs`, headless, half a step over); in a fixed step (`Time::in_fixed`, crate), where the step left it, so a trail records the step's point lifted at the step's height. A child draws with its climber's eased lift (the walk down, as for any entity). Without a climber or a map with floors this costs nothing; with them, a map lookup and a look at the map's stair index per climber (`MapStairs::climbing`, `stairs.rs` below), whatever the number of runs.
  - **What a map spawns on a higher floor is on it.** Every object and class tile a layer on floor `f` ≥ 1 spawns carries `Floor::On(f)` (`spawn::layer_child`, which reads the layer entity's `LayerInfo::floor`): at landing, from an edit (`spawn_class_tile`), from a stream's chunks (`follow_chunk_swaps`) and on a reload (new objects, and class tiles respawned; a kept object whose layer moved to another floor follows it, `reload::follow_floor`, losing on the ground floor only the floor its layer gave it). So their colliders are on that floor, and what a game gives them draws lifted (`a_higher_floor_s_class_tiles_and_objects_are_on_that_floor`, in `reload/tests.rs`; `a_class_tile_sprite_on_a_floor_draws_lifted`, e2e). The ground floor's get none, as before, and nor does a layer entity, so the lift never walks a layer's collider parts and chains. A tile object is drawn by the tile drawer through its layer's lift and its entity's `DrawLift` is not added again (`a_tile_object_on_a_floor_is_lifted_once`, e2e).
- `stairs.rs`: stairs (spec "Floors: details settled at planning", Stairs, whose bottom is settled here as the run's foot). **Runs:** `StairRun` (crate) `{ from, to, cells (by row), bottom, top, grid, offset }`, in the map's pixels in ground space: the stair cells (tiles whose `TileData::stairs` names a floor, by the tilesets' `StairSource`s, `kinds::stairs_of`) of one tile layer leading to one floor `to`, joined through `MapGrid::neighbours` (`joined`), `from` the layer's floor. Its top is the mean centre of the sides it shares with floor `to`'s footprint cells outside the run (the cells of `to`'s tile layers standing where its bottom tile layer does, and the stairs up to `to` there); a shared side's centre is halfway between the two cells' centres, on every grid (centrally symmetric cells). Its bottom is its foot (`ends`): the run climbs from the mean centre `m` of the sides it shares with cells outside both the run and the footprint toward its top, `u = normalize(top - m)`, and the bottom is the farthest of those sides along `u`, `top + u * min(dot(s - top, u))`, so a straight run eases from its foot to its top over its whole length (`a_run_s_lift_eases_over_its_whole_length`; `m` alone takes in the sides along the run and sits about halfway up it, which left the lift flat over the run's lower half). Progress is `clamp(dot(p - bottom, top - bottom) / |top - bottom|², 0, 1)` (`StairRun::progress`). Stairs naming a floor the map lacks, their own floor or a lower one stay plain tiles, warned once a map (`stairs_to_a_missing_floor_or_down_warn_once_and_stay_plain`; the edge's footprint leaves them out without a word, so this is the one warning); a run leads nowhere and is left out, warned once a map (the warning says why), when `to` has no footprint (no tile layer, or a bottom tile layer of hexagons the tracer does not take: `stairs_up_to_a_floor_without_an_edge_lead_nowhere`), when its layer gives the footprint no stairs (standing elsewhere than `to`'s bottom tile layer, another grid or ground offset), when it shares no side with the footprint (no top), every side (no bottom), or its top lies on `m` (no way up) (`a_run_that_leads_nowhere_is_warned_once_and_ignored`). **One footprint rule:** which layers give floor `to`'s footprint, and what each gives, is `collision::FootprintRule` (`of(floor, layers)`: the first enabled tile layer on the floor is its bottom, `NoFootprint` without one or for untraceable hexagons; `footing(floor, grid, offset)`: `Tiles` on the floor, `Stairs` below it, standing where the bottom does), which the edge's `plan_edge` and `runs_of` both call over the map's enabled tile layers, so a floor's edge and its stairs never disagree; it allocates nothing, as `plan` runs every step. Layers whose grid places nothing (`Placement::Nothing`) give no stair cells and no footprint cells, as in `footprint_kinds`. **Limit:** a run meets its upper floor only at its top: one that touches the footprint along a long side (stairs running along a platform's face) has its top beside the run and climbs across it, so stairs are turned out from the face (the skill says so). **Where they live:** in `MapStairs::runs` (crate, a component on the map beside `MapFloors`, taken away with a root that goes, `root_discarded`, and at the next step once a reload takes `MapFloors` away; read by `climb_stairs` and the lift through `Stairs`, by map entity, and by `MapsView::floor_navigation` for its links), made by `sync_stair_runs` (`FixedSet::Movement`, before `climb_stairs`), which keeps beside them what it read: each enabled tile layer's `Place` (entity, floor, `GridKey` and ground offset) with a `kinds::Tracked` and the stair cells of each chunk holding any, the tilesets' stamps and `StairSource`s, and the warnings given. Each step it reads again the chunks whose version moved (`changed_chunks`, one layer at a time, never `drain_dirty`) and makes every run again from the stair cells, and indexes them (below), when any did; a new `MapFloors` (landed, or a reload with other heights: `Ref::is_changed`; nothing else writes it), a change of the enabled tile layers or their places, or tilesets whose stamps moved with other stairs (`MapStairs::stairs_kept`, the comparison `shapes_kept` makes) reads every chunk again (`runs_follow_edits_to_the_stairs`: an edit, a fill, a tileset saved with new stairs, reloads moving a layer and raising a floor). `MapFloors` is never written for them, so `lift_floored`'s `Changed<MapFloors>` does not lift every entity on a floor for an edit of stairs. A disabled map is not synced and catches up when enabled. A map without floors has neither component and costs nothing; an idle step is a version look per tile layer and a stamp look per tileset, allocating nothing. An edit made in `Update` reaches the runs (and the lift) at the next fixed step. **The index:** `MapStairs::put` is the one writer of `runs`, and indexes them as it writes them: by place (each `GridKey` and ground offset runs stand at, a `StairPlace`), each run's cells with the run's index, sorted by row, then by run. `climbing(at, floor)` takes the cell `at` is in at each place (usually one), binary-searches it there, and answers the first run in `runs` holding it with `floor` one of its two, as a look at every run in turn did: a cell in two runs (up from one floor on one layer, on from the next on another) answers for each floor (`a_cell_in_two_runs_answers_for_each_floor`, against that look at points over two maps, one with runs standing at two places). So a climber's lookups cost the same however many runs its map has (a binary search over the stair cells of a place, one comparison more for twice the cells), and allocate nothing (`a_frame_with_entities_moving_on_floors_allocates_nothing`, `tests/tilemap_alloc.rs`, holds a climber easing on stairs). A stream's chunks landing and unloading reach the runs and the index as edits do, through `changed_chunks` (`stairs_follow_a_streamed_map_s_chunks`: a run in a generated chunk goes as the chunk unloads, and a climber where it was, still on the map, climbs nothing). **Limit:** every run is made again, and indexed, when any tile layer's chunk changed: an edit anywhere on the map, a stream landing or unloading chunks. A run's top and foot read its upper floor's tile layers and the other layers' stairs up to it, so making again only the changed layer's runs would miss an edit of the storey above them; making again a floor's runs would hold, but on a map of two storeys every tile layer gives floor 1's footprint something, as a stream's unbounded layers do, so it would make every run there too. On `floors_frame_timing`'s map (64 runs of two stairs) an edit off the stairs costs the sync 14.7 µs at the median (`docs/backlog/tilemap.md`, Floors and Limits). **Climbing:** `Climber` (prelude, `#[require(Floor)]`) opts an entity in. `climb_stairs` (chained after the sync, before `FixedSet::Physics`) returns at once with no climber or no map with runs; otherwise, for each enabled climber on `Floor::On(n)`, the map with floors under its `GlobalTransform` (where the last step's propagation left it: the character's one step of lag is accepted) by `floors::find_areas` and `MapArea::holds` (the lift's lookup), and the run there with `n` one of its floors (`MapStairs::climbing`): the floor becomes `to` at `t ≥ 0.5`, else `from`. A change writes `Floor` (re-filtered by that step's physics sync) and sends `FloorChanged { entity, from, to }` (prelude, registered with the engine's messages beside `MapEvent`). **A floor written elsewhere wins:** a climber whose `Floor` changed since `climb_stairs` last ran (`Mut::is_changed`; its own writes are not seen as changes) is skipped that step, since it still reads where the last step left it: a game's teleport in `update` that sets `Floor` with `Transform` keeps that floor (`a_teleported_climber_keeps_the_floor_its_game_gave_it`), and the next step reads the new place. The stairs run before `apply_kill_zones` (`gameplay::apply_kill_zones`, crate, named for the order), whose respawn's `set_if_neq` then overrides whatever the stairs did in that step (`a_climber_respawned_from_the_stairs_keeps_its_checkpoint_s_floor`, headless); either order would hold with the change check, and this one also covers a respawn whose floor equals the climber's (`a_kill_zone_s_respawn_wins_over_a_climb_in_the_same_step`: a climber crosses halfway in the step a ground-floor trap springs, with a ground-floor checkpoint, and the reverse order would leave it on floor 1). A climber spawned this step skips its first step (its `Floor` was just added). Halfway, not where it leaves the run: the upper floor's edge encloses its stairs (`collision.rs`, the footprint) and the lower floor may have walls where the stairs arrive, so switching at the middle lets a character leave the run at both ends without opening either (`a_climber_changes_floor_halfway_up_and_halfway_down`, headless: a top-down climber walks up a two-cell run, one change, held on floor 1 by its edge, and back, one change, stopped by a ground wall). A non-climber keeps its floor and its floor's lift on stairs (`a_non_climber_keeps_its_floor_on_stairs`); a climber on `Every`, or on neither of a run's floors, passes over it unchanged (`a_climber_on_every_floor_or_another_floor_passes_over_stairs`). **Limits** (the halfway design's, in the skill too): a run must be at least as long as the climber is wide, or at the switch it overlaps the ground's walls at the top or the upper rim at the foot; stepping off a run's lower half sideways snaps the drawn height to the floor's (the ease applies on the run's cells only); stepping onto a run's upper half sideways from the ground floor puts the climber on the upper storey (the halfway rule). Hex maps climb as any (`a_climber_climbs_stairs_on_a_hexagonal_map`). Tests in `stairs/tests.rs`.
- `kinds.rs`: what a cell is, for collision or light, pure so a landing job runs it off the main thread: `ShapeSource` (a tileset's tiles with shapes, read once: each tile's drawn size and shapes, the tileset's offset; A kept chunk stays kept after it lands: it unloads unchanged without being put in again, and its copy is still there. - `collision.rs`: `sync_map_colliders` (`FixedSet::Movement`, before physics' `rebuild_tile_colliders`, so an edit made in `update` collides in that step; `register_tilemap_systems` in `mod.rs`) keeps each spawned map's colliding tile layers in a `TileColliders`, with the crate's `MapCollision` on the map entity. Colliding layers (`LayerInfo::collides` on a root with collision) group by grid (shape, cell size and origin: `kinds::GridKey`), floor, place (where the layer stands: its ground offset, `floors::ground_offset`, its offset less its floor's height above the ground floor) and collision material: the group of the map's grid (`TileMap::grid`) on the ground floor, no offset and the map's material unions into one `TileColliders` on the map entity, any other gets its own on its first layer's entity (whose `Transform` carries the place), so two colliding layers meet without a seam unless they differ in one of the four, and two layers of different shapes or floors never share a grid. Each grid is on its group's floor (`TileColliders::floor`, `Floor::On(floor)`; physics puts its parts on it, the ground floor's with no `Floor`), so the floors collide apart (`layers_on_different_floors_collide_on_their_own_grids`: two layers at one offset on floors 0 and 1; a ground layer and a floor 1 layer both drawn at floor 1's height, which grouped by drawn offset had shared the ground layer's entity; and a floor 1 layer drawn at the origin above its floor's bottom layer, which had joined the map's own grid). A cell takes the strongest kind across its group (`Solid` > `Shape` > `Slope` > `OneWay`); on a tie (two shapes, two slopes) the upper layer's wins. An IntGrid layer collides by its values (`intgrid_table`: an override by value, else by name, else the default names `solid`, `one_way`, `slope_*`, all ignoring ASCII case; an override to `TileCell::Shape` names no shape and collides as nothing, which `plan` warns once a map, `an_int_grid_shape_override_collides_as_nothing_and_warns_once`), any other by its tiles' shapes not marked `occluder`, placed where the tile draws (`placed`, the drawing's rules: a tile stands on its cell's bottom edge from the left, `tile_offset` moves it, flips turn it: diagonal, then horizontal, then vertical) and classified (`classify`): a full-cell rectangle `Solid` (`OneWay` if one-way), three cell corners `Slope` (the right angle opposite the corner left out), anything else a `Shape` added once per distinct geometry (`ShapeKey`, points on a 1/46,080 tile grid). A tile with solid and one-way shapes collides with its solid ones only (`CellShape` has one `one_way`). A colliding layer with parallax other than 1 collides where it would draw without it (parallax is a rendering effect, as in Tiled, and a collider moving with the camera is not a body physics can hold) and the dev log warns once a map per layer (`a_colliding_layer_with_parallax_collides_where_it_is_and_warns_once`). Each tile's kind is worked out once per group (`kinds`), from the map's `ShapeSource`s. Material: the properties `collision_layer`, `collision_mask` (a 32-bit int: -1 is every bit), `friction` and `restitution`, a layer's over its map's over `TileColliders`' defaults (an ice layer saying `friction` 0 is a grid of its own); a wrong type warns once a map per layer and property, and what is under it counts.
  - **A raised floor's edge** (spec "Floors: details settled at planning", Collision): each floor `f` ≥ 1 with a tile layer has a footprint, the cells holding a tile in its tile layers that stand where its bottom tile layer does (its grid and ground offset; one standing elsewhere is left out, warned once a map) and the cells of tiles that are stairs up to `f` (`TileData::stairs`, by the tilesets' `StairSource`s) in lower floors' tile layers standing there (stairs leading down are plain tiles, `stairs_join_the_upper_floor_s_footprint`). Which layers give it, and what each gives, is `FootprintRule` (`plan_edge`), which the stairs share (`stairs.rs` above). It is a group of its own (`GroupKey::edge`, its layers' `Footing`s: `Tiles` on the floor, `Stairs` below it), its cells `Solid` (`kinds::footprint_kinds`), traced as any grid (on every grid shape: `a_floor_s_edge_traces_on_every_grid`) with the map's material, on floor `f`, on a `MapFloor { floor }` entity of its own, a child of the map at the floor's ground offset (`spawn_edge` at landing; spawned through commands on a later build). A grid of its own because a cell takes the strongest kind across its group: a wall on the floor would union into the footprint's `Solid` and lose its outline. Outlines are hollow, so what is on the floor walks inside its edge and never off into the air, and meets the floor's walls, while the ground floor passes under (`a_raised_floor_s_edge_keeps_a_character_on_it`, `the_floor_filter_holds_between_tiles_and_entities`, both headless with top-down characters). On the ground floor a raised area blocks only where its own tiles collide, so a bridge stands over open ground. It is tracked like any group (`Tracked` over every layer it reads), so it follows edits (only the edited chunk's part takes a new shape: `a_floor_s_edge_follows_edits`), reloads (`a_reload_rebuilds_a_floor_s_edge`: cells and stairs in place, a layer moved to another place or floor rebuilt there) and a stream's chunks (`a_streamed_floor_s_edge_follows_its_chunks`). **Limit:** a cell whose only tile on the floor is a class tile is a hole in the edge (the tile leaves the grid as it spawns, at landing, from an edit, a stream or a reload, and is not drawn there, so the hole matches what is drawn), while picking (`floor_cell_at_world`) counts the class tile and only drawn layers, where the edge counts hidden layers too: picking can name the floor at a cell its characters cannot enter, and the other way round. Stairs on a lower floor's layer standing elsewhere are left out without a warning (only the floor's own layers warn). An edit keeps the edge's entity; every full build replaces it (`take_down` despawns it, the build spawns a new one), which the docs tell games. The edge's grid is no surface (`TileColliders::surface`, crate, set false in `build`): it is no wall to navigation (`MapsView::navigation` reads the map's and its layers' grids, and takes a floor's footprint from the tiles through `Footprint`, which shares the rule; `NavigationGrid::rebuild_from_world` skips grids that are no surface) nor a surface to particles (they skip the parts of such a grid), so neither module names a tile map type. A map without floors has no edge and keeps its groups, targets and parts as they were (`a_map_without_floors_keeps_its_groups`); with no `MapFloors` its sync plans no footprint, so its step costs what it did.
  - **The first build** comes with the map's landing (`prepare`, `land`; see `spawn.rs` above): every grid on its target with its `Traced`, so physics only spawns the parts and inserts their shapes as built. Physics packs a grid's outlines by chunk (a part for the loops starting in each 32-cell chunk; physics module notes, Packing), so a map's part count follows its area, not its islands. Whatever builds the grids again after that (enabled again, a layer's flags, the tilesets or the overrides changed) builds at once on the main thread. The landing frame of a 256 x 256 four-layer map adds about 0.03 ms for terrain and about 0.2 ms for 1,024 separate islands in 64 parts (`spawning_a_256_map_adds_under_half_a_millisecond_to_any_frame`, ignored, release, timed after a warm-up since a process's first second runs slow; a part per island took about 1 ms, nearly all of it Rapier taking in the colliders).
  - **Chunks, not cells:** a group's grid is bounded by its layers' largest bounds (`TileColliders::new`), or unbounded (`TileColliders::unbounded`) when a layer is, its cells signed. A build classifies each chunk any of its layers has (an IntGrid layer's IntGrid chunks too, `kinds::build_chunks`), across them (`kinds::union_kinds`), and writes it whole (`TileColliders::put_chunk`, crate: a cell outside the grid or past `MAX_REACH` left out, the latter warned once a grid), with no dense vector of the group's rectangle. There is no cap of the module's own: a 10,000 x 1 level collides end to end (`a_colliding_layer_wider_than_8192_cells_collides`); an unbounded group's cells collide within 186,411 cells of (0, 0) (physics module notes, Sparse and signed), and past that they draw and cast shadows but do not collide. That reach is the outline lattice's, not where f32 positions stay precise: a `Transform`'s pixels are 1/4 px apart there and 1/8 px a million px out, so precise play keeps within about a million px (physics module notes). Parts sit at their chunks' top-left, so a test reading a part's points adds the part's translation (`physics::in_grid_space`, crate tests).
  - **What changed:** every step the group's layers' `TileGrid::id` and `version` are compared with the last seen (never `drain_dirty`, which one consumer would empty for the others); for a layer that moved, each chunk whose version moved (`kinds::changed_chunks`) is classified again across the group and written whole (`TileColliders::set_chunk`, crate), which lists only the cells whose kind changed, as `set` would, so physics' loop-local rebuild stays local. An IntGrid layer whose names (seen when `TileLayerData` changes) now give other kinds re-reads all its chunks. A change of what the grids are built from (the colliding layers and the layers of the floors' footprints, their entities, grids, floors, places, materials and bounds, whether each has an IntGrid, the map's grid, the overrides, a tileset's shapes, or its stairs on a map whose floors have footprints) builds every group again (new `TileColliders`, which physics builds whole); an unbounded layer growing a chunk is an edit, not such a change. A despawned map takes its colliders with it (the map-level parts are its children, a layer-level grid's are its layer's, and its floors' edges are its children). A map whose root is put over or taken away (no `MapLayers`, or no `MapRoot`) loses its grids and `MapCollision`, its floors' edges at once (`root_discarded`, `drop_edges`). A disabled layer entity does not collide. A map with collision owns the `TileColliders` on its entity: one a game put there is replaced.
  - **Other grids** (the design: `docs/specs/2026-10-31-tilemap-shapes-design.md`, "Collision baked into the outline points, not a sheared parent" and "Tile shapes on non-square grids"): an isometric, staggered or oblique group's grid holds the cells of the grid's square lattice (`MapGrid::lattice`) and takes its `to_pixel` (`TileColliders::with_lattice`; `kinds::lattice_of`, none for an orthogonal grid at the origin, which keeps the bare tile size bit for bit), so physics traces on squares and maps the points (physics module notes, Lattices). Isometric and oblique grids index their cells as their lattice does, so their chunks, bounds (`TileColliders::new`) and edits are the orthogonal ones. A staggered grid's cells go to `Lattice::lattice_cell` (`kinds::reindexing`, `kinds::scatter`), which a rectangle from (0, 0) does not hold, so its grid is unbounded: a build gathers each chunk's kinds into lattice chunks and writes those whole (`put_chunk`); a changed chunk's cells are set one by one at their lattice cells (`TileColliders::set_cells`, crate), and an unloaded chunk's leave the same way, as empty, never through `unload_chunk`, since its lattice cells straddle several lattice chunks that other chunks' cells share; a lattice chunk left holding nothing goes (`a_staggered_map_collides_as_the_isometric_map_of_the_same_cells`, `a_streamed_staggered_chunk_unloads_cleanly`). A tile's shapes reach their cell's square through `kinds::Placement` (an isometric or staggered tile from where `MapGrid::tile_anchor` stands it, back through the lattice, so a polygon on the diamond's four vertices is `Solid` and three of them a `Slope`; an oblique tile in its cell unskewed, as on orthogonal maps, skewing with the grid). One-way tiles (and IntGrid `OneWay` cells) off an orthogonal grid collide both ways, `Context::both_ways` noting it and `warn_both_ways` warning once a map (`a_one_way_tile_off_an_orthogonal_map_collides_both_ways_and_warns_once`). A hexagonal group's grid takes its hexagons instead (`TileColliders::with_hex`, from `MapGrid::hex_cells`, at the grid's origin), so physics keeps the hexagons' sides as they are, in parts of at most 128 edges (physics module notes, Outlines, Hexagonal grids); its cells are the storage's own, chunk for chunk, so its builds, edits and unloads are the orthogonal ones. A tile's shapes reach their cell through `kinds::Placement::Hex` (`HexPlace`, every cell alike): the image's pixels from where `MapGrid::tile_anchor` stands it, moved by the tileset's offset, mirrored by its horizontal and vertical flips, then turned by `TileFlip::quad_turn(true)` (bit 29 is the 60° turn there, never a diagonal flip) about the tile quad's middle, the pivot drawing uses (`quad_turn` lives in `tile.rs` so both turn alike), in units of the cell's box (`HexCells::cell_box`: a shape's tile units on a hexagonal grid). A tile with a polygon that is its cell's whole hexagon after its flips and before its turns is `Solid` whatever its turn, and joins the hexagons' outline: its points, repeats dropped and points along a side that are no corner's (within half a pixel), are the cell's corners, each within half a pixel of one, and no other, the corners being the outline's own with repeats and points along a side dropped (a side-0 cell's four, a rectangle's four on a grid whose side is the cell's length or longer: `whole_hexagon_shapes_on_degenerate_grids_are_solid`). Any other is a `TileCell::Shape`, whose polygons, flipped and turned, are closed rings of their own in their chunk's parts of rings, apart from its sides, wherever they lie, wound clockwise (a self-crossing one its hull) by physics, neither traced nor merged with the neighbours (`shape_blocks`: a polygon holds the cell's centre). A colliding layer of hexagons the tracer does not take (`HexCells::traceable`: over 16,384 px, or under 2 px) is left out of the plan, warned once a layer in plain words (`a_hex_grid_too_big_to_trace_warns_once_and_builds_nothing`). Each chunk owns the units of its cells and, where it reaches them, a bounded group's right and bottom borders, so the chunks' edges together are the boundary with nothing twice, and nothing at a chunk border. An unbounded group (one with an unbounded layer) has no such border: the borders right of and below its last cells are the next chunk's, built as that chunk is though its layers have none (the cell (-1, -1)'s right and bottom borders are chunk (0, -1)'s and chunk (-1, 0)'s). Its chunks are those whose cells, the column left of them and the row above are cells `i32` holds (`SHADOW_CHUNKS`: all but the first and last chunk of `i32`'s range on each axis). A chunk's edges are chained greedily (`chains`: from the points more edges leave than reach, then round the loops left; straight runs merged; each point's edges a run of the edges sorted by start, found through one map made at its full size: a list a point, maps grown from empty and a chain made for every start tried were half a dense chunk's rebuild) into `LightOccluder2D::chain`s, each a child of the group's first layer marked `MapOccluder { chunk }` (prelude; `chunk` an `IVec2`, signed as `TileGrid::chunk_of` gives it), its entity at the centre of its corners' box (lighting's reach test reads the entity's translation less the chain's extent, so a small light at a chunk-long chain's end still reaches it), so a chain spans one chunk at most. A build compares what it reads with that. The sides owned by the chunk's cells that reach a cell whose solidity changed are read again (`Boundary::hex_cell_edges`: the scan of the cells it picks, in the scan's order) and merged with the kept edges; the chains that lost or turned an edge, and those through a corner of a changed cell one of whose sides the chunk owns (found among the edges of that cell and the cells beside it: three cells meet at each corner), are chained again with the new edges (`Boundary::hex_chain_edges`, a subset of a block's edges chained by their cells, an edge whose next one is not in it ending its chain), and only their entities are despawned and spawned again. An edge's next one is decided by the three cells at its end, so every chain an edit does not reach still runs as a whole build would run it, and a chunk holds as many chains as a whole build of its cells makes, however many edits it takes (`a_hex_chunk_edited_many_times_keeps_its_chain_count_near_a_fresh_build`: after each of 500 edits as many as a fresh build, where the design allows twice). Named for `TileMap::bounds`, which they agree with on a map nothing edited, not for `TileGrid::extent`, which for an unbounded grid is its chunks' rectangle. A bounded layer refuses cells outside it (its `bounds`); an unbounded one takes any cell, so `set_tile` and `fill` make the chunks a tile goes in and return true for every cell they change (`writing_a_tile_far_away_on_an_unbounded_layer_collides_and_casts_shadows`; on a streamed map, only in its resident chunks: `stream.rs`), but a tile `fill` that would make more than `MAX_FILL_CHUNKS` chunks (a class tile's too, whose cells would each spawn an entity) is refused before anything is made, sub-layers included: 0, and the dev log warns once a layer (`FillRefused`, a world resource of the layers warned about; `a_fill_making_more_chunks_than_a_map_holds_is_refused_and_warns_once`). `clear_parts` reads a sub-layer's chunks in the region (`chunks_within`) where their cells lie, only the region's rows and columns of each: a `set_tile` on an LDtk layer of 16 full sub-layers costs about 0.9 µs where reading every cell of each chunk cost 8.8 µs (release, measured once; 6a made 16 lookups), `an_edit_across_chunk_borders_clears_only_its_region_of_each_sub_layer`.
- `wang.rs`: `MapsView::set_terrain` (Wang auto-tiling, Tiled's terrain brush with corrections on) and `WangSet::best_tile` / `color_index` / `ANY` (public, for procgen). Each `WangTile` carries its tile's probability, copied at load (`build_tileset` builds the sets after the tiles; both readers share it), and `Tileset`/`WangSet` carry the tileset's `transformations` (hflip, vflip, rotate, preferuntransformed). The rules follow Tiled's observed results, never its source (GPL): a paint asks one colour of the kind's positions; the cells sharing one pick again, row by row, painted cell included, each from the candidates (every tile at every flip the transformations allow, turns before mirrors, plus the empty cell, which wins over a tile with no colour) that have the asked colour and what the cells that picked before it show; among those the closest to what the cell shows now (colour distances: steps through tiles holding both colours, 0 the empty colour; a colour no chain reaches rules a candidate out), then an untransformed tile if the set prefers, then probability times its colours' probabilities, drawn by a hash of the cell (Tiled draws at random, so ties cannot match it). A cell that picks a different tile passes the change to the neighbours across an edge whose colours disagree, first-in first-out, within a Chebyshev square around the painted cell as many cells out as the set's longest colour chain plus one (two colours sharing a tile: two cells; deep water, water, sand, grass: four; `Matcher::longest_chain`; on a staggered map two more, measured with correction chains that never settle: `wang-*-long-b` stops `chain + 3` rows out along the half-step axis, and only that axis is measured, the other taken as the same square); one nothing fits keeps its tile and, if it holds a tile of the set or is the painted cell, the set warns once per tileset version (`WangWarned`). Cells read through their flips (D, then H, then V); class tiles are not touched. Every cell the paint names (`Painting`) is a lattice cell (`MapGrid::lattice`): the layer's own on orthogonal, isometric and oblique maps, the isometric lattice's on staggered ones, mapped through `lattice_cell` and `grid_cell` where the layer's tiles, bounds and class tiles are read and the edits go out. That is what Tiled's brush does on a staggered map (`paint-wang.js` paints a cell's lattice corners there: one corner fills the four cells around a lattice vertex, one edge the two lattice cells beside it; a corner (x, y) is the top vertex of grid cell (x, y)'s lattice cell), and procgen's `to_terrain_map` works the same way, so what it builds matches (`staggered_terrain_matches_what_tileds_brush_paints`). A hexagonal layer has no lattice: `set_terrain` warns once (`WangWarned`, under an empty key), changes nothing and returns false. On an unbounded layer a paint takes any cell and makes the chunks it writes in, as `set_tile` does (`painting_terrain_outside_every_chunk_of_an_unbounded_layer_makes_its_chunks`), but a cell whose reach, and the neighbours a cell at its edge reads, would leave `i32` (within the set's longest chain plus two cells of `i32::MIN` or `i32::MAX`, `reach_fits`) is refused, `set_terrain` returning false, where `cell + offset` panicked in a dev build and wrapped to the far end of the world in release (`painting_terrain_at_the_ends_of_i32_is_refused`); a bounded one keeps a paint within its bounds. The pick's candidates, colour distances and tile-to-wangid index are built once per `WangSet`, on its first pick (`WangCache`, a `OnceLock`; a clone or a reloaded tileset builds its own), so `best_tile` costs a scan of the candidates (about 16 µs at 255 tiles with every flip, down from 60). A set is read-only once built, so the cache cannot go stale: its fields are `pub(crate)` and read through accessors (`name`, `kind`, `colors`, `tiles`, `transformations`), sets are made by `WangSet::new` or the loaders, and no mutator exists (a `compile_fail` doctest pins that a game cannot write a field, and a passing one beside it, the same code reading through an accessor, that nothing else fails it). Add a `&mut self` method only with a caller, and have it drop the cache. Limits: Tiled draws at random among equal fits, so a tie cannot match it (fixtures are built to have one fit); a class tile keeps its colours out of corrections, so painting beside one leaves a seam; an empty cell touched by a chain is judged by its zero colours; erasing in a set with no bare-corner tile warns once per set version. The test fixtures `wang-*` were painted by Tiled: `tests/fixtures/tilemap/tiled/scripts/paint-wang.js` (every job built to have one fit, since Tiled draws at random among equals).
- `reload.rs`: hot reload. `reload_maps` (`PreUpdate`, chained after `spawn_maps`, with `Allow<Disabled>` so a disabled map follows its files and is current once enabled) looks only in frames where a `TileMap`, `Tileset` or `LdtkProject` was added or changed (three `MessageCursor`s; never a store's change list). A spawned map follows its files: its map file or LDtk project with what their loaders read (templates, the Tiled project, LDtk level files: each a `cx.read`, so saving one runs the loader again), and its tileset files. `content_stamp` is their loads (`AssetServer::load_job`, which moves when a file's new value lands: not when a reload starts or fails, a dependency's reload checks it again, or code changes it); a map whose stamp differs from its `MapApplied` (crate, on the map entity: the stamp, each layer's editor id, shape and place in ground space, each object's data and layer, and each class tile by layer editor id and cell, as the files had them) is brought to its asset by `apply_reload`, then `MapEvent::Reloaded` goes. So a tileset image's save, which only touches its tileset and maps, redraws (the padding is stamped by image versions) and reloads nothing; a failed save changes nothing and sends nothing (`a_broken_save_keeps_the_last_good_map`); a file several of a map's files read (the Tiled project: its map and its tilesets) loads each again, the loads landing in any order and frames apart, so the map follows each as it lands, one `Reloaded` a load, ending as saved (`a_project_save_whose_loads_land_apart_ends_with_the_new_defaults`; a test waiting for the first `Reloaded` after such a save saw the tileset's load alone now and then, and waits for every file's load: `until_reloaded_from_every_file`); a map whose asset keeps its last good value after a failed check (a tileset saved that the map no longer fits) waits for the next good load; an asset made in code (no `load_job`) never reloads, though its map follows its tilesets' files. An LDtk project saved without the map's level leaves the map as it is, warned once per load. A tileset file saved naming a larger image, and reloaded before that image, fails its check against the old image (too small) and keeps its last good value; the image's reload then only checks that old value again and never retries the failed load (a registry limit: asset module notes, Reloads), so the tileset stays old until it is saved again. Writing both at once (a `git pull`, a script) can hit it; a fix is the registry's.
  - **Layers** match by editor id (`LayerInfo::id`: Tiled's layer id, LDtk's layer definition uid, one instance a level). A layer the file still has, of the same kind and, for a tile layer, the same bounds (`TileGrid::bounds`: a bounded grid's size, or none for an unbounded one, so an infinite map's layer keeps its entity whatever chunks a save gives it), grid (`MapGrid`: shape, cell and origin, so a kept layer's `TileLayerData::grid` is always the file's), offset, floor and place in ground space (`ground_offset` against the new asset's `MapFloors`, which moves with its floor's bottom layer), keeps its entity: its `MapLayerRef` (index, name), `LayerInfo` and `ImageLayer` are put over only where they differ, an object or image layer whose place in ground space changed moves (its `Transform`'s translation set to the new `ground_offset`, so a Tiled group moved moves its layers, their objects keeping their entities and places in them: `kept_object_and_image_layers_move_with_a_saved_offset`; and one on a storey moves when the storey's height does), and its `TileLayerData` takes the file's cells through `TileGrid::assign`, the IntGrid's changed chunks named too (`values_differ`: collision reads IntGrid values through grid versions). An infinite map's layers diff by their chunk sets (`assign` over the chunks either grid has): a chunk the save dropped is emptied and kept, one it added is made, one it changed moves on, and the others keep their versions, the dropped chunk's colliders and shadows going and the new one's coming in the next frame (`saving_an_infinite_map_that_moves_its_chunks_reloads_by_chunk`). **A streamed map** (a `StreamState` on its root) diffs the file's chunks alone (`stream::file_chunks` of the new asset): `stream::before_reload` first hands the chunks the save moved between the file and the stream (`stream.rs` above), its unbounded layers take the file's cells through `assign_where` over the file's chunks, and the class tiles a kept layer lists outside them (`listed_class_tiles`: the stream's, dead entries included, which count as a player's change) stay listed and untouched, so the chunks the stream generated or brought back keep their cells, versions and class tiles, and a tileset save, or any reload of a map built in code with nothing in its asset, changes none of them. So only the chunks a save changed draw, collide and occlude again, each the way an edit does: a chunk collider changes shape in place, keeping its entity and handle, so a character standing on ground the save did not change keeps its contacts and floor (`a_reload_keeps_the_ground_a_character_stands_on`, with chunk versions and shadows of the untouched chunks pinned). Any other layer (a new id, another kind, or a tile layer of another size, grid, offset, floor or place in ground space: `LayerShape::Tiles` holds the whole `MapGrid`, so a tile layer moved to another floor, or whose floor's height moved under it, respawns in its new ground space: `a_reload_that_moves_a_layer_to_another_floor_respawns_it_in_ground_space`) spawns afresh (`spawn::spawn_layer`, shared with the landing; a map saved with another grid, its orientation changed in Tiled, respawns its tile layers, and their class tiles, colliders and shadow chains are built again from the new layers, none of the old left: `a_map_saved_with_another_grid_respawns_its_layers`), and a layer gone, or replaced, despawns after its objects moved out. The map's `MapFloors` is put over where the save changed it, and taken away when no layer is above the ground floor any more. There is no landing job for a reload: it runs on the main thread (a dev-time action; cost below).
  - **Objects** reconcile by id (`reconcile`, pure: `keep`, `update`, `spawn`, `despawn`), given their tiles' data first as at landing, so a tileset save that changes a tile's class or properties updates the tile objects taking them, in place (`a_tileset_save_reaches_the_tile_objects_that_take_its_tile_s_data`). Whether the file changed an object is judged against `MapApplied` (its data and layer then), never the entity's components, which the game may have moved. One unchanged keeps its entity untouched (its `Transform`, parent and components as the game has them); one changed takes a new `Transform` and `MapObject` (put over, so `Insert` observers run and `Add` ones do not, and the game's components stay), moving to its new layer's entity if its layer changed; an object the file added or changed with no live entity spawns as at landing (entities first, then `MapIds`, then the components, so observers resolve references); a removed id despawns. Ids are unique in files Tiled and LDtk write; a repeated id counts once on a reload (`MapApplied` notes the first, a landing's `MapIds` names the last spawned, so the first duplicate stays untracked). Class tiles reconcile by layer and cell (`reconcile_class_tiles`), judged against `MapApplied` too (never the live `MapTile`, which the game may change): one the file left as it was keeps its entity untouched (its `MapTile::layer` told a new index), one changed takes a new `MapTile` and `Transform`, and any other live one (a cell the file emptied, or one an edit placed) despawns. On a tile layer spawned afresh, the class tiles that lived on its old entity spawn again on the new one.
  - **What the game took away stays away** unless the save adds or changes it: an object or class tile the game despawned (a coin collected, an enemy killed), or a class tile a runtime `set_tile` replaced or cleared (its cell then takes the file's grid, which holds no tile there), is not spawned again while the file leaves it as it was (`new_and_removed_objects_spawn_and_despawn`, `class_tiles_reconcile_by_layer_and_cell`, `a_tile_layer_spawned_afresh_keeps_away_the_class_tiles_the_game_took`); a save that changes it spawns it. The file does win over the game's tile edits: a dug tile comes back.
  - **Limit, a tileset save that changes shapes:** collision builds every group again (new `TileColliders`), so the floor part under a character is replaced (a new entity and handle, `CharacterState::floor` names the new one, and `CollisionStopped` is sent), though nothing falls through and `on_floor` holds. Image saves and tileset saves that keep the shapes rebuild nothing. Follow-up: reclassify every chunk through `TileColliders::set` instead, as a map save does.
  - **Cost** (`reload_timing`, ignored, release, a 1024 x 1024 map of four layers, on the main thread): `apply_reload` costs about 1 ms per 4 M cells whatever the save changed, since it copies every grid out of the asset and compares it (1.0 ms with one tile changed, 1.4 ms with every chunk); only what follows is in proportion to the change: 40 to 80 µs for the next step's collision and `PostUpdate`'s shadows with one tile, about 80 ms with every chunk of every layer (as a first build on the main thread). Left out of the measure: the registry's main-thread `check` and insert of the new value as it lands, `take_class_tiles`' scan of every chunk of each kept tile layer when the root registers classes (the timed map registers none), and the renderer's rebuild of the visible changed chunks. A dev-time hitch.
- `padded.rs`: each tileset's tiles copied into cells of a padded `Image` of its own, each cell a little bigger than its tile (edges extruded to the cell's edges), so no read the tile shader makes reaches a neighbour (spec `docs/specs/2026-11-04-tile-padding-design.md`). Each tile keeps K clean levels (`clean_levels`): the largest k for which rounding each side up to a multiple of 2^k adds at most a quarter of it, at most 3 in the atlas (its pages keep four levels); its cell is the tile so rounded (`cell_for`: 16 px tiles keep their size, 256 x 352 take 256 x 384 with K 7, 120 x 140 take 128 x 160 with K 5, 129 px take 160, the worst kind), starts on a multiple of 2^K, and the image's sides are multiples of the largest 2^K among its cells, so the GPU's levels (sized by rounding down) halve it exactly up to there and every cell is whole texels of every level up to its K. Cells are not square, nor one size per tileset: an image collection's pictures each take a cell of their own size (one 512 px castle and fifty 32 px flowers about 2.2 times their pixels with the levels, `padded_bytes_stay_near_the_art`). Tiles of one cell size go in a grid of the column count that makes the smallest image within the side limit (the squarer of two alike, then the wider: Sketch Town's 14 pictures in 7 columns of 2, 1792 x 768, about 1.45 times their pixels with the levels); mixed sizes are packed in shelves, tallest first, each block on the first shelf with room on a multiple of its own 2^K, at widths up to the longest row (`arrange`, `pack`: up to 129 stepped widths (`MIXED_WIDTHS`) and the full width tried). An animation's frames each take a cell of its largest frame's size (and its tile's), `columns` to a row in one block of their own, so the draw steps frame `k` to `(k % columns, k / columns)` cells from the first; every frame is painted at its tile's size, as before. The layout is made first as a texture of its own (K uncapped); one whose sides both come out at 256 px or less is made again with K at most 3, which only shrinks its cells, so it sits in the atlas. Every padded image is `Image::borderless` and has no margin: over 256 px it is a texture of its own with no border and every level (the levels past each cell's K go unread), its cells from texel 0; smaller, it sits in the atlas where a slot's origin on 8 texels and its 8 texel border (`atlas::BORDER`) keep every cell whole texels of the page's four levels (a full atlas's fallback texture is borderless too, so the cells still start at texel 0). The tile fragment stages (`fs_tile`, `sample_cell`; render module notes, Tile chunks) pick the level from the UV's derivatives, cap it at the tile's K (carried in each instance), and read the two levels round it, each kept half a texel of its own level inside the cell: no read reaches a neighbour at any level (`big_tiles_and_mixed_collections_show_no_neighbour_at_any_zoom`, `turned_flipped_and_animated_tiles_and_tile_objects_show_no_neighbour`, `a_squashed_layer_and_a_turned_camera_show_no_neighbour`, `testing/tile_bleeding.rs`), and a tile zoomed out a little keeps its outer texels (`a_tile_zoomed_out_a_little_keeps_its_outer_texels_sharp`). Known limit: a tile drawn smaller than its level K asks for (at most a few pixels wide) reads level K, which can shimmer slightly as the camera moves, never showing a neighbour. Pixel art samples nearest at the full-size level through the sprite's entries (`fs_base`), clamped half a texel inside the cell. `PaddedLayout::gpu_bytes` is the padded image with the levels its home keeps (an atlas page's four, or a texture of its own's whole chain) and `art_bytes` its pictures' own; `padded_sizes` reads each kept padding's, by its tileset's name (`PaddedSize`). `PaddedLayout` says where each tile sits (`PaddedCell`: origin, size, clean levels) and where an animation's frames do (`PaddedAnimation::frame`); `PaddedTilesets` makes one per tileset, again when the tileset or an image of it changes (in place, keeping the handle), and frees those no round of `get`s (one a frame) has asked for in `KEEP_ROUNDS` (120, as long as the batch keeps an undrawn chunk): a level despawned and another of the same tileset spawned soon after (restarted, or the next level) draws with the same padding, where padding again would cost its landing job the padding and its landing frame a new texture's first write (about 0.4 to 0.6 ms for the 256 x 256 map's tileset in `benches/tilemap.rs`; `a_padding_unasked_for_a_while_is_kept_and_then_freed`). The cost is memory: a padded image (up to 8,192 texels a side, 268 MB of RGBA, with its texture) outlives its last map by those 120 frames, so a level change between two big tilesets holds both for two seconds, about twice the peak. The cache is the world's (`PaddedCache`, a lock `init_world_resources` inserts and the drawer holds each frame, where taking a resource out and putting it back each frame would move an entity twice; inserted before the first scene enters, so a map spawned in its `on_enter` is padded off the frame too, `a_map_spawned_entering_the_first_scene_lands_with_its_tilesets_padded`): a map's landing job pads the tilesets it has no padding of yet and makes their textures' levels (`Image::prepare_upload`) on its thread (`to_pad`, `ToPad::pad`), and its landing hands them over (`adopt`), so its frame only writes them (`a_landing_pads_its_tilesets_off_the_frame_and_hands_them_over`); a tileset padded before and changed since is padded again by the drawer, at once and in place, as below (one grown past the atlas into a texture of its own draws clean from it: `a_tileset_that_grows_past_the_atlas_pads_again_and_draws_clean`). An inline tileset is known by its `Arc`'s address, so what is kept of one (its padding, a refusal) holds the allocation (a `Weak`) and no tileset made later can take the address and get its padding (`a_gone_embedded_tileset_s_address_is_held_while_its_padding_is_kept`). A tileset whose padded image would pass the device's largest texture side draws nothing, and the dev log warns once per version of it, naming it (`a_tileset_too_big_to_pad_draws_nothing_and_warns_once`), or names the tile when even its cell would not fit; it is not padded again until it changes, and nothing of the oversized image is made. The limit is the cache's (`PaddedTilesets::set_texture_limit`, which `init_gpu` calls with the device's `max_texture_dimension_2d`; landing jobs carry it in `ToPad`): 8,192 texels under the `Limits::default()` the engine asks for, which the engine does not raise. A larger padded image was refused by the atlas (`atlas.rs`, the image and its border past the limit) with no warning and drew nothing (`a_padded_image_past_the_device_s_texture_side_is_too_big`). Under it: 16,384 tiles of 64 px (128 by 128 cells of their own size), 65,536 of 32 px, 262,144 of 16 px, 3,264 of 120 x 140 px (cells of 128 x 160), 672 of 256 x 352 px, and a collection of 1,024 pictures of 256 px. Several padded pages per tileset would lift it. The module is public but doc-hidden, for `tests/tilemap_alloc.rs` (a cache hit allocates nothing) and `benches/tilemap.rs`. A padded image prepared ahead is a first upload, written at once; one padded again in place is made in code, written at once too (below). An animated tile keeps its own cell, and its frames a block after the still tiles; unequal frame times repeat frames to a common beat (past 128 cells, the mean time). `render/tiles.rs` turns a chunk into instances in layer-local pixels (lane layout in its module docs), and its `TileDrawer` draws spawned maps' layers (an image layer as one quad per view, its image repeating across it; render module notes) and their tile layers: a map's tilesets padded each frame it exists (a disabled map's too, so its images stay; a disabled map draws nothing, `Disabled` not reaching its layers), hidden layers skipped, each visible chunk built once into the batch's chunk store and drawn at the layer's interpolated transform. A layer entity's own `RenderLayers` replace the map's for what it draws. Each padding gets a `serial` never given before, which the chunks' look stamp includes, so a tileset padded again rebuilds the chunks drawn from it. `TileGrid::chunk_version` and `version` stamp every change from one process-wide counter, so a version never repeats, across grids and clones too: a layer whose `TileLayerData::tiles` is swapped for another, even one filled the same way, redraws. A fill or `mark_all_dirty` is one change, one version; consumers keep their last-seen versions. A padding made again in place is made in code, so its upload has nothing made ahead and the renderer writes it at once, never over frames (graphics module notes, Reloads): a re-padded tileset's texture and its chunks' new places change in the same frame (`a_tileset_padded_again_never_draws_its_old_image_with_new_places`, a 768 x 640 padding padded again at 800 x 768, over a frame's upload budget, in 25 columns where 24 were: every tile's UVs changed with the image's size). A padding made again in place must never have its upload prepared ahead (`Image::prepare_upload`, as a landing's first padding has): a large new version would be written over frames while the old texture draws, and the chunks would need to keep the old layout until the new texture shows: with `prepare_upload` added that test fails within about fifteen frames of the save, a tile showing its neighbour (frame 14 and frame 15 in two runs: the frame depends on how long the reload takes).

## Gotchas

- `tiled/`: Tiled's formats as registry assets. `raw.rs` holds what a file says (serde structs named as Tiled's JSON names them; `xml.rs` fills the same ones from TMX, TSX and TX, so every non-zero default is an `Option` and `build.rs` owns the defaults), `data.rs` decodes gids and layer data (CSV, base64, gzip, zlib, zstd via `ruzstd`, all bounded by the layer's cell count: a small file of each compression inflating to 8 MiB fails, as does data cut short or corrupted, except that Tiled writes zstd frames without a checksum, so bytes changed inside one can decode to other tiles, never to more cells), `build.rs` turns raw into `TileMap` and `Tileset` and holds the checks (a layer's gids become cells in place, `Builder::cells`, and go into the grid by rows, `TileGrid::write_rows`), `json.rs` the `TiledMapLoader` (`.tmj`, `.tmx`) and `TiledTilesetLoader` (`.tsj`, `.tsx`), picking the reader by extension, `objects.rs` the objects of object layers and templates, `wang.rs` Wang sets, `project.rs` the Tiled project file and the property resolution (`Props::read`, `resolve_properties`).
- A path written in a file joins that file's folder; the registry normalises it and refuses one leaving the asset root. An inline tileset's paths resolve against the map's folder.
- **A dependency that fails is named where the file names it.** `load_dep` notes each dependency's normalised path and place (`DependencyPlaces`, crate-private on `TileMap`, `Tileset` and `LdtkProject`, its `Debug` only a count so the XML/JSON twins compare), and each loader's `dependency_failed` turns the server's `AssetError::Dependency` into `place: "path" did not load: why`, as `load_dep` does for one that fails under `load_now`: an inline or LDtk tileset's image names its tileset, an image layer's its layer, an external tileset's file its `tileset N` and that file's error its image (`a_tileset_image_that_does_not_load_fails_the_map_naming_it_and_the_tileset`, both ways, and the LDtk twin). A `.world`'s maps keep the server's error, which names the map.
- External tilesets are dependencies and are not loaded while the map loads, so a gid is matched to a tileset by `firstgid` alone. - Group layers flatten: offset adds, opacity and tint multiply, parallax multiplies, visibility ands, a non-normal blend passes down, and a group's `floor` passes to its layers that name none (the nearest group's that names one; `a_group_layer_s_floor_and_offset_pass_to_its_children`, which pins that `collides` and `y_sort` do not pass). Only that is read of a group (`Props::read_one`: its own `floor`, else its project class's default for it): its other properties are neither converted, validated nor counted against the file, and its class is never warned about, so a map with groups and no floor loads exactly as it did (`a_group_with_a_property_the_project_cannot_read_still_loads`, `a_group_whose_class_the_project_lacks_neither_warns_nor_refuses`, `a_group_class_s_floor_default_puts_its_layers_on_that_floor`). Layer ids stay as written; a layer without one gets the next free id.
- **Infinite maps** (`infinite` in either format): each tile layer is an unbounded grid of its chunks (`RawChunk`: JSON's `chunks`, TMX's `<chunk>` elements under `<data>`, whose `<tile>` elements read as the layer's do), each decoded in the layer's encoding and compression and written at its own `x`, `y`, `width` and `height` (never an assumed 16: `editorsettings.chunksize` is only how Tiled cut them), so chunks of any size land where Tiled put them, negative cells included (`an_infinite_map_s_chunks_land_where_tiled_put_them`, `chunks_of_any_size_and_every_encoding_load`, and Tiled's chunks of 24 x 24 straddling the engine's borders at x 32 and -32 with a tile layer in a group, `chunks_straddling_the_engine_s_chunks_and_a_grouped_layer_land_where_tiled_put_them`, whose world rectangle holds the grouped layer too, `an_infinite_map_s_world_rectangle_holds_its_grouped_layers_and_straddling_chunks`; over fixtures Tiled saved: `scripts/make-fixtures.js`, the `infinite*` maps). A chunk Tiled left out (it leaves out empty ones, though its JSON layer's `startx`, `starty`, `width` and `height` still count them: never read) has no engine chunk. The map's `width`, `height` and a TMX layer's (0) are not read. Before anything is decoded, every chunk is checked (`check_chunk`: its `x`, `y`, `width` and `height` all stated, `RawChunk`'s being `Option`s in both readers, where a missing one read as 0 (`a_chunk_that_does_not_state_its_place_or_size_fails_naming_it` and its TMX twin; a chunk lacking its place is named by its order in the layer, `chunk 1 (counting from 0)`), a size of a cell or more, within a layer's cells, its cells within `i32`, and their pixels, their outlines' box on the map's grid in exact `f64` (`MapGrid::exact_box`), within `i32`, so a chunk at x = 2,147,483,600 fails, as does one past the cells an `i32` holds, and one up and right whose pixels fit as 64 x 32 squares but not as diamonds, whose x is the column less the row) and counted as the engine will allocate it (`chunked_cells`): a layer counts the more of the cells its chunks hold (what is decoded) and the cells of the engine's chunks they reach (1,024 for each, since a chunk is made whole where a tile goes: a 1 x 1 chunk takes 1,024, a 16 x 16 one at (24, 24) reaches four), the engine chunks found from the chunks' places and sizes, keeping no more of them than a layer holds, so 65,537 one-cell chunks 32 cells apart fail a layer's cap and scattered chunks cannot allocate gigabytes (`infinite_chunks_count_the_engine_chunks_they_take_before_allocating`); a chunk's data must then hold its cells before it is decoded. Errors name `layer "name", chunk (x, y)` and, for a gid, its cell (`hostile_infinite_chunks_are_refused_before_allocating`; the TMX reader's own path, short CSV, a negative width and an `x` past `i64`, `hostile_tmx_chunks_are_refused_naming_their_place`, the last failing in the reader with its line and column). A chunked layer in a bounded map, or an infinite map's layer of data, fails; an infinite layer of no chunks or empty data loads empty. Nothing is sized from the chunks' rectangle: a file with chunks at (0, 0) and (1,000,000, -1,000,000) loads and lands holding two (`a_file_s_chunk_a_million_cells_out_loads_and_lands_holding_only_its_chunks`).
- Limits, each a load error naming the place: 1<<26 cells a layer or map, 256 tilesets a map, 1,048,575 tiles a tileset, an infinite map's chunks within the pixels an `i32` holds. **Cells are counted as the engine allocates them:** a grid makes each `CHUNK_SIZE` chunk whole where a tile goes, so a bounded layer counts every chunk it spans, ceil(w / 32) x ceil(h / 32) x 1,024 cells (`engine_cells`, `layer_cells`: an 8192 x 8192 layer is 2^26 either way, a 10,000 x 64 runner 641,024, a 1 x 1 layer 1,024, and a 1 x 2^26 layer, within a layer's cells, is 2^31 and refused, where the sparse grids would have let a small zlib file allocate 8 GiB; `bounded_layers_count_the_engine_chunks_they_span_and_the_spec_s_maps_fit`, `a_thin_bounded_layer_counts_the_engine_chunks_it_spans_before_allocating`), and an infinite layer the more of the cells its chunks hold and the cells of the engine chunks they reach (`chunked_cells`, above). A map's tile layers take at most 1<<27 such cells together (`MAX_MAP_CELLS`, 512 MiB of cells: an 8192 x 8192 map of two layers, a 1024 x 1024 one of 128), counted from the layers' sizes before any is decoded or allocated (`tile_cells`, groups included), so a small file of many big layers (a 1 MB `.tmj` of 40 zstd layers once asked for 10 GiB) fails with `map: its layers hold N cells, past the M a map holds` (`a_map_whose_layers_hold_too_many_cells_together_is_refused_before_decoding_them`). An LDtk level counts its layers the same way (`level_cells`: every non-entity layer from `__cWid` and `__cHei`, empty ones too, each grid, a sub-layer each, as its whole chunks, an IntGrid layer's values once more, one a cell; a layer spanning more chunks than a layer holds fails naming it, `a_thin_level_counts_the_engine_chunks_its_layers_span_before_allocating`), and the project's levels share the same cap, since a project holds all its levels at once (`a_level_whose_layers_hold_too_many_cells_together_is_refused_before_allocating`). Unsupported: layer blend modes other than normal, add, multiply and screen.
- **Grids:** `build::grid_shape` reads a map's `orientation` and its parameters from `RawMap` (`staggeraxis`, `staggerindex`, `hexsidelength`, `skewx`, `skewy`, the same attributes in TMX) into `TileMap::shape`, each left out defaulting as Tiled's reader does (measured: `y`, `odd`, side 0, skew 0; an orientation left out is orthogonal, as before) and a parameter of another grid's not read; an orientation, axis or index Tiled has none of (Tiled itself takes an unknown stagger value as its default; the engine names it), a negative `hexsidelength` (Tiled takes one; `side` is unsigned) and a skew past `i32` fail naming the value (`unknown_grid_values_and_a_negative_hex_side_fail_naming_them`, `tmx_grid_values_load_and_unknown_ones_fail_naming_them`). Fixtures: `scripts/make-fixtures.js`' shapes section saves `shape-*` maps in both formats (every grid `measured.json` samples, filled with cell-shaped tiles of four colours, the outer ring empty; infinite isometric, staggered, hexagonal and oblique maps; a hex map of all sixteen flag combinations on a marker tile; a hex map with a colliding layer), its collision section's `collide-hex-y-odd` and `collide-hex-x-even` (hexagons 120 x 140 and 140 x 120 of side 70, both formats) whose `walls` layer holds a clump of three whole hexagons, a tile whose shape is a small triangle placed plain, turned 60° and flipped horizontally, shapeless tiles round them, and a tile of a second tileset (80 x 180 and 180 x 80, its tileset moved by an offset, its shape a triangle) flipped and turned 60° in one cell and turned 120° in another, over external tilesets (`tilesets/collide-hex-y.tsj`, `collide-hex-x.tsj`, `collide-hex-y-tall.tsj`, `collide-hex-x-tall.tsj` and their TSX twins, written by hand as TSX and saved by Tiled, scripting being unable to give a tile collision shapes), `worlds/shape.world`, and `renders/shape-*.png`, Tiled's `toImage()` of each bounded one, whose cell centres the loaded grids are held to (`odd_and_degenerate_grids_place_as_tiled_measures`; a hexagon whose side is longer than its tile has outlines the step tall, 4 px past the tile box Tiled sizes its render by), and which the renderer's tests compare whole frames with; every tile pixel in them is opaque or clear. `every_grid_loads_in_both_formats_with_its_parameters` and the twin test hold the TMX and TMJ of each to the same map.
- **Objects on every grid** (`tiled/objects.rs`, `MapGrid::object_point` and `object_step`): `build_object` reads an object's position through the map's grid, so the data holds layer pixels. Isometric positions are tile-height units along the diamond's axes (`((px - py) / th * tw / 2 + origin.x, (px + py) / 2)`), oblique ones unskewed pixels, and staggered and hexagonal ones plain pixels. On the first two a rectangle, ellipse (32 points) or capsule becomes an `ObjectShape::Polygon` of its projected outline with a zero `size`, polygons' and polylines' points are projected as steps from the projected position, and points and text keep their box at the projected position. A tile object keeps its pixel size and moves from its alignment point to its top-left corner in screen space; `Alignment::parse` takes the map's shape because an unspecified alignment means bottom-centre on isometric maps (bottom-left elsewhere, `bottomleft` named always meaning bottom-left). Rotation stays what `MapObjectData` always held, a turn about `position` in screen space, which is what Tiled does on these grids (measured: formats note section 9; `a_rotated_object_matches_tileds_corners_on_every_grid` holds every grid's objects to `measured-objects.json`).
- A tileset's `tileoffset` is kept as `Tileset::tile_offset`, and its image's transparent colour (`transparentcolor` in JSON, `<image trans>` in XML, with or without its `#`) as `Tileset::transparent`, which the padding keys out (`paint`: a texel of its RGB pads clear, its alpha not compared; `a_tileset_s_transparent_colour_pads_transparent`); an image layer's is `ImageLayer::transparent`, which the drawer keys out of a copy of the picture (`render/tiles.rs`, `keyed_image`: made once per version of the picture, again in place when it changes, let go 120 frames after it was last drawn; `an_image_layer_s_transparent_colour_draws_clear`). A collection tile's own `<image trans>` is not read: Tiled keeps none (it drops one when it saves the tileset), so Tiled draws none either.
- **What loads otherwise than the editor shows it warns once per file** for each feature, naming its first place and how many more (`Unread`, collected in `Props` and LDtk's `Defs`, warned when the load succeeds): Tiled's tile render size "grid" (fill mode with it), a collection tile's sub-rectangle of its image (`x`, `y`, `width`, `height`), a render order other than right-down on an orthogonal or oblique map (Tiled draws the other grids right-down whatever they say, and oblique ones in their render order, measured: `render_order_warns_only_on_orthogonal_and_oblique_maps`), a top-down object layer (Tiled's default; TMX leaves `draworder` out for it) two of whose tile objects overlap in the file in the other order from their bottom edges' (`drawn_out_of_order`: elsewhere file order draws as Tiled's sort does, so Tiled's default warns only where it shows), a polyline among a tile's collision shapes; LDtk's entity tile render mode where it draws otherwise than stretched over the box (any mode but `Stretch`, unless the box is the tile's size, or `FitInside` and `Cover` with the box the tile's shape), and a parallax layer with `parallaxScaling` (LDtk zooms it). A capsule among a tile's collision shapes collides as its outline (`capsule_points`: two half circles of 8 edges), as an ellipse does (16 points) (`editor_features_drawn_otherwise_warn_once_a_file_naming_each`, `ldtk_features_drawn_otherwise_warn_once_naming_each`, `a_capsule_among_a_tile_s_collision_shapes_collides_as_its_outline`).
- The map's `parallaxoriginx` and `parallaxoriginy` (TMX attributes, TMJ fields) are `TileMap::parallax_origin`, which the drawer measures parallax from (`render/tiles.rs`, `parallax`: a layer's offset is `(1 - factor) * (camera centre - origin)` in map space, so a camera centred on the origin shows every layer unmoved, as Tiled draws it; `a_parallax_layer_is_where_it_would_be_with_the_camera_on_the_map_s_parallax_origin`). LDtk has none: zero.
- Objects: `MapObjectData::position` is always the top-left corner of the object's rectangle in map pixels and `rotation` (radians, clockwise on screen) turns it about that corner. Tiled rotates about the object's own position, which for a tile object is the tileset's `objectalignment` point (bottom-left by default), so a tile object's position is moved to where its top-left corner ends up. A map cannot look inside an external tileset while it loads, so a map with tile objects `cx.read`s the `.tsj` or `.tsx` for its `objectalignment` (`objects.rs::read_alignment`). Ids are Tiled's, as strings; object-reference properties are `PropertyValue::Object(id)`. Fields of `RawObject` an instance of a template can override are `Option`s: the instance starts as the template's object, its own fields win and properties merge by name. A template tile's gid is relative to the template's tileset, matched to the map's by resolved path (a tileset the map lacks is a load error). Templates load from `.tj` and `.tx`. The XML reader (`roxmltree`) leaves an absent attribute `None` and never defaults it, writes class members and numbers as the JSON would hold them, and bounds group nesting by `build.rs`'s `MAX_DEPTH`; its errors read `line L, column C: what`. An XML fixture's properties must be in Tiled's JSON order (alphabetical) for the twin test, which compares every `xml/` fixture with its JSON export field by field.
- The Tiled project (`project.rs`): `[tilemap] tiled_project` in Ryo.toml, `EngineBuilder::tiled_project` over it, else `project.tiled-project` at the asset root if there is one (a configured file that is missing is a load error). The loaders are registered with the path (`with_project`) after `insert_registry`'s default ones. It is read with `cx.read`, so saving it loads the maps again. `Props::read(place, raw, class)` resolves a thing's properties: written values lie over the class member's default, members it omits take the default, nested classes layer (instance over the member's default over the nested class's own), enums read by name (flags as a `List`). Tiled 1.12's list properties (`"type": "list"` with an array of `{type, propertytype, value}` items in JSON, `<item>` elements in XML, which the XML reader turns into the JSON's items) read as a `PropertyValue::List` of their items, each converted by its own type (a class item resolved against its class, an enum by name, a list item recursively, an unset color left out), and so does a class member's list default, whose items name their custom type `propertyType` as the project file names a member's (Tiled reads them so; with `propertytype` it drops the type); a written list replaces the default whole. Without the project or the class, what is written is kept (a list member of an unknown class keeps its typed items) and `engine_warn!` names the class once per file. Classes holding several members of classes multiply, so a few classes can ask for billions of values: one thing's properties may take 65,536 values to resolve, and a file's things 4,194,304 together (`Budget`), past which the load fails naming the place (`classes_fanning_out_past_the_cap_fail_naming_the_cap`); nesting, lists included, stops at 16 deep.
- Wang sets load as written (`wangid` is clockwise from the top, 0 for no color); colors and tile ids are checked.
- `LayerInfo::occludes: None` means "as `collides`"; `lit` defaults to true.
- The reserved layer properties, read into `LayerInfo` by `tiled/build.rs`: `collides`, `occludes`, `y_sort` and `lit` (bools; another type counts as left out), `floor` (`storey`: a whole number from 0 to 255, `Properties::int` taking a float with no fraction; any other value or type refuses the map with an error naming the layer, `layer "upper": property "floor": "two" is not a whole number from 0 to 255`, as a tile's `stairs` names its tileset and tile; `a_floor_that_is_not_a_whole_number_from_0_to_255_refuses_the_map`), and `filter` on tile and object layers (`layer_filter`: the string `linear` or `nearest`, in any case; left out, of another value or another type, `Filter::Default`, which samples as the target does, nearest for pixel art). The tile drawer samples a layer's chunks and tile objects by it (`render/tiles.rs`), so a pixel-art game's painted art drawn zoomed out reads the padded copy's smaller copies (`a_layer_s_filter_picks_its_sampling_whatever_the_target`, `testing/tile_bleeding.rs`); image layers keep the default, as LDtk's layers do (they have no custom properties).
- Spawned maps: the map entity gets a child per layer (`MapLayerRef`, the layer's `LayerInfo` as a component, a `Transform` at the layer's offset, less its floor's height on a floor above the ground (`ground_offset`); `TileLayerData` for a tile layer, `ImageLayer` for an image layer), plus `MapIds` (objects by id), the crate's `MapLayers` (layer entities by index) and `MapFloors` when some layer is above the ground floor. `TileLayerData` is the entity's own copy of the tiles (`tiles`, a `TileGrid`), IntGrid and grid (`grid`, the layer's `MapGrid`), with every chunk marked dirty at spawn (a new `TileGrid` has none) so the chunk renderer builds each once. Objects are children of their object layer: `MapObject` with a `Transform` at the centre of the object's rectangle (its point; a polygon's or polyline's origin, Tiled giving those no size), turned by its rotation, polygon points relative to it; a tile object keeps its `tile`, and a hidden object spawns with `visible: false` (games use hidden markers; drawing skips it). Tiles of a class the root registers are taken out of the copied grid and spawn as `MapTile` children of their layer at the cell's centre on the layer's grid (`MapGrid::cell_center`), listed by cell in the layer's `LayerTiles` (crate). The objects' entities are spawned and `MapIds` inserted before the `MapObject`s go on, so an `Add<MapObject>` observer can resolve references.
- Games name the lifecycle events (`Add`, `Insert`, `Remove`, public in `ecs/kernel.rs`), so the skill teaches three routes to an object's behaviour: an observer of `Add<MapObject>`, which runs as each object arrives (`a_game_s_observers_see_each_object_arrive_whole_and_go`: once per object, its fields filled and its references resolved, and `Remove` once per object as the map goes); `MapEvent::Spawned`; and a system over `Added<MapObject>`. The observer and the system also see the objects a reload spawns; one it updates in place fires `Insert` only. At observer time the map's layers, `MapIds`, colliders and shadows are in, and so is every object's entity, though objects later in the file lack their `MapObject` yet and class tiles come after; a body the observer puts down stands on the ground (`a_body_on_a_map_reported_ready_never_falls_through` spawns one from an `Add<MapObject>` observer), while physics queries see nothing of the map until the next step.
- Game observers run as each part arrives and may despawn the map, a layer or an object before its turn: the spawn checks each is alive (`get_entity_mut`, never `entity_mut`), skips what is gone, and stops (no `Spawned`) once the map is gone. Every child is spawned as `(Transform, ChildOf(parent))` first and its observed components inserted after (`child_of`): bevy runs `Add` observers before `ChildOf`'s insert hook, so an observer that despawned the parent of a child spawned whole would orphan it and fail the hook's queued `Children` update on the gone parent.
- **Class tiles follow whole chunks** on an unbounded layer of a map whose root registers classes: `follow_chunk_swaps` (`PreUpdate`, after `reload_maps`, so before anything collides or draws) keeps on each such layer what it last followed (`ChunksFollowed`, crate: the grid's id and version, and the chunks it had). A chunk unloaded since (one it had that the grid lacks) despawns its class tiles; a chunk loaded since (`loaded_since`) despawns the ones it had and has its tiles of a registered class taken out of the grid and spawned, as a landing's are (`loading_a_chunk_back_rebuilds_it_as_new`: a new entity). A layer with no load or unload since it last looked (`TileGrid::swapped`) costs a look and allocates nothing, so maps that never stream pay nothing more (`tests/tilemap_alloc.rs` measures an idle streamed frame). A grid it has not followed yet is noted: on a layer it never followed (one just landed) the chunks loaded whole so far are followed as loads, since a stream lands chunks in the map's own landing frame, before the follower's first look (`chunks_landing_in_the_map_s_own_landing_frame_spawn_their_class_tiles`; noted only, their class tiles stayed in the grid and drew as tiles), and on a layer whose grid a game swapped in nothing more is done. The loaded chunks are walked by row and the tiles found spawn in row order across them, as a landing's; taking them out of the grid is folded into the chunks' loads (`TileGrid::fold_into_load`), so a stream does not count it as a player's change. The table of the map's tiles of a registered class is the map's `ClassTable` (crate, on the map entity), built again only when its tilesets' stamps change, so a stream landing chunk after chunk does not rebuild it for each layer and frame; `MapRoot`'s discard takes it. Edits are not followed: they place their own class tiles. A class tile in a chunk its grid lacks is in no chunk that can unload, and stays: an edit placing one on an unbounded layer clears an absent cell, making no chunk, so a stream loads each chunk it lands into every unbounded layer (empty cells for a layer its generator filled nothing of) and refuses edits outside its resident chunks, the precondition for such a class tile to go with its chunk (`a_class_tile_edited_into_a_layer_the_generator_left_empty_goes_with_its_chunk`).
- Runtime edits place class tiles too: `set_tile`/`fill` with a tile whose class the root registers spawn a `MapTile` (the grid cell cleared) instead of writing the grid; a class tile on a cell any edit touches is despawned first (the same tile again changes nothing). A plain `fill` takes the class tiles in its region through `LayerTiles` and fills the grid in one go; a class-tile `fill` goes cell by cell. `tile()` reads the grid, so a cell holding a class tile reads `None`. `MapObject::reference` reads `PropertyValue::Object(id)`, treats Tiled's unset `"0"` (and an empty id) as none, and checks the entity is alive (no bookkeeping system: a despawned object just stops resolving).
- A map waits while its asset (or, for a map built in code, a tileset asset it names) loads; one despawned meanwhile (a scene's `SceneCleanup::WORLD`) or no longer a `MapRoot` is not in the query and spawns nothing; a disabled one waits until enabled. A failure is reported once per `Arc<AssetError>` (the registry shares one per failure), and a missing LDtk level once per project version; a failed map whose file is saved fixed spawns. `MapRoot`'s `on_discard` hook takes down a spawned map's layers (and `MapIds`, `MapLayers`, `MapApplied`, the reported failure, its stream's `StreamState` and its `ClassTable`, and despawns the entities its chunks owned, `ChunkOwned`) when the root is replaced or removed, so a new root spawns, and streams, afresh.
- `MapsView` finds layers through `MapLayers` (by index, or the first by name), returns `None`/`false`/0 for a map not spawned, a missing layer, a non-tile layer or a cell outside, and marks `TileLayerData` changed only when an edit changed a cell. World/cell conversions take a layer (the grid is per layer) and walk the `Transform`s of the layer and its ancestors as they are now, not `GlobalTransform`, so a map moved this frame converts before propagation; `world_to_cell` gives cells outside the layer too, and `None` for a layer scaled to nothing.
- `ldtk/`: LDtk 1.5.x projects (`.ldtk`) as `LdtkProject` assets, one `LdtkLevel` per level with its `TileMap`. `raw.rs` holds what a file says (LDtk's JSON names), `build.rs` the conversion and the checks (reusing `tiled::build`'s `fail`, `join`, `load_dep`, `check_tileset`, `check_used_ids`), `mod.rs` the types and `LdtkLoader`. A newer `jsonVersion` than 1.5 is a load error naming both versions; older loads. Tilesets are shared: `LdtkProject::tilesets` in definition order, each level's map embeds them all (a tile's `tileset` is the definition's index). A tileset with no `relPath` (LDtk's built-in icons) is a `Collection` with nothing to draw. Its per-tile data reads as Tiled's tile classes and properties (`tile_data`): a tile's enum tags (`enumTags`, in the tag enum's order) give it its class, the first of them, and a bool property `true` a tag; its `customData` is the string property `custom_data`; a tag or data on a tile past the tileset's count fails naming the tileset (`tile_enum_tags_and_custom_data_read_as_class_and_properties`). `.ldtkl` levels are read with `cx.read` (saving one loads the project again); paths join the project's folder.
- LDtk levels: `layerInstances` list the top layer first, so layers are reversed (layer 0 draws first). `LayerKind::Tiles::grid` is a layer's grid: the map's `grid()` for Tiled and for `add_tile_layer`, for LDtk an orthogonal one of the layer's own `__gridSize`, whose grid, int grid and cell counts follow it (`__cWid` and `__cHei` must match the level's pixels over the grid). The map's `tile_size` and `size` come from the bottom tile layer; a level with only entity layers uses `defaultGridSize`. Tiles whose cell is outside the level (auto-tiles of stamps reach past the edge) are dropped. **Stacked, offset and translucent tiles split a layer** (`plan_tiles`): LDtk saves a layer's tiles in display order (the first beneath the next), several in a cell where rules or stamps stack them, at a pixel offset into their cell where a rule moves them, and with an alpha `a` where a rule fades them (1.4 on). Each distinct (stack depth in the cell, offset in the cell, alpha) is a sub-layer (`SubKey`), part of its layer: the layer's own (depth 0, on the grid, opaque; always there, holding the IntGrid and `collides`), then its sub-layers by depth and, within a depth, as LDtk first lists them, each a tile layer named after the layer with `#1`, `#2`..., `LayerInfo::part_of` the layer's uid, its `offset` its tiles' offset and its `opacity` their alpha (both its own, over the layer's), `collides` and `occludes` false, no IntGrid. They are listed after the editor's layers (`build_map`), so `MapLayerRef::index`, and the z of every layer (`root z + index`), stay LDtk's order whatever stacking a save adds (`a_later_layer_keeps_its_place_whatever_stacking_a_save_adds`). The drawer draws a sub-layer as part of its layer (`render/tiles.rs`: the layer found among `MapLayers` by id, so a disabled or gone one takes it along): that layer's `visible`, `y_sorted`, `lit`, parallax, blend, tint, `RenderLayers`, z and interpolated transform, its own offset and opacity over them, its draws tying on the layer's entity just after the layer's own, so a y-sorted layer's stacked rows sort with a character as the layer's do (`a_y_sorted_ldtk_layer_s_stacked_tiles_sort_with_a_character_as_one_layer`, `hiding_an_ldtk_layer_or_keeping_it_from_a_camera_takes_its_stacked_tiles_too`). A game sets a flag on the layer alone. Edits by the layer (`edit::set_tile`, `fill`, and Wang painting through them) clear the cells they touch in its sub-layers (`parts_of`, `clear_parts`; `an_edit_by_name_on_a_split_ldtk_layer_replaces_the_whole_cell`), and `tile` reads the layer's own tiles; a game editing `TileLayerData::tiles` by hand edits the layer's own tiles only. Its `LayerInfo::id` is a hash of the layer's uid and its key with the top bit set (`sub_layer_id`, probed past a clash within the level), so a reload matches it to the sub-layer of the same tiles wherever a save moves it (`a_sub_layer_keeps_its_id_when_a_save_adds_another_before_it`). Past 16 a layer (`MAX_SUBLAYERS`: each is a level-sized grid and a draw a chunk, so 16 bounds a layer at 16 times a plain one's cells, a 1024 x 1024 layer's at 64 MiB) the keys holding the most tiles are kept and every other tile goes to the kept sub-layer whose cell is free and whose offset, then depth, then alpha are nearest, warned once per layer with the count. The sub-layers' cells count toward the level's cap. Measured on LDtk 1.5.3's bundled samples (read by hand, not committed: their art is third-party): every tile of every level now draws (WorldMap_Free_layout, with its `.aseprite` tileset swapped for a blank PNG, kept all 11,684 where 5,069 were lost; Typical_TopDown_example all 2,798, its 240 translucent and 150 offset tiles at their alpha and place), with up to 43 sub-layers a project, and the cap snaps at most 28 tiles of a layer (AutoLayers_6_OptionalRules' rule-heavy IntGrid layer, 44 keys) to a nearest sub-layer (`stacked_offset_and_translucent_tiles_load_as_sub_layers`, and the GPU test `an_ldtk_level_s_stacked_offset_and_translucent_tiles_draw_as_ldtk_shows_them` over `stacked.ldtk`). IntGrid layers hold their auto-tiles as `tiles` and `collides` is true, every other layer's is false. Parallax is `1 - parallaxFactor`. Entity `position` is the top-left (`px` less pivot times size), rotation 0, `class` and `name` the entity identifier, `id` the iid. Field values by `__type`: Point is a `Class` "Point" with `cx` and `cy`, Tile a `Class` "Tile" with `tileset` (identifier), `x`, `y`, `w`, `h`, EntityRef `Object(iid)`, enums `Enum { ty, value }`, arrays `List`; an unset field (null value, or an unset reference) is left out, and null items of an array are skipped. Worlds: a single-world project has one `WorldLayout` named "World" and levels whose `world` is `None`; with multi-worlds each world has its name. A `Neighbour::level` indexes the same world's `WorldLayout::levels`. Linear layouts (`LinearHorizontal`, `LinearVertical`) write no positions, so levels follow one another from the origin, each after the last one's width (or height) plus LDtk's own spacing of 48 px (`World.reorganizeWorld` in LDtk's source; the JSON docs only say "one after the other"). Parallax `1 - factor` is what LDtk's editor shows (it shifts a layer by camera times factor); `parallaxScaling` has no counterpart and warns (above). Level backgrounds images are not loaded; `TileMap::background` is `__bgColor`, else the level's `bgColor`, else the project's `defaultLevelBgColor`.
- **Worlds** (`world.rs`, `tiled/world_file.rs`). `TileWorld` is a Tiled `.world` file: `layout: WorldLayout` plus a `Handle<TileMap>` per level (dependencies, so the world is ready when its maps are; a map that fails fails the world, naming it). An LDtk world is a `WorldLayout` of the project, named by `WorldRef::Ldtk { project, world }`. Tiled neighbours are computed from the rectangles (`direction`): touching along an edge is a cardinal, at a corner a diagonal, overlapping `Overlap`; levels are named by their `fileName` as written. Every listed map is read through `cx.read` (so a save of it reloads the world too), its header only (`map_rect`): its grid is built from the raw fields as its load builds it (`grid_shape`, `MapGrid::for_map`), never by loading the map. **Every map is placed by its tile origin** (the spec's reading: Tiled documents none for infinite or non-orthogonal maps, and its `tiled.loadWorld` returns nothing headless, re-checked with Tiled 1.12.2, so it could not be measured): the world's `x`, `y` are where its pixel (0, 0) sits, `WorldLevel::origin` (cell (0, 0)'s corner, or a bounded isometric map's box's top-left), and its rectangle (`position`, `size`, what neighbours and `levels_in` use) is `grid.bounds_of` its cells from there: a bounded map's every cell (its listed size if it has one), an infinite map's tiles' bounds, whatever `width` and `height` the entry gives (`a_world_places_isometric_and_infinite_hex_levels_by_their_grid_bounds`, over `worlds/shape.world`) (Tiled writes them for every listed map, so with a listed size the rectangle started at the origin and missed the map's negative part: `an_infinite_map_listed_with_a_size_takes_its_tiles_rectangle`). The bounds come from `tiled::build::infinite_bounds`, which counts the map's cells against the caps, then checks, decodes and places its chunks as its load does (`chunked_grid`, shared with the load) and takes their rectangle with `tiles_bounds`, the function `TileMap::bounds()` uses, so the two cannot disagree (`a_world_places_an_infinite_map_by_its_tile_origin`). An infinite map with no tile gets its origin cell as its rectangle, so it can still spawn (`an_empty_infinite_map_in_a_world_takes_its_origin_cell`). A bounded map's `origin` is its `position` unless an oblique skew is negative (its box reaches left or up of its (0, 0)), LDtk's always; `spawn_levels_in` puts each root at its level's `origin`. A bounded map listed with a `width` and `height` takes that size from its box's top-left. Patterns (a pattern's maps are placed as listed ones without a size): the registry cannot list a folder (the web has none), so a pattern is supported only as literal text (escaped punctuation allowed, optional `^`/`$`) with up to two `(\d+)`/`(-?\d+)` numbers; anything else is a load error naming the pattern and "not supported" (a bare `.` is a literal dot, as names are built, never matched; `.*` fails on the `*`; only an unescaped trailing `$` anchors). Placement uses checked i32 arithmetic: a multiplier or offset that overflows fails the load naming the `.world` and the pattern. A probe reads each map (its rectangle as a listed map's without a size), and the map then loads again as a dependency. Names are built by filling the numbers (one is x, two are x then y) and probed with `cx.read`: row by row from y = 0, x from 0 to the first missing file, stopping at the first row whose x = 0 file is missing, at most 1,024 probes. Maps sit at `(x * multiplierX + offsetX, y * multiplierY + offsetY)`. Numbers are written plainly (`to_string`), so zero-padded names (`room_01_02.tmx`) are never built (a literal `0` before a group reaches 00 to 09 only), and numbering from 1 finds nothing, as do negative indices and the maps past a gap (`a_pattern_finds_numbers_from_zero_written_plainly`); a pattern that finds no file fails the load; a world that needs more lists its maps. No regex crate on purpose.
- `MapRoot::world_level` / `ldtk_world_level` make a root with `MapSource::WorldLevel`, which stands for no map until its world loads: `resolve_world_levels` (first thing in `spawn_maps`) swaps the source for `MapSource::Map` or `MapSource::Ldtk`, so every other map system sees only those two (`root_map` reads `None` for an unresolved one). A level the world lacks, or a world that failed, fails the root once per world version. `MapsView::spawn_levels_in(world, area)` spawns a root, a `WorldLevelOf { world, level }` and a `Transform` at the level's position for each level at world depth 0 overlapping the area (`WorldLayout::levels_in(area, depth)`: touching an edge is not overlapping) that has no `WorldLevelOf` for the same world on any entity, disabled ones included (`Allow<Disabled>`: a game parks a level by disabling its root; looked up with the default filter, a parked level spawned again on top of itself), and returns only those entities; a despawned level spawns again. `spawn_levels_in_with(world, area, depth, configure)` streams the given depth and passes each bare root, with its `WorldLevel`, through the game's `configure` before it spawns; the source is put back afterwards, so only the root's settings are the game's and the map stays the level's. `WorldLevel::depth` (and `LdtkLevel::depth`) is LDtk's `worldDepth`, 0 for a Tiled world: streaming one depth keeps a floor above or below from stacking onto the one played (`an_ldtk_world_streams_one_depth_at_a_time`, over `worlds.ldtk`, whose `Upper` lies on depth 1 over `C`). Nothing spawns until the world's asset is loaded. Streaming (when to spawn and despawn) is the game's call. **Hot reload:** each spawned level is an ordinary map root, so saving its map reloads it; saving a `.world` reloads the `TileWorld` (and a level whose map file is read for its size), spawned levels stay where they are, and the next `spawn_levels_in` uses the new layout. Moving or respawning spawned levels on a world save is not done. `WorldLevelOf.level` is an index into the layout at spawn time, so a `.world` save that reorders levels can make `spawn_levels_in`'s dedupe mismatch until the game respawns its levels.
