# AGENTS.md - Particle System

## Integration with Engine

The engine never runs particles. The scene owns a `ParticleSystem` and drives it itself (see `examples/demos/particles/main.rs`):

1. In `update`: `self.particle_system.update(ctx.world, dt)`. This spawns, simulates, applies attractors, and refreshes the emitter particle counts that `is_finished()` reads. Emitters and attractors act at their `GlobalTransform`: an emitter spawned this step is already placed (inserting a `Transform` places the entity), but one moved by editing its `Transform` this step emits from its old position until the step's propagation. **On a floor** (a storey), an emitter with a `Floor`, or below an entity with one, emits where it draws: lifted by its `DrawLift`, as the tile map's `lift_floored` last found it (each fixed step after propagation, and each frame). An emitter spawned this step has none yet: a burst spawned and fired in one `update` fires where it stands, a storey's height below its sprite; fire it on a later step, or spawn it unparented and without a `Floor` at the point it should draw from (its holder's position plus `MapsView::drawn_lift`). Its particles then fly, collide and are attracted in ground space: the colliders and attractors are where their entities stand, so a lifted emitter's sparks pass through the storey's drawn surface and meet the colliders a storey's height below (a recorded limit).
2. In `render(gpu: &mut GpuRenderContext)`: `self.particle_system.collect_for_render(gpu.world, gpu.batch)` (sprites upload when first drawn). Each particle is a world-space quad centred on its position, `size` world units across, on render layer 0: every camera draws it through its own view.

Particles go into the `render` batch and sort by `z_order` (default 0) with entities, other sprites and trails: at equal z they draw after entities and before trails. Give the particle sprite a lower z to sit behind entities. The UI is drawn last.

## Gotchas

- **One-shot cleanup** - nothing despawns finished emitters. Check `is_finished()` and despawn.
- **Layer masks need emitter layers** - an attractor's `with_layer_mask(mask)` only affects particles whose emitter set `with_layer(bits)` with a shared bit. Emitters default to layer 0, which only unmasked attractors (mask 0) affect.
- **Disabled entities and `enabled = false` emitters** - their live particles lose gravity and collisions (the per-emitter lookup only holds enabled emitters on entities that are not `Disabled`). A `Disabled` emitter's particles are also not drawn.
- **Empty ranges** - a min above its max (or a zero spread or zero-size shape) samples the min; it never panics.
- **Randomness** - spawn variation comes from the system's `StdRng`: `new()`/`default()` seed it from entropy, `with_seed(n)` makes it reproducible for a given engine version (headless frame comparisons, replays).

## Collisions

`ParticleEmitter::with_collision(CollisionConfig)` makes `ParticleSystem::update` collide that emitter's particles with the world (in `legacy/mod.rs`, the geometry in `contact.rs`, config types in `collision.rs`):

- **Colliders** - entities with `GlobalTransform` + `Collider`, gathered once per update and only when some emitter has a collision config. Trigger colliders and `Disabled` entities are skipped. No `RigidBody` is needed. Geometry is the collider as physics places it (its entity's world transform, then its `offset` and `rotation`):
  - a circle is a circle;
  - a `Segment`, `Polyline` or `Segments` collider is its edges (`ColliderGeometry::Edges`, world-space edges in the system's `edges` list, with their box for a quick reject): a particle meets an edge whose nearest point is within its radius, its normal the edge's turned to the side the particle is on (from the edge's end at an end), or an edge its move crossed this update, at the crossing, its normal to the side it came from (`edge_contact`; a crossing is met before any edge it merely ends near, the earliest first, else the nearest). A tile map's colliders are such edges, a chunk's outlines packed in one collider whose box is mostly air: by box, particles bounced off the air in it (`particles_meet_a_tile_part_s_edges_not_its_box`). The crossing keeps a fast particle from passing through an edge, which has no inside to catch it (`a_particle_crossing_an_edge_within_an_update_still_bounces`). Only a tile part's loops bound something solid: a `TilePart` that is not one-way, whose `Segments` (or, for a chunk of one loop, closed `Polyline`) physics winds clockwise on screen round its ground, holes included, so the ground is on the same side of every edge. They are stored turned round so each edge's left normal points out (unless the placement mirrors them), with `solid: Some(reach)`, `reach` half the cell of the `TileColliders` the part is a child of (`solid_contact`). A move into the ground across an edge is met where it crossed in; a move out is not met. Otherwise the part's nearest point to the particle decides, locally: within `reach` on its inner side (by the edge's normal, or at a corner the sum of its edges' normals) the particle is in the ground and goes out there, so landing dust spawned round the feet ends on top (`a_particle_starting_just_inside_tile_ground_ends_on_top_of_it`); on its outer side within the radius it touches. Local, because the loops round a place may lie in several parts: a room carved from a wall has its own loop, likely in another chunk's part, and a parity test over one part's edges judged every particle in such a room inside the wall mass and moved it to the mass's far edge each update (`a_particle_in_a_room_whose_wall_s_outline_lies_in_another_part_drifts_untouched`). The half-cell bound keeps a part's loop from claiming air a thinner wall away; ground deeper than that is not judged, and a particle in a thin floor leaves through the nearer face. A hole alone in its chunk, a closed polyline wound as a hole's, takes the tile rule too (`a_particle_in_a_room_alone_in_its_part_bounces_off_its_walls`). Every other collider's edges (a segment, any polyline, closed or not, a one-way run, a game's own `Segments`) are two-sided, as physics treats them: the side is the one the particle came from (`particles_stay_in_a_game_s_closed_polyline_arena_and_bounce`);
  - every other shape is its world bounds (`Collider::world_bounds`), a box.
- **Filtering** - a collider counts only if `collider.layer & config.collision_mask != 0`. A tile map storey's edge (the parts of a `TileColliders` whose crate-internal `surface` is false, as the map sets it on the grid that keeps characters on a raised floor; read in the same lookup as the part's cell) is no surface, and never counts (`a_raised_floor_s_edge_is_no_surface_for_particles`). The collider on a particle's own emitter entity is always ignored (particles spawn inside it). Each particle is a circle of `config.radius`. `Bounce` resolves every overlapping collider in turn (so a particle sliding on a floor still hits a wall gathered after it) but sends at most one event per update (a corner hit is one event); `Die`, `Stick` and `Event` stop at the first contact. Each collider is resolved once per update, so a particle squeezed between nearly opposing colliders can end an update overlapping one of them; the next update corrects it, and a gap narrower than the particle can't be resolved at all.
- **Responses** - `Die` removes the particle. `Bounce` reflects velocity along the contact normal with the restitution, then pushes the particle just outside the surface. `Stick` puts the particle on the surface, its radius out from the contact point along the normal (as `Bounce` places it: a fast particle that crossed an edge sticks where it crossed, not where its move ended inside, `a_fast_stick_particle_sticks_on_the_surface_it_crossed`), zeroes velocity and sets the private `stuck` flag: no more forces or movement, but it still ages, fades and dies. `Event` passes through.
- **Messages** - opt-in with `CollisionConfig::with_events()`: `Die` and `Stick` write one `ParticleCollision` message (read with `ctx.messages::<ParticleCollision>()`). `Bounce` emits only on contact entry (the particle touched no collider last update) and only when the impact speed along the normal is at least `MIN_BOUNCE_EVENT_SPEED` (20 px/s, also in the prelude). A particle resting on a surface stays touching, so it emits nothing more, whatever the gravity or dt. `CollisionResponse::Event` always emits on entry. Both entry checks use the private `touching` flag (not-touching to touching), which is per particle, not per collider: moving straight from one collider into an overlapping or adjacent one (or sliding along a floor into a wall) doesn't notify again. Events carry the emitter and collider entities, the particle position, the surface normal and the velocity before the response.
- **Cost** - brute force (every colliding particle against every gathered collider, and against every edge of an edges collider whose box its move reaches), which is fine for hundreds of particles against dozens of colliders. A tile map's chunk collider can hold hundreds of edges: particles over a busy map test them all.
- **Tunnelling** - collisions are checked at particle positions once per update, so very fast particles can pass through thin boxes and circles. Use thicker colliders or lower speeds. Edges also catch a move that crossed them.
