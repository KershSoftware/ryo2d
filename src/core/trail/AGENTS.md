# AGENTS.md - Trail & Line Rendering System

## Integration with Engine

- `record_trail_points` (`systems/trail.rs`, `FixedSet::Trails`, after propagation) calls `trail.update(x, y, dt)` every fixed step for each `Trail2D` with its entity's `GlobalTransform` translation (plus its `DrawLift` on a floor, so a trail on a storey records where its entity draws) and the fixed timestep. Recording and aging therefore follow game time (paused, slowed, stopped by hitstop). Manual `update()` calls are only needed for custom positions.
- A `Trail2D` without a `Transform` is still drawn (its points are absolute world positions) but never records or ages. A `Line2D` needs a `Transform`: its points are offsets from the entity's world translation (rotation and scale are not applied), and on an interpolated entity it draws at the blended position, with the sprite.
- **Drawing:** this module only holds the components. Each frame the engine turns every trail and line into a joined polyline on the 2D batch's line stream (`render/batch/lines.rs` ports the per-point rules: width by taper, colour by fade or age, `u` by texture mode, and copies the points; the vertex stage builds the triangles, `shaders/include/line_pieces.wgsl`, with `polyline.rs` the CPU reference it is tested against; `shaders/core/line.wgsl` draws them). Each trail or line is one draw-list item, so it sorts by `z_order` with entities and `Scene::render` sprites (equal z: after entities and sprites) and neighbours with equal blend (and texture) share a draw call. The stream holds as many points and triangles a frame as one storage binding of the device does (`StreamCap`, `render/batch/lines.rs`); a line past that draws nothing, and the log is warned once.
- **Joins and caps:** `LineJoin::{Miter (default), Bevel, Round}` on both `Trail2D` and `Line2D` (a miter longer than four times the width is bevelled); `LineCap::{Butt, Round, Square}`. Every corner gets one wedge on its outside, so translucent corners are no darker than their segments.
- **Edges:** `with_edge(LineEdge::{Smooth, Hard})` on both (`edge()`, `set_edge`); `None`, the default, takes the target's: Hard on the pixel-art target, Smooth elsewhere. A Hard line draws each pixel whole or not at all, one pixel a column for a line of one pixel, as pixel art draws lines; Soft and Glow keep their shape across it (render module notes, The 2D Batch, Lines; `a_hard_line_is_one_pixel_per_column_at_any_angle`, `a_line_s_or_trail_s_edge_reaches_its_line_table_entry`).
- **Blend and filter:** `with_blend(BlendMode)` on both (`Additive` for lasers and glows); `Trail2D::with_filter(Filter)` for a textured trail. A tiled texture repeats inside its atlas slot (`sample_wrapped`).
- **Colour:** line colours are taken as linear (as 2a's trail renderer did), so a line looks lighter than a sprite of the same `Color`.
- **Lighting:** trails/lines are drawn before lighting, so they are lit like sprites (dark in unlit areas). `with_lighting(false)` opts out: the item is drawn by the `unlit_world` pass onto the lit scene after lighting and before post-processing. Regression tests in `tests/e2e/headless_engine.rs`: `lines_render_when_lighting_is_on`, `unlit_lines_stay_bright_in_the_dark`, `unlit_lines_render_without_lighting`.

## Laser Fade-Out Effects

- `set_enabled(false)` stops recording **and hides** the trail. For fade-outs, use `set_recording(false)`: aging and lifetime removal continue while it still renders.
- Points are only removed by age when a lifetime is set (`with_lifetime`, or the `fading_laser` preset).
- **Static fade** (whole line fades): `Trail2D::fading_laser(color, width, secs)` + `populate_line(x1, y1, x2, y2, n)` + `set_recording(false)`, spawned with a `Transform`.
- **Moving tracer** (recommended; the beam visibly sweeps away): spawn an invisible fast-moving entity with a `Trail2D` that has a lifetime. On reaching the target, call `set_recording(false)`. Despawn when `point_count() == 0`.

## Shader

`shaders/core/line.wgsl` (composed with the camera, colour and sampling includes) has `fs_main` and `fs_textured`. Style (Solid, Soft, Glow) and `TrailEffect` (Lightning, Noise, UvScroll, Ribbon) travel in each line's `params`, in the line stream's line table with its emission, a textured line's atlas slot, its points, join and cap (`render/batch/lines.rs`, `build_effect_params`; `LineInfo`), which the vertex stage reads by the line its triangle's point names. It builds each vertex from the points (`line_pieces.wgsl`), decodes its sRGB colour to linear and multiplies it by the emission, moves it along its `across` direction for a displacement effect (by its effect `u`: at a corner point the point's own, so every piece's copy there moves alike and no crack opens), then pushes it a pixel of the camera's image out (`CameraGlobals::px_per_unit`) as far as its limit lets it. Every style covers each pixel by its edges' distances in pixels (no derivative), the coverage multiplying its straight alpha; Soft and Glow shape by the pixel's distance from the centre line over the half width, averaged over the pixel and toward the shape's mean as the line narrows below 3 pixels of half width, and under half a pixel cover as a line of half a pixel, dimmed, so a thin glowing trail (a lightning's tapered tail) stays whole and fades as it narrows; a textured line's texture runs on across the fringe rather than being squeezed inside it (the render module notes, Lines, name the tests). A displacement effect shears each piece between its points, so a steep jag draws narrower across its slant than the trail's width (`polyline.rs`, the shear's limit). A line's edge (`LineEdge`, `with_edge`; `None` takes its target's: Hard where the camera snaps, the pixel-art target and a pixel-art game's texture cameras, Smooth elsewhere) rides in its line table entry (render module notes, Lines, Edges).

## Tips for AI Agents

1. **Trail vs Line**: `Trail2D` for moving objects (auto-records), `Line2D` for static/explicit shapes.
2. **Fade modes**: position fade (`.with_fade(true)`) stretches on fast objects. `.with_fade_distance(px)` fades consistently regardless of speed. Time fade (`.with_lifetime()`) suits slow/stationary sources.
