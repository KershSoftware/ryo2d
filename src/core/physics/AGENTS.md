# AGENTS.md - Physics Module

Rapier2D 0.36 behind `Physics` (a resource), driven by the systems in `src/core/physics/sync.rs`, plus the character controller, one-way colliders, trigger messages and tile colliders that gameplay (`src/core/gameplay/`) builds on. Usage is covered by the `ryo-physics` and `ryo-character` skills; the designs and their decisions are in `docs/specs/2026-10-13-physics-simulation-design.md` (the simulation) and `docs/specs/2026-10-14-physics-gameplay-design.md` (characters, one-way, triggers, tiles).

| `query.rs` | `QueryFilter`, `Hit`, `Overlap`, `PointHit`, `Touch`, and the `Physics` query, force and contact-state methods |

| `test_util.rs` | Test worlds and helpers (see Notes) |

`rapier2d` is named only in this module. The engine's glam (0.31) and Rapier's (0.33) are different crates: every vector crossing the boundary goes through `convert.rs`.

## Units

The game works in pixels. `PhysicsConfig::pixels_per_meter` (default 100) is Rapier's `length_unit`: tolerances, sleep thresholds and the speed cap are tuned for objects about a metre across. Games should choose it so their typical moving bodies are roughly 0.5 to 2 m: 100 suits sprites of roughly 50 to 200 px, and pixel-art games with 16 to 32 px characters should consider 32. Changing it changes masses and tolerances, not gravity. Smaller bodies also cost more: Rapier 0.36's broad phase pads every collider by 0.04 m (4 px at 100 px/m), so 8 px bodies make many more candidate pairs than 40 px ones (`benches/physics.rs` times both sizes). The default stays 100 (owner decision, 2026-10-14). Gravity is in px/s² (default `(0, 980)`, +Y down), a collider's `density` is mass per square metre, forces are mass·px/s² and impulses mass·px/s. `solver_iterations` (default 4) sets the solver effort. `Physics` converts at its edges; nothing else does.

## The Step

`FixedSet::Physics`, every fixed step while `PhysicsPaused` is false (the scene manager sets it from the top scene's `is_physics_paused` and the stack shape). It is one exclusive system, `run_physics_step`, running physics' own schedule `PhysicsSchedule` (made on first use), chained as `PhysicsSchedule::new` lists them; no game system can be ordered between them. Tests that probe the chain add systems with `test_util::physics_schedule`. `FixedSet::Movement` runs just before it: gameplay's chain (`src/core/gameplay/AGENTS.md`) and physics' one system outside the step, `rebuild_tile_colliders` (see Tile Colliders), so the game's, the presets' and the tile maps' writes all reach this step. The chain:

1. `forget_removed`: joints, colliders and bodies whose components went away (despawn included; a removed body's joints go inert); entities that lost their `RigidBody` or their parent are queued so their colliders find a new owner, and bodies that lost their parent are recorded (`recheck`) so the next sync compares them.
2. `track_disabled`: disabled entities (bodies, colliders, joints) leave the simulation; re-enabled ones come back with the edits made meanwhile (`Transform`, `Velocity`, `RigidBody`, `Collider`, `Joint`). A re-enabled joint is queued for `sync_joints`, so it is made after its bodies are registered and moved. It records its tick (`Physics::chain_tick`): a `Transform` changed after it is not in this step's world poses.
3. `propagate_transforms`: world poses are current before physics reads them.
4. `register_bodies`: new bodies at their world pose; an edited `RigidBody` is applied in place. A body refused for a non-finite pose (logged once) is retried every step until its pose is finite; its colliders wait for it. A dynamic body made kinematic or fixed drops the velocity physics wrote back; a velocity the game set in the same tick is kept.
5. `sync_colliders`: colliders that changed, moved, scaled or changed owner (registered, reparented, reshaped, re-placed).
6. `push_changes`: a body whose world pose changed is moved (a kinematic body travels there over the step); a changed `Velocity` or `AngularVelocity` sets its velocity; velocity-driven kinematic bodies (not characters) are driven; last, the queued forces and impulses are applied.
7. `move_characters`: every character is swept by its `Velocity` (see Characters).
8. `sync_joints`: new and re-enabled joints are made; edits apply in place or remake the joint (see Joints). After `push_changes`, so a joint made as the game moves its body anchors where the body was moved.
9. `step_simulation`: Rapier steps by `time.fixed.timestep()`, so physics follows game speed, pause and hitstop. Afterwards joints pulled past their break force are removed from Rapier.
10. `read_back`: simulated bodies write their pose into their local `Transform` (through their parent) and their velocities. Every write is worked out before any lands, each parent placed where the write-back will leave it (its continuous rotation, not Rapier's wrapped angle, which rounds differently at large angles). It is an exclusive system that advances the world's change tick before it writes, and records that tick (`Physics::write_tick`, see Sync Rules).
11. `emit_messages`: the step's contacts and forces become `CollisionStarted` / `CollisionStopped` and `ContactForce`; trigger overlaps become `TriggerEntered` / `TriggerExited`; broken joints send `JointBroken` and lose their `Joint` component.
12. `update_trigger_contents`: `TriggerContents` is added to new triggers (and removed from colliders that stopped being one) and brought up to date for the triggers that changed, disabled ones included (`Allow<Disabled>`): a trigger spawned disabled has it, and one disabled (which empties it, Rapier stopping its overlaps) is not left listing what was inside.

The order is the dependency order: removals and disables first so nothing below touches a stale handle, propagation before anything reads a world pose, registration before colliders (they need an owner) and colliders before pushes, characters after pushes (they read the game's fresh writes), joints after pushes, and messages last so they describe the step just taken.

`Last` also runs `forget_removed` and `track_disabled` every frame, before `clear_trackers`. Removal events survive two tracker clears, and at render rates above the fixed rate no fixed step may run in between; without it a despawned body would stay in Rapier. In `Last` an entity that lost its body or parent is recorded for relinking too. Added and edited bodies are tick-based and wait for the next fixed step.

`max_delta` (250 ms by default) caps the game time one frame adds, after speed scaling, so a stall cannot run an unbounded number of steps: a 250 ms stall at 60 Hz runs 14 fixed steps (the timestep rounds to whole nanoseconds), at most 15.

## Sync Rules

- **Change-detected, then told apart by tick or by value.** Physics' write-back is an ordinary change (propagation must see bodies move), so change detection alone cannot tell it from a game write.
  - **By tick:** `read_back` records the change tick it runs at (`Physics::write_tick`). At the next step a root body whose `Transform` last changed at that tick is where Rapier left it, and so is its own collider if its `Collider` did not change since (its placement on the body depends only on its scale and the `Collider`); velocities last changed at that tick are Rapier's. `push_changes` and `sync_colliders` skip these without looking at Rapier, unless the body is in `Physics::recheck`: with 1,000 awake bodies that is most of the sync's work.
  - **By value, everything else:** a game write between the write-back and the next sync (a later tick, even when it writes the same value), a nested body or collider (its world pose also depends on its ancestors), and the bodies in `recheck`. Those are bodies that lost their parent (their local `Transform` became their world pose under the old tick), and bodies whose `Transform` the game changed inside the physics chain after its propagation (a system the scheduler put between them): `read_back` overwrites the translation and rotation, as it always did, but its write stamps the whole component, so a new scale would hide under its tick. Velocities written there are overwritten whole by the write-back, as before, so they need no recheck. A pose or velocity is pushed only when it differs from the body's, beyond `1e-3` px and `1e-4` rad (`convert.rs`). A body nobody touched stays asleep.
  - **Read through `get`:** the comparison reads Rapier with `get` and takes `get_mut` only to write. `get_mut` marks a body modified, and Rapier then reprocesses it in the next step.
- **The write-back tick is physics' alone (an invariant).** A body's `Transform`, `Velocity` or `AngularVelocity` last changed at `read_back`'s tick holds only what the write-back put there, or the body is in `recheck`; otherwise a game change is taken for physics' own and never pushed. Two things keep it so. A game change made inside the chain before `read_back` is caught by comparing its tick with `Physics::chain_tick` (`a_scale_written_inside_the_physics_chain_reaches_the_collider`). And no other write lands at its tick: each system runs at a tick of its own, but commands applied at a sync point take the world's current tick, the one the next system is handed. So `read_back` is exclusive and advances the tick before fetching its parameters: wherever the scheduler puts a sync point, its commands land at another tick (`a_command_applied_right_before_the_write_back_is_pushed`). Keep it that way: as a regular system it would share its tick with commands applied just before it. `the_write_back_skip_leaves_every_trajectory_as_it_was` runs a scene through every sync path with the skip on and off and compares a trajectory checksum: keep it passing when changing the sync.
- **Read back** only awake dynamic bodies' and velocity-driven kinematic bodies' poses, and characters' (`Physics::drive_to` marks them driven, see Characters); a body writes zero velocities on the step it falls asleep. Rotation is written back continuously (no jump at the turn), unless the body locks its rotation.
- **Kinematic bodies** are moved by writing `Transform` (the body travels there over the step, carrying what rides it) or by `Velocity` / `AngularVelocity` (input only, never read back). When both are written in one step the `Transform` wins, but a `Transform` write that leaves the pose unchanged does not count. A parent's move counts too: a velocity-driven kinematic body under a parent the game moves does not integrate its velocity that step. A game that keeps its own movement velocity must not store it in the engine's `Velocity` on a kinematic body, or physics will keep integrating it (bullet-hell's barriers keep theirs in a game component). Characters are kinematic bodies whose `Velocity` the controller sweeps instead (see Characters).
- **Invalid input** (a non-finite pose, a degenerate shape, non-finite joint values) is logged once per entity for as long as it keeps a body, collider or joint: leaving the simulation and coming back (a pickup scaled from zero each time it respawns) does not log again. `forget_removed` forgets an entity's warnings when its last physics component goes, registered or not.
- **Forces and impulses** are queued on `Physics` and applied at the end of `push_changes`, just before the step. While physics is paused they accumulate and apply together at the next step. Forces last one step; impulses are instant. They are ignored for bodies that are not dynamic.

## Hierarchy

- A collider belongs to the nearest `RigidBody` at or above its entity; one with no body above it is a fixed collider of its own. Bodies may sit anywhere in the hierarchy.
- A collider's *placement* is its pose relative to the body plus the linear map of its shape (the world scale, mirroring and rotation), so colliders follow world scale. `Collider::world_bounds(global)` gives that geometry; particles, navigation and the overlay use it, so everything agrees with physics. A zero scale takes a collider out of the simulation until it grows.
- Physics reads world poses from `GlobalTransform` and writes a body into its local `Transform` through its parent. A body's rotation is its local direction through its parent's linear map; its own scale (mirroring included) stays with its shape.
- Under a rotated parent with non-uniform scale a child's shape is sheared; shear is approximated by the convex hull of the mapped shape.
- Squash and stretch (a scale effect) on an entity with a collider scales the hitbox too: put the sprite on a child and scale that.

## Shapes

`shape::build(shape, map)` makes the Rapier shape. A rect stays a cuboid under a diagonal map (scale and mirroring only); a circle stays a ball under a uniform scale, a capsule a capsule. Under any other map (skew from rotated non-uniform parents) a rect becomes the hull of its mapped corners and round shapes become 24-point polygons. Polygons are always hulls (concave shapes are several colliders). Degenerate shapes (a zero, negative or non-finite size, fewer than three polygon points, a zero-length segment, a polyline without an edge, a flattening map) and zero scale leave the simulation, logged once per entity. parry panics on some degenerate hulls, so `shape.rs` refuses them before Rapier sees them.

## Events and Contact State

- An `EventCollector` handler captures the first contact point and normal during the step, while Rapier still holds the pair; `emit_messages` reads the collected list afterwards.
- Trigger overlaps are not collisions: `emit_messages` writes `TriggerEntered` / `TriggerExited`, counted per body and kind (`Physics::trigger_overlaps`, keyed by trigger, body and whether the body's colliders are triggers: a body's solid colliders enter at the first one inside and exit when the last leaves, and its trigger colliders the same apart from them), and `update_trigger_contents` keeps `TriggerContents` on every trigger, listing a body once while any of its colliders is inside. Counting the kinds apart keeps a sensor that arrives first (an enemy's aggro radius) from hiding its body's own arrival, which the trigger behaviours act on (they ignore trigger entries). A stop is counted under the kind the collider entered with, if its `is_trigger` changed since. `Owner::trigger` marks trigger colliders, so `step` can tell a sensor pair's sides apart; two overlapping triggers each report the other. `trigger_contents` loses a trigger's entry when the collider is removed, and a later stop for it does not make it again (`despawned_occupied_triggers_leave_no_contents_behind`: bullet-hell's pooled bullets are triggers).
- `Physics::departed` keeps removed colliders' owners until their `Stopped` is reported, so `CollisionStopped` still names entities that were just despawned (either may be gone from the world).
- `contacts`, `overlaps` and `in_contact` read Rapier's narrow phase, so they see solid contacts and trigger overlaps as the last step left them. They report per collider: a collider touching two of a body's colliders is listed for each.
- Queries (`raycast`, `shape_cast`, `overlap_*`, `nearest`) also see the world as the last step left it; a body spawned or moved this tick is not in them until the next step. `QueryFilter` skips triggers by default. `nearest` reports the closest surface point and whether the query point is inside; a ray starting inside a collider hits at distance 0 with a zero normal.

## One-Way Colliders

`Collider::one_way` holds the direction things arrive from, in the collider's own space (`one_way()` is `(0, -1)`, from above), so it turns with the collider. Rapier's collider `user_data` carries it (`one_way::encode`: a flag bit, then the unit direction's bits; zero or `None` is 0) and `ActiveHooks::MODIFY_SOLVER_CONTACTS` is set only on such colliders. `Physics::step` passes `OneWayHooks`, whose `modify_solver_contacts` calls `update_as_oneway_platform` with `ONE_WAY_ANGLE` (45°): a contact pushes only while its normal is within that angle of the direction. Rapier compares the normal leaving collider 1 in collider 1's space, so when the one-way collider is collider 2 the direction is reversed and re-expressed in collider 1's space. A zero direction is solid from every side (warned once).

Characters meet one-way colliders through their sweep's query filter instead (the hook only shapes the solver's contacts, and a character is moved by its sweep):

- **The rule:** a one-way collider blocks a character only when the character started the sweep entirely on its solid side (`one_way::character_above`): the character's lowest point along the collider's world direction is no more than `ABOVE_TOLERANCE` (1 px) below the collider's highest point along it. Both are support points (parry's `SupportMap`), so a round character standing on a tilted platform holds; a world box would stick out along a turned direction by `r·(sin θ + cos θ − 1)`, past the tolerance for a 12 px circle at 8°. A shape without a support map (a polyline) is measured by its own box, turned with it. Since the collider is measured by its highest point, its solid side should be flat across the direction: a one-way polygon with a sloped top holds a character only above its peak, so a tilted platform is a turned rect or segment (the `Collider::one_way` rustdoc says so). Anything else (from below, from the side, part-way through) passes, so a jump goes up through a platform and lands on it once clear. The rule is in the `solid` predicate, built from the character's shape and its start pose: the push-out, the character's own move, the floor probe and the push start from the step's start pose; the carry starts from where the own move left the character (`a_jump_clear_of_a_one_way_platform_is_not_carried_back_into_it`), so a lift takes its rider up through a one-way platform and leaves it on one it sinks through. The sweep is continuous, so a fast fall onto a one-way segment lands (`a_fast_fall_lands_on_a_one_way_segment`).
- **`CharacterState::floor_one_way`:** the floor the probe found is a one-way collider.
- **Drop-through:** only a character standing on a one-way floor drops: `CharacterController::drop_through` then records that collider (`CharacterState::drop_from`, taken before the step's probe), and while the drop lasts the sweep ignores every one-way collider (so a character standing across the seam of two drops through both, and one-way platforms stacked closer than its height are fallen through together). Anywhere else (solid ground, the air) the flag clears at once and nothing is ignored, so a drop pressed the step before a landing does not skip it. A drop is over when the character is entirely below the collider it dropped from (`one_way::character_below`), when it lands (only something solid can hold it during a drop, and the rule lets it out of the platform it still overlaps), when the collider is gone or no longer one-way, and on a teleport (`CharacterState::teleported` forgets `drop_from`, so the teleport's own move finds nothing to drop from and clears the flag through the normal path). Without the landing rule a drop onto a floor less than the character's height below the platform never ended. `move_characters` clears the flag when it is over. A game that clears the flag itself ends the drop, and the next one starts afresh.

## Joints

- A `Joint` component on any entity names two body entities. Entries are made by `sync_joints` once both bodies are simulated; until then the joint is inert (logged once) and retried each step. Despawning a body leaves its joints inert; disabling the joint's entity removes it until it is re-enabled.
- Anchors are resolved at registration: a world point (`at`) becomes each body's local anchor then. A hinge's frame on b is turned by the bodies' relative angle at registration, so revolute limits and position-motor targets count from there (Box2D's referenceAngle); a slider's position is the distance between its anchors along the axis. A fixed joint ignores `with_anchors` and keeps the relative pose it was made at.
- Edits apply in place (a break force is only recorded; motor, limits, collisions and the kind's own values, such as a spring's rest length, replace Rapier's joint data from the poses it was made at) or remake the joint (bodies, kind, anchors). An unchanged joint never wakes its bodies. An edit to an inert joint is only recorded.
- **Breaking** is decided after the step: a joint whose impulse exceeds `break_force` is removed and reported. Break force reads every impulse Rapier keeps: the linear impulse of locked axes (revolute, fixed, a prismatic's cross axis), `limits[axis].impulse` (rope, a prismatic's range) and `motors[axis].impulse` (spring, a prismatic's motor). Each is one solver substep's impulse (Rapier keeps only the last substep's), so the force is the sum's length divided by `dt / num_solver_iterations`. That measures a sustained pull, a load held through a step; a sudden jerk is amplified or missed depending on the substep it lands in, so it may break a joint early or not at all depending on timing and solver iterations. The rustdoc says so.

## Characters

A `CharacterController` makes its entity a character: a kinematic body with locked rotation (required, so one is added when there is none) swept by its `Velocity` each step. The `Collider` on its own entity is the swept shape; the sweep collides with what that collider's layer and mask allow (Rapier's `QueryFilter::groups`), never with triggers or its own body.

- **When:** `move_characters` runs right after `push_changes`, so it sees the game's writes from this tick, while Rapier's queries see the world as the last step left it, plus the colliders registered this tick: Rapier adds a collider to its broad phase only at the step, so `sync_collider` puts each new one in the tree as it is added (`Physics::make_queryable`, through `BroadPhaseBvh::set_aabb`, whose change the step's broad-phase update then takes in as its own). A level spawned with the player, or a tile map rebuilt under a character, is there for that step's sweep. Re-enabled and moved colliders are not refreshed: their pose in Rapier is only brought up to date at the step. It never writes `Transform`: `Physics::drive_to` sets the body's next kinematic position and marks it driven, so `read_back` writes the pose back at the write-back tick (the invariant above holds). `push_changes`' velocity drive skips characters.
- **Teleports:** a game write to a character's `Transform` puts the body there without a sweep (`Physics::was_teleported`; `push_changes` set the body's next kinematic pose to it) and resets `CharacterState` to in the air touching nothing, with `stuck` set, which ends a drop-through (see One-Way Colliders). The character then moves by its `Velocity` from there in the same step: the move starts at the body's next pose composed with the collider's pose on the body (the collider still holds the old pose until the step), pushes out of whatever it was put in first, has no carry, snap or momentum (it stood on nothing), and is driven to the target plus its move, in the target's rotation (the move started from it, so a write that turns a character keeps the turn, `a_teleport_that_turns_a_character_keeps_the_turn`; other moves keep the body's rotation). A correction mid-flight therefore costs no step of motion (`a_teleported_character_keeps_moving_that_step`), and a respawn onto a floor reports `landed` on that step, once, and no `left_floor` (`a_teleport_onto_a_floor_lands_once_on_that_step`), unless that step's `Velocity` rises more than the skin (a floor the move rises off is not its floor, see Surfaces).
- **The sweep** is `sweep.rs`, a port of Rapier 0.36's `move_shape`. Rapier's 2D controller measures a hit's slope with the signed `Vec2::angle_to`, so surfaces whose normal leans one way of up (with up `(0, -1)`, normals with x < 0) are never walls and always hold: slopes rising that way can be walked up at any angle, ledges that way are never stepped, and a character falling while it presses against a wall that way sticks to it. The port measures the angle unsigned, snaps only when the caller says (`snap`: on the floor last step and the move does not go up; Rapier's slide nudge lifted flat moves and turned its own snap off on the way down slopes), leaves out Rapier's kinematic-platform friction (the carry below replaces it; never add both) and reports `sliding_down_slope` only for a slide down a surface facing up but too steep to hold. It also reads a slide within `LEVEL` (1e-4 of its length) of level as level, neither climbing nor slipping: parry's normal for flat ground is sometimes `(0, -0.99999994)`, and Rapier's exact test took a run along it for a slip down a floor that holds and stopped the whole run, for good where the pose repeated (at a platform's edge; `a_run_along_flat_ground_never_stalls`). And its casts apply parry's fallback for a hit right at the start of a cast with the threshold in world units (`StartFallback`, a dispatcher wrapping parry's): for a pair of support-mapped shapes parry takes the normal from a contact query, or reports no hit for the pair when the move leaves the surface, for a time of impact under 1e-4, tuned for metres. Every slide after a hit casts from the skin nearly tangent to the surface, where parry's GJK normal can be tens of degrees off at about 4e-4 px: a character sliding down a 1000 px wall left it for a step every few steps (`a_character_pressing_into_a_tall_wall_slides_down_it_on_either_side`) and a run along a 1000 px floor lost up to half a step, or stopped along a polyline outline (`a_run_along_a_long_floor_keeps_its_pace`). The threshold is `1e-4 * length_unit` (the nudge's). Composite shapes (polylines such as tile outlines, compounds) are cast part by part through the wrapper, so each segment gets the fallback and a segment the move leaves is no hit while the rest of the polyline still counts (`a_run_inside_an_outline_room_stops_at_its_wall_on_either_side`); dropping the whole collider instead would let a run along an outline's floor pass through its own wall. Heightfields keep parry's own path (the engine makes none). The move's casts and the floor probe use it (see Surfaces), not the snap or autostep casts. GJK still loses sub-pixel precision on very large colliders (a run along a 4000 px or longer floor sinks into it by up to 0.16 px at times). The `*_on_either_side` tests pin the symmetry. Drop the port when Rapier measures the angle unsigned. Push-out stays with Rapier (`move_shape` with no translation measures no angle): it runs first when `stuck` or when there is no move. Since a cast that ends within the skin sets `stuck`, it runs on most steps in contact: in the benchmark's 100-character tile scene on about 65% of character steps, moving the character on about 3%, and leaving out the push-outs only a cast within the skin asks for made that frame roughly 15% faster (measured at the end of sub-project 3b; Rapier's push-out makes broad-phase queries and allocates). That is not free: without them the same scene had characters on the floor on 63% of steps instead of 70%, so the push-out also keeps contacts settled. `stuck` holds when the last move ended within the skin of or inside something, when the last push-out still moved the character (Rapier moves it at most a quarter of its height a call), when it was teleported, and for a new character. A falling character's casts never report an obstacle it starts inside (parry finds no hit when the motion does not approach the penetration normal), so without `stuck` it would stay inside until it stopped. Two characters on one point (spawned or respawned there) get the same push-out normal (parry's contact for two coincident shapes picks one axis's default sign) and were pushed the same way every step, together, off a floor's edge or along a wall. So before the push-out `coincident_nudge` moves a character a few hundredths of a pixel (`NUDGE`) off each kinematic collider whose centre is within `COINCIDENT` (0.01 px) of its own, the lower `Entity` left and down, the higher right and up, and each push-out then pushes away from the other: tall shapes split sideways, wide ones stack (`two_characters_spawned_on_one_point_*`, `two_characters_respawned_to_one_point_separate`). It looks only at the narrow phase's pairs for the character's collider (what it touched at the last step), so it costs a walk over its contacts on the steps that push out, and no query; on the step two characters are spawned together there is no pair yet, so they split from the next.
- **Settings:** `KinematicCharacterController` holds them: up `(0, -1)`, the skin (`offset`, absolute; 1% of the collider's height by default), `max_slope` for both climbing and slipping, autostep from `step_height` (min width half the collider's width, never onto dynamic bodies), snap (a fifth of the height by default). Rapier's `normal_nudge_factor` is in world units, so it is scaled by the length unit (`1e-4 * length_unit` px). Top-down: no autostep or snapping, every surface can be climbed and none holds, so it slides along everything and has no floor or ceiling.
- **Surfaces:** after the character's own move (before the carry), a probe casts the shape down `FLOOR_PROBE` (2 px) beyond the skin; what it finds is the floor when its normal is within `max_slope` of up (`floor`, `floor_normal`, the touched point). The probe (`sweep::probe`) keeps no gap and does not stop at penetration, and goes through `StartFallback`, so what the character starts touching or overlapping is a hit only when the probe goes into it: a neighbour or a wall flush against its side, which a probe down runs along, does not hide the floor. Characters do end up flush: spawned side by side their width apart, or left touching by the push-out (Rapier moves a character only out of an overlap deeper than 1e-5, by at most its quarter-height budget, so three characters spawned 10 px apart were pushed to exactly 20 px), and the sweep keeps its skin only from what a move runs into. A probe that stopped at penetration (parry's default) took the neighbour for its first hit, at time zero with its side's normal: both characters were off the floor for good, losing coyote time and the jump (`two_characters_standing_side_by_side_stay_on_the_floor_and_can_jump`, `three_overlapping_characters_pushed_apart_stay_on_the_floor`, `a_character_flush_against_a_wall_stays_on_the_floor`). When that probe finds no floor, a second one keeps the skin, as the sweep's casts do, reaching `FLOOR_PROBE` beyond it: the sweep stands a character on what is within its skin, so a ledge's edge or another character's corner that its side is level with, or up to about 0.28 px past (where the skin-rounded corner's normal is still within 45°), holds it up although the bare shape does not overlap it. The gapless probe saw no floor there (or, exactly level, a degenerate sideways normal), the sweep's floor-like hit is left to the probe and clips nothing, so the character hung in the air, off the floor with its fall growing (980 px/s after a second on a ledge's edge; a co-op player jumping in place beside another, landing on its corner, could not jump again). Standing there was no better: a jumper perched on a flush neighbour's head corner, its feet inside the neighbour's skin, wedged it (the neighbour's sweep went back and forth between the floor and the perched corner: it could not jump, and walked 2.8 px or not at all), and slipping off a rounded corner landed twice. So a floor-like hit that holds up no more than the skin of the character's bottom is a corner, not a floor. That is asked of the character narrowed by its skin on each side (a `Cuboid` with half-width `hx - skin`): probed down, gapless, within the same reach, does it find anything floor-like? If so the character is held up by more than its skin and stands: the platform past a polyline's corner vertex, the other side of the seam of two floors, a post thinner than the skin under its middle (`a_character_stopped_at_a_platforms_edge_stands_there`, on a box, a tile row and a polyline, 1 to 12 px over either edge; `a_character_over_a_polyline_vertex_or_a_seam_stays_put`; `a_character_on_a_post_thinner_than_its_skin_stands_on_it`; the preset's `a_player_letting_go_at_a_tile_edge_stands_there`). It is not measured by the part the probe hit: at a polyline's convex vertex the probe ties between the top run and the side run and often reports the side run, of no width, with the top's normal, so every tile and polyline edge read as a corner and a 16 px character fell off a tile platform with up to 5 px over it (a preset player stopping with half of itself on it), or was shoved back on with 8 to 12 px over it. The part's own reach along X (`Physics::corner_shift`, support points, the hit sub-shape of a polyline or compound) only skips the narrowed probe when the part alone holds up more than the skin, and sets how far to move: away from it, until the part ends a skin past the character's nearer side. Then the floor is probed again from there and the character falls past the corner to what is beside it, landing once (`a_character_jumping_in_place_beside_a_flush_neighbour_lands_beside_it`, gaps 0 to 0.28 px; `a_character_falling_flush_past_a_ledge_corner_does_not_hang_in_the_air`; `a_tile_aligned_character_jumping_beside_a_flush_block_lands_on_the_ground`; the preset's `a_player_beside_one_that_jumped_in_place_moves_and_jumps`). A pixel under it is a floor (`a_character_overlapping_a_ledge_by_a_pixel_stands_on_it`). Not when the move goes onto the corner (`desired` toward it): a step up leaves the character only its remaining run onto the step, and a landing on a platform's near edge has only begun to cross it; without that a step up was undone (`stepping_up_a_ledge_keeps_the_floor`). Only boxes are narrowed (the engine's rect characters): a round character (capsule, circle) meets a corner it barely overlaps with a steep normal, a wall, and already slides off it, and other shapes keep the floor they find. A character walking off an edge leaves it at most a skin early, its overlap then a skin or less. The skin is only the fallback: a single probe keeping it meets everything within the skin at its sides at time zero, where a neighbour's contact normal can lean a hair into the probe (about 1e-4) and hide the floor (tried: it broke `snapping_keeps_a_character_on_a_downward_slope` and `a_character_holding_into_a_touching_neighbour_keeps_its_footing`). The snap's cast is unchanged. Last, a floor the character's own move rose off by more than the skin is not its floor, though the probe still reaches it: the probe looks 2 px past the skin whichever way the character moves, so rising past a ledge's top or a flush neighbour's head (or a jump slow enough to stay within reach) found a floor mid-rise, `landed` and then `left_floor`, and the platformer preset refilled its coyote time there, a free second jump (`rising_flush_past_a_corner_does_not_land_on_it`, `the_take_off_step_leaves_the_floor_once`, the preset's `rising_past_a_ledge_gives_no_second_jump`). The move is the sweep's own (`moved`, its snap included), measured along the floor's normal and against the floor: a walk up a slope moves along it, the push-out is not part of it, and a rider's own move on a rising platform is a step's gravity into it, the carry coming after. Against the floor, because a jump off a lift keeps the lift's rise (momentum), so a lift rising faster than the jump's top speed catches the character up while it still rises in the world: for a floor that did not carry the character last step, the floor's motion at the touched point this step (`floor_motion`) is taken off the move before it is measured (on the floor that carried it, the move is already in the floor's frame). Measured in the world, a lift at 300 px/s was rejected as a floor every step and a tapped jump fell through it, 977 px below its top. Landing is relative too: on the `landed` step the floor's rise (`platform_velocity` along up, when it rises) is taken off the wanted velocity before it is clipped, as leaving adds it; the carry moves the character with the floor from then on, and a velocity still holding the rise lifted it off again, landing and leaving step after step (`a_jump_off_a_fast_lift_lands_back_on_it`: lifts at 200 to 600 px/s, jumps at 60 to 300 px/s, one `left_floor` and one `landed` each; the preset's `a_tapped_jump_off_a_fast_lift_lands_back_on_it`). The landing step does not carry (the character was in the air), so on a fast lift it sinks into the lift by about a step of the lift's motion until the push-out lifts it out: 4 px for a step at 200 px/s, 11 px for two steps at 600 px/s. The slide's nudges (1e-4 length units each) stay far under the skin. A move from the floor that does not go up (the `snap` flag: on the floor last step, `desired` not rising; it holds whether or not snapping is on, `with_snap_to_ground(0)` included) keeps its floor whatever it measures: a step up rises onto its floor by the step's height (`stepping_up_a_ledge_keeps_the_floor` fails without that), and it keeps a run down a slope or over a crest on it. A jump under 0.8 to 2.8 px of headroom (more than twice the skin, and within the probe's reach past it) rises past the skin, is stopped by the ceiling and comes straight back: it reports `left_floor`, then `landed` on the next step (or the one after), where the probe kept its floor before; under less headroom it stays on the floor. The sweep's hits are told apart against up the same way: ceilings within `max_slope` of down, everything else a wall (top-down: all walls). Normals point out of the surface, toward the character. `hits` lists every collider the sweep touched. The queries skip colliders disabled this tick (the `solid` predicate checks Rapier's `is_enabled`; the broad phase keeps them until the step), so a disabled floor or wall goes at once, as a despawned one does; the same predicate holds the one-way rule (see One-Way Colliders). A collider disabled only through its body is flagged by Rapier at the step, so it still counts for that one step.
- **Carry:** the floor a character stood on last step carries it by `Physics::floor_motion` at the touched point: a kinematic body from its pose to its next pose (moved by `Transform` or velocity, turning included; `push_changes` sets the next pose before `move_characters` runs), a dynamic body by its velocity there, a fixed, removed or disabled body not at all. The queries see the floor where it is, not where the step takes it, so the character's own move is swept and the floor probed first, as if the floor stood still, and the carry is swept after it, past everything but the floor's body (walls and ceilings still stop it). One sweep of the move plus the carry sank into a rising floor by a step's gravity every step (16 px in a second under the default gravity, whatever the floor's speed) and floated a step's motion above a sinking one; `a_character_stays_on_top_of_a_rising_or_sinking_platform` pins it. `floor_point` stores the touched point moved on by the floor's motion, where the next step's carry starts, so a turning floor's carry follows it round (`a_character_keeps_its_place_on_a_turning_platform`). A dynamic floor is carried by its velocity at the start of the step, so on one that speeds up or slows down the character drifts by about `a·dt²` a step.
- **Momentum:** `platform_velocity` is the floor's motion at the touched point over the step, divided by `dt` (zero off the floor). On the step a character leaves the floor (a jump, a step off the edge, the floor despawned or disabled) the last step's `platform_velocity` is added to `Velocity`; on the step it lands, the new floor's rise is taken off (see Surfaces), its horizontal speed is not (the platformer preset handles that).
- **Pushing:** with `push` (the default), after the move `KinematicCharacterController::solve_character_collision_impulses` gives the dynamic bodies the move ran into Rapier's approximate impulses: the translation left at each hit, along its normal, as a velocity against the body's velocity there, weighted by both masses. The character's mass is its collider's (Rapier's `Collider::mass`, so from its density or mass; 1 when that is not positive). Kinematic and fixed bodies are never pushed. A character standing on a dynamic body weighs on it the same way. Without `push`, dynamic bodies stop it as walls do.
- **Velocity:** `Velocity` is the game's wanted velocity going in and the real one coming out: each wall and ceiling normal takes out what runs into it, and the floor takes out the fall only (so standing on a gentle slope does not creep); leaving a floor adds its momentum (above). The carry is not part of `Velocity`. A slope too steep to climb stops a run into it rather than turning it into a climb (the clip may not make the character rise faster than it already did; `clip`). `velocity_before` keeps what was asked. A non-finite `Velocity` is not applied (the character stays, its velocity becomes zero). `move_characters` writes `Velocity` at its own tick, not the write-back's; the next `push_changes` sees a changed velocity on a kinematic body and ignores it.
- **Warnings** (once per entity): a controller on a body that is not kinematic is not moved; a controller without a collider on its own entity is not moved; a non-finite velocity.

## Tile Colliders

`TileColliders` (requires `Transform`) is a grid of `TileCell`s that builds its own colliders as child entities marked `TilePart`; the pure geometry is `tiles.rs`.

- **Outlines:** every filled cell adds its corners wound clockwise; an edge two cells share runs both ways and cancels, leaving the boundary, which is chained into closed loops (holes included) with straight runs merged. A long floor is one segment of a polyline, so there are no seams to snag on. Where two regions touch only at a corner the sharpest turn keeps their outlines apart. Slopes are 45 degree half-cells whose diagonal joins the floor without a seam. Characters sweep polylines through `StartFallback` (see Characters).
- **Hollow:** an outline is a polyline, so a body that ends up inside a solid region is not pushed out. `solid_rects()` builds merged rectangles (and polygons for slopes) instead: solid interiors, but seams between rectangles.
- **One-way:** each horizontal run of `OneWay` cells becomes one `Collider::segment(..).one_way()` along its top.
- **Rebuild:** `rebuild_tile_colliders` (registered by physics in `FixedSet::Movement`, so a change made in `update` is built before that step's physics) reconciles every map each step rather than trusting `Changed`. `TileColliders` carries a crate-internal `generation` (from a global counter; new grids and every `set` that really changes a cell take a fresh one), and a built map gets a `TileRecord` with the generation and the public settings (floats by bits, so a NaN size does not rebuild every step). An enabled map whose record is missing or different has all its parts despawned and rebuilt; a `set` that changes nothing (or is out of range) returns false and rebuilds nothing. Disabling the map entity (`Disabled`, as pooling does) or removing the component despawns the parts and drops the record; enabling builds them from the cells as they then are, so a `set` while disabled is not lost. Parts are queried with `Allow<Disabled>`. The grid's layer, mask, friction and restitution go on every part. A non-positive or non-finite tile size builds nothing (`Physics::warn_once`). With a kinematic `RigidBody` on the map entity the parts move with it.
- **Bounded:** the geometry functions clamp the scanned rows and columns to what `cells` can supply, and a grid over `MAX_CELLS` (8192 × 8192) is empty and logged, so a bad size costs nothing.
- **Which entity:** the parts are the colliders. With no body on the map each part is its own fixed body, so collision messages, `CharacterHit` and `CharacterState::floor` name a part (games follow `ChildOf` to the map); with a body on the map they name the map. A rebuild despawns every part and spawns new ones, so `CollisionStopped` / `CollisionStarted` are sent again for what touched the map, and a character standing on it keeps standing, its `floor` now the new part: the old parts leave Rapier in `forget_removed` and the new ones are queryable as they register (`make_queryable`, see Characters), so the sweep never meets a step without the map (`a_character_keeps_standing_on_a_map_whose_outline_splits`: no `left_floor` or `landed`; `a_character_running_at_a_wall_on_a_map_rebuilt_every_step_stops_at_it`).
- **Navigation:** `NavigationGrid::rebuild_from_world` skips `TilePart` colliders and blocks the grid's solid and slope cells exactly (`block_tiles`), from the map's translation only (a turned or scaled map is not followed).

## Notes for AI Agents

1. **Never name Rapier types outside this module** and never `unwrap` in library code; vectors cross `convert.rs`.
2. **Do not poll the old API.** There is no raw event list on `Physics`: game code reads `ctx.messages::<CollisionStarted>()` and friends.
3. **A write to a `Transform` is a teleport** for a dynamic or fixed body and for a character, and a move for any other kinematic body; keep write-backs ordinary changes so propagation sees them.
4. **Debug overlay (F2)** draws Rapier's own geometry through `DebugDraw`: dynamic, kinematic, fixed, trigger, joint and contact colours from `config::debug::colors`; sleeping bodies and disabled colliders are dimmed. Rapier's style lengths (contact normals, body axes) are in metres, so `debug.rs` scales them by `length_unit`.
5. **Tests** use `test_util.rs`: a world with the physics systems registered and `run(world, schedules, steps)`. Anything about stepping needs a fixed `dt`.
