# AGENTS.md - 2D Lighting System

## Architecture

The lighting system uses a **deferred lighting approach**:

1. **Light Accumulation Pass**: lights are batch quads (`batch.rs`), drawn additively into each lit camera's light buffer (`Rgba16Float`, so light above 1 is kept). A light draws on the cameras whose `RenderLayers` it shares and whose view its box meets.
2. **Shadows**: once a frame the shadowed lights the lit cameras see are planned (`plan.rs`) and their maps drawn into the shadow atlas (`atlas.rs`) before any camera pass (Conventions). Every light a lit camera sees then draws in one instanced draw (`light.wgsl`, `LightStep::Add`), and a light with an atlas slot reads its own shadow there: from the pixel's offset from the light (ground space: the light and the pixel are lifted alike on a storey) it finds the view (`view_of`, `include/shadow_view.wgsl`, shared with the map shader so the two cannot disagree), the row (slot x 4 + view), the tangent `local.y / local.x` in the view (`in_view`) as the row's coordinate `(t + 1) / 2`, and, for a light of source size 0 (a hard shadow), makes one `textureSampleCompareLevel` at the row's centre (`textureDimensions` gives the atlas's size) of `distance / radius - bias`, where `bias = 1.5 x distance x (2 / width) / (1 + t²) / radius`, 1.5 of a texel's world width there (the tangent maps to the row linearly, so a texel covers less angle towards a view's ends), which keeps a surface from shadowing itself while the shadow starts at its occluder (`a_shadow_starts_at_its_occluder_near_a_view_s_diagonal`). The comparison sampler filters along the row, so an edge blends over a texel. `shadowed` is 1 minus the result, and the light adds `light x (1 - strength x shadowed) + shadow_rgb x light x strength x shadowed` (strength the shadow colour's alpha): a black shadow at full strength removes the light, a coloured one lets its colour through, and the strength applies once however many occluders lie between (the map holds the nearest). Volumetric beams and glows are held back by the same fraction. A light without a slot (shadows off, past the cap, not seen) samples nothing and is fully lit. One light's shadow never darkens another light.
   - **Soft shadows** (`Light2D::with_soft_shadows(size_px)`: the radius of the light's source in pixels, in the instance's `uv_rect.z`; not finite or below 0 is 0, a hard edge). A light of size `s` searches an arc of `min(s / d, 0.35)` radians round the pixel's angle (d its distance from the light) with `taps / 2` `textureLoad`s of raw distances (`ShadowQuality::taps`: 4, 8, 12, 16, in the atlas's filter uniform, binding 2 of `batch::shadow_layout`, written when the quality changes); with nothing nearer than the pixel (less its bias) it is lit. Else, `b` the farthest blocker found, it filters an arc of `min(s x (d - b) / b / d, 0.35)` radians with `taps` comparisons spread evenly over it and averages them: the penumbra is narrow where an occluder meets its shadow and widens away from it (`a_soft_shadow_hardens_where_it_meets_its_occluder`). The farthest blocker, not the average: behind a wall of touching closed occluders their shared sides block nearer than the wall's far face, and the wider arc an average gives turns look-ups into the wall's body, which only its far face shadows, so light leaked through (`no_light_leaks_through_a_map_wall`). Each look-up turns the pixel's direction by its own angle and finds its own view and row through `view_of` and `in_view` (an arc crossing a view's end carries on in the next view's row, view 3 wrapping to view 0), samples at the row's centre (never across rows) with its own bias, and the angle maps to the row continuously, so an edge moves smoothly with its light (`a_soft_edge_moves_smoothly_with_its_light`). Both loops run in non-uniform control flow, which is why they use `textureLoad` and `textureSampleCompareLevel`. Limits: the search arc reaches the lit side of the penumbra only while `d < 2b`, so farther behind an occluder its outer part is cut off (lit at once, about a third of the penumbra 120 px behind a block 44 px from a size 12 light); and the comparisons show as `taps` plateaus across a wide penumbra (12 at `High`).
3. **Composition Pass**: `lit = scene × (ambient + light)`, plus atmosphere scattering/fog, in float with no clamp to 1 (the look rolls bright results off later). The light and the result are limited to 65504, the largest f16, so a huge intensity cannot make NaN.

Scenes control lighting through the `LightingSettings` resource (`ctx.lighting()`): `set_ambient(AmbientLight::...)`, `set_atmosphere(..)` and `set_enabled(bool)`. The `LightingRenderer` is engine-owned; `present_frame` copies the settings into it before each frame. Volumetric light is per light (`Light2D::set_volumetric`); there is no global switch.

- **When lighting runs:** with no override (`enabled()` is `None`), lighting turns on once any `Light2D` exists and then stays on, also after the lights are gone and across scene changes. `set_enabled(true)` gives a dark scene before any light exists; `set_enabled(false)` forces it off.
- `SceneCleanup::LIGHTING` resets the settings (ambient, atmosphere, the override) to their defaults; it does not switch off a renderer that a light already turned on.
- The UI is never lit: it is composited after lighting and post-processing.
- **Per camera:** the `lighting` pass runs for every camera. Each lit camera has light targets of its own (`LightTargets`: accumulation buffer, scene copy and compose uniforms, sized to its image), created on first use and dropped with the camera. Lights and shadows draw through the camera's uniform buffer (its `view_proj`), so they move, zoom, turn and shake with the sprites. A camera drawing straight into the scene image composes into the lit image; a camera with an image of its own is lit in place (the compose pass reads the scene copy and writes the camera's image).
- Each camera's compose uniforms are its own buffer: queue writes all land before the command buffer runs, so a shared buffer would give every camera the last one's values.
- Lights are batch quads (`lighting::batch`), drawn by the `lighting` pass into each lit camera's light buffer; `LightingRenderer` is engine-internal and keeps the per-camera light buffers, the scene copies and the compose pass.
- **Colours are sRGB in the API.** Every light, shadow, ambient and atmosphere colour decodes through `Color::to_linear` before it reaches the GPU, so `Color(128, 128, 128)` is mid-grey light. Preset colour bytes keep their bytes and now name the sRGB colour they say.
- God rays are an HDR effect: `god_rays.rs` puts the god-ray lights in frame coordinates (0 to 1 across the whole frame) from the main camera's view and gives the effect the atmosphere's settings. Their threshold is linear luminance.

## Limits

- **No light cap**: each lit camera draws the lights on its `RenderLayers` whose reach meets its view in one instanced draw, shadowed or not. A light with a radius or intensity that is not positive and finite, or a position that is not finite, draws nothing.
- **At most 512 shadowed lights a frame** (`MAX_SHADOWED_LIGHTS`, or a quarter of the device's largest texture): past it the lights nearest a lit camera's centre keep their shadows and the rest draw unshadowed. A frame's edges are at most what the device's largest buffer holds (`ShadowAtlas::max_edges`: 8,388,608 under `Limits::default()`): the planner gathers the nearest lights' edges first and drops whole lights' edges past it (`dropped_edges`; those lights draw unshadowed), and the renderer warns once a run naming the count.
- **A lit frame allocates nothing for its occluders**: the occluders' query is kept on the engine (`occluder_query`), the planner reads each occluder the index finds through it and places its vertices as it walks them (`LightOccluder2D::placed`), and its vectors are reused, so a shadowed light over a tile map's thousands of chains collects nothing (`tests/tilemap_alloc.rs`).
- **One light image per camera, whatever the storey**: a light on a floor is blocked only by its floor's occluders (Floors, below), but it adds into the camera's one light buffer, so it still brightens whatever lies under it on screen, on every storey, without that storey's shadows. Lighting art per storey would need a light buffer per storey or a storey per pixel (spec "Floors: details settled at planning", `docs/specs/2026-10-31-tilemap-shapes-design.md`).
- **Shadows read the occluder index**: no lit frame gathers every occluder; each shadowed light reads only the occluders whose bounds meet its box (Conventions).

## Conventions

- **Artist-friendly units**: colors use 0-255 sRGB, not 0.0-1.0. Radius is in pixels. Intensity above 1 shows (the look rolls it off).
- **Placed by `GlobalTransform`**: lights draw at their entity's world translation (lifted on a storey: Floors, below); occluders use its world translation and rotation (not its scale). On an interpolated entity both are blended with its sprite (`render_global`).
- **Coordinate space**: occluder vertices are local space (relative to the entity), CCW winding: CCW on screen, which with y down is a negative signed area, so `casts`' normal `(-edge.y, edge.x)` faces out.
- **Chains**: `LightOccluder2D::chain` is an open run of edges (no edge from the last vertex back to the first; `is_closed` is false), wound as a polygon's: each edge casts exactly as it would as an edge of the polygon its boundary closes, so chains that together close a ring cast as that polygon does (`plan.rs`: `a_chain_casts_as_the_polygon_it_bounds`). Occluders that touch, each a closed polygon, cast their touching edges' shadows through each other; tile maps build their occluders as chains of their boundary for that reason (`tilemap/occluders.rs`).
- **Reach by placed bounds**: a light reads the occluders whose placed vertices' box meets its own (the index), not by their entities' places: a tile map's chunk-long chains have their entities at their boxes' centres, up to 256 px from their ends, and a 64 px torch at a wall's end must still cast (`a_small_light_at_the_end_of_a_chunk_long_wall_casts_its_shadow`). `LightOccluder2D::extent` (its farthest vertex from the entity, kept from construction) pads an interpolated occluder's index bounds.
- **The occluder index** (`index.rs`, the crate's `OccluderIndex` resource): each enabled occluder's world bounds (`LightOccluder2D::placed_bounds`, its placed vertices' box) by entity, listed in a uniform grid of `INDEX_CELL` (512 px) cells; an entry covering more than 1,024 cells is kept in a list every query checks. `update_occluder_index` runs in `PostUpdate` after `PostUpdateSet::Transforms` (so after the tile maps' occluder rebuild, which runs before propagation) and places again only what changed: `Changed<GlobalTransform>` (a parent's move too) or `Changed<LightOccluder2D>` (`set_enabled` too), the removals of either component (despawns too), and entities given or losing the ECS's `Disabled` (two observers queue them, as no change filter shows it). A disabled occluder has no entry. An interpolated occluder's bounds hold its previous and current placements, padded by the extent times 1 - cos(half the turn) so every `render_global` blend lies inside; it is placed again each frame until the two agree. A frame where nothing changed touches no entry (`a_static_scene_updates_nothing`). `query_into(min, max, scratch, out)` gives each entity once in `entity_order` into the caller's vector, allocating nothing once warm (the caller keeps an `IndexScratch`, a mark per entry, and the vector). A removed `GlobalTransform` is refreshed only for an entity with an entry.
- **Shadow planning** (`plan.rs`, `plan_shadows` into the `ShadowPlan` the `LightingRenderer` keeps; called once a lit frame by `present_frame` through `LightingRenderer::plan_shadows`, before the light items, each of which carries its light's slot (`LightingRenderer::shadow_slots`, `light_item`), with the lit cameras' views (layers, `CameraView::bounds`, centre), each occluder read where it is drawn (`render_global`) on its floor (`floor_and_lift`), and a cap of `MAX_SHADOWED_LIGHTS` or a quarter of the device's `max_texture_dimension_2d`, whichever is less): of the frame's lights, those that cast shadows, would draw (`light_instance`) and whose box (lifted) meets a lit camera's view they share layers with each get a slot, at most `MAX_SHADOWED_LIGHTS` (512) or the caller's cap, the nearest a sharing view's centre first (ties by light index), slots numbered in light order; `rows` is four a slot. A slotted light reads the index over its box where it stands, then per occluder: floor (`Floor::meets`), enabled and finite, not a closed one holding the light (even-odd on its placed vertices; a chain has no inside; a light within `ON_EDGE` of its boundary (1e-4 px, or a millionth of the largest coordinate where that is more, as f32 steps grow far from the origin) stands outside it, so a light on an edge or corner is shadowed by the far edges and lights the side it faces: `a_light_on_an_occluder_s_edge_lights_the_side_it_faces`), then per edge `casts` (facing away, near a spot's cone with its margin; a spot shape that is not finite reads as `light_instance` draws it), a length above 1e-4 and the light not on it (within `ON_EDGE`: it covers no angle). The slots are numbered in light order, and the edges gathered nearest light first, so the edge limit (Limits) drops the farthest lights' first. Each casting edge is a `ShadowEdge` (end points and light in ground space, the radius, the row: slot x 4 + view) once per view it crosses: views 0 right (-45° to 45°), 1 down, 2 left, 3 up, by `atan2(dy, dx)`, from the end the other lies counter-clockwise of (the sign of `(a - light).perp_dot(b - light)`, not the rounded angles) round to it, an end on a boundary entering both. A spot's cone is read as it draws (`batch::drawn_cone`, shared with `light_instance`). The vectors are cleared and reused, so a warm plan allocates nothing.
- **The shadow atlas** (`atlas.rs`, `ShadowAtlas` on the `LightingRenderer`; spec `docs/specs/2026-11-11-shadows-design.md`): a `Depth32Float` texture (`RENDER_ATTACHMENT | TEXTURE_BINDING`), `ShadowQuality::texels` wide (the player's `Settings::shadow_quality`), four rows a slot, its height the plan's rows rounded up to 64 within the device's largest texture; recreated when the width changes or more rows are needed, never shrunk. The frame's encoder clears it to 1 and draws every planned edge in one instanced draw (6 vertices an edge, `shadow_map.wgsl`), before any camera pass (`WgpuRenderer::render_frame`, through `LightingRenderer::encode_shadows`; internal work, not a pass of the pipeline), and only when the plan has rows. Per edge the vertex stage turns both ends into the row's view (looking along +x), clips them at 0.5 px in front of the light along the view (both behind: no area; one: moved to that plane along the edge), and draws a quad one texel tall at the row's texel centre (clip y `1 - 2 (row + 0.5) / height`, times w), clip x the end's tangent in the view times w, w its distance along the view, so the GPU's clipping trims it to the view's ±45° and interpolates the world point correctly; each end reaches 0.25 texel past itself along the row. The fragment writes `frag_depth = distance(world point, light) / radius`, clamped to [0, 1]; depth test `Less`, writes on, so the nearest occluder wins. Texel x of view row r covers the tangent `2 (x + 0.5) / width - 1` of the angle within the view. The light shader reads it through `ShadowAtlas::light_group` (group 1 of the light pipeline, `batch::shadow_layout`: the depth view, the comparison sampler, `LessEqual`, linear, and the soft filter's uniform of `taps`), made again only when the texture is, so a steady frame makes no bind group; a change of quality writes the filter uniform once. `ShadowAtlas::builds` counts the textures and edge buffers it made (`HeadlessEngine::shadow_atlas_builds`: `the_atlas_grows_and_changes_quality_between_frames` holds a settled frame to none).
- **Tile occluders** (`tilemap/occluders.rs`, tilemap module notes): a spawned map's occluding layers (`LayerInfo::occludes`, else as they collide) keep `LightOccluder2D::chain`s of the boundary of what occludes, per 32-cell chunk, children of the first occluding layer marked `MapOccluder { chunk }` (prelude; the chunk signed, an `IVec2`), rebuilt per changed chunk in `PostUpdate` before transforms propagate, so an edit casts that frame. A tile's shapes marked `occluder` block light and do not collide; without any, its collision shapes occlude. Layers occlude together per floor and ground offset, and a higher floor's chains carry its `Floor::On`.
- **Floors (storeys)**: a light or occluder is on a floor, read in `engine/extract.rs` (`floor_and_lift`): its `DrawLift` if it has one (written by the tile map's `lift_floored` on an entity with a `Floor` and everything below it, so a torch held by a character on floor 1 is on floor 1), else its own `Floor` unlifted (a map's shadow chains, `ParentPlaced`, never get a `DrawLift`: `a_map_chain_s_own_floor_filters_shadows`), else the ground floor; so a game's own occluders without a `Floor` are on the ground floor. The planner skips an occluder whose floor does not meet the light's (`Floor::meets`: its own floor's and `Every`'s block it; a light on `Every` is blocked by all). The map is drawn where the light and its occluders stand (ground space); the light draws at its lift, as its quad (`light_item`), its camera box and its god-ray position do, and its shader measures each pixel's offset from the lifted light, which is its ground offset, so its shadows line up with the storey's lifted art (`a_held_light_on_a_floor_casts_that_floor_s_shadows_drawn_lifted`, `a_floor_light_and_its_shadows_draw_lifted`). An occluder's own lift is not read, so shadows draw at the light's lift, off the occluder's art where the two differ (a recorded limit, `docs/backlog/tilemap.md`): two maps giving one storey different heights, a light off every map with floors among a storey's occluders, and a light on `Every` facing a higher storey's occluders. A light on `Every` has no lift (`lift_floored` gives `Every` none), so it draws at ground height. A scene without floors has every light and occluder on the ground floor with no lift.

## Notes for AI Agents

- Lighting is visual: validate changes by running `examples/demos/lighting`, `lighting-volumetric`, or `shadows`.
