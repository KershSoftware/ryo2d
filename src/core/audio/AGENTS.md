# AGENTS.md - Audio Module

The audio engine: kira 0.12.6's manager on the audio device (or on kira's mock backend), the bus tree a mixer asset describes, the live voices and their limits, 2D spatial sound, the one music player, and what follows the game clock, hot reload and the web's first gesture. Clips, sound definitions, music and the mixer are registry assets (asset module notes, The asset types). The spec is `docs/specs/2026-10-24-audio-design.md` (where it and the code differ, the code is current); what it costs and the output latency are measured in `docs/backlog/audio.md`, with the measurements behind the stream decoder. Using it from a game is the `ryo-audio` skill; the worked example is `examples/demos/audio`.

## The frame's audio work

`audio_system` runs in `PostUpdate`, after `PostUpdateSet::Camera`: the scenes' updates and their systems have queued their plays and voice commands, and the cameras (the default listener) and transforms are where this frame leaves them. It takes the `AudioEngine` out of the world (`try_resource_scope`) while it reads the stores, as each play does; the resource is a box, so that moves a pointer (moving the whole state, about 4 KB, took most of an idle frame's audio time). In order:

1. adds the real delta to the engine's clock (cooldowns run on it, so a paused game still cools down);
2. applies the voices' queued commands (stop, pause, resume, volume, pitch) that scene code did not apply as it ended, in the order they were sent;
3. builds the bus tree again if the mixer landed or changed and no play built it first (and applies a snapshot asked for before it landed);
4. follows the registry's `AssetEvent`s for clips, sound definitions and music through its own `MessageCursor`s: switches what plays to reloaded data (a definition saved onto another bus moves its loops there), forgets what it remembered of a failed asset whose file changed, and all it remembered of a freed one;
5. steps the music player: a track that finished loading takes over;
6. starts the plays that waited for their sounds (`start_waiting`): a loop starts as it was played, counting against its `per_frame`, a one-shot is refused, and a sound that failed ends its plays;
7. releases the voices whose emitter went (despawned, disabled, taken away or put over);
8. finds the listener and brings every spatial voice's gain, pan and cutoff in line with its place (a loop at or beyond its maximum distance goes virtual; a virtual one's place is noted, nothing sent);
9. plays the emitters queued to play (each entity once a frame, and as many of one sound as its `per_frame` allows; the rest wait their turn), and the emitter plays systems queued for entities that were not emitters yet as their buffers applied (`play_awaited`);
10. follows the game clock and the scene holds: holds and lets go the buses that pause with the game or follow time scale, and the voices whose owner is held, and sets the following voices' rates;
11. ranks the virtual loops, if any, and brings back those that may come back, and resumes the streamed comebacks whose decoders have frames (Virtual voices, below);
12. warns once about an error the device's stream reported;
13. moves the virtual loops on by the frame's real time, and renders it on the mock backend, in whole 128-frame chunks at 48 kHz (the remainder carried, at most 1 s a frame);
14. retires the voices that ended (counting the virtual ones left) (a stream its decoder reported failed is stopped with its fade and warned about once here), drops the music track whose voice ended, steps the snapshot blend and the ducks onto the bus gains, drops a retired tree's send tracks once no voice plays on it, lets a ringing tree go once its tail has rung out, fading it out, and drops it once the fade has played (`ring_out`);
15. counts the frame: the next frame's starts count afresh against `per_frame`, and the voices started this frame have been rendered.

A `Voice`'s methods only queue a command (a crossbeam channel) and read an atomic state, so a `Voice` is `Send`, cheap to clone, and usable anywhere. The commands are applied as the scene code that sent them ends (`in_scene` applies the inbox when it is not empty) and in the update, so what `is_playing` reports changes by the next update, and a change of 10 ms or less (the plain forms) made in the frame a voice started, which kira has not rendered (`live.frame == frame`), applies with no tween: its first audio is at the level set. A longer `_over` fade made then ramps from the level the play started at, so a bed faded up from silence starts silent. Plays themselves run at once in the caller (`AudioView::play*` takes the engine out of the world for the call), so the voice returned is live, waiting for its sound, or already stopped if refused; a play applies the voices' queued changes first, so a voice whose stop was just asked for holds no room under its sound's limit or `per_frame`. A system's plays are the exception: deferred to where its commands apply (Systems, below).

## Playing

The play path (`AudioEngine::play`): build the mixer if it landed or changed (`apply_mixer`, gated on its version, so a scene's first one-shots reach buses its `load_now` of the mixer adds), resolve the definition (a clip played directly takes the empty definition: `sfx`, 0 dB, no spread, no limit, priority 128, one start a frame), wait for it or refuse it if it is still loading (below; a failed one warns once per id), pick a clip by weight avoiding the last `no_repeat` picks (never every clip), draw the gain and pitch from the ranges, resolve the bus (a missing one plays on the nearest bus above it, warned once per name, but not while the mixer that may add it is still loading), hear it from its place (a one-shot at or beyond its maximum distance is culled), refuse it past its `per_frame` or inside its cooldown, refuse a one-shot with no room (kira's room on the track, then the limits: `Room`, below) while a loop out of range or with no room goes virtual (Virtual voices, below), start it (static or streamed), stop the planned victims (a loop goes virtual), count the start, and log it.

- **A sound starts at most `per_frame` voices a frame** (`Sound::per_frame`, 1 by default and at least 1; `per_frame: 0` fails the load), counted in `frame_starts` by its definition, or its clip for a bare clip, across every bus and place, before any limit: copies started together play in step and add up (ten are +20 dB), the burst the spec's anti-goal names. A start past it is refused (`REFUSED_PER_FRAME`); the music player's track is not counted. A play that waited for its sound counts as it is played and again as it lands in a later frame (`Pending::frame`, `Request::counted`), so the same loop played twice while it loads starts once. A voice stopped in the frame it started is taken back (`uncount`), as a stopped voice holds no sound's room. Emitters of one sound spawned together take turns (`play_spawned` keeps the rest queued for the next frames), as dropping one would leave it silent for good.
- **A sound still loading** (`wait_for`, `Pending`): a loop, and any play of a definition still loading (it is not known to loop until it lands, as the registry inserts a definition only with its clips), waits as a voice that reports playing, takes the game's commands (`change_pending`: stop ends it, pause and resume set its state, volume and pitch its game layers) and starts through `start` once its sound is ready (`start_waiting` each update), as the same `Voice`, in the scope that played it, with its fade-in; it starts silent and paused if the game paused it meanwhile, and held if its bus or its scene holds it. `play_emitter` waits the same way: its play is the entity's, starts where the entity is when it lands, and ends if the entity goes first (`emitter_gone`). A one-shot is refused at that point (`REFUSED_LOADING`, warned once per sound in debug builds, `warn_loading`), as is a bare clip still loading at once. A theme's UI sound (`play_ui`, `Request::ui`) never waits: one still loading is refused at once, with no warning, as a game cannot load a theme's sound ahead (a file theme's sounds start loading with the theme, a code-made theme's at the next UI pass). A sound that fails ends its plays with the registry's one warning. A scene's exit ends its waiting plays; it is a list of its own, not `Playback::Waiting`, as a voice in the table needs a clip and a bus.
- Ranges and clamps: a voice's own gain is -60 to +24 dB (kira is silent at -60), its pitch -48 to 48 semitones; a rate is `2^(semitones/12)`. The sound's level and pitch are kept unclamped (`level`), the clamp applying once, to their sum with the game's change, so a reload that moves a definition past +24 dB and back returns it where it was.
- **The game's changes are a layer** (`game_db`, `game_pitch`): `Voice::set_volume` and `set_pitch` set them, relative to the sound's level as played (`gain_db`, `pitch`: the definition's, the drawn spread and `PlayOptions`, moved by reloads); `own_db` and `own_pitch` are the clamped sums.
- **A live voice holds strong handles to what it plays** (its clip, or its music), so the registry never frees a clip under playback, and a scene may drop its handle while the sound plays out. A streamed clip's bytes (`ClipBytes`, an `Arc` of the file as the loader read it) are shared by its streams.
- A refused play returns `Voice::stopped()`, one shared static, so a refusal allocates nothing; it is logged `Refused` with its `reason` (`Culled` for an out-of-range one-shot) and, by design, not warned about (a burst against a limit is normal), but for a one-shot played while its sound loads, warned once in debug builds.
- Each definition's state (its recent picks, its last start for the cooldown) is kept by id and pruned on its `AssetEvent::Removed`.
- `PlayOptions` adds to the definition: `position`, `persist`, `volume_db`, `pitch`.

## Systems (`Audio`)

- **A system plays through `Audio`**, a `SystemParam` of `Option<Res<AudioEngine>>` and a system buffer (`Deferred<AudioQueue>`). It reads the engine and never borrows it mutably, so systems that play sound conflict neither with each other nor with systems that read the engine (two unordered pass a schedule's ambiguity check at `LogLevel::Error`). `AudioView` could not serve: a play needs the stores and the engine mutably at once.
- **Its plays are deferred** (the owner's ruling): each is queued (`Queued::Play`, `Queued::Emitter`, and the mix's `Snapshot`, `ClearSnapshot`, `BusVolume`, `Setting`) and applied where the system's commands apply: `SystemBuffer::apply` at its schedule's end or a sync point, which runs `EngineState::run_queued` with the engine taken out of the world; a system run without the world to itself (an observer) puts the queue in the world's commands (`queue`), which apply at the world's next flush: `World::trigger` does not flush, nor does `in_scene` as its section ends, so an observer triggered from scene code may apply its plays outside the scene's section, and they need not be the scene's (a loop among them can outlive it). `Audio`'s rustdoc says so; the section does not flush, as no game here uses observers and a flush at each section's end would change when every command applies. The cost: a refusal is seen one sync point later.
- **Its voice exists at once**: `queued_voice` makes it with a fresh id from the voice table's count, an `AtomicU32` reachable through `&EngineState`, which the plays made at once share, so no two voices share an id. It reports playing until the play applies, which starts it as that voice (`play_from`'s `voice`, as a waiting play's `Request::voice`); a play refused then (any refusal, a culled one-shot, a sound that failed) leaves it stopped (`settle_queued`).
- **Its changes wait for its start.** A change made before the play applies sits in the voices' channel, and a play made at once in between (an exclusive system's, a scene's callback) applies the channel first: a change for a voice neither in the table nor waiting for its sound is kept in `held` with its frame, and applied as its queued play starts (`settle_queued`), so a plain change made in the system that played it applies with its start (the start-frame rule) and an `_over` one ramps from its play's level. Held changes left after two frames (`HELD_FRAMES`: a buffer applies by its schedule's end, in the frame it was played) are dropped, so a change for a voice that ended costs nothing for long; an empty `held` costs nothing.
- **An emitter play waits for its entity.** bevy applies a system's buffers in the order of its parameters, so in `fn spawn(mut audio: Audio, mut commands: Commands)` the play applies before the `Commands` that spawn its emitter. A queued `play_emitter` whose entity is not an emitter as it applies (`emitter_position` finds none: not spawned yet, despawned, disabled, no `AudioEmitter`) waits in `awaited` for the engine's next update (`play_awaited`, after the play-on-spawn emitters), by when the system's commands have applied, and plays there as its queued voice, its held changes with it; still no emitter, it is refused (`REFUSED_NO_EMITTER`, logged `emitter`, warned once in debug builds). The mirror case, `fn explode(mut commands: Commands, mut audio: Audio)` playing an emitter and despawning its entity: the despawn applies first, and the `AudioEmitter`'s `on_discard` hook keeps where it was and its sound for the frame (`discarded`, emptied at the update's end), so the queued play, finding no emitter, plays there (`play_discarded`): unfollowed and the engine's, a one-shot playing out and a loop ending with its fade (`release_voice`), as an emitter's voices do as its entity goes. The order of a system's parameters does not decide whether an emitter plays.
- **Ownership is the scope running as the queue applies**: a scene's schedules run, and apply their buffers, inside its `in_scene` section, so a scene's system plays the scene's voices; a global system's are the engine's. The player's volumes are not `Audio`'s: they are `ctx.settings()`, and the engine sets them on the `AudioEngine`. Music, `set_mixer`, `seed` and `voices_on` stay `AudioView`'s.

## Ownership and scene exits

- A voice belongs to the scene whose code played it. `SceneSlot::call`, the scene systems and the delivery of a scene's UI node events run under `in_scene(world, scope, ..)`, which sets the engine's `scope`. A voice played with `persist`, or outside every scene (a global system or observer), is the engine's; one played from an `AudioEmitter` is its entity's.
- **A scene's exit** (pop, switch, clear, reset; the manager calls `release_scene` at each) stops its loops, its one-shots paused or held, and its one-shots with more than `FINISH_AT_EXIT` (2 s) left that are not on `ui` or a bus under it, each with its sound's `fade_out` (at least 10 ms), and lets its other one-shots finish. A confirm sound played just before a switch is heard out, a 20 s voice-over does not run on into the menu, and a paused one-shot never resumes over the next scene when the clock does. What is left counts from the voice's position to the end of what it plays (`length`, its region or the whole clip, a stream's from its region's start), at its rate. `persist` opts out. Its plays still waiting for their sounds end.
- **Music is the engine's**: no scene's exit stops it. `SceneCleanup::MUSIC` (not in `DEFAULT`) stops it through `stop_scene_music`, fading over the track's crossfade, when the scene whose cleanup holds it is switched away from or reset: cleanup runs only in `SceneManager::switch` and `reset`, never on `pop`, so a pushed scene that plays a track of its own stops it in its `on_exit` (`stop_music`), or the scene below asks for its track again in `on_resume`.
- **An emitter's voices** belong to its entity, not to the scene that played them, and are held with the scene that added the emitter (The game clock). They are released as its entity is despawned, disabled (a pool putting it back), loses its `AudioEmitter` or has another put over it: its loops and its paused or held one-shots stop with their fades, and its one-shots playing play out whatever their length (a death cry as its entity despawns; the 2 s rule is the scene exit's). The `on_discard` hook lets them go at once (`emitter_gone`, released in the next `audio_system`; the engine keeps each entity's emitter voices by id, `emitter_voices`, so it looks at no other voice, and an entity with none, such as any entity disabled in a world with emitters, costs one lookup), so an emitter put over it, or a pooled entity put back and handed out in one frame, starts afresh rather than taking the old voices.

## Voice limits, priority and the cap

- **Priority is 0 to 255, higher more important** (Unity's convention is the opposite), 128 by default (`Sound::DEFAULT_PRIORITY`). Music plays at 255, and only a music play may stop music.
- A play is checked against its definition's limit (its voices on its bus's subtree), then each bus limit from its bus up to `master` (every voice on that bus's subtree), then the cap on its bus's own track. The definition's limit and the bus limits stop voices by the definition's rule; a bus limit when the definition has no limit, and the cap, stop the least important voice, then the quietest (as heard, falloff included), then the oldest. `PreventNew` refuses.
- **Who may be stopped:** never a more important voice than the new play, and never an equally important one started in the same frame. Beside `per_frame` (which already holds one sound to its starts a frame), the same-frame rule keeps many starts in one frame (of a sound that allows them, or of many sounds) from stopping their own starts in turn, so kira starts and stops sounds nobody hears; with it, they refuse past the limit. A more important play may still take a lower-priority voice started this frame.
- **Victims are planned before any stop** (`Room`, into `victims`): a play that one limit would make room for but a later limit refuses stops nothing. A rule's victim stops with its own sound's `fade_out` (at least 10 ms), the cap's with 10 ms; each is logged `Stolen`. A victim that loops (never music) goes virtual instead, its kira sound fading out with that fade, logged `Virtual` (`stolen`), unless it ends (`Victim::ends`): picked by its own sound's limit under a rule that ends loops (`StopOldest`; `ends_loops`), or by a bus limit while its own sound's rule ends loops. A loop's fate follows its own sound's rule, so a footstep under a default `StopOldest` limit filling the bus does not end the fire on it; the cap's victims always go virtual. A virtual victim (`StopOldest` counts its sound's virtual loops) stops at once: logged `Stolen`, no fade.
- **The cap is 96 voices per bus track** (`VOICE_CAP`). A kira track holds a fixed 128 sounds (`TrackBuilder::sound_capacity`, kira's default), and a stopped sound holds its place until its fade ends, so the 32 above the cap are room for stolen voices to fade out. The engine asks kira's room before a play (`room_on`); kira can still refuse one in a race of nanoseconds (its arena marks a stopped sound's slot free before it puts it back on its free list), which the engine handles as any refusal for want of room (`Refusal::Full`: logged `full`, warned once per bus). A voice stopped (its stop queued, which a play applies first, or fading out) does not count toward a limit, the cap or its sound's `per_frame`, so a `PreventNew` sound stopped and played again in one frame starts, but it holds its kira place until its fade ends: many long `fade_out`s on one bus can fill the track, and plays there are then refused, warned once per bus.
- `StopFarthest` counts a voice with no position as at the listener (distance 0), as it does every voice while there is no listener, so among those it stops the oldest.
- A loop the cap, or a limit whose rule ranks, would refuse, or would stop to make room, goes virtual instead, and comes back when there is room (Virtual voices); `PreventNew` and `StopOldest` end it as they end a one-shot (`ends_loops`; `make_room` returns the rule of the limit that refused). Under those two a sound's virtual loops count against its own limit (`Room::counts`, its `Scope::Sound` under a rule that ends loops), and a loop under them meets that limit alone before its distance and kira's room (`start`'s `own_ends`, `make_room` with `Limits::Own`): `PreventNew` refuses while its limit is full of loops, virtual or heard (logged `limit`, whatever the track's room), and `StopOldest` ends the oldest of them. Out of range, or with kira's track full, it then goes virtual cutting nothing else; only one that would be heard meets the bus limits and the cap (`Limits::All`). If the cap has no voice to give it goes virtual, its own limit's steals planned again, so its sound stays within that limit. The ranking counts heard voices only, which holds under the two as their total stays within the limit. Otherwise a virtual voice counts against no limit: `Room::counts` leaves it out of the bus limits, the cap and a ranking rule's limit, as `voices_on` and the ducks do.

## Virtual voices

The owner's ruling, "loops only": a loop that is out of range, or cut by a limit or the cap, keeps its place silently and comes back when it is audible and there is room. One-shots are unchanged. Only the rules that rank (`StopQuietest`, `StopFarthest`, `StopLowestPriority`, and the cap's order: the cap, or a bus limit for a sound with no limit) make a loop virtual; `PreventNew` and `StopOldest` end loops (`ends_loops`), count their own sound's virtual loops against its limit, which a loop under them meets before its distance and kira's room (the bus limits and the cap only once it would be heard), and are outside the loudness ranking both ways, for the idioms that rely on them: the newest wins under `StopOldest` (one bed, or one alarm, at a time, the newest replacing the last, for good), and the first is kept under `PreventNew` (a second play reports stopped while the first plays, heard or virtual: a far `PreventNew` loop holds its limit, silent, until it ends). FMOD and Wwise make virtualizing a choice of the limit's too.

- **What goes virtual** (`Playback::Virtual`: its place in seconds of what it plays, and whether it was stopped; no kira sound, its distance low-pass's track let go, which plays the old sound's fade out): a loop (decoded or streamed; plain, placed or an emitter's) that a ranking limit or the cap refuses at its play (`limit`), whose play finds kira's room on its track held by voices fading out (`full`, which the cap would otherwise refuse; such a loop is not warned about, as it is not refused), that a play takes the place of under a ranking limit or the cap, or under a bus limit while its own sound has no limit or ranks (`stolen`, with the victim's fade: a loop's fate follows its own sound's rule), that a virtual loop outranks (`displaced`, fading out over 50 ms as the other fades in), or that is at or beyond its maximum distance (`range`: at its play, or as `follow_listener` finds it there once it has stayed as it is for `STAY_PUT`, its sound fading out over 10 ms from the silence it is at). Music is never virtual, and no virtual loop takes music's place. `per_frame` and cooldown refusals stay refusals; a loop that goes virtual at its play counts as a start (its `per_frame`, its cooldown, its picks), and coming back does not. Each is logged `Virtual` with its `reason` and its kira sound's fade, in place of the `Start` or `Stolen` it would have had.
- **It is a voice in the table**, so every rule that reaches voices reaches it as it reaches a heard one: its owner's (a scene's exit and an emitter's going end it, `persist` keeps it), the holds (`held`: the game's pause, its bus's hold and its owner's hold freeze it, and its owner's hold is logged on it), the game's commands (`apply` keeps volume and pitch on its layers for when it is heard, keeps a pause, and a stop ends it), reloads and rebuilds (`replay` gives it its new source, regions, level and bus with no restart, so it comes back on what is current; `retune` gives every loop of a reloaded definition its `limit` and `priority` as they now are, which the ranking reads). What counts heard voices leaves it out: the limits and the cap (all but its own sound's limit under `PreventNew` or `StopOldest`, where it counts), `voices_on` (`virtual_on` counts it: `AudioView::virtual_voices_on`), the ducks, the low-pass count. Its `Voice` reports playing; `Voice::is_virtual` reads a flag the engine sets.
- **It moves on** with the mock's render (`advance_virtual`), by the frame's real time times its rate (its pitch, and the time scale its bus follows), unless it is paused or held, wrapping in what loops of what it plays (its `loop_region` within its region, or all of it). Advancing as kira renders keeps a voice that goes virtual or comes back in one update in step: it comes back where it would be had it played on, within a frame.
- **The ranking** (`rank_virtual`, after `follow_game` so the holds are this frame's) runs only while a voice is virtual (`virtual_count`, kept as voices go virtual and come back, and recounted as `retire_ended` drops voices), and not before the web's unlock (the tree has no tracks; the unlock's rebuild moves virtual loops as it moves the rest). A virtual loop may come back when it plays (not paused, not held), it has been virtual for `STAY_PUT` (250 ms) or has never been heard (`unheard`: virtual from its play, or held from its start; the stay-put is against flapping, and a loop judged out of range from a listener a frame stale, or kept out by a limit for a frame, has not flapped), and a placed one is inside `RETURN_RANGE` (95%) of its maximum distance (`SpatialVoice::near`, kept by `follow_listener` for every placed voice, which sends a virtual one nothing). The scopes it competes in are those its play was checked against (`each_scope`: its definition's limit, each bus limit up, its track's cap), tallied once over the real voices (`Ranking::count`: each scope's count, and its weakest movable voice, the least important, then the quietest as heard, then the oldest, movable being neither music nor moved in the last `STAY_PUT`, nor waiting to give up its place to a streamed comeback, nor, in a limit's scope (a sound's or a bus's), a loop whose own rule ends loops: those are outside the ranking both ways, and only the cap's order still ranks them). It fits when each scope has room, or its weakest gives way to it: less important, or as important and `SWAP_DB` (3 dB) quieter as heard; one voice a scope, none under `PreventNew` or `StopOldest` (a loop virtual by the cap or its distance finds room there or waits); and kira has room on its track. Those that fit come back most important and loudest first, each checked again against the tallies as the comebacks before it left them (counted again after each), through `realize`: its sound starts at its place at its level as heard now, fading in over `VIRTUAL_FADE` (50 ms; one never heard over its own fade-in if longer, as a held start's first resume does), with a distance low-pass while there is room for one; its start frame is still its play's, so the start-frame rule does not apply. A streamed comeback starts paused (`starting`) and is resumed with that fade by `resume_started` in the first update after its decoder has given frames (`StreamFailure::has_frames`, set by `ClipDecoder::decode`): kira's streaming sound runs its fade while it plays the silence of its decoder's start (the probe, the seek and the first decode run on its new thread), so a fade begun at the play could be over before its audio, a step. Its place falls behind by that start, as any stream's start does. The voices it takes the place of give it up as its audio starts (`displacing`, pairs of a voice's id and its comeback's beside the table, so a frame with none reads no voice for them; displaced in `resume_started`), so the two crossfade over their 50 ms: until then they sound and count (the scope is one over for those frames, kira's room having been asked for the comeback's slot, and a voice waiting so is not taken twice), and if the comeback never sounds (stopped, virtual again, gone, or its decoder failed, which `has_failed` reads before its resume) they play on. A pause or a hold of the comeback delays the swap with it. A decoded comeback's victims give up their place at once, as it fades in at once. A reload's switch and a rebuild's move are left as they are: they stop the old copy at once, so the gap of the new stream's start stays whatever its fade; closing it would keep the old copy playing until the new one has frames. The voice it outranks is `displace`d: a loop goes virtual, a one-shot stops with its `fade_out` (logged `Stolen`). Coming back is logged `Real` (`room` or `outranks`, with the 50 ms); the ranking is not logged.
- **Why the hysteresis is what it is**: 3 dB is a difference a listener hears as a step, so loops a decibel or two apart (two crickets at about the same distance) do not trade places; 250 ms keeps a voice from going and coming back faster than its fades (50 ms each way) can be heard as more than a flutter; 95% of the range is where every falloff is near silence (Linear -26 dB, Inverse about -51, Log -57), so a loop at the edge of its range does not come and go.
- **Bounds**: at most `MAX_VIRTUAL` (1024): past them a loop that would go virtual is refused (`virtual`), and one stolen or displaced stops, as a one-shot does. The ranking's lists are kept (`Ranking`; the placeholder that stands in while it runs is empty, as a `Default` with room would allocate each frame), so a frame with virtual voices that moves none allocates nothing (`tests/audio_alloc.rs`). The voice table holds virtual voices with the heard ones; it grows at a play, as it did, never in a frame's update.
- **What it costs**: a frame with 256 virtual loops ranked against 64 heard (`virtual_256`, `benches/audio.rs`) is held to 20 µs (7.1 µs measured); one with 1,024 ranked in three scopes each (`virtual_1024`, printed with no target) reads about 38 µs, the ranking about 22 µs of it; a play's limits look at every voice in the table, virtual ones included, so a play costs more with hundreds virtual (0.67 to 0.75 µs beside the 256, against 0.46 to 0.50 µs; `docs/backlog/audio.md`). A frame with none virtual ranks nothing, but each placed voice is asked whether it is near enough to come back and whether its loop has left its range (`voices_64` +0.11 µs).

## The bus tree and the mix

- **A bus is a kira track** (`BusTree`, built from `plan(mixer)`: the standard six first, then the game's by depth). `master` is a sub-track of kira's main track at 0 dB; master's gain goes on the main track, so send returns, which kira mixes into the main track beside `master`, are under it too. The standard buses always exist; a mixer adjusts them and adds up to 64 of its own (`MAX_BUSES`), 16 of them send buses at most (`MAX_SEND_BUSES`; kira's send-track room is set to 112, seven trees' worth, `SEND_TRACK_CAPACITY`, so retired trees' send tracks fit beside the live one's). kira holds 128 sub-tracks per track (its default), and the engine's tracks nest (each tree's `master` under the main track, the standard buses under `master`, a game's buses under theirs, voice filter tracks under their bus), so no parent nears it: a rebuild adds one `master` under the main track for as long as the retired tree plays out.
- **Gain layers** (`GainLayers`) add in dB: the mixer's volume (or the game's, `set_bus_volume`), the player's setting on the curve (standard buses only; a game's bus inherits through its parent), the active snapshot's, the duck's, and on `master` the focus loss's; the sum is at most +24, and silence if any layer is silent (a setting of 0 is silent whatever the others add). A snapshot or a duck never overrides a slider, and a slider moved under a snapshot is heard at once. A player's settings saved by an engine whose volumes were amplitudes (a `system.json` without `format_version`) are converted as they load to the point on the curve heard at the same dB, √v (config module notes).
- **Sends** go to send buses (a bus another sends to): a kira send track, which plays no voices (a sound on one plays on its parent, warned), has no children and sends nowhere. kira takes a send after the sender's own volume and before its parent's, so each route's volume is its level plus the gains of the buses above the sender, except `master`'s (on the main track, over every return): a return follows its sender's sliders whichever send bus it reaches, and is tweened with `set_send`. `master`'s effects do not process returns, since kira mixes send tracks beside `master`'s track.
- **Snapshots** (`Blend`): one active at a time, blended from wherever the mix is over the snapshot's `blend` (0.3 s by default). A snapshot is a full preset: a bus it does not name, or names without a `low_pass`, blends back to 0 dB and open. dB blend in a line and cutoffs in a line of octaves (how a sweep sounds even), stepped once a frame with each step tweened over the frame (`glide`, `frame_glide`: the frame's length, at least 10 ms and at most 100 ms, `MAX_GLIDE`, so a hitch does not leave the mix trailing it by the hitch's length). The blend's time is a sum of the frames' `Duration`s, so it lands on the frame its time runs out. A bus any snapshot names a `low_pass` for gets a low-pass on its track at build, open at 20 kHz (`OPEN_HZ`) until a snapshot closes it. The blend is kept by bus name, so the active snapshot survives a rebuilt tree, carrying on with the time it had left; a rebuild whose mixer lacks it blends back to open over its whole blend. A snapshot asked for before the mixer lands waits for it (`pending_snapshot`; the last call wins): one waiting for a mixer that fails to load is warned about once by name, and one whose mixer fails its check applies to the buses as they are.
- **Ducks** (`DuckState`): while any voice plays on the trigger's subtree, the target's duck layer moves to `by_db` over `attack`, and back to 0 over `release` after. It is bus gain stepped each frame, not audio-rate side-chaining; two rules on one target add their dips.
- **Focus loss** reaches the engine from `WindowEvent::Focused` (`engine/window.rs`): the mixer's `on_focus_loss` (`Keep` by default, `Duck(dB)`, `Mute`) is a layer on `master`, smoothed over 10 ms.
- **A rebuild** (a mixer landing, changing, or set in code with `AudioView::set_mixer`; the web's unlock) builds a whole new tree and retires the old one. kira cannot move a sound between tracks and fixes a track's effects and sends when it builds it, so loops restart on the new tree at their position, crossfading over 10 ms, onto the bus their definition names (a clip's onto its old bus's name, or the nearest above either), while one-shots play out on the old tracks: every track is built with `persist_until_sounds_finish(true)`, so kira keeps a retired track until its sounds finish once the engine drops its handle. A retired tree keeps the volumes it had (only `master`, on the main track, still reaches it), for as long as it plays out and rings: the player's other sliders, snapshots and ducks changed after the rebuild do not reach its one-shots or its tail (up to 10 s), which go on at the level they had. The old tree retires after the loops have moved (`retire`). A track that cannot switch to its changed definition moves as it was (warned as unswitched); a loop that cannot move at all is warned about once and plays on where it was. A mixer that fails its check keeps the tree as it is, with a warning.
- **A retired tree rings out its effects' tails** (`RingingTree`, `ringing`): kira removes a dropped track as its last sound ends, which would cut a reverb's or a delay's tail mid-ring, so a tree whose effects ring is kept, handles and send tracks, until no voice plays on it and its tail has passed since (`ring_out`; counted from the frame the engine first finds it quiet, and no sooner than its moved loops' old copies have faded, 10 ms after the rebuild, so a save while tuning a reverb leaves the old copy's tail ringing). A kira copy still fading on it counts as a voice there (`fading_copies`, `sounds_on`: a moved loop's old copy over 10 ms, and a loop gone virtual, whose copy fades over the fade it was stopped with, up to its `fade_out`), as nothing else marks the tree: the voice has moved to the live tree. Its tail is its longest chain of effects (`BusTree::tail`): from a bus voices play on up through each bus above it to `master`, or from any of those into a send bus, each effect's tail added, at most 10 s (`MAX_TAIL`), counting only the tracks kira made. An effect's tail (`EffectDef::tail`) is the time its output takes to fall 60 dB: a `Delay`'s echoes fall `feedback_db` each `seconds`, so `seconds × 60 / |feedback_db|` (kira already gives the first echo at `feedback_db`); a `Reverb` is kira's Freeverb (its `reverb.rs`), whose slowest comb filter (1640 samples at its 44.1 kHz reference, scaled with the rate: `REVERB_COMB`, 37.2 ms) loses `20·log10(feedback)` dB a pass and whose four all-pass filters in series (1655 samples, `REVERB_ALL_PASS`) lose 6 dB a pass, so `3·REVERB_COMB / -log10(feedback) + 3·REVERB_ALL_PASS / log10(2)`: the feedback comb filter's 60 dB decay time, `T60 = -3·τ / log10(g)` (Schroeder's comb, as in J. O. Smith, *Physical Audio Signal Processing*, "Schroeder Reverberators"); about 1.5 s at a feedback of 0.8 and 2.8 s at 0.9, and 10 s (never silent) at 1. Filters, EQ, compressors and distortion have none. A tree is never dropped while it rings: once its tail has passed (capped at 10 s, it may still ring), or as a rebuild past four ringing trees (`MAX_RINGING`) lets the oldest go, it is let go (`let_go`): what still plays on it stops, and its `master` track and send tracks (whose returns kira mixes beside `master`) fade to silence, each over 10 ms, and it is dropped 50 ms after (`FADING_KEPT`: kira removes a dropped track before it reads the commands sent with it, so the tree waits for a device to have rendered the fade, a buffer of up to 40 ms). A ringing tree costs the mixing thread its tracks each 128-frame chunk, as the live tree does: a reverb (about 100 KB of filters) and a delay under a second cost little, but kira's `Delay` shifts its whole buffer each chunk, so a 10 s delay holds 3.84 MB and moves about 1.4 GB/s while it rings, and the live tree and four ringing copies of it, after rapid saves, about 7 GB/s for up to 10 s, which a slower machine may underrun on (`docs/backlog/audio.md`). A tree with no tail is dropped at once (`drop_retired`: its handles go, kira keeping each track while its sounds play, and its send tracks are held by generation in `retired_sends` until its last voice ends and no copy fades on it). kira's send-track room (`SEND_TRACK_CAPACITY`, 7 × 16 = 112) is counted for the moment a rebuild builds its tree, as the old tree retires only after: the outgoing live tree's 16, the new tree's, the four ringing trees', and one more, a tree let go and fading out or a tree with no tail whose one-shots still play. More than one of those at a rebuild (three rebuilds within 50 ms while four trees ring, or one-shots still playing on two retired trees without tails) can still fill it, and a send bus then has no track until the next rebuild, warned.
- **The tree is built at once**: a play, `snapshot`, `clear_snapshot`, `set_bus_volume` and `voices_on` run `apply_mixer` first (gated on the mixer's content version, two store lookups when nothing changed), `AudioView::set_mixer` builds directly, and the frame's update catches a mixer nothing played after. So a mixer loaded with `load_now` in `on_enter` is built before that scene's first play, and its one-shots, which never move between trees, play on their own buses. A tree built between updates holds its buses from the last update's game clock (`game`), so a voice played on it while the game is paused starts held.
- **The check** (`Mixer::check`, a file's load and a mixer set in code alike) refuses every value kira's effects would panic or misbehave on, read from kira 0.12.6's `effect/`: a `Delay` under 1 ms (`MIN_DELAY`: kira's delay splits what it processes by its whole frames, and panics on the mixing thread at none), an `Eq` `q` under 0.01 (`MIN_Q`, which kira takes a lower one as), a `Compressor` threshold outside -60 to +24 dB (kira computes in `f32`, where one far below overflows to NaN and poisons the mix), and NaN, infinite and out-of-range values elsewhere (`EffectDef`'s rustdoc has each range). Within those, kira clamps the rest itself (a filter's cutoff and an EQ's frequency to half the device's rate, mixes to 0 to 1), the distortion's drive (±48 dB) never reaches the silence kira would divide by, and the panning and volume effects are not offered.
- `voices_on(bus)` and every bus limit count the bus's whole subtree.

## The game clock

- **Holds are per voice**, not kira's track pause: a bus that pauses with the game holds its voices while `time.game` is paused, and one that follows time scale while the scale is at or below 0.01 (`HOLD_SCALE`; pause counts as 0). Each held voice is paused in kira where it is, over 10 ms, and resumed from there. A kira track pause would freeze a game's bus under it that should play on, keep a stopped voice's fade from finishing (a released loop would fade out over the next menu instead of at once), and cannot reach a retired tree's tracks.
- **A scene that is not updated is not heard** (`hold_scenes`, `hold_scopes`, `owner_holds`): the scene manager tells the engine which scenes it holds, those below `update_from` when the top scene `holds_audio_below` (true by default), as the stack changes (`enter_top`, `pop` before `on_resume`, `clear`) and after each step (`sync_pauses`, beside `PhysicsPaused`); the engine is touched only when that set changes. A voice on a bus that pauses with the game is held while its owner is (`owner_held`, `owner_holds` over an `Owner`): a scene's voice while its scene is held, and an emitter's while the scene that added the emitter is (the world's entities stop with the scenes below, as physics does). `AudioEmitter`'s `on_insert` hook records the scope current then (`emitter_added`, into `emitter_scopes`: the scene whose code, systems or UI node observers added it), and its `on_discard` forgets it (`emitter_discarded`); a disabled entity keeps it, so a pool's emitter handed out again is still its scene's. An emitter added outside every scene has none, and a scene's exit (`release_scope`) drops its emitters' entries, so an entity that outlived its scene has none either: both keep the rule from before scenes were known, held while any scene is held. An emitter orphaned while scenes are held is held from the next update, though the held set did not change (a pause menu switching to another that holds the same scenes). A pause menu's own emitter thus plays on above the level it holds. The top scene's voices, the engine's (music, `persist`, global systems') and voices on buses that do not pause with the game play on. `held` is the bus's hold or the owner's, so a voice both hold resumes once both let go; a start (a pending loop landing included) computes both, so one played or landing while its scene is held starts held. An owner's hold is logged on the voice (`Pause`, `Resume`), a bus's on the bus. A voice not heard yet (held from its start) logs one `Resume`, as it is first heard, with its fade-in: its own resume while it is held is not logged apart, so a resume and its bus's letting go in one frame log once.
- A hold is the engine's and leaves the voice's own `pause` alone: a held voice still reports playing, and one paused by its own call stays paused when the game resumes. The voices' own pauses and resumes reach kira in the same pass, so each voice gets at most one pause or resume a frame.
- **A voice played while its bus is held** starts paused and silent, and its first resume takes its own fade-in (kira's resume would replace a fade-in still to run). That covers the frame the clock resumes in, such as a `Reset` from a pause menu (resumed in `SceneCleanup::TIME`, before the next scene plays), while the buses are still held from the frame before.
- **Time scale**: a voice on a bus that follows it plays at its pitch's rate times the game's speed, capped at 8 (`MAX_SCALE`: the game clock allows 100, which would swamp kira's mixing thread), gliding over the frame (at most 100 ms, `MAX_GLIDE`). A held voice keeps its rate.
- **Hitstop does not hold audio** (`GameTime::of` leaves it out): it is a visual freeze, and the hit's sound playing through it is the impact.
- Defaults: `sfx`, `ambience` and `voice` pause with the game; `sfx` and `ambience` follow time scale; `ui` and `music` do neither, so menus and music carry on under a pause. A game's bus does as its parent unless the mixer says.

## Spatial sound

- kira's 3D spatial tracks are not used: 2D needs only gain, pan and a filter, each a tweened change on the voice.
- The listener is the first `AudioListener`'s `GlobalTransform`, else the `MainCamera`'s, read in `audio_system` after the cameras, as the fixed steps and propagation left them (uninterpolated). Its rotation turns which way its right is. With none, every voice is heard as if at it. `ListenerQuery` keeps its queries, so a frame's search allocates nothing.
- Each frame a spatial voice's gain, pan and cutoff follow its offset: `falloff_gain_db` (Linear: amplitude in a line, -6 dB halfway; Inverse: `(1 - t) / (1 + 15t)`, the inverse-distance law of a source in the open heard from a fifteenth of the span, tapered to silence, about -25 dB halfway; Log: even in dB, -30 dB halfway; silent at the maximum), `pan_for` (`dx / pan_width`, -1 to 1), and the distance low-pass (open within the minimum distance, closing in even octave steps to `low_pass_at_max` at the maximum). A change smaller than can be heard is not sent (gain, pan and cutoff thresholds), so still voices cost nothing; one sent glides over the frame (at most 100 ms, `MAX_GLIDE`).
- **A distance low-pass needs a kira track of its own** (kira's effects are per track): a `VoiceFilter` sub-track under the bus's track, at most 32 at once (`MAX_FILTERED`); a voice past them plays unfiltered, its gain and pan still following.
- A one-shot at or beyond its maximum distance is culled (never starts); a loop there is virtual, taking no voice, until the listener comes within 95% of that distance (Virtual voices). A sound with no `spatial` played at a place plays as if at the listener, warned once.
- **Emitters**: `AudioEmitter`'s `on_insert` hook queues a play-on-spawn unless its entity is `Disabled` (a pool's); the world's first emitter adds observers of `Add<Disabled>` (let its voices go) and `Remove<Disabled>` (queue a play-on-spawn). A recycled entity of a `RecycleOldest` pool is disabled and enabled again (ecs module notes, Pooling), so its emitter stops and plays as a released and acquired one does. A queued entity plays once a frame, once its sound has loaded. Emitter voices follow the entity's `GlobalTransform` each frame.

## Music

- One player (`MusicPlayer`): the current track (playing, paused, or fading out until its voice ends) and a track asked for while it loads, which takes over once loaded while the current one plays on. Asking for the track already playing changes nothing (a paused one resumes), so every scene can ask for its track as it enters. A failed request changes nothing, not even a track still loading, and warns once.
- A switch fades the current track out and the new one in over the new track's `crossfade` (1 s by default); with none playing, it fades in over its `fade_in` (0 by default). `stop_music(fade)` fades over at least 10 ms and forgets a track still loading.
- **An intro and its loop are joined into one sound** (`join`, on the loader's thread): kira starts sounds and applies start times once per 128-frame chunk, so a loop scheduled at the intro's end cannot be sample-exact, while one sound whose loop region starts on the frame after the intro's last is. Both are decoded (`mode: Stream` is refused for them) and must share a sample rate. One clip with a loop region loops on kira's loop region and streams when long.
- A track plays on `music` or a bus under it; a definition naming another bus plays on `music`, warned once. `tempo` and `meter` are recorded for interactive music and not read by the player.

## Clips and streams

- The loader decodes a clip of up to 10 s (`ClipMode::Auto`) to a `StaticSoundData` on its thread, so a play costs a clone of an `Arc`. A longer clip keeps its encoded bytes and the sample rate and frame count its header gives (read by `stream::open_probed`, which also refuses anything but mono or stereo), so music does not sit decoded in memory; each play streams it. The header is read once: under `Auto` the probe's reader, past the header, decodes a short clip whole (`Probed::decode`, each packet's frames in order, converted as kira's own decoding converts them, an error failing the load as kira's does), `Decode` is kira's decode alone, and a streamed clip keeps the file's `Vec` the loader read (`ClipBytes`), never copied. A definition's `mode` forces either; `mode: Stream` on a file the probe cannot read (garbage, or a header that gives no length) fails the definition's load with the probe's error, naming the definition and the clip, not its first play. Mono and stereo only: kira's frames are stereo and its decoding refuses any other layout (`UnsupportedChannelConfiguration`), so the loader refuses one up front, streamed or decoded, rather than at play.
- **Streams play through the engine's `ClipDecoder`**, not kira's own decoder: making one stores only the bytes, the rate and the frame count, and the probe, the codec's setup and the seek to the start position wait for its first `decode`, on kira's decoder thread. A streamed play therefore costs the main thread only kira's buffers and its thread's spawn (about 24 µs measured), where kira's own decoder opens the file on the calling thread (0.3 to 1.2 ms for an OGG). Its frames are exact after a seek (each packet's frames placed by timestamp, those before the target dropped, with a preroll for MP3), and it always gives the window's frame count (a file that ends early is padded with silence), so a stream started partway (a reload switch, a rebuild) ends, and a loop wraps, at the file's end: kira's decoder miscounts frames after a Vorbis seek and its decoder thread spins at the end instead.
- A streamed clip's `region` is the decoder's window, and kira's slice is left unset: kira seeks a sliced stream's decoder by the slice's frames but asks it for the file's, so a region far into a long file would decode everything before it first.
- **A stream fails as it plays, not at `play`**: a file that cannot open, or whose format reader fails partway (an error other than the end of the stream, or a codec error that is not one packet's), fails on kira's decoder thread. `ClipDecoder` never returns the error, as kira would cut the stream with no fade: it reports it through the voice's `StreamFailure` (read with kira's own errors in `take_error`) and gives silence to the window's end, and the engine stops the voice in the frame's update with its sound's `fade_out` (at least 10 ms; a bare clip and the music over 10 ms), cut to end before that silence reaches playback (`failure_fade`): kira's decoder thread decodes up to 16,384 of the stream's frames ahead of playback (`DECODE_AHEAD`; 341 ms at 48 kHz, less at a higher playback rate), and reports the failure as it reaches it, so the fade is at most that, less the frame the engine took to see it and 10 ms for kira to apply the stop: about 315 ms at 48 kHz and 60 frames a second, so a 1 s `fade_out` on an ambience bed whose file fails does not end in a cut. A failure the decoder reaches with less decoded ahead (at a loop's wrap or just after a start partway) leaves less time, and one the engine sees late (a long frame) can still cut at the failure point, from a fade at least begun. The clip is warned about once by path and refused until it changes. A packet the codec cannot decode (`DecodeError` or a per-packet `IoError`) is skipped as silence.
- kira is at least 0.12.6, which ends a stream dropped without being stopped (`StreamingSound`'s `Drop` marks it stopped); 0.12.5 leaks its decoder thread, as when an engine or an audio world is dropped with a stream playing.
- A header's length can be an estimate: an MP3 with no Xing, Info or VBRI tag gets one from its bitrate, so such a streamed loop can wrap after a short silence or cut its end; a FLAC without `total_samples` loads only with `ClipMode::Decode`.

## Hot reload

- **A clip** (saved, or changed with `Assets::get_mut`): its playing loops and the music playing it switch to the new data where they are, crossfading over 10 ms (a decoded clip starts at once; a streamed one starts a new stream at the old position); its one-shots finish on the old data; new plays use the new.
- **A sound definition**: applies to the plays after; its playing loops take the change in its volume and pitch as a delta to the sound's level (`Tuning`: each keeps the spread it drew and its play's options) with the game's layer still on top. Saved onto another bus, its loops move there where they are (`replay`, crossfading over 10 ms, as a clip's reload does) and its one-shots finish where they are, so its limit counts the loops on their new bus. The definitions are followed before the clips, so a loop moved to a new bus starts on its clip as the store has it and its clip's reload in the same frame does not switch it again.
- **A switch** (any of these, and a rebuild's move) stops the old sound over 10 ms, logged as it played (its bus and gain: a rebuild's move names the old copy's bus on the retired tree, which the voice's bus no longer is by then), and starts the new one. One partway through a fade from silence (its fade-in, a resume's, a music crossfade's; `FadeIn`) carries on with what is left: the new sound starts as far below its level as the fade is and rises over the rest on its volume (kira fades a sound in only from silence; the eased-out curve is self-similar, so its rest is the same curve). The engine's own gain changes then take the rest of that fade (`gain_tween`); the game's `set_volume` takes over from it.
- **Loops that cannot switch** play on as they were (natively only when their bus's track has no room, as when a reload switches many loops on one bus and the old sounds hold the room while they fade): one warning for what was reloaded, at its content version, saying how many (`Unswitched`, `warned_unswitched`). A track whose switch fails at a rebuild (its definition names a clip not loaded yet) moves to the new tree as it was, warned as unswitched; only a loop that cannot even restart stays on the retired tree (`warn_stuck`).
- **Music** (its file, its clip, or its intro or loop file, which the loader reads with `LoadContext::read`, so their change reloads the music): the track switches, where it is, to what the saved definition says, crossfading over 10 ms. Each switch starts it again where it is, so a `get_mut` every frame would restart it every frame.
- **The mixer**: a rebuild (above).
- A save that fails to load or check changes nothing (`ReloadFailed`, the last good value kept).

## The web

- **The device opens at the player's first gesture**: a key pressed (not Escape, which browsers do not count as activation), a mouse button, or a touch lifted, and where the browser reports it only while `navigator.userActivation.isActive`. `window_event` calls `audio::unlock`, which makes kira's manager inside the gesture's event (winit dispatches it in the DOM handler's stack): kira keeps the browser's audio context to itself, so one made outside a gesture could never be resumed. A gamepad never unlocks it (browsers grant no activation for pad input).
- Until then the engine has no manager: one-shots are refused (logged), and loops and music wait as `Playback::Waiting` voices, started at the unlock by the rebuild onto the new manager, each fading in over its own fade-in. The unlock is not a pause: its buses hold as the game clock last stood (`game`), so it logs no bus `Pause` or `Resume`. `AudioView::waiting_for_gesture()` tells the game, so it can show "click or tap to start".
- The web has no streaming and no worker threads: every clip decodes, inline in the asset pump (one job a frame at least), so a long clip is a one-off hitch of its whole decode (a 30 s OGG is about 15.5 ms natively, more on wasm) and holds its decoded frames (about 23 MB a minute at 48 kHz stereo).
- The web has no files: a web game passes its assets as its source (`EngineBuilder::asset_source`, a `MemorySource` of embedded bytes). The engine looks for the mixer with `AssetSource::exists(MIXER_PATH)`, so the source must hold it there, or the game sets one with `set_mixer`. `audio_buffer_frames` does not apply (the browser picks the buffer).

## The device and the mock backend

- **Native start**: `AudioEngine::open(device, audio_buffer_frames)` opens the default output device through kira's cpal backend (`cpal-realtime` raises its thread's priority), its buffer fixed at `audio_buffer_frames` (`Ryo.toml [system]`) cut to the device's supported range when given; a device that refuses the size opens with its default, warned. A smaller buffer cuts latency and costs more callbacks, with more risk of underruns on a busy machine; the measured trade is in `docs/backlog/audio.md` (512 frames at 48 kHz, the default here, is heard 12.9 ms after its callback; 256 frames, 7.6 ms). When the default device changes, kira reopens its stream with the same fixed buffer, and a new device that refuses that size stops the sound for the session (kira's restart, not the engine's), so a game leaves it unset unless it needs the latency.
- **No device**: a headless engine (`HeadlessEngine`, `RYO_HEADLESS`), or a device that fails to open, runs the same mixer on kira's mock backend at 48 kHz: everything works, nothing plays aloud, and a failed device warns once with no retry. The mock renders inside `audio_system`, on the frame's thread, by the frame's real time, so a headless run's clock, tweens and sample positions advance as on a device (one fixed timestep a step). `mock_render_time()` (doc-hidden) is the last frame's rendering, which the benches leave out of the main thread's time.

## Determinism and testing

- Plays pick clips and draw gains and pitches from a `SmallRng`, seeded from entropy, except headless: `Engine::new` seeds it with a fixed value (`HEADLESS_SEED`), as `testing::audio_world` does, so a headless run's or a test's variations repeat run to run. `AudioView::seed(u64)` seeds it for replays, tests and captures. `Voice::pick()` reads what a play picked and drew (the clip's index and id, its gain and pitch as started).
- **The audio log** (`log.rs`, doc-hidden): a ring of the latest 4096 entries (held up to twice that and halved at once, so a push is cheap), off by default so a game pays nothing. `HeadlessEngine` turns it on before its start scene enters (`audio_log()`), as does `testing::audio_world`. Each entry has its kind (`Start`, `Stop`, `Pause`, `Resume`, `Gain`, `Pan`, `Rate`, `Refused`, `Culled`, `Stolen`, `Virtual`, `Real`), the voice's id (0 for a refused play, or a bus's change), its sound's and source's paths, its bus, gain as heard, pan, rate and fade, and on a `Refused` entry its `reason`: the rule first (`per_frame`, `cooldown`, `limit`, `full`, `undecodable`, `locked`, `unlock`, `loading`, `emitter`, `virtual`; the `REFUSED_*` constants), then a few words; on a `Virtual` or `Real` entry why it moved, the same way (`VIRTUAL_*`, `REAL_*`). Tests assert on it that a limit refused, a pause held `sfx` but not `ui`, a scene's hold paused its voice, a slider moved a playing bus, a scene exit stopped its loops with their fade. The log and its entry stay doc-hidden: they are test and debug tooling whose fields follow the engine, not API a game builds on.
- **Test worlds** (`testing/mod.rs`): `audio_world()` is `asset_world()` plus a `Time` and an engine on the mock with its log on, seeded with `HEADLESS_SEED` as a headless engine is (so a test's picks repeat without a call; `AudioView::seed` overrides); `audio_world_over(files)` reads a `MemorySource` the test keeps, to save over a file and `reload` it; `audio_step(world, dt)` is one frame (clock, pump, `audio_system`, then `clear_trackers`, as the engine's `Last` does: taking the engine out of the world writes a removal message that only clearing reuses). `metered_audio_world()` and `metered_audio_world_over(files)` (crate tests) put a `Meter` effect on kira's main track, before `master`'s gain: `frames()` is each chunk's last frame, `rendered(n)` the last `n` frames rendered, for measuring levels, balance and bands.
- **What is heard, not only logged**: the spatial tests also measure what the mock renders, decoded and streamed, from `play_at` and from an emitter: each falloff curve's gain at three distances (within 0.5 dB; they match to 0.01), a full pan width to either side (silent on the far side, +3 dB on the near: kira's constant-power `Frame::panned`), equal ahead, an emitter crossing (each frame's first 64 frames within 0.03 of where the last frame ended, which a pan sent without its glide fails, and its last at its place's pan, within the 96% of the glide a frame's whole chunks render), and the distance low-pass on a 200 Hz and 8 kHz pair (near the maximum the high tone falls about 45 dB more than the low one). The engine's spatial output is what it logs: a gain logged right but sent wrong fails them, where the log's tests would pass.
- **Guards**: `tests/audio_alloc.rs` counts an idle frame's allocations (0, fresh, after sounds played, and with virtual voices ranked each frame); `benches/audio.rs` holds 64 voices (32 spatial with a low-pass) to 50 µs of main-thread time a frame, 256 virtual loops ranked against 64 heard to 20 µs, `play` to 5 µs and every reload landing to 0.5 ms (measured: 4.2 µs, 7.1 µs, 0.50 µs, at most +149 µs); 1,024 virtual loops are printed beside them, with no target (about 38 µs). A streamed `play` costs about 24 µs, almost all of it kira's stream buffers and the decoder thread it spawns per stream: an exception to the spec's 5 µs, reported to the owner, which the bench holds at 100 µs only to catch a regression (`docs/backlog/audio.md`).

## Conventions

- **Every change is smoothed by at least 10 ms** (`MIN_FADE`): a voice's volume, pitch, pause, resume and stop, a bus's gain, a cutoff, a send. Shorter jumps click. The exception is a change to a voice in the frame it started, which nothing has heard. `Voice::set_volume_over`, `set_pitch_over`, `pause_over` and `resume_over` take a longer fade, as `stop` does.
- **Fades to and from silence are shaped** (`fade_to_silence`: `Easing::InPowi(FADE_POWER)`, `fade_from_silence`: `OutPowi`, `FADE_POWER = 4`): kira fades between -60 and 0 dB in a line of dB, which leaves a crossfade about 27 dB down in its middle; at this power each side is about -3.75 dB halfway, near an equal-power crossfade. Stops, pauses, resumes, fade-ins, holds and music crossfades use them; a fade of `MIN_FADE` or less stays a line (`shaped`), and changes between two audible levels (gain, pan, rate, bus gains, sends, cutoffs) stay linear (`tween`).
- **An idle frame allocates nothing for audio**: the frame's lists (emitters spawned, orphaned voices, victims) are kept and reused, the listener's queries are kept, and a refused play shares one stopped `Voice`.
- **Every failure says so once**: a missing or broken file is the registry's `AssetError` with its path, warned once; the engine warns once per definition that did not load, per bus name that does not exist, per snapshot name, per clip played at a place without spatial settings, per undecodable clip version, per full bus, per reload that could not switch its loops (with how many), and in debug builds per sound a one-shot was played of while it loaded (but a theme's UI sound). Refusals by `per_frame`, limit, cooldown or culling are logged, not warned. A device stream's error is warned once, saying sound may have stopped and to restart the game if it is silent (kira gives up when no device can take the stream).
- **Ids, not handles, in what the engine remembers** of an asset (definition state, warned sets), except what a voice plays, which it holds on purpose; per-definition state is pruned on `Removed`. What it remembers of a failure goes when the asset's file changes: a clip's failed decode (`undecodable`, kept by content version, so a clip changed in code is a new version) on `Modified`, the warnings given once about a definition or a music (`failed_sounds`, `warned_sounds`, `warned_flat`, the music player's `warned` and `warned_buses`) on a `Modified` from its file loading again, and a definition or music that did not load on its `Added` as it loads afresh (a file's only: one made in code is added at the pump after its first plays), so a fixed file plays, and is warned about again should it fail again. A `Modified` is a file's when the registry's load of it moved (`AssetServer::load_job`, a number each load and reload of a file takes) since the engine last saw it (`file_loads`, `file_reloaded`); one from code (`Assets::get_mut`, a definition's volume ramped each frame, say) forgets no warning, so it is not given again each frame. A failed asset whose handle is dropped sends no event, so its entry stays (one id).
- `AudioView` and the emitter hooks do nothing in a world without an `AudioEngine` (one the engine did not make): a play there returns a stopped voice, so code that plays sounds runs in any test world.
- Engine constants live beside their use with the reason in their rustdoc (`MIN_FADE`, `MAX_GLIDE`, `FADE_POWER`, `FINISH_AT_EXIT`, `VOICE_CAP`, `HOLD_SCALE`, `MAX_SCALE`, `MAX_FILTERED`, `DECODE_THRESHOLD`, `MAX_VIRTUAL`, `STAY_PUT`, `SWAP_DB`, `VIRTUAL_FADE`, `RETURN_RANGE`); a change to one is a change to the documented behaviour, so the `ryo-audio` skill and the rustdoc change with it.

## Known limits

- No loudness metering, master limiter, audio-rate side-chain or 3D audio (spec, Non-goals). Virtual voices are loops only (the owner's ruling): a one-shot a limit stops does not come back. A mixer can put a `Compressor` on `master`, which does not reach send returns.
- Interactive music (beat-quantized transitions, stems, stingers, game parameters) is not here: `Music::tempo` and `meter` are recorded for it.
