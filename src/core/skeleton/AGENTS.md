# Skeleton module notes

2D skeletal animation on Spine's model, written clean-room from Spine's public user guide and format documentation (nothing here is taken from Esoteric's runtime source, which is never read or cited): a `SkeletonData` asset (bones, slots, region, mesh, point and bounding-box attachments, skins, animations, events), read from a `.skeleton.ron` file or built in code; a `Skeleton` component that shows one on an entity, posed in each fixed step by its `SkeletonAnimation` (tracks, crossfades, layers, queues); `BoneSocket` children that ride its bones; `SkeletonEvent` and `SkeletonTrackEvent` messages; and drawing through the entity drawer, as a sprite is drawn.

- Using it from a game is the `ryo-skeleton` skill; the signatures are in `ryo-api` (Skeletal animation).
- The design and its decisions are in `docs/specs/2026-11-12-skeleton-design.md`. Where it and this file differ, this file and the code are current.
- What it costs, and every limit restated for a reader of the backlog, is in `docs/backlog/skeleton.md`; the bench is `benches/skeleton.rs`.
- The demo is `examples/demos/skeleton` (its `assets/README.md` records how the rig's parts are packed and placed).
- Not built: path constraints, constraints bound to a skin, clipping attachments, linked meshes, and reading another tool's files. IK, transform and spring constraints are applied (Constraints in a step and Springs, under In the world), animations key their values (Constraint keys, under The animation state) and a game overrides them (Constraint overrides, under In the world). The spring is the engine's own secondary motion, in place of Spine's physics constraints.
- Each rule below names the test that pins it.

## Data (`data.rs`, `attachment.rs`, `skin.rs`, `curve.rs`, `animation.rs`, `constraint.rs`)

- `SkeletonData` is immutable once built. Everything goes through `SkeletonDataBuilder` (`builder.rs`), which checks it in `build` (`validate.rs`); the loader builds through it too.
- The builder refers to bones, slots and skins by name. Animations hold `BoneId`/`SlotId` and event indices, and region attachments hold region indices: places in the order added, which `bone_id`, `slot_id`, `region_index` and `event_index` look up.
- `SkeletonError { item, problem }` names the item as `bone "arm"`, `slot "head"`, `region "torso"`, `event "step"`, `skin "red"`, `skin "red" slot "head" attachment "hat"`, `animation "walk"`, `constraint "leg"`, `animation "walk" timeline 2 (rotate)`, `mix "idle" to "run"` or `default mix`. The first problem found is reported.
- In the prelude, the module's generic names are prefixed (aliases; the types keep their short names in `ryo2d::core::skeleton`): `SkeletonClip` (`Animation`), `SkeletonCurve`, `SkeletonSkin`, `SkeletonTimeline`, `SkeletonAttachment`, `SkeletonRegion`, `SkeletonInfluence` (`Influence`), `SkeletonEventData`, `SkeletonEventValues`, `SkeletonEventAudio`, `BoneInherit` (`Inherit`), `BoneSpace` (`Space`, a bone override's; particles have a `Space` of their own), and the animation state's `SkeletonTrackEntry`, `SkeletonTrackEntryId`, `SkeletonTrackEventKind` and `SkeletonMixBlend` (`TrackEntry`, `TrackEntryId`, `TrackEventKind`, `MixBlend`), and the constraints' `SkeletonConstraint` (`Constraint`), `SkeletonConstraintId` (`ConstraintId`) and `SkeletonConstraintOverride` (`ConstraintOverride`), and the names that would read as an entity transform's, or not say whose they are, carry their kind: `IkConstraintOverride` (`IkOverride`), `TransformConstraintSetup` (`TransformSetup`), `TransformConstraintMix` (`TransformMix`), `TransformConstraintOffset` (`TransformOffset`) and `TransformConstraintOverride` (`TransformMixOverride`); `IkConstraint`, `IkSetup`, `IkValues`, `TransformConstraint`, `SpringConstraint`, `SpringSetup`, `SpringValues` and `SpringOverride` keep their names.
- The names (bones, slots, skins), each slot's bone and the skins themselves sit in one shared `SkeletonIndex` (`SkeletonData::index`, crate-internal), as the animations' names, durations and mixes sit in the `AnimationCatalogue`: a skeleton entity keeps the index, so it answers by name between steps without the asset store, and an animation state keeps the catalogue. New data is a new index and a new catalogue.
- **A region attachment is placed by its pivot** (`RegionAttachment::pivot`, `along`). `pivot`, a pixel of its region from the top-left (x right, y down, as the image is drawn), is what `x`, `y` and `rotation` place: that pixel lands at `(x, y)` in the bone's space. Left out it is the region's centre, and an attachment with neither `pivot` nor `along` is placed by the arithmetic it had before pivots, bit for bit (`loaders::skeleton::files_without_pivots_load_unchanged`). `along`, a second pixel, turns the image so the line from the pivot to it lies along the bone's x axis, and `rotation` is added to that. Pixels become the bone's units by `width` over the region's pixel width and `height` over its pixel height, so a region drawn larger keeps its pivot on the same pixel. Scale and mirroring act about the pivot, and the line's direction is taken after the attachment's own scale, mirroring included, so the line lies along the bone under any scale (`draw::a_region_placed_by_its_pivot_matches_the_centre_method`, against the centre placing worked out from the same two pixels, at pixel size, drawn larger evenly and not, under `scale_y: -1`, offset and turned on the bone, by both pixels and by the pivot alone; `draw::along_lays_the_pivot_line_along_the_bone`, mirrored on either axis or both and unevenly scaled, with the pivot and with the centre; the pixel test `a_limb_placed_by_its_pivot_draws_where_its_centre_placing_does`). Rule we chose: it is worked out as the quad is drawn (`RegionAttachment::quad`, from the region's pixel size in the data), not stored at build. The pixel size is the file's rectangle, which a reload of the image does not change (a new rectangle is new data), and the work is one branch for a region without a pivot and a square root more for one with `along`. Meshes need nothing: their vertices are already in the bone's space.
- A mesh attachment (`MeshAttachment`: region, UVs, triangles, hull, vertices, colour) holds its vertices either `Unweighted` (positions in the slot's bone space) or `Weighted` (`WeightedVertices`: one flat list of `Influence`s, each a bone, a position in that bone's space and a weight, with each vertex's range in it). `position_count()` counts what a deform key must match: a vertex each when unweighted, an influence each when weighted. `WeightedVertices::new` scales each vertex's weights to sum to 1 (`a_valid_mesh_builds_weighted_and_unweighted`); a vertex whose weights do not sum to a finite number above 0 is left as given for validation to refuse. The prelude has `MeshAttachment`, `MeshVertices`, `WeightedVertices` and `SkeletonInfluence`.
- Bone timelines key rotate, and translate, scale and shear either combined (`Vec2` keys) or per axis (`TranslateX` … `ShearY`, `f32` keys).
- A deform timeline (`Timeline::Deform { slot, attachment, frames }`) keys the shape of the mesh a slot shows under one attachment name. A key is a `Vec<f32>`: an offset from the setup shape, x then y, for each of the mesh's `position_count()` in their order, so one a vertex in the slot's bone's space for an unweighted mesh, and one an influence in that influence's bone's space for a weighted one (a keyed shape turns with the bones it rides). An empty key is no offset, the setup shape; a key is whole or empty, never part of a mesh. What it keys, for the duplicate check and the mixing cover rule, is its slot's attachment by name: `Timeline::target` gives `Target::Attachment(slot, name)`, borrowing the name, with the one property `deform`. Bone and slot timelines keep the targets and properties they had (`Target::Bone`, `Target::Slot`), so nothing is allocated to compare two timelines, and the comparison is made when a mix starts, not in a step (`validate::two_deform_timelines_for_one_slot_and_name_fail` for what a deform keys; `draw::a_deform_moves_each_position_in_its_bone_s_space_before_it_is_weighted` for the positions' order and the space each offset is in; `apply::an_empty_key_is_the_setup_shape`).
- A constraint's timeline keys one constraint: `Timeline::Ik { constraint, frames }` (kind "ik") keys an IK constraint's `IkValues` (`mix`, `softness`, `bend`, `compress`, `stretch`), `Timeline::TransformMix { constraint, frames }` (kind "transform mix") a transform constraint's six mixes (a `TransformMix`), and `Timeline::Spring { constraint, frames }` (kind "spring") a spring's `SpringValues` (`mix`, `wind`, `gravity`; the rest of a spring's settings are its setup's). Each key is whole: a value a file's key leaves out is filled from the constraint's setup by the loader, and code gives every value. Its target is `Target::Constraint(id)` with the one property "values", so two timelines for one constraint in an animation clash, of any kind, and a crossfade's cover rule treats the constraint as one property (`validate::two_timelines_for_one_constraint_fail`; `state::a_crossfade_between_two_keyed_mixes_has_no_dip`).
- Colours (`SlotData::color`, `RegionAttachment::color`, colour keys) are 8-bit sRGB `Color`s. The pose keeps each slot's colour in float (`Vec4`, sRGB with alpha, 0 to 1), so blends never round; drawing clamps it and converts it to 8 bits.

## The file (`src/core/asset/loaders/skeleton.rs`)

- `SkeletonLoader` reads `.skeleton.ron`; its rustdoc holds the whole format as a commented example, which a test loads (`the_documented_example_loads`). Everything the data holds is expressible: every bone field and inherit mode, slot colour and blend, regions on several images, region, mesh, point and bounding-box attachments, several skins, events with default values and audio, combined and per-axis bone timelines, attachment, colour, deform, draw-order and event keys (with value overrides), a curve on every value key, and the mixes.
- Choices where the brief was silent: images are named in `images` and regions refer to them by name, their paths from the asset root; a region attachment's size is its region's unless written; a key is `(time, value)` or `(time, value, curve)`, the curve `Linear` when left out; a draw-order key lists every slot by name (`(0.25, ["head", "torso"])`, or `None` for the setup order); an event key is `(time, "event")` or `(time, "event", (int: 9, volume: 0.3))`; the mixes are a map, `{ default: 0.1, pairs: [("a", "b", 0.2)] }`.
- A region attachment is `Region(region: "r", x:, y:, rotation:, scale_x:, scale_y:, width:, height:, pivot: (x, y), along: (x, y), color: "#..")`, `pivot` and `along` left out for none, in the region's pixels, so nothing is converted (`region_pivots_load_as_the_builder_builds_them`, against its builder twin, with an `along` at the pivot, an `along` at the centre with the pivot left out and values not finite, each failing naming the file and the attachment; the every-feature file and its twin carry both; the rustdoc example places its torso by them, `the_documented_example_loads`).
- A mesh is `Mesh(region: "r", uvs: [..], triangles: [..], hull: 4, vertices: .., color: "#..")`, the `hull` 0 and the colour white when left out. Its `vertices` are `Unweighted([(x, y), ..])` or `Weighted([[("bone", x, y, weight), ..], ..])`, a list of influences for each vertex. Positions are in pixels and no angle occurs, so nothing is converted. A weight is normalised as it loads (`a_mesh_attachment_loads_weighted_and_unweighted`; the twin comparison in `a_file_using_every_feature_loads_as_the_builder_builds_it`). A mistake in a mesh fails naming `skin .. slot .. attachment ..` (`a_mistake_in_a_mesh_names_its_attachment`).
- A slot's block in an animation holds its deforms as a map from attachment names to keys: `"banner": (deform: { "flag": [(0.0, []), (0.5, [0, -6, 0, -6, 0, 6, 0, 6], Bezier(0.25, 0, 0.75, 1)), (1.0, [])] })`. A key is `(time, offsets)` or `(time, offsets, curve)`, the offsets x then y for each position, in pixels, and `[]` the setup shape; a name written twice in the map fails at its line, and a name no skin has in the slot fails as validation refuses it, with the file, the animation and its timeline (`a_deform_timeline_loads_and_matches_the_builder_s`, which also names the animation and timeline of a mistake in one; `a_mistake_in_the_file_names_the_file_and_the_item`; the twin comparison in `a_file_using_every_feature_loads_as_the_builder_builds_it`; the rustdoc example has one).
- Timelines come out in a fixed order: each bone in file order (rotate, translate, scale, shear, then translate x, translate y, scale x, scale y, shear x, shear y), each slot (attachment, colour, then its deforms in the file's order), each constraint in the file's order (its IK keys, then its transform keys, then its spring keys), then draw order and events, so a validation error's `timeline N` follows the file.
- An animation's `constraints` block maps constraint names to `(ik: [keys])`, `(transform: [keys])` or `(spring: [keys])`. An IK key's value is `(mix:, softness:, bend:, compress:, stretch:)`, a transform key's `(rotate:, x:, y:, scale_x:, scale_y:, shear_y:)` and a spring key's `(mix:, wind:, gravity:)`, each value optional: one left out is the constraint's setup value, and a `bend` the setup leaves out too is the default for the constraint's bone count (`the_bend_setting_loads_keys_and_overrides`; `constraint_timelines_load_and_match_the_builder_s`, the design's example against its builder; the every-feature file and its twin carry all three kinds with left-out values and a curve; the rustdoc example carries all three, `the_documented_example_loads`). A name the file's `constraints` list lacks fails naming the animation and the name; keys of another kind than the constraint's fail as validation refuses them; a misspelt value fails at its line (`a_mistake_in_the_file_names_the_file_and_the_item`).
- Names the file uses that nothing declares (an animation's bone, slot or event, a region's image, an attachment's region, a mesh influence's bone) fail as a `SkeletonError` naming the animation, region or `skin .. slot .. attachment ..` and the name.

- Constraints (`constraint.rs`) as data: `SkeletonData::constraints()` lists them in the order they apply (`constraints_keep_the_order_they_were_added_in`), and `constraint(name)` finds one. The builder's `ik`, `transform_constraint` and `spring` hold names until `build`; each constraint's bones and target are `BoneId`s. A constraint is `Constraint::Ik(IkConstraint)`, `Constraint::Transform(TransformConstraint)` or `Constraint::Spring(SpringConstraint)`; names are unique across the kinds.
- **A spring** (`SpringConstraint { name, bone, setup }`) gives one bone secondary motion and has no target: `Constraint::bones()` is its one bone and `Constraint::target()` is an `Option`, `None` for a spring (`validate::a_spring_builds_on_its_one_bone_with_no_target`). Its `SpringSetup` holds what it moves (`x`, `y`, `rotate`, `scale_x`, each 0 to 1, all 0 by default), `inertia` (0.5), `strength` (100), `damping` (5), `wind` and `gravity` (0, units a second squared, y down), `limit` (5000, units a second) and `mix` (1). With only `rotate: 1`, those give a bone 40 long at 60 Hz an underdamped swing (a natural period of about 0.63 s, the speed's damping a quarter of critical) that trails a body starting off at 180 units a second by about 12.5 degrees at most and settles to within 0.001 rad about 2.2 s after the body stops (`constrain::a_spring_lags_a_moving_parent_and_settles` runs those defaults). `SpringValues::of(setup)` is what a pose starts each step with. A transform constraint's angles (the offsets' `rotation` and `shear_y`) are radians in the data.
- What a step's constraint pass needs of the bones is worked out once, as the data is built (`ConstraintBones::of`, called by `validate::build` and kept in the shared `SkeletonIndex`), so a step searches nothing: for each constraint the bones to place again after it, in bone order (an IK constraint's or a spring's own bones and every bone below them); for a transform constraint its bones in bone order, whatever order it names them in, each with the bones to place again after it (the bones below it, and in a local mode that bone first); and the bones whose own values a constraint changes (an IK constraint's, a spring's, a local transform constraint's), in bone order, each once. Data without constraints has empty lists (`constrain::a_constraint_places_the_descendants_of_its_bones_again`, which reads the lists of a rig whose bones below the arm are not next to one another, of a transform constraint naming a bone and its descendant, in both modes, and of a spring; `constrain::a_skeleton_without_constraints_keeps_no_constraint_state`).
- The file's `constraints` list (after `slots`) holds `Ik(name:, bones:, target:, mix:, softness:, bend:, compress:, stretch:, max_stretch:, uniform:)`, `Transform(name:, bones:, target:, mix: (rotate:, x:, y:, scale_x:, scale_y:, shear_y:), offset: (rotation:, x:, y:, scale_x:, scale_y:, shear_y:), local:, relative:)` and `Spring(name:, bone:, x:, y:, rotate:, scale_x:, inertia:, strength:, damping:, wind:, gravity:, limit:, mix:)`. A spring's settings left out are `SpringSetup::default()`'s, and a key's values left out its setup's; it names no target, and a `target` written fails at its line (`springs_load_with_their_defaults_and_keys_as_the_builder_builds_them`, against its builder twin, with a key's left-out values, and with a bone that is not one, a spring that moves nothing, one out of order, keys of another kind each way, a key's mix out of range and misspelt settings and key values; the every-feature file and its twin carry two springs, one with every setting and one with defaults, and a spring key with left-out values and a curve; the rustdoc example carries one and a key, `the_documented_example_loads`). A setting left out is `IkSetup::default()`'s (mix 1, the rest off, and `bend` left for the build to make the default for the bone count), and a transform's mix and offset left out are all 0. `bend` is written `Positive`, `Negative` or `Pose` (`the_bend_setting_loads_keys_and_overrides`: two bones bent `Negative`, a chain and one bone leaving it out). `max_stretch` is a number of at least 1 or left out for no limit, and a key never sets it: a key's `IkValues` hold the setup's, and a key whose `max_stretch` differs from its constraint's fails to build, naming `key N's` (`a_key_cannot_carry_its_own_stretch_limit`; the loader fills it, so files are unaffected: `a_file_s_constraints_load_and_match_the_builder_s` and `a_file_using_every_feature_loads_as_the_builder_builds_it` load it; `a_stretch_limit_below_one_or_on_a_chain_fails_to_load` refuses it below 1 or on a chain). The offsets' angles are degrees. `a_file_s_constraints_load_and_match_the_builder_s` loads the three examples against their builders, `a_file_using_every_feature_loads_as_the_builder_builds_it` and `the_documented_example_loads` carry all three kinds, and `a_mistake_in_the_file_names_the_file_and_the_item` pins an unknown target, a value not finite and a misspelt setting.

## Validation rules

Every rule below has its own test in `validate.rs`, which asserts the error's full item and that the problem names the reason.

- Names are unique per kind (bones, slots, regions, events, skins, animations), and so is an attachment name within a skin's slot, and a mix pair.
- Parents come before children. At most 255 bones and 255 slots (`bone_id` and `slot_id` give `None` past the 255th).
- References resolve: parents, slots' bones, attachments' skins, slots and regions, timelines' bones and slots, event keys' events, mixes' animations.
- Values are finite: bone setups, attachments, event defaults and overrides, key times and values, curves, durations and mixes. Durations and mixes are not negative.
- Rules we chose where Spine's docs are silent:
  - The default skin (`SkeletonData::DEFAULT_SKIN`, "default") always exists, first in `skins()`, and may be declared once without being a duplicate.
  - Every timeline has at least one key. Key times run from 0 to the animation's duration, strictly increasing, except event keys, which may share a time (several events at one moment).
  - An animation has at most one timeline per property of a bone or slot: two rotate timelines on one bone fail, and so do a combined translate and a translate x on one bone (the combined one keys both axes); translate x and translate y together are fine. At most one draw-order and one event timeline an animation.
  - A bezier's x controls lie in 0..=1, so its time never runs backwards.
  - A draw-order key lists every slot exactly once (or is `None`, the setup order).
  - A region is not empty; a bounding box has at least three vertices.
  - A region attachment's `pivot` and `along` are finite (`a_region_pivot_or_along_not_finite_fails`, which also builds a pivot outside its image: a joint may lie past the edge), and `along` is not its pivot, the region's centre when `pivot` is left out, since no line would run from one to the other (`a_region_along_at_its_pivot_fails`).
  - No animation is named `<empty>`: that is the animation state's empty animation (`SkeletonAnimation::EMPTY`), and a track event's animation name must tell the two apart.
- A mesh (each rule names the skin, slot and attachment, and has its own test in `validate.rs`; the limits are ours, `u16` indices):
  - its region is in range (`a_mesh_region_outside_the_regions_fails`);
  - it has at most 65,535 vertices (`a_mesh_of_more_than_65535_vertices_fails`) and one UV per vertex (`a_mesh_with_a_uv_count_other_than_its_vertices_fails`);
  - it has at least one triangle (`a_mesh_with_no_triangles_fails`), its indices come in threes (`a_mesh_with_triangle_indices_not_in_threes_fails`) and each is below the vertex count (`a_mesh_triangle_index_past_the_vertices_fails`);
  - its `hull` is 0, or from 3 up to the vertex count (`a_mesh_hull_of_one_or_two_or_past_the_vertices_fails`);
  - every UV, position, influence position and weight is finite (`a_non_finite_mesh_value_fails`);
  - every influence's bone exists (`a_mesh_influence_of_an_unknown_bone_fails`) and its weight is not negative (`a_mesh_influence_with_a_negative_weight_fails`);
  - every vertex's weights sum above 0, after normalising, so a vertex with no influences fails too; and to a finite number, so weights that are each finite and together overflow fail as well: they could not be normalised, and skinning relies on weights that sum to 1 (`a_mesh_vertex_whose_weights_sum_to_zero_fails`).
- A deform timeline (each rule's test is in `validate.rs`):
  - its slot exists, it has keys at finite times from 0 to the duration in strict order, its values are finite and its curves good, as every timeline's are (`a_deform_timeline_is_checked_as_every_timeline_is`);
  - its attachment is a name some skin has in its slot, the default skin or any other (`a_deform_naming_an_attachment_no_skin_has_in_its_slot_fails`: a name in no skin, a name only another slot has, keys that are all empty, and a name only a skin other than the default has, which builds). Rule we chose: a slot can only show what a skin has in it, every skin is built before a timeline is checked and the data cannot change afterwards, so such a deform could never apply; and since a slot showing another attachment is passed over in silence, a misspelt name would otherwise do nothing and say nothing. The error is the timeline's, `animation "wave" timeline 0 (deform)`, and names the attachment and the slot. Only the name is checked: what the attachment is, and whether a mesh there holds the keys' count, stay for when the deform is applied, since a skin may hold a region under the name and a reload may change a mesh's count;
  - an animation has at most one for a slot and attachment name. Two of one slot for different names do not clash, and none clashes with its slot's attachment or colour timeline (`two_deform_timelines_for_one_slot_and_name_fail`);
  - every key is empty or as long as the others (`deform_keys_of_different_lengths_fail`);
  - a key holds an even count of values, an x and a y a position (`a_deform_key_of_an_odd_count_fails`). Rule we chose: an odd count fits no mesh, so a number left out of a file fails as it loads, naming the key, where it would otherwise only be a deform skipped at run time.
- A constraint (`constraint "leg"`; each rule has its own test in `validate.rs`, the item `constraint "<name>"` and the reason's words asserted):
  - at most 255 constraints and a name used once across the three kinds (`too_many_constraints_or_ik_bones_fail`, `a_constraint_name_used_twice_fails`);
  - every bone and the target is a bone (`a_constraint_s_unknown_bone_or_target_fails`), a bone is listed once (`a_constraint_s_bones_must_be_distinct`), and the target is neither one of the bones nor below one: a bone cannot follow what it carries, found by walking `parent` links (`a_constraint_s_target_among_or_below_its_bones_fails`);
  - IK has one to 16 bones (`too_many_constraints_or_ik_bones_fail`), each after the first an immediate child of the one before (`an_ik_bone_that_is_not_its_predecessor_s_child_fails`) and every bone inheriting normally (`an_ik_bone_that_does_not_inherit_normally_fails`); `mix` is 0 to 1 and `softness` finite and not negative (`an_ik_mix_or_softness_out_of_range_fails`);
  - IK settings that cannot apply are refused rather than ignored: `softness` above 0 on other than two bones, `compress` on other than one, `stretch` or `uniform` on three or more (`an_ik_setting_that_cannot_apply_fails`), and a `max_stretch` not finite, below 1 or on three or more bones (`a_stretch_limit_below_one_fails`); a `max_stretch` without `stretch` is allowed, since an override can turn stretch on later and the limit then holds (the same test builds it), and a `bend` of `Pose` on one bone or two, or `Negative` on one (`pose_on_two_bones_and_a_bend_on_one_bone_fail`);
  - an IK setup that leaves `bend` out is built with the default for its bone count, `Positive` for one bone and two and `Pose` for a chain, so the data's setups and every `IkValues` hold a bend (`pose_on_two_bones_and_a_bend_on_one_bone_fail`);
  - IK lengths: with two or more bones the last bone's `length` is above 0, and with one bone and `stretch` or `compress` it is too (`an_ik_tip_without_a_length_fails`); a lone bone that only points needs none;
  - a transform constraint has at least one bone and finite mixes and offsets; a mix outside 0 to 1 is allowed (`a_transform_constraint_without_bones_or_with_a_value_not_finite_fails`);
  - allowed, each pinned by a case that builds: a target above the constraint's bones (`a_constraint_s_target_among_or_below_its_bones_fails`), a transform constraint naming a bone and its own descendant (`a_constraint_s_bones_must_be_distinct`), and exactly 255 constraints (`too_many_constraints_or_ik_bones_fail`).
- A spring (`constraint "hair"`; each rule's test is in `validate.rs`, the item and the reason's words asserted):
  - its bone is a bone, as every constraint's (`a_spring_builds_on_its_one_bone_with_no_target`, which also uses a name an IK constraint has);
  - every value is finite; `x`, `y`, `rotate`, `scale_x`, `inertia` and `mix` are 0 to 1; `strength` is above 0; `damping` and `limit` are 0 or more (`a_spring_value_not_finite_or_out_of_range_fails`, which builds every value at its edge);
  - it moves something: one of the four amounts above 0 (`a_spring_that_moves_nothing_fails`);
  - one that moves rotation or scale is on a bone whose `length` is above 0, since its tip is what lags; one that moves only its place needs none (`a_spring_moving_rotation_or_scale_on_a_bone_without_length_fails`);
  - a strand's order: a spring listed before the spring of an ancestor of its bone fails, naming the earlier spring, the later one and both bones, so a child lags its parent's sprung place. Other constraints between them, springs on bones side by side in either order, and two springs on one bone (the second sways the place the first left: `constrain::a_strand_lags_link_by_link`) build (`a_spring_listed_before_its_bone_s_ancestor_s_spring_fails`).
- A constraint's timeline (the item `animation "walk" timeline 3 (ik)`, `(transform mix)` or `(spring)`; each rule's test is in `validate.rs`):
  - its constraint exists and is of the timeline's kind, the reason naming the constraint's kind (`a_constraint_timeline_for_an_unknown_constraint_or_the_other_kind_fails`; for springs, each way, `a_spring_key_out_of_range_or_for_another_kind_fails`), and no other timeline of the animation keys it (`two_timelines_for_one_constraint_fail`; for springs the same test as above);
  - a spring key's `mix` is finite and 0 to 1, its `wind` and `gravity` finite, each reason beginning `key N's` (`a_spring_key_out_of_range_or_for_another_kind_fails`);
  - its keys are checked as every timeline's are (times, order, curves), and their values as a setup's: an IK key's `mix` 0 to 1 and `softness` finite and not negative, a transform key's mixes finite (outside 0 to 1 allowed); and an IK key sets no switch its constraint could not have, by the setup's own rules (a `bend` the constraint cannot have, `softness` above 0 on other than two bones, `compress` on other than one, `stretch` on three or more, `stretch` or `compress` on one bone without a length). Each reason begins `key N's` (`a_constraint_key_out_of_range_or_with_a_switch_it_cannot_have_fails`; for `bend`, `pose_on_two_bones_and_a_bend_on_one_bone_fail`). `uniform` is not keyed: a setup may set it with neither switch, and it applies whenever one is on, by the setup, a key or an override. `max_stretch` is not keyed either: a key's must equal its constraint's (none, or the same number), or it fails with a reason beginning `key N's` (`a_key_cannot_carry_its_own_stretch_limit`).
- Not checked: slots' setup attachments and attachment keys are names looked up in the skin at run time, so a name no skin has is allowed (it shows nothing). Nor that the attachment a deform names is a mesh, or that its keys are as long as a mesh's `position_count() × 2`: what a name shows is the skins' to decide, so that is found when the deform is applied (Deform keys, under The animation state).

## Pose and world transforms (`pose.rs`)

- `BonePose` is a bone's local transform (setup, or as animations leave it), and the `Pose` holds one per bone (both crate-internal: no public call gives or takes either), the bones' transforms in skeleton space and in the world, and each slot's colour (float sRGB, `pose::srgb` of the setup colour) and attachment and the draw order, all set from the setup by `Pose::setup`, and each slot's deform offsets and each constraint's values (both below). `Pose::reset` puts the local transforms, colours, attachments, deform offsets (to none), draw order and constraint values back to the setup without allocating, and leaves the skeleton-space and world transforms as last computed (`reset_puts_a_changed_pose_back_to_setup`); the animation state calls it at the start of every step's apply. It zips the pose with the data, so a pose made from other data would be reset in part and in silence: debug assertions refuse one, and the skeleton's step makes the pose anew whenever its data changes.
- **Deform offsets** (`Pose::deforms`, a `SlotDeform` for each slot): the offsets this step's animations left the mesh the slot shows, and the step before's for drawing between the two. Each is a list of an x and a y for each position of that mesh, and a list with nothing in it is no offset, the setup shape. Rules, each with its test:
  - The pose holds none until a deform key first moves a mesh of the skeleton, and one for each slot from then on, so a skeleton no deform plays on allocates nothing for them and its reset, step and settle loop over nothing (`reset_puts_a_changed_pose_back_to_setup`).
  - `reset` empties this step's lists and keeps their room and the step before's offsets; a skeleton's step swaps each slot's two lists first (`step_deforms`), so the last step's become the previous ones and no list is allocated again (`deform_offsets_pass_from_step_to_step_and_stay_with_their_attachment`, `component::a_steady_step_takes_no_more_room_for_its_deforms`).
  - Offsets belong to the attachment a slot shows. `deforms_follow_slots`, called once the slots show what a step leaves them showing, makes a slot's previous offsets its current ones wherever it shows another name than at the last look: an attachment that has just come takes its own shape at once (its own deform's, or the setup shape), and is not drawn from the offsets of the one before, which may hold as many positions (the same `pose.rs` test; `component::a_deform_resets_when_the_slot_s_attachment_changes`, by an attachment key, by the game's `set_attachment`, hidden and handed back). So the offsets return to none when a slot's attachment changes, unless a deform of the new one applies in that step.
  - `settle_deforms` makes every slot's previous offsets its current ones: `Skeleton::settle` calls it with the bones', so a first placing (a step after a missed one, a pose outside a step, a flip) draws its meshes in the shape they have (`component::a_first_placing_draws_its_deform_as_it_is`).
- **Constraint values** (`Pose::constraints`, a `ConstraintValues` for each constraint, in the data's order): an IK constraint's `IkValues`, a transform constraint's `TransformMix` and a spring's `SpringValues`. `Pose::setup` gives each its constraint's setup values and `Pose::reset` puts each back to them in the list the pose has, so every step starts from the setup's, with an animation state or without, and a pose of data without constraints holds none and has no room for any. The constraint pass applies the values the pose holds, not the setup's. Between the reset and the pass the animation state's constraint keys write them, `Pose::clamp_constraints` holds an IK constraint's `mix` within 0 to 1 and its `softness` at 0 or more, and a spring's `mix` within 0 to 1, once every track has applied (`state::a_spring_mix_is_clamped_after_the_tracks`: added over itself, a mix held at 1 and at 0, the wind, which has no range, not), and the game's constraint overrides are written over them (Constraint keys, under The animation state; Constraint overrides, under In the world) (`constrain::the_pose_s_constraint_values_return_to_setup_each_step_without_allocating`, by the list's pointer and capacity; `constrain::a_skeleton_without_constraints_keeps_no_constraint_state`).
- A bone's local linear part: x axis `scale_x * (cos(rotation + shear_x), sin(rotation + shear_x))`, y axis `scale_y * (-sin(rotation + shear_y), cos(rotation + shear_y))`. With y down this turns clockwise, as the engine does.
- `compute_world` is one pass over the parent-first bones (`place_bone` for each, into `skeleton_space`), then `apply_root` (`world = root × skeleton_space`). The skeleton's step runs the two itself when bone overrides apply, setting each overridden bone just before it is placed; `parent_space` and `direction_space` are what a world-space override is converted through (In the world, below). A child's origin is always its parent's full world transform applied to `(x, y)`: translation is inherited in every mode. Its linear part is `M * L`, where `L` is its local linear part and `M` comes from the parent's world linear part `P` (axes `p_x`, `p_y`) by mode:
  - Normal: `M = P`.
  - Only translation: `M = I`.
  - No rotation or reflection: `M = R(θ) * diag(s, u) * R(-θ)`, where `θ` is the parent bone's direction (the angle of `p_x`), `s = |p_x|` its length scale along itself and `u = |det(P)| / s` its area per unit length across itself. The child keeps its own world angle and is stretched along the parent bone's own axes, wherever the parent points: a parent turned 90 degrees and scaled (2, 1) stretches the child vertically. The parent's shear is dropped: written as `P * R(-θ)` it would skew the child's axes, which is a rotation the mode removes, and a mirrored parent's shear has no single unmirrored form. The absolute determinant takes out reflection and keeps the parent's area. If `p_x` has collapsed (under 1e-6), `θ` comes from `p_y` turned back a quarter, with `s = 0` and `u = |p_y|`, the limit of the normal path; with both axes collapsed `M` is zero.
  - No scale: the child is rigid, pointing where normal inheritance points it. Its world x direction is `d = normalise(P * (cos(r + shear_x), sin(r + shear_x)))`, its y direction `d.perp()` (a quarter turn clockwise), negated when `det(P) < 0` so the parent's mirroring stays. Then the child's own scale and shear apply in that frame (its linear part with the rotation taken as `-shear_x`). Taking the direction from `P` applied to the child's own direction, not from `p_x`, makes it match normal inheritance exactly under a skewed parent (a chain of turns under non-uniform scale), and the perpendicular unit frame takes out every scale and skew. If `P` squashes that direction flat (a zero-scale parent), the child is turned by the parent's rotation (from `p_x`, else from `p_y` turned back a quarter) at full size; with both axes zero it keeps its own axes.
  - No scale or reflection: as no scale, but the y direction is always `d.perp()`, so a mirrored parent leaves the child pointing as normal inheritance does but unmirrored.
- Rule we chose: the inherit modes act in skeleton space. `root` (the entity's transform) is applied to every bone after the pass, whatever its mode, so turning, scaling or flipping the entity turns, scales or flips an only-translation or no-rotation bone too (Spine's own flip flips every bone).
- `BonePose::affine` is a bone's own transform in its parent's space: that linear part at `(x, y)`. A root bone is placed by it (`normal_inherit_composes_parent_and_child`), and the IK tests compose their bones with it.

## Solving IK (`ik.rs`)

`one_bone`, `two_bones` and `chain` turn one bone to point at a target, or two bones or a chain of them so the last one's tip reaches it. They are pure: they take bones' own values (`BonePose`) and a target point, and write those bones' rotation and scale (a chain's rotations alone). They know nothing of a pose, the data or a step: the constraint pass calls them, two bones through `two_bones_following`, which carries a knee from step to step (`constrain.rs`; Constraints in a step, under In the world; The knee follows its last bend, below). `IkValues` is what an IK constraint's setup gives and animations key (`mix`, `softness`, `bend`, `compress`, `stretch`, `max_stretch`; `bend` an `IkBend`, `Positive`, `Negative` or `Pose`; `max_stretch` an `Option<f32>` the keys leave as the setup's), taken from an `IkSetup` by `IkValues::of(setup, bones)` (`the_values_a_constraint_starts_with_are_its_setup_s`), which resolves a `bend` left out for the bone count as the build does, so a key built from a setup bends as the setup does (`a_chain_key_built_from_its_setup_follows_its_pose`) and in the prelude; `uniform` is not keyed, so the functions take it beside the values. The solves are worked out from the design's rules and from geometry (the law of cosines, rotation matrices; a chain's is the engine's own, Spine having no chains). The tests named here are `ik::`'s own; they find positions by composing the bones' own transforms (`BonePose::affine`), as placing a pose composes them, and never through the solve's own formulas.

- **What a caller does.** Before: it brings the target into the space of the first bone's parent (back through `Pose::parent_space`, and not at all when that is flat), and passes the bones' own values as animation and the game's overrides left them, the last bone's data `length`, the constraint's values and its setup's `uniform`. For two bones the second is the first's immediate child and both inherit normally, which validation holds. After: only own values are written, so it places the bones, and everything below them, again.
- **Only rotation and scale are written.** Each bone's `rotation`, and under stretch or compress the first bone's `scale_x` (with `uniform` its `scale_y` too). A bone's place and its shear are never written, and nor is the second bone's scale. The solving tests compare every other value to the bit (`assert_only_turned`).
- **Nothing is written**, so the bones are the same to the bit, when (`a_target_at_the_first_bone_s_origin_changes_nothing_and_makes_no_nan` for all but the first):
  - the mix is 0 or less (`one_bone_points_at_its_target_at_each_mix`, `a_half_mix_turns_each_bone_half_way_the_short_way`);
  - the target is on the first bone's origin, within 1e-6 of a unit: there is no way to turn toward it;
  - a value given is not finite (a bone's, the target, the length, the mix or the softness), or a value that would be written is not (huge values that overflow). No solve writes a NaN or an infinity, whatever it is given: the test sets every input, alone and in pairs, to each of fourteen extremes;
  - two bones: the first bone is flat (the determinant of its linear part is within 1e-12 of 0, as a zero scale on either axis makes it), or part two has no length (the second bone's `scale_x` is 0, or `length` is not above 0).
- **Mix** blends each rotation the short way from the value passed in toward the solved one (`apply::shortest`); a mix of 1 is the solved pose. The tests start a bone more than half a turn, as plain numbers go, from where it is solved (`one_bone_points_at_its_target_at_each_mix`: 135 degrees to -135 at half mix is 180, not 0; `a_half_mix_turns_each_bone_half_way_the_short_way`, both bones). A mix above 1 is taken as 1 (`a_mix_above_one_is_taken_as_one`: one bone compressed, two bones stretched and a second bone on the first's origin are the same to the bit at mixes of 1.5, 4, 1e30 and the largest float as at 1; taken as written, 1.5 would mirror the compressed bone).
  - **Limit: a part mix jumps.** At a mix strictly between 0 and 1, a bone jumps by a full turn times the mix when its solved rotation passes half a turn from the rotation passed in: it is turned part of the short way, and there the short way changes sides. At a mix of 1 the pose does not jump (`a_part_mix_jumps_when_the_solved_turn_passes_half_a_turn`: one bone, and the first of two).
- **One bone** points its x axis at the target. The axis is drawn at `rotation + shear_x`, and the other way under a negative `scale_x`, so the solved rotation is the target's angle (turned half round under a negative `scale_x`) less `shear_x`, as a world-space bone override finds it (`one_bone_under_a_negative_scale_or_a_shear_still_points_at_its_target`). Pointing needs no length (`one_bone_points_at_its_target_at_each_mix`).
  - **Stretch and compress.** The bone's reach is `length × |scale_x|`. With `stretch` and a target beyond it, or `compress` and one nearer, `scale_x` is multiplied by `1 + (distance / reach - 1) × mix`, and `scale_y` too with `uniform`: at a mix of 1 the tip is on the target. Stretch never shortens and compress never lengthens (`one_bone_stretches_and_compresses_to_its_target`). With a `max_stretch` the ratio `distance / reach` is taken no higher than it before the mix, so a target beyond it is pointed at and the bone stretched by the limit; the limit is at least 1, so compress is never limited (`stretch_stops_at_its_limit_and_the_limb_points_on`). A bone with no reach (no `length`, or a `scale_x` of 0) cannot be scaled to its target, so its scale is left and it still turns (`a_target_at_the_first_bone_s_origin_changes_nothing_and_makes_no_nan`).
- **Two bones** under an even first bone (An even bone, below) are a triangle of two rigid parts, both measured in the first bone's parent's space, in the pose passed in:
  - **The parts.** Part one runs from the first bone's origin to the second's, as the first bone's matrix places it: the second bone may sit off the first's axis, and part one is then longer than its `x` and at an angle to the first bone's x axis (`two_bones_reach_with_the_second_bone_off_the_first_s_axis`). Part two runs from there to the tip, `length` along the second bone's x axis: its `scale_x` scales it, its `shear_x` turns it, and the first bone's matrix scales it again (`two_bones_reach_a_target_on_each_bend_side`, whose rigs are a second bone scaled and sheared, one with a negative `scale_x`, a first bone scaled by 2, and one turned by equal shears).
  - **The triangle.** With part one `l1` long, part two `l2` and the target `d` away, the joint is `(l1² - l2² + d²) / 2d` along the line to the target and `√(l1² - that²)` off it (the law of cosines, as a length along and a length across; both parts' directions come from that one pair of numbers, so the tip stays on the target when the limb is nearly straight, where an angle from an arc cosine is coarse). Part one is to point at the joint and part two from the joint to the target.
  - **The rotations.** Turning the first bone turns part one by as much, whatever the first bone's scale and shear, so its rotation is the angle part one is to have less the angle part one has under an unturned first bone. The second bone's x axis reaches the first bone's parent through the first bone's linear part as solved, so its rotation points that axis (as one bone's is pointed) along part two's direction brought back through that linear part.
  - **The bend side** is judged in the first bone's parent's space: with `bend` `Positive` part two is turned the positive way from part one (clockwise on screen), and the cross product of the two parts is positive; with `Negative`, negative (`two_bones_reach_a_target_on_each_bend_side`, which checks the joint's place and the cross product). The bones' own scales do not change the side. Every direction is found in the parent's space and only then brought back through the bones' own matrices, so a first bone mirrored by its own scale, `(1, -1)` or `(-1, 1)`, puts the joint where an unmirrored one does (`a_first_bone_mirrored_by_its_own_scale_keeps_the_bend_side`).
  - **Out of reach and too near.** A target beyond `l1 + l2` lays both parts along the line to it. One nearer than `|l1 - l2|` folds part two back along part one: the first part points at the target when it is the longer, and away from it when the second is. The tip does not jump as the target crosses either limit (`a_target_out_of_reach_straightens_the_bones_toward_it`, `a_target_too_near_folds_the_bones`, each on the unit limb, parts of 40 and 30, and on a limb off the axis under a first bone scaled `(2, 2)`, parts of 41.76 and 30).
  - **A walked target.** `the_bend_side_holds_and_nothing_jumps_as_the_target_moves` steps a target across the line the first bone lies along as it starts, round a full circle, and straight out through the fold and the full reach, on both of those limbs, on both bend sides, with and without softness, at a mix of 1. At every step the joint is on the side asked for (never on the other, and well on its own wherever the limb is neither folded nor straight), and the tip moves no farther than its target did.
  - **Softness.** With the full reach `m = l1 + l2` and `s` the softness, `m` at most, a target `d` away with `d > m - s` is reached for as if it were `(m - s) + s × (1 - e^(-(d - (m - s)) / s))` away. The tip follows its target out to `m - s`, at the same slope where the softening begins, then nears `m` and stays short of it, the joint still bent to its side (`softness_slows_the_tip_as_the_bones_straighten`: five distances either side of `m - s`, one far beyond, and a softness above `m`, on the unit limb; and four distances on the limb off the axis under a first bone scaled `(2, 2)`). Limit: far enough past `m - s` an `f32` cannot tell the softened distance from `m`, and the bones are then straight: 20 softnesses past it, the same test finds the tip at the full reach.
  - **Stretch** is one bone's, on the first bone: with `stretch`, a softness of 0 and an even first bone, a target beyond `m` multiplies the first bone's `scale_x` by `1 + (d / m - 1) × mix`, and its `scale_y` too with `uniform`. Both parts are then laid along the line to the target on the first bone as stretched: once only its x scale has grown, it holds a part one off its axis at another angle, and no longer scales every direction alike, so part one's angle and the second bone's rotation are taken from the bone with the scales about to be written. The tip is on the target when the second bone lies on the first's axis, or with `uniform` wherever it lies. Off the axis without `uniform` it is on the line and a little short of the target, the design's recorded limit: with the second bone at `(40, 12)` and a length of 30, a target 120 away is met 3.23 short, under a first bone mirrored by its own scale or turned by equal shears too (`two_bones_stretch_to_a_target_out_of_reach`). `max_stretch` limits two bones the same way: the factor is `min(d / m, max)` before the mix, `uniform` uses the capped factor, and a target beyond the limit leaves the limb stretched by it, straight and pointing at the target (`stretch_stops_at_its_limit_and_the_limb_points_on`; a limit that is not finite changes nothing). Two bones are not compressed: `compress` is one bone's, and a target too near folds them with their scales left (`a_target_too_near_folds_the_bones`).
  - **An even bone** scales every direction alike, to a rounding: `|scale_x|` and `|scale_y|` within a millionth of the larger of each other, and `shear_x` and `shear_y` within 1e-6 rad of each other or of half a turn apart. Only such a first bone takes the triangle above and stretches; any other is solved in its own space (An uneven or sheared first bone, below). Rule we chose: the design says "even and unsheared". Equal shears turn a bone, and shears half a turn apart turn it and flip one axis, as a scale of `(1, -1)` does: neither takes its axes out of square. So a bone with both shears at 0.25, or with a `shear_y` of half a turn (as `f32` rounds it), stretches, and one with only `shear_x` set, or with scales 1 and 1.000002, does not (the same test). The band is a rounding and no wider because near the folded and the straight reach a joint moves as the square root of a distance: a band of 1e-4, the triangle missing by up to part two × 1e-4 inside it, moved the knee 0.66 of a unit as a bone's scale crossed its edge on a limb of parts 40 and 30 (`the_walk_meets_the_closed_form_at_an_even_bone`, below).
  - **A second bone on the first's origin** (part one has no length: validation allows it): nothing the first bone does moves the joint, so the first bone is left exactly as it is and the second is pointed at the target, by the mix (`a_target_at_the_first_bone_s_origin_changes_nothing_and_makes_no_nan`).
- **An uneven or sheared first bone.** When the first bone's own scale is uneven, or its axes are sheared out of square, part two's length in its parent's space changes as the second bone turns, and the triangle would miss. In the first bone's own space both parts are rigid, so the tip's distance from its origin follows one number, the bend: the angle from part one to part two there, 0 straight and half a turn folded (`Bent`, `uneven_rotations`). An uneven or sheared ancestor is not this case: the caller brings the target into the first bone's parent's space, where both solves are exact.
  - **The side.** The cross product of the two parts in the parent's space is the first bone's determinant times their cross product in its own space, so the side asked for is the bends of one sign there, the other under a first bone that mirrors. Each side of a sheared bone has its own reaches (`an_uneven_first_bone_reaches_its_target`, whose first bones include one mirrored by its scale, `(-1.5, 1)`, and which checks the cross product on both sides).
  - **The rule.** The side's farthest bend is found; from it the solve walks toward folded to the first bend at which the distance comes down to the target's, having never been below it on the way, and turns the first bone so the tip is on the target. So every distance the side reaches is reached: the walk goes on past a dip and the rise after it. A target beyond that farthest reach leaves the limb at that bend, pointing at the target; one nearer than the least distance on the walk to folded (the nearest), at that least bend, pointing at it (`an_uneven_first_bone_reaches_its_target`, `a_squashed_and_sheared_thigh_reaches_past_a_dip`: first bones scaled `(0.6, 1)` sheared -0.1 and `(1, 1.8)` sheared -0.2, parts of 40 and 40, whose distance from the farthest bend dips, rises and then falls almost to the origin, meet targets at 0.3 and 0.5 of the farthest reach on both sides; stopping at the dip missed the first by 32.8). The farthest is straight for some bones and not for others: under `(1, 2)` with parts 40 and 30 it is 75.7 away, beyond the straight 70. The nearest is folded for some and not for others: under `(2, 1)` with parts 50 and 100 the distance falls to 81.6 and rises again to the folded 100. Softness measures its reach from the farthest bend (`m` is that distance). Stretch applies only to an even bone, as before.
  - **The walks.** The square of the tip's distance is a constant plus cosines of the bend and of twice the bend, so its third derivative is bounded by those cosines' sizes, and each step goes only as far as the distance is sure to keep rising or falling (the chain's `fall` method, `sure_falling`), at most `BEND_STEP` (a quarter turn). The farthest walk goes from straight to folded, stepping `BEND_SETTLED` (π/65,536) over each turn, and takes the farthest place walked. The second walk goes from there toward folded the same way, over every turn, keeping the least distance it has passed; the first step that ends at or below the target's distance holds the crossing, and is halved 30 times; with none before folded, the least bend is given. Both are worked in `f64`, at most `BEND_WALK` (96) steps each, deterministic and with no allocation.
  - **It reaches.** `an_uneven_first_bone_reaches_its_target`: first bones scaled `(1.2, 1)`, `(1.5, 1)`, `(2, 1)`, `(1, 1.5)` and `(-1.5, 1)` and sheared by 0.3 and 0.6 either way; the second bone on the first's axis and off it at `(40, 12)`, length 30, started two ways; both sides; four directions; every twentieth of the way between the side's nearest and farthest, found by sampling the bend 8,192 times (not by the solve): the tip is on the target to 0.01 (7.6e-5 at worst), bent to its side, rotations alone changed; at 1.2 times the farthest it is on the line at the farthest reach, and at half the nearest on the line at the nearest.
  - **No jump.** `an_uneven_first_bone_reaches_without_a_jump_as_its_target_moves`: under `(1, 2)` (40, 30), `(2, 1)` (50, 100) and shears of 0.3 and 0.6 either way, both sides, the target walked in steps of a thousandth of the farthest reach along two lines from 1.2 times it to inside the nearest, one across, and circles beyond the farthest, at it, at the straight distance, between the reaches, at the nearest and inside it: the chain tests' halving rule finds no joint jumping 1% of the farthest reach (0.0012 the largest, the square root at the folded reach), and the tip is on every target it can reach. The walk starts from the same farthest bend whatever the target, so the bend is a continuous function of the target's distance wherever the distance falls all the way from that bend to the nearest, as on these rigs; the limit below is where it does not.
  - **The switch.** An even bone keeps the triangle to the bit (`an_even_first_bone_is_unchanged`: 20,000 random even limbs, mirrored, turned by equal shears, flipped by shears half a turn apart and within the rounding band, the second bone off the first's axis, scaled and sheared, with random targets, mixes, softness, stretch, its limit and `uniform`, against the solve as it stood, kept in the test; 400,000 were checked once). At an even bone the walk meets the triangle (`the_walk_meets_the_closed_form_at_an_even_bone`: over random even limbs, targets from a tenth of the reach to 99.5% and from 100.5% to 140%, joints within a ten-thousandth of the reach), so across the band's edge the limb moves at most 0.003 (the triangle's rounding near straight) toward targets off the two reaches, and never farther than the two methods differ on one even bone; exactly at a reach, where a joint moves as a square root, those roundings alone set the joints 0.067 apart.
  - **The knee follows its last bend (in a skeleton).** The rule above is the fresh rule: `two_bones` takes it, with no state. - **A chain** (`chain(bones, length, target, mix, side, joints)`) turns two or more bones, each the child of the one before, so that the last one's tip reaches for a target. Validation gives it three to sixteen; two bones go to `two_bones`. It is the engine's own. The chain is given one shape from a family of shapes that runs through its animated pose, the one whose tip is as far from the first joint as the target is, and is then turned about its first joint onto the target. So the shape follows the target's distance alone and the target's direction only turns the chain: a target that moves does not make the chain pop, within the limits below (it settles steeply into its nearest shape, it flips when the way its pose closes changes, and a pose wound past half a turn may jump as its target moves out). Its tests find every position by composing the bones' own transforms (`chain_joints`), never from the solve's scratch.
  - **What a caller does.** Before, as for the other solves: the target in the space of the first bone's parent, `bones` first to last in their own values as animation and the game's overrides left them (each inherits normally from the one before, which validation holds), the last bone's data `length`, the constraint's `mix`, the side its `bend` keeps (`IkBend::side`: 1 for `Positive`, -1 for `Negative`, none for `Pose`, whose family is the one below), and `joints`, a `Vec<Vec2>` it keeps for this, made with `chain_room` entries for its longest chain. After: it places the bones, and everything below them, again. What `joints` holds afterwards is the solve's own, and not for reading.
  - **A chain kept to a side** (`bend` `Positive` or `Negative`) is solved from straight, not from its pose: its family is the chain laid straight and curled as an even arc, every joint turned as much as the others the side's way, further the nearer the target, and laid straight toward a target at least its length away; that shape is then turned onto the target as the pose's is. The walk is the closing walk below (`fall`, with its step bound, from straight, the side's way, as far as a joint folded half a turn), the one a pose with no bend of its own takes, so the shape is a continuous function of the target's distance, the same to the bit every run, and no turn of the tip's distance hides inside a step.   - **The family** (`a_chain_starts_from_the_animated_pose`, on a pose turned 0.5, -0.3 and 0.8 rad at its joints):
    - **Opened.** A target farther than the pose's tip opens the pose: every turn is the pose's times one factor, from 1 toward 0, less the farther the target. The pose keeps its shape, scaled.
    - **Closed.** A nearer target closes it: one even turn is added to every joint, the way that brings the tip nearer (for a pose bent one way, the way it bends; for an S, whichever way that is), and more the nearer the target. The chain curls further as an arc, and a joint bent against the rest is carried with them.
    - **As far as its tip** the shape is the pose's own, turned onto the target.
    - **The pose decides the side.** A chain bent the positive way and its mirror image, sent to one target on the line they mirror in, keep their bends to their own sides and stay mirror images.
  - **Out of reach.** A target at least the chain's length from the first joint lays every part along the line to it, which is where opening ends (`a_chain_out_of_reach_lies_straight_toward_its_target`: from the length itself to ten thousand units away, and a straight chain, which is turned and not bent).
  - **A pose with no bend of its own** has no way to bend, and is closed the positive way, as an even arc from straight: every joint turned as much as the others (`a_straight_chain_bends_into_an_even_arc_the_positive_way`: chains of 4 and 16 parts, targets along their line, behind their first joint and off their line; four parts of 25 to a target 75 away turn 0.656 rad a joint). Rules we chose:
    - A pose is straight when every joint is within `CHAIN_NEAR ×` (0.001 of) its length of one line through the first joint, the line to whichever joint is farthest from it. The target has no part in it, so a target that leaves a straight chain's line does not change which rule applies: the test moves one 0.09 and 0.11 off the line of a chain 100 long, and the chain moves less than 0.2.
    - The arc bends the positive way (clockwise on screen, as two bones bend unless told otherwise). A chain with no bend of its own has no side, and this is the engine's choice for it.
    - A pose within that tolerance of a line counts as having no bend, whichever way it leans: the test's leans the negative way by 0.0001 rad a joint, and is bent the positive way. One bent 0.01 rad a joint the negative way has a bend of its own, and closes the negative way, evenly.
  - **Finding the shape.** The family is walked from the pose in steps, for the first step in which the tip's distance from the first joint crosses the target's, and that step is halved `CHAIN_HALVINGS` (24) times. A step changes the chain's swing (how far apart its parts point) by `CHAIN_STEP` (an eighth of half a turn) at most, and a walk is `CHAIN_WALK` (96) steps at most. Three rules make it one continuous family:
    - **The closing walk stops at the first turn of the tip's distance**, to within `1 / CHAIN_SETTLED` of its most step (or a little before, where the distance has all but stopped falling), or where a joint has folded half a turn, and a nearer target gets that nearest shape. Or it runs out of steps first: a walk is `CHAIN_WALK` (96) steps at most, and a pose whose sure stretches stay small for that long stops short of its first turn. It is rare: 11 of 150,000 random poses of sixteen bones (turns up to 1 rad) did, the worst losing reach of 0.026 of the length near its first joint: its nearest shape is that much farther out than its first turn. Where it stops is still the pose's alone, whatever the target, so the shape stays continuous (`ik::a_chain_whose_closing_walk_runs_out_of_steps_stops_short_of_its_first_turn`: the pose of sixteen bones that lost the most reach of 150,000, whose first turn is 0.0058 of the length from the first joint and whose nearest shape is 0.0323).     - **No turn can hide inside a closing step.** Along the closing family the square of the tip's distance is a sum of cosines of the added turn `c`, `Σ lᵢ lⱼ cos(aᵢ - aⱼ + (kᵢ - kⱼ) c)` over every two parts (`l` a part's length, `a` its angle in the pose, `k` the count of joints before it), so its third derivative is at most `Σ lᵢ lⱼ |kᵢ - kⱼ|³`, from the parts' lengths alone. At each place the walk takes the slope of that square and the slope's rate, and steps only as far as the slope is sure to stay below 0 under that bound (the first root of `slope + rate × t + bound × t² / 2`, by Taylor's theorem), and never more than the most step. So the distance falls across every step, a crossing in a step is the only one there, and halving finds it. Near a turn the sure stretch shrinks, quickly at an ordinary turn and by about half a step a step at a flat one, and the walk stops once it is under `1 / CHAIN_SETTLED` (1/4096) of the most step: within that of the first turn, or where the distance has all but stopped falling. A nearest shape near the first joint, `m` from it, turns the chain about the first joint by about `√(2δ / m)` rad over the target's last `δ`, which is the limit of a target passing over the first joint, approached. A turn that only just happens, a shallow dip in the tip's distance, has the shape cross most of the dip over a small way of the target. Walked in steps of a millionth of the length, a joint moves up to 1.2% of the length there: over the random poses above, 10 to 25 of each 4,000 move more than 0.5%, every one at a nearest shape within 0.054 of the length of the first joint and all but one within 0.013; of 400 poses drawn about the two shallow dips above one moves 1.1%, and 0.3% at a step of a ten millionth. Pinned on the steepest of those random poses (`a_chain_settles_steeply_into_its_nearest_shape`: parts of 11.90, 15.63 and 24.58 turned 0.782 and -0.976, nearest shape 0.00316 of the length out, walked in along sixteen lines to 0.002 of the length by the not-pop test's measure): a joint moves at most 0.0116 of the length in steps of a thousandth and 0.0015 in steps of a ten thousandth, held under 1.5% at both.
  - **Full reach is continuous** (`a_chain_does_not_jump_as_its_target_crosses_its_full_reach`). As a target nears the chain's length the factor goes to 0 and the chain to straight, which is how it is laid beyond that. A target walked out through the limit in steps of a hundredth, a thousandth and a ten thousandth of the length moves the tip exactly as far as itself (pinned under 1.05 times), and no joint more than 0.8 of the square root of the step times the length (0.63 measured): a joint's sideways place goes as the square root of how far inside the limit the target is, as the joint of two bones does.
  - **Rotations from joints.** Each part has turned from where it stood, and turning a bone turns its part and every part after it. So a bone is turned by what its part turned more than the part before it, and the first bone by what its part turned. Under a bone mirrored by its own scale (the determinant of its linear part is negative) the bones after it turn the other way for the same turn of their parts, so that difference is negated for a bone with an odd count of mirrored bones before it in the chain. `a_mirrored_bone_in_a_chain_passes_its_turn_on_reversed` solves a chain and four twins with the same joints (a bone mirrored by `scale_y`, by `scale_x`, the first bone mirrored, and two bones): each twin's joints come to where the plain chain's do, closed, opened and out of reach.
  - **Only rotations are written**, each turned the short way by the mix from the value passed in. `a_chain_changes_only_rotations` compares every other value of every bone to the bit, on a chain whose bones sit off their parents' axes and are scaled, mirrored and turned by shears; most of the chain tests check the same of their own solves (`assert_chain_only_turned`). A target on the pose's own tip asks for the pose, and turns no bone by more than a rounding. `a_chain_at_half_mix_turns_each_bone_half_way` sends a chain to a target behind it, where one bone's turn is more than half a turn as plain numbers: it goes the short way, and half and a quarter of the way at those mixes. A mix of 0 or less changes nothing, and one above 1 is taken as 1, to the bit.
  - **Nothing is written** (`a_chain_with_a_part_of_no_length_or_a_value_not_finite_makes_no_nan`, but for the second case):
    - with fewer than two bones, or a chain of no length (every joint on one spot);
    - when the target is on the first joint, within 1e-6 of a unit: there is no way to turn toward it, as for one bone and two (`a_target_passing_over_a_chain_s_first_joint_turns_it_half_round`);
    - when a value given is not finite (a bone's, the target, the length or the mix), or one that would be written. The joints and the chain's length are checked once they are found, and every rotation before any is written. Either of those two checks alone stops every case the test has, and the test fails without both: it sets each value of three bones, the target, the length and the mix, alone and in pairs, to fourteen extremes.
  - **A part of no length** (within 1e-6 of a unit: a bone on its parent's origin, or a last bone with no x scale or no `length`) points nowhere. Its joint stays with the one before it and its bone is not turned; the bone after it carries the turn (the same test).
  - **A hitch where opening hands over to closing.** At the pose's own tip distance the shape is the pose on either side, so nothing jumps, but the joints' direction of motion turns there: by under 2° on the fourteen chains bent one way, 57° to 71° on those in an S (moving 0.11 to 0.20 as fast on the closing side), and over random poses by 15° to 29° at the median and up to 160°. A target passing through that distance shows a hitch, not a pop. Not pinned by a test.
  - **The same result every run.** The solve reads only what it is given, in a fixed order with fixed counts of steps and no tolerance in its search. A second run, in the first run's scratch or in one another chain's solve left, gives every bone the same bits (`a_chain_gives_the_same_result_every_run`, with one case for each way through the solve).
  - **Cost.** A solve walks the family (one pass over the parts each) 32 to 34 times at the median and 40 to 48 for one target in a hundred inside the length, on random poses of 3 to 16 bones; the most found over 400,000 solves of 3 to 16 bones, poses turned up to 3 rad a joint, is 99. The bound is 124 for a closing or straight solve (`CHAIN_WALK` steps, `CHAIN_HALVINGS` halvings and four more) and 219 for an opening one (two walks). Sixteen bones take 2.3 µs a solve at the median and three bones 0.7 µs (random poses turned up to 0.6 rad a joint, targets out to 1.1 of the length, release build, the median of three runs, load 6.5), against 1.9 and 0.65 µs for the walk with steps of one size; the bound is about seven times the median.
  - **The scratch.** `chain_room(bones)` is how many entries one solve fills, three a bone and one more: the joints, each part as it stood, and each part's length beside an angle. `chain` fills `joints` to that, and the constraint pass makes its scratch with that room for a skeleton's longest chain. A second run with as many bones, or with fewer, takes no more room and gives none back, and a scratch made with `chain_room` entries does not grow on its first solve (`a_chain_takes_no_more_room_on_a_second_run`: the list's pointer and capacity).
  - **Limit, pinned: a chain curls and does not fold.** Closing adds an even turn at every joint, so a chain reaches inward only as far as an arc of its parts curls: a target nearer than its nearest shape is not reached, and the chain takes that shape with its tip on the line to the target (`a_chain_curls_and_does_not_fold_onto_a_near_target`). Equal parts bent evenly, or in a symmetric S, curl onto their first joint; equal parts with turns at random do not, stopping 0.10 to 0.12 of the length from it at the median and up to 0.52 (2,000 poses each of 3, 4, 8 and 16 parts, turns up to 1 rad). Unequal parts stop short of it: parts of 30, 20 and 10 at 0.27 of their length, 30, 25, 25 and 20 at 0.07, 10, 30, 15, 25 and 20 at 0.006. Over random poses (parts of 5 to 30, turns up to 1 rad either way) the nearest shape is 0.11 to 0.14 of the length from the first joint at the median and 0.28 to 0.34 for one pose in ten; for gentler poses 0.05 to 0.11 and 0.12 to 0.29. So of targets drawn at random from 5% to 30% of the length (among those the chain's parts could reach at all), 61% to 91% are met, by pose and by count of bones; of those from 30% to 50%, 95% to 100%; and from half the length out all but one in a thousand. With the target's distance itself drawn evenly over 5% to 30%, so that as many targets lie near the first joint as far from it, 48% to 70% are met. A rig that must touch a point near its root is not a chain's work.
  - **Limit, pinned: a chain changes sides when the way its pose closes changes.** This is the limit of a chain bent the pose's way (`Pose`, a chain's default); a chain kept to a side does not change sides (A chain kept to a side, above). A chain closes the way that brings its tip nearer. For a pose bent one way that is the way it bends; for an S, bent both ways at once, "the way the pose bends" is not defined, and of random poses with turns of both signs (4, 8 and 16 bones, turns up to 0.6 rad a joint, tip beyond 0.6 of the length) 10% to 12% close against the sign of their total turn. Whenever the animation carries the closing way through a change of sign while the target is nearer than the pose's tip, the chain flips to the other side of the line to its target:
    - **Swinging through straight.** Poses bent 0.01 rad a joint either way close to opposite sides, mirror images: with the target at half the chain's length a joint is 0.70 of the length from where its twin is; at 90% of the length 0.38; at 99.5%, 0.09 (`a_chain_changes_sides_when_its_pose_swings_through_straight`).
    - **An S-shaped sway**, a wave travelling down the chain (the usual tail animation), never passes through straight, yet with its target held still it flips twice a cycle: four parts of 25 swayed 0.4 rad a joint jump 0.72 of the length with the target at half the length and 0.35 at 90%; sixteen parts of 10 swayed 0.2 rad, 0.66 and 0.23 (`an_s_shaped_sway_changes_sides_when_its_target_is_nearer_than_its_tip`, a cycle in 2,000 frames, each jump pinned within 0.03, the pose never bent less than 0.19 rad at its most bent joint).
    - A target farther than the pose's tip only opens the pose, and does not flip it.
    - What an author does: a chain that is to reach inward is kept to a side (`bend: Positive` or `Negative`), or kept bent one way throughout its animation, as a limb is given a bend direction. An S-shaped sway does not count as bent one way. So a chain is fit for a rope or a cable, or a tail or a tentacle held in a one-sided curve, reaching for a target from about its nearest shape out; it is not fit for a side-to-side or S-wave sway while its target is nearer than its tip.
  - **Limit, pinned: a pose wound past half a turn may jump as its target moves out.** A pose opens toward straight to reach farther, and along opening the square of the tip's distance changes with the factor as `-Σ lᵢ lⱼ Δᵢⱼ sin Δᵢⱼ` over every two parts, `Δ` how far apart they point in the pose. So no pose whose parts point half a turn or less apart can jump: there the tip's distance falls as the factor grows, for any pose. One wound further can have its tip come in as it opens before it goes out, so the walk bends it further while that takes the tip out, and only opens it for a target farther than that brings: there a joint jumps (`a_wound_up_pose_may_jump_as_its_target_moves_out`: sixteen parts of 10 turned 0.45 rad a joint, wound 6.75 rad, jump half the chain's length as their target passes 35.2 of their 160). With uneven parts a pose just past half a turn can jump: three bones of 55.1, 5.2 and 47.4 turned -1.58 and -1.70 (their parts 3.28 rad apart) jump 0.27 of the length as their target passes 0.90 of it, and of poses with parts of 2 to 60 and turns up to 2.2 rad a joint wound past half a turn, 16% of three bones and 19% of four jump. Over random poses with parts of 5 to 30, walked out from the tip, 6,000 of each: 3.2% of sixteen bones turned up to 1 rad a joint, 3 of eight bones turned so, and none of three or four bones turned up to 1 or 1.5 rad or of sixteen turned up to 0.6; each of those that jumped was wound more than 4.2 rad.
  - **Limit, pinned: a target passing over the first joint turns the chain half round.** On the first joint the target gives no way to turn, and nothing is written, as for one bone and two. Either side of it the chain has one shape, the nearest its pose comes, pointed at the target: so pointed opposite ways (`a_target_passing_over_a_chain_s_first_joint_turns_it_half_round`). A target that misses the first joint by a thousandth of the chain's length turns the chain round it quickly and without a jump.
  - **Limit: uneven and sheared bones.** A chain is exact while every bone but the last scales every direction alike. Under a bone with an uneven scale or a shear the parts after it change length as they turn, and the tip may miss. Such chains, and flat ones, give finite values in their rotations alone (`a_chain_with_a_part_of_no_length_or_a_value_not_finite_makes_no_nan`). No figure is pinned for the miss.
  - Not taken: reaching for the target from the animated pose afresh at each step, in sweeps back from the tip and out from the first joint, with a closing step where the sweeps stopped short. It met nearer targets (99.5% of those a chain's parts allow), but it can land in either of two shapes: on the 168 strokes above a joint jumped more than 1% of the length 91 times, on 53 of the strokes, by up to 0.95 of the length, where the target crossed the chain's own line, passed behind the first joint or passed over a joint of the pose.
  - Not taken: closing a pose by scaling its turns, as opening does. It does not pop either, but a pose all but straight with one joint bent more than the rest folds at that joint (1.8 rad there and 0.09 at the others, for a target at half the length, where the even turn gives 0.49 and 0.47), and it reaches inward less (29% to 53% of targets within 30% of the length).

## Transform constraints (`transform_constraint.rs`)

`in_the_world` and `in_its_own_values` move one bone toward a target bone, each property by its own mix: the first on transforms in the skeleton's space (the world modes), the second on bones' own values (the local modes). They are pure, as the IK solves are: the constraint pass reads the target, takes the bones in order and places them again (Constraints in a step, under In the world). Their rules are worked out from the design and from the matrix of a bone (x axis `scale_x × (cos(rotation + shear_x), sin(rotation + shear_x))`, y axis `scale_y × (-sin(rotation + shear_y), cos(rotation + shear_y))`). The tests named here are `transform_constraint::`'s own; their expected values are worked out from the matrices and the rules, and compared with what is read back from the result's matrix.

- **Six mixes and six offsets** (`rotate`, `x`, `y`, `scale_x`, `scale_y`, `shear_y`). A mix of 0 leaves its property as it was to the bit; one alone at 1 moves its property to the target's plus the offset and leaves the other five (`in_the_world_copies_each_property_by_its_own_mix`, `in_the_world_relative_..`, `in_its_own_values_..`, `in_its_own_values_relative_..`: in a local mode the other five and the x shear to the bit; in a world mode the place to the bit, the parts of the matrix the property does not touch to the bit, and what is read from an axis that was rescaled or turned to 1e-5, since a rescaled axis's angle is read again through rounding). A mix may be below 0 or above 1: at -1, 0.5 and 2 each property goes where its rule says, in all four modes (`mixes_of_minus_one_a_half_and_two_go_where_the_rule_says`).
- **How a world mode reads a transform.** Rotation is the way the x axis points; each scale is an axis's length; shear is how far the y axis is from square to the x axis, on the transform's own side (the side its determinant's sign says), read by the arctangent of the axes' cross and dot products (an arccosine loses its precision near 0 and half a turn).
- **World, toward the target** (the default): the place moves toward the point `(offset.x, offset.y)` in the target's space; the bone is turned, both axes together, the short way toward the target's x axis's angle plus `offset.rotation` (`a_world_half_mix_jumps_when_its_target_passes_half_a_turn`, whose first part goes 1.783 the short way where the plain difference is 4.5 the other); each axis is rescaled toward the target's length plus the offset; the y axis alone is turned to the shear the rule gives, keeping its length.
- **World, relative:** the offset's point in the target's space times the mix is added to the place; the bone is turned by the target's angle plus the offset, times the mix; each length is multiplied by `1 + (target's - 1 + offset) × mix`; the shear adds the target's plus the offset, times the mix.
- **Local, toward the target:** `value += (target's own + offset - value) × mix` for rotation, x, y, both scales and the y shear. **Local, relative:** rotation, x, y and the y shear add `(target's own + offset) × mix`; each scale is multiplied by `1 + (target's own - 1 + offset) × mix`. The x shear is never touched. A local mode reads the target's own values, so a target that an earlier world-mode constraint holds is copied as its own values stand (as animated, overridden or set by an earlier IK or local constraint), not where the world mode holds it: a world result is not in own values (`constrain::an_own_values_mode_copies_the_target_s_own_values_not_where_a_world_mode_holds_it`).
- **A local rotation is the plain number**, never taken the short way: an own rotation is what keys set and may pass a full turn. So a gear at half the speed of another, a local relative rotate mix of 0.5, turns smoothly through its target's full turn (`an_own_values_relative_half_mix_turns_smoothly_through_its_target_s_full_turn`: the target's own rotation from 0 to a full turn in 64 steps, no step of the gear more than π/32 + 1e-4; the copy test's target is turned 7.5, more than a turn, and is copied as 7.75).
  - **Limit, pinned: a world mode with a mix that is not a whole number jumps when its target passes half a turn** (`a_world_half_mix_jumps_when_its_target_passes_half_a_turn`): the way an axis points is only known within a turn, so the short way changes sides there. A bone at 0 turned half way toward a target going round a full turn in 64 steps moves half the target's step each step but one, where it jumps half a turn.
- **Mirroring is the bone's own** (`a_mirrored_bone_stays_mirrored_and_an_unmirrored_one_unmirrored`). A length is never below 0, and a shear is set on the bone's own side and kept within 0.001 rad of a quarter turn either way (`SHEAR_ROOM`), so a world mode never turns an axis round: a mirrored bone stays mirrored and an unmirrored one unmirrored, toward a mirrored target or not, in both world modes. Pushed past what it can be, a length is 0 (the bone is flat) and a shear stops 0.001 short of a quarter turn. A mirrored bone's own-side shear is the negative of the `shear_y` it was built with (its y axis is turned round). A target flipped by a negative `scale_x` reads in the world modes as turned half a turn and mirrored: its matrix is the same as one turned π more with its y axis turned round, so a bone copying its rotation points the way the target's x axis now points, and stays unmirrored (`a_target_flipped_by_its_x_scale_reads_in_the_world_as_turned_half_a_turn`).
- **A mirrored target mirrors the rotation and shear offsets** (`a_mirrored_target_mirrors_the_rotation_and_shear_offsets`): under a target whose determinant is negative, `offset.rotation` and `offset.shear_y` change sign in the world modes. The place offset is a point in the target's space, mirrored with it.
- **A flat bone or target changes nothing it cannot, and makes no NaN** (`a_flat_bone_or_target_changes_nothing_it_cannot_and_makes_no_nan`). An axis shorter than 1e-6 on the bone or the target leaves rotation, scale and shear alone in the world modes; the place still moves. Any input that is not finite (the bone, the target, a mix, an offset), or a result that is not (3e38 added to 3e38), leaves the bone as it was, to the bit, in every mode.

## Curves, applying an animation and events (`curve.rs`, `apply.rs`)

- `Curve::value(t)` eases a fraction of the span between two keys. The ends are exact (0 gives 0, 1 gives 1), so every key's value is hit exactly. Stepped gives 0 until 1. A bezier is solved for the parameter whose x is `t` (Newton's method kept inside a shrinking bracket, bisecting where a step would leave it or the slope is flat; it stops at an x error under 2e-7 or a bracket under 1e-7 wide), then its y is taken; the x controls lie in 0..=1, so x is monotonic and the answer unique.
- `apply(animation, data, pose, time, alpha, blend, direction)` blends one animation into the pose. `time` is the animation's own time; the caller wraps a looping track. Rules we chose where Spine's docs are silent or where we keep only part of Spine's model:
  - Before a timeline's first key it leaves the pose alone, whatever the blend; after its last key the last value holds. Between keys the value is the first key's plus the change times the first key's curve value.
  - Bone keys are relative to the setup pose: rotate, translate and shear add to the setup value, scale multiplies it. Call that the target.
  - Replace moves each bone property toward the target by alpha (`value += (target - value) * alpha`). Rotation goes the short way round: the difference is wrapped to -180..=180 degrees first. Keys themselves interpolate as written, so keys 0 and 360 make a full turn; the wrap applies only to blending toward the pose. Shear blends without wrapping.
  - Add adds the target's change from setup times alpha (`value += (target - setup) * alpha`): the key for rotate, translate and shear, and `setup * (key - 1)` for scale.
  - Colour keys are absolute. Components are interpolated in float, in sRGB (not linear light), and both blends move the slot's colour toward the key by alpha: colours do not add. Nothing is clamped or rounded in the pose (a bezier can overshoot); drawing clamps.
  - Attachment and draw-order keys are stepped and apply only when `direction` is `In`, whatever the alpha or blend: a step cannot be part-applied. The animation state passes `In` to at most one entry of a track, the one that owns the stepped keys (see "Stepped keys" below), and `Out` to the rest. A `None` attachment key hides the slot; a `None` draw-order key restores the setup order. Bone and colour timelines ignore the direction.
  - Deform keys are offsets from a mesh's setup shape, and are not applied where the other timelines are: what a slot shows is not settled until every track has applied and the game's attachments are written over them. `apply_layer` notes each deform timeline it meets, with its time, its alpha (the entry's times the timeline's fade) and its blend, as a `DeformStep` in the order applied, and `apply_deform` blends one into its slot's offsets later (The animation state, Deform keys). One at no alpha is left out: it moves nothing, makes no list in a pose nothing has moved, and is not told of if it does not fit. Replace moves each offset toward the key's by alpha (`offset += (key - offset) × alpha`); add adds the key's times alpha. Between two keys the key is the first key's offsets plus the change times the first key's curve value, and after the last key its offsets hold. An empty key is offsets of 0, not no key: replace moves what a lower layer left toward the setup shape, and add adds nothing. A slot's list stays empty while nothing has moved it (`a_deform_key_moves_its_vertices_by_alpha`, `an_empty_key_is_the_setup_shape`, `an_add_blend_adds_a_deform_key_times_its_alpha`; for a layer at no alpha, `state::a_deform_that_does_not_fit_is_skipped_with_one_warning`). The plain `apply` applies its deform keys at once, to the default skin's meshes.
  - Constraint keys (`Timeline::Ik`, `Timeline::TransformMix`, `Timeline::Spring`) write the constraint's values in `Pose::constraints`. The numbers (an IK key's `mix` and `softness`, a transform key's six mixes, a spring key's `mix`, `wind` and `gravity`) ease between keys by the first key's curve and blend as a bone's values do: replace moves each toward the key by alpha from what the layers below left, add adds the key's difference from the constraint's setup times alpha (`an_ik_key_sets_its_constraint_s_values_by_alpha`, `a_transform_key_sets_its_mixes_by_alpha`, `an_add_layer_adds_a_constraint_s_difference_from_setup`; `a_spring_key_sets_its_values_by_curve_and_alpha`: half way through a bezier key, at half alpha, a stepped key held, and added). An IK key's switches (`bend`, `compress`, `stretch`) are stepped: the key at or before the time gives them, whatever the curve, and they apply only when `direction` is `In`, as attachment keys do, whatever the alpha or blend (the same IK test, at a key's time and just before it, and mixing out). Nothing is clamped here: the animation state clamps after every track (Constraint keys, under The animation state).
  - Event timelines do nothing in `apply`.
- `MixBlend` (`Replace`, `Add`) is public, for tracks. `MixDirection` (`In`, `Out`) is crate-internal.
- `events_between(animation, last, now, looping, out)` pushes the event keys in (`last`, `now`] onto `out`, in time order, as (unwrapped time, event index, values). Times are the track's unwrapped time: a looping animation's keys repeat every duration from 0, so a span over several loops fires each loop's keys once, and contiguous spans never fire a key twice. Rules we chose:
  - The first step passes a negative `last`, so a key at 0 fires on it, and on each later loop (at 1 x duration, 2 x duration, ...).
  - Not looping, only the animation's own keys fire. A zero-length looping animation fires its keys once, as if not looping.
  - Nothing fires when `now <= last` (or either is NaN). Reverse playback is the animation state's to mirror into forward times.
  - A loop boundary is never missed: dividing a time by the duration can round across a boundary in f32 (2.7 / 0.9 is just under 3), so the search visits one loop more on each side than division gives, and the (last, now] test drops the extras. Each key's unwrapped time (`loop * duration + key`) is computed the same way every step, so contiguous steps fire it exactly once. A key at the duration and the next loop's key at 0 can come out one ulp apart in either order; a stable insertion pass keeps the output in time order without allocating.
  - One span fires the keys of at most the last 1,000 loops (`MAX_LOOPS`), those ending at the one holding `now` (1,001 when `now` sits on a boundary that division rounds under); the skipped loops' keys are not fired. The loop over them is also bounded by its length (`take(MAX_LOOPS + 2)`), so a mistake in the arithmetic fails a test and cannot run on or grow memory.
- `apply_layer` is `apply` with a fade per timeline (a factor on the alpha) and a turn memory per rotate timeline: given one, a Replace rotation blends by the turn nearest the one it took last step (the short way on the first), so a turn that would cross half a circle mid-mix keeps going the way it started. Without one (`apply`, and an entry alone on its track at full strength) it blends the short way each step.
- `events_crossed` is `events_between` playing backward when asked: a key at `k` is crossed `duration - k` into each loop. Each crossed key carries its own time in the animation too. Float-to-int casts and the loop sums saturate, so an infinite or huge `now` neither overflows nor runs past the 1,000-loop cap (`an_infinite_or_huge_span_neither_overflows_nor_runs_long`).
- The animation state calls `apply_layer` and `events_crossed`. `apply` and `events_between` are their plain forms (no fades, no turn memory, forward only), compiled for tests only: the tests of the rules above go through them.

## The animation state (`state.rs`)

`SkeletonAnimation` (a component) holds tracks numbered 0 to 63 (`MAX_TRACKS`; a higher number warns once per skeleton and does nothing: `a_track_past_the_last_warns_once_and_does_nothing`). A track is a chain, the current entry last after the entries it is mixing from (oldest first, at most 16 in all), and a queue. `TrackEntryId` finds an entry (playing, mixing out, queued or kept) until its `Dispose`. Each state numbers its own entries from 0, so a handle is unique within its `SkeletonAnimation` only, and a game matching `SkeletonTrackEvent`s compares the `entity` with the `entry` (`an_entry_s_handle_is_unique_within_its_own_state_only`). Rules, each pinned by the test named:

- **Each step** (`advance_and_apply`, crate-internal, called by the skeleton step): take up the data (below), advance every track, start the queued entries that are due, push the event keys and track events, end finished mixes, then reset the pose to the setup pose and apply the tracks from the lowest up. The reset makes every step a pure function of the tracks: nothing an animation set last step lingers when no entry keys it now (`attachments_belong_to_the_entry_mixing_in`). So whatever else writes the pose (a game's attachment or bone override) must write it after this call, every step.
- **Time** (`an_entry_s_speed_scales_its_time_but_not_its_mix`, `zero_length_animations_and_zero_mixes_are_finite`): an entry's track time begins at its start (0 unless set: Start, below) and advances by `dt × time_scale × the entry's speed`. A negative or non-finite `dt`, `time_scale` or speed counts as 0, and track times stay finite (an overflow holds at the largest float), so no non-finite time reaches `apply_layer` or `events_crossed`. `is_complete` is true once a started entry's track time has reached its duration; an entry that has not started (queued, or kept until the data lands) is not complete. It reads the time alone, so it also turns true for an entry that passes its end while mixing out, which sends no `Complete`.
- **Limit of track time** (`a_looping_entry_s_time_steps_coarser_after_days`): it is an `f32` that counts every loop, so a looping entry left playing for days moves in coarser steps: 2 ms after 4.6 hours (2^14 s), 8 ms after 18 hours (2^16 s), and 31 ms after 3 days (2^18 s), where a 60 Hz step rounds up to one and the animation runs near twice as fast. After 6 days (2^19 s) a 60 Hz step no longer moves it and the entry stops. Setting the animation again starts its time from 0.
- **Layers:** track 0 applies over the setup pose; a higher track over the tracks below, by its alpha (clamped to 0..=1, NaN is 0) and blend. An `Add` entry adds its change from setup times its alpha (`a_higher_track_at_half_alpha_blends_half`, `an_additive_track_adds_over_track_zero`).
- **Starting:** `set_animation` makes the entry current at once, disposes the queue (`set_animation_and_clear_drop_what_is_queued`), and the old current mixes out over the new entry's `mix_duration`: the data's mix for the pair, else its default mix (`an_entry_mixes_in_over_the_data_s_mix_for_its_pair`); the field can be changed until the mix ends. The first entry on an empty track has nothing to mix from and shows at once at its alpha (`a_layer_fades_in_after_set_empty`). An entry replaced before it was ever applied (two calls in one frame) is dropped, not mixed from: it gets its `Interrupt`, `End` and `Dispose`, and the new entry mixes from the one before it. The empty animation is the exception: it stays, so `set_empty` and `set_animation` in one frame mix the new entry in from nothing (`an_entry_replaced_before_it_shows_is_dropped`).
- **Chain length** (`a_track_mixes_from_at_most_fifteen_entries`): a chain holds at most 16 entries (`MAX_CHAIN`): the current one and the fifteen it is mixing from. When a seventeenth joins, the oldest ends (`End`, `Dispose`), whatever weight it has left: at once when a call adds it, and at the end of the step when a queued entry that starts in the step does, so the oldest still fires the event keys it crossed in that step (`an_entry_the_cap_ends_in_a_step_still_fires_that_step_s_keys`). Why 16: a chain fills only while new entries join faster than mixes finish, and the worst ordinary case is a state machine that flickers, setting a new animation every step. Measured at 60 Hz (`the_entry_the_cap_ends_has_little_weight_left`), the entry the cap then ends weighs 0.7% under a 0.5 s mix and less under a shorter one (a mix shorter than 0.25 s is over before the chain fills), so nothing shows; under a 1 s mix it weighs about 11%. A cap of 8 would end an entry weighing 36% under a 0.5 s mix. The cap also bounds a step's work, and a chain whose mixes cannot finish. That last case is a skeleton whose clock is stopped: there the oldest entry has all the weight, and the pose jumps to the next oldest.
- **Mixing** (`a_crossfade_is_half_way_at_half_its_mix`, `a_crossfade_does_not_dip_toward_what_is_under_it`): the mix fraction `m` is the incoming entry's mix time over its mix duration, its mix time counting skeleton time (`dt × time_scale`, not its own speed). The incoming entry applies at `alpha × m`. Rule we chose, from first principles: each entry mixing out applies, per timeline, at the alpha that makes its weight in the result fall from its own to 0 linearly with `m`, whatever the incoming entry keys. When the incoming entry (Replace) keys all the timeline's properties at applied alpha `y`, the outgoing timeline applies at `alpha × (1 - m) / (1 - y)`, so after the incoming lerp it weighs exactly `alpha × (1 - m)`; at alpha 1 that is full strength and the crossfade is a plain lerp, with no dip toward the tracks below. When the incoming entry does not key it (or adds), it applies at `alpha × (1 - m)` and fades to what is under the track: the setup pose on track 0, the tracks below on a higher one. Down a chain the factors multiply: with mixes `b` then `c` over entries that all key a property, the three weigh `(1 - b)(1 - c)`, `b(1 - c)` and `c`. "Keys" means one timeline of the incoming animation keys every property the outgoing timeline keys (a combined translate is not covered by a translate x alone; that case dips a little). Before its first key that timeline applies nothing, so until then it replaces nothing of the outgoing timeline, which applies at `alpha × (1 - m)` and fades toward what is under the track; when the key comes, the pose takes the key's value at the incoming entry's weight, which is the animation's own step, weighted by the mix (`a_timeline_before_its_first_key_does_not_hold_the_outgoing_entry_up`). In a longer chain, the entries newer than the incoming one fade the outgoing timeline as they fade the incoming one's, before that key and after it, so the outgoing weight is the same on both sides of the key (`an_outgoing_weight_holds_when_a_middle_entry_reaches_its_first_key`: of three entries with both mixes half done, the oldest weighs 0.25 before the middle one's first key and after). In a chain of three or more, past a newer entry that does not key the property (or that adds), the older ones assume nothing newer keys it, so they fade a little faster there: a small dip. What is promised: an outgoing timeline's weight in the result is at most its applied alpha times the product of `(1 - m)` over the mixes of the entries newer than it, and is 0 when its mix ends, so ending the entry changes nothing in the pose. Where every newer entry keys the property and replaces, the weight is exactly that product, whether or not their timelines have reached their first keys, so it never rises while the mixes run. Past a newer entry that does not key the property it is lower (the dip), and how it moves under that bound is not promised: it can creep up a little while an entry above mixes in at partial alpha. `a_crossfade_does_not_dip_toward_what_is_under_it` works an example of each case, the two dips included.
- **Deform keys** (`apply_deforms`, which a skeleton calls once its slots show what the pose leaves them showing: In the world, The step). The deform timelines the entries applied are blended into the slots' offsets in the order the tracks applied them, each at the alpha and fade the rules above give any value timeline. So:
  - **Layers and blends** are a bone's: a higher track replaces what the tracks below left by its alpha, or adds its key times its alpha (`an_add_deform_adds_to_the_layer_below`).
  - **A crossfade between two animations that key one slot's attachment by one name has no dip**: the property the cover rule compares is (slot, attachment name), so the outgoing key keeps `1 - m` of the result and the incoming one `m`, with none of the setup shape in it (`a_crossfade_blends_two_deforms_without_a_dip`: at 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%, on a layer at half alpha, three in a chain, and either side of the incoming timeline's first key).
  - **A crossfade to an animation that keys no deform there**, or to the empty animation, returns the shape to the setup shape linearly with the mix, with no jump at either end or when the outgoing entry ends; the other way, the deform grows in from the setup shape (`a_crossfade_from_a_deform_to_none_returns_to_setup_with_no_jump`).
  - **A deform covers another only if their keys are one length** (or either's are all empty, which fit any mesh). Keys of another length are skipped wherever the other's apply (below), so they replace nothing of it: the outgoing deform fades with the mix, as under an animation that keys none, and is not held at full strength to the mix's end to drop at once (`a_deform_of_another_length_replaces_nothing_of_the_one_it_mixes_from`).
  - **When it applies.** Only while its slot shows its attachment name, and the attachment the skins worn have under that name in the slot is a mesh holding as many positions as its keys (`position_count() × 2` floats; keys that are all empty fit any mesh). What the slot shows is what the step leaves it showing: the attachment keys of every track, then what the game set. So a deform of a lower track is judged by a higher track's attachment key, and a deform of an attachment the game put in the slot applies (`a_deform_whose_slot_shows_another_attachment_is_skipped_in_silence`, `component::a_deform_moves_what_its_slot_shows_once_the_game_and_the_skins_have_their_say`). One that does not apply writes nothing: the mesh keeps its setup shape, or what the deforms that do fit give it.
  - **The warning.** A deform whose slot shows its own attachment name, and finds there what it cannot move, is skipped and tells the log, once per skeleton per animation, naming the animation, the attachment, the slot and the reason: the attachment under its name is not a mesh, or the mesh holds another count of positions. Both are mistakes in the content, or a skin whose mesh is another shape. Not once a step, a loop, or each time a skin is put on and taken off (`a_deform_that_does_not_fit_is_skipped_with_one_warning`, through three loops of the animation; in the world `a_skin_swap_to_another_shape_skips_the_deform_without_repeating_the_warning` and `a_reload_that_changes_a_mesh_s_vertex_count_skips_its_deform`; and for a weighted mesh, whose positions are its influences, `a_reload_that_changes_a_weighted_mesh_s_influences_skips_its_deform`, where a save that holds one of four corners by two influences leaves the keys' four positions short of the mesh's five, with the vertex count as it was). The state keeps the names of the animations told of (the data's own `Arc<str>`, so only the list's growth allocates), at most 64 (`MAX_WARNED`); past that a misfit is skipped in silence (`deforms_that_do_not_fit_warn_up_to_a_bound`). **The names are forgotten when the state takes up new data**, so each version of a skeleton's data is told of afresh, and the 64 are a version's: a mesh cut again and saved is what a reload is for, and a deform that still does not fit it, or no longer does, is said once more, not once a step; a save that fits says nothing (`new_data_is_told_again_of_a_deform_that_does_not_fit`, the bound in `deforms_that_do_not_fit_warn_up_to_a_bound`; in the world `a_reload_that_changes_a_mesh_s_vertex_count_skips_its_deform`, where a save with another count and a later one with a region under the name are each told of once and a save that does not load is told of nothing). Only the deform's list is forgotten: the module's other once-per-name warnings (an unknown animation, bone, slot or skin) outlive a reload as before.
  - **A slot that shows another attachment, or nothing, is passed over in silence.** Showing another attachment than a deform's is ordinary play, and nothing is said of it however long it lasts: a higher track's attachment key; an animation that keys a deform for each of two names of one slot (they do not clash), of which the slot shows one at a time; a crossfade from an animation that deforms one attachment to one that shows another, where the outgoing deform is skipped from the step the incoming entry has weight; and a game's `set_attachment` (`a_deform_whose_slot_shows_another_attachment_is_skipped_in_silence` for the first three and for a deform of an attachment the slot never shows; `component::a_deform_resets_when_the_slot_s_attachment_changes` for an animation of two names and for the game's attachment). Rule we chose for a slot that shows nothing, whether it is hidden (an attachment key of `None`, or the game's `set_attachment(slot, None)`) or shows a name no skin worn has in it: the same. Nothing is drawn there to be wrong, and both are how a game does without a part: a skin cannot hide an attachment, so a character without its cape hides the slot or leaves the cape's skin off, while its animations still key the cape's deform (`a_deform_that_does_not_fit_is_skipped_with_one_warning`).
  - **Allocation.** The list of a pose's deform steps keeps its room, and holds the last pose's alone however often a skeleton is posed without its deforms being taken (`the_deform_steps_of_a_pose_keep_their_room`).
  - **Not built:** linked meshes (a mesh that draws another's vertices and takes its deform keys), a deform's own timeline attachment, and sequence keys. Spine's files key a deform under a skin's name and may leave a key's leading and trailing zeros out; here a deform is keyed by slot and attachment name, for whichever mesh of that count the skins worn show there, and a key is whole or empty.
- **The previous entry's time:** it keeps advancing while it mixes out, as Spine's default is; with the incoming entry's `hold_previous`, it holds its time (`a_mixing_out_entry_stops_firing_past_its_event_threshold`). The flag lives on the incoming entry, as its name says.
- **End of a mix:** once an entry has mixed in fully, every older entry ends (`End`, `Dispose`; `track_events_follow_start_interrupt_end_complete_dispose`).
- **Stepped keys** (attachments, draw order) belong to the newest entry of the chain whose applied alpha (`alpha × m`) is above 0, and no other entry applies them. So an entry mixing out keeps setting them until the entry replacing it has weight: at 0% of a mix (a queued entry that starts exactly on its delay, or a `set_animation` while the skeleton's clock is stopped) the outgoing entry's attachments and draw order still show, and from the first step with weight the incoming entry's do (`stepped_keys_stay_with_the_outgoing_entry_until_the_incoming_has_weight`). An entry whose own alpha is 0 never has weight, so a layer at alpha 0 forces nothing (`a_zero_alpha_layer_does_not_force_its_attachments`), and the entry it replaced keeps them while it lasts. When the outgoing animation keys a slot's attachment and the incoming one does not, then once the incoming entry has weight the slot shows what the tracks below set, else its setup attachment, for the rest of the mix and after (`attachments_belong_to_the_entry_mixing_in`). The empty animation is such an incoming entry: it keys nothing.
- **Constraint keys** (the rules of applying one are under Curves, applying an animation and events):
  - **Mixing**: a constraint's numbers blend through layers and crossfades as a bone's values do, and the cover rule treats a constraint as one property, so a crossfade between two animations that key it moves each number in a straight line from one key to the other, with no dip toward the setup's, on a layer at partial alpha too (`a_crossfade_between_two_keyed_mixes_has_no_dip`). A crossfade to an animation that keys none fades the keyed values to the setup's, or the tracks below's, linearly with the mix: a keyed IK mix fades with its animation and the limb leaves its target without a pop (`constrain::a_keyed_mix_fades_with_its_animation_without_a_pop`: from a mix of 1 to an animation with no IK timeline over a setup mix of 0, over 30 steps the tip's distance from its target grows every step, from 0 to the whole way, and no step moves it more than a twentieth of the whole way and 0.01).
  - **Switches** follow the stepped-key rule below: the newest entry with weight applies them, so in a crossfade they are the incoming entry's from its first step with weight; if it keys none for the constraint, the tracks below's or the setup's stand from that step, though the outgoing entry still has most of the weight (`a_constraint_s_switches_change_at_their_key_and_follow_the_attachment_rule_in_a_crossfade`, which also changes them at their key and keeps a lower track's under a layer keying none).
  - **Clamped after the tracks** (`an_ik_mix_is_clamped_after_the_tracks`): once every track has applied, an IK constraint's `mix` is held within 0 to 1 and its `softness` at 0 or more (an additive layer or a bezier can carry either past its range), and not after each track: track 0 at 1, an additive track adding 1 and a third track half way to 0.6 give 1.3 and then 1, not 0.8. A transform constraint's mixes are not clamped: they may be below 0 or above 1.
  - **Off at 0**: a keyed IK mix of 0, or keyed transform mixes all 0, turn the constraint off for as long as they are keyed: the pass passes it over and its bones are left to animation and the game's bone overrides, to the bit as if the data had no constraint (`constrain::a_keyed_mix_of_0_turns_a_constraint_off`, with a world-space override on a limb's bone).
- **Rotation memory:** an entry keeps a turn per rotate timeline (see `apply_layer`) while it is in a mix or applies at less than full strength (`alpha × m < 1`). So a bone keeps turning one way through a mix (`a_crossfade_keeps_each_bone_turning_one_way`), and a layer at partial alpha does not jump by alpha × 360° on the step after its mix ends (`a_layer_at_partial_alpha_keeps_turning_one_way_after_its_mix`). An entry alone at full strength keeps none, since the way round makes no difference to where the bone ends up, so its next mix starts afresh, the short way. The memory is one of the entry's own buffers: no step allocates for it. Two limits of keeping it for as long as an entry applies at less than full strength. It winds: the remembered turn is the whole angle from the pose under the entry to its target, so under a layer whose target keeps going round it grows by 2π a revolution, and as an `f32` it steps coarser as it grows: after 2^14 revolutions (4.5 hours of one turn a second) it is near 103,000 radians, where an `f32` moves in steps of 0.45°, so the turn is up to 0.2° off and the layer's rotation moves in steps of 0.45° times its alpha. And a partial-alpha layer's resting angle depends on its history: it rests at the pose under it plus alpha times the remembered turn, and a turn that has wound k times round rests alpha × k × 360° from where a fresh entry showing the same key rests (the short way). Setting the animation again starts the memory afresh.
- **`set_empty(track, mix)`** plays the empty animation (`<empty>`, keying nothing) as the current entry with that mix: the old entry fades to the setup pose on track 0, or to the tracks below on a higher one. When the empty entry has mixed in and nothing is queued after it, the track clears (`set_empty_fades_a_layer_out_then_clears_it`). An entry queued after it mixes in from nothing: a layer fading in (`a_layer_fades_in_after_set_empty`). `current` does not report an empty entry. **Called again** while the track's current entry is already the empty one with nothing queued after it, it does nothing, whatever mix it is given: the fade keeps the timing it started with (`set_empty_again_while_a_track_fades_out_changes_nothing`). Otherwise each call would stack another empty entry, and a game calling every step would never let the fade end. With an entry queued behind the empty one the call is made as ever (it drops the queue). What a game calling every step still gets: once the fade is over the track is clear, so the next call starts an empty entry afresh, which lasts its mix; its `Start`, `End` and `Dispose` come once a mix, not once a step (every step only at a mix of 0).
- **`clear(track)`** ends what plays at once (`End`, `Dispose`, newest first) and disposes the queue (`set_animation_and_clear_drop_what_is_queued`). From the next step the track's properties are what is under it: nothing fades. **`clear_all()`** does it for every track, the lowest first, and before the data lands drops every kept call (`clear_all_stops_every_track_at_once`): for a skeleton taken from a pool, whose tracks are still its last life's, so that the animation set next has nothing to mix from.
- **Queue** (`a_queued_entry_starts_after_its_delay`): an entry starts when the previous entry's track time reaches its `delay`. A `delay <= 0` is turned into the previous entry's end plus the delay (at least 0) when added, and the field holds the result. The end is the previous entry's duration, or for a looping entry the end of the loop it is in when the call is made (2.25 s into a 1 s loop, 3 s). On an empty track it starts after `delay` seconds of skeleton time, and with `delay <= 0` it is current at once, as if set. Its mix is the data's for the pair (the previous entry, it). A step past the start carries the rest into the new entry (track time and mix time), and several queued entries can start in one step (`several_queued_entries_can_start_in_one_step`). An entry that starts in a step has what was left of the step when it was due, and one that starts behind it in the same step has no more than that: an entry due before the start of the one it follows (a `start` past its `delay`) gets the time that one got, not the whole step (the same test).
- **Queue length** (`a_track_queues_at_most_sixty_four_entries`): a track's queue holds at most 64 entries (`MAX_QUEUE`). `add_animation` past that does nothing and returns `None`, with one warning per skeleton, so a game that queues every step cannot grow the queue without bound; a refused call tells nothing, and there is room again as queued entries start. Calls kept until the data lands fill the queue the same way: those it has no room for are disposed (a `Dispose` alone).
- **Events** (`events_fire_once_across_loops_large_steps_and_time_scales`, `events_from_several_tracks_come_in_time_order`): each entry fires the keys its track time crossed this step (`events_crossed`, from before 0 on its first step, or from its start when that is past 0: Start, below), with the key's time in its animation. A stopped clock fires nothing, but for an entry's first step: that puts it at its time 0, so a key at 0 fires then, once, even with the clock stopped. The step's events are ordered by when in the step each was crossed, across tracks; ties keep track order.
- **Event threshold** (`a_mixing_out_entry_stops_firing_past_its_event_threshold`): an entry mixing out fires keys up to the track time at which the incoming mix reaches its `event_threshold` (`replaced_at + threshold × mix_duration × speed`), clipped exactly whatever the step size. At the default 0, nothing fires after it is replaced. `replaced_at` is a track time the entry was at: the delay of the queued entry that replaced it, or where the entry began the step if that is later (a `start` past the delay), so an entry replaced where it starts still fires through its threshold (`an_entry_started_part_way_fires_only_the_keys_after_its_start`). One exception to "nothing fires" at threshold 0: an entry that a frame posed (`apply_unadvanced`) and a call replaced before its first step is not dropped, having been shown, so it takes that step mixing out, and its span is the first step's, from before 0 up to where it was replaced, 0: its key at 0 fires (the same test as the threshold's; an entry replaced before anything showed it is dropped and fires nothing).
- **Reverse** (`reverse_plays_backward_and_fires_events_backward`): the animation's time is `duration - t` (wrapped when looping, held at the end otherwise), and events are mirrored the same way, so keys fire backward, each loop.
- **Start** (`TrackEntry::start`, seconds, 0 unless set): where in its animation an entry begins. It is a public field, like `speed` and `delay`, read once, when the entry takes its first step: only then are the animation's duration (a kept entry has none until the data lands) and the entry's `looping` and `reverse` settled, so a setter could do no more than store it. An entry's track time begins at its start, as if the entry had already played from 0 to there: what lies at or before the start has passed, and every other rule holds as it stands. With a start of 0 nothing differs. Each rule and its test:
  - **Pose and time** (`an_entry_starts_at_its_start`; in the world, `an_animation_started_part_way_poses_and_fires_from_there`): posed before its first step (the frame it appears in), the entry shows its animation at the start, and its first step moves on from there. A queued entry begins there too, plus the rest of the step that starts it. `track_time()` reads the start before the first step: wrapped or held (below) once the data is known, as given while the entry is kept.
  - **Events** (`an_entry_started_part_way_fires_only_the_keys_after_its_start`): at a start past 0 the first step's span begins at the start, exclusive, so the keys at or before it do not fire, and those after it fire once as ever. A looping entry started at `t` fires the keys in (`t`, duration] in its first loop and every key in each later loop. At a start of 0 the span begins before 0 as before, so the key at 0 fires with the entry's `Start`, on a stopped clock too. An entry posed and then replaced before its first step takes that step mixing out, and its event threshold counts from its start (`install` records where it stood, not its unstarted time); so does one whose queued follower was due before its start (Event threshold, above).
  - **Complete and the queue** (`a_started_entry_completes_when_its_animation_ends`): track time counts from the start, so a looping entry completes when its animation next ends (`duration - start` later) and each duration after. The queue reads the same track time: an entry queued at the end of a 0.5 s entry that started at 0.25 starts a quarter of a second on, and a `delay` the previous entry's start is already past is due at once.
  - **Past the end** (`a_start_past_the_end_wraps_when_looping_and_holds_at_the_end_otherwise`): a looping entry's start wraps into the animation (the loops skipped are neither fired nor completed, and its time does not start coarse); any other entry holds at the animation's end, showing its last frame, `is_complete` from its first step, with nothing fired and no `Complete`: its end has passed, as a key has. Whether a key has passed is judged on the start as given, before the wrap: a start of whole loops shows the animation at 0 with the key at 0 passed (it fires a loop later), where a start of exactly 0 fires it. So an entry restored with `start = track_time()` never fires a key again that it had fired, with one exception, below. A zero-length animation is all at 0: started past 0, its key and its `Complete` have passed.
    - **Limit: a start of whole loops, saved again before its first step.** `track_time()` reads the start wrapped once the data is known, so an entry started at a whole number of loops (3.0 on a 1 s loop) reads back 0.0 until it has stepped, and 0.0 as a start fires the key at 0. A game that loads such an entry, saves again while no step runs (paused) and restores from that save fires the key at 0 a second time. Not pinned by a test; keeping the start as given until the first step would close it.
  - **Bad values** (`a_start_that_is_negative_or_not_finite_counts_as_zero_with_one_warning`): a negative or non-finite start counts as 0, with one warning per skeleton, given when an entry with one takes its first step.
  - **After the first step** (`a_start_set_after_the_first_step_does_nothing`): the field is not read again, whether the entry is current or mixing out.
  - **Reverse** (`a_reverse_entry_s_start_is_measured_from_the_animation_s_end`): the start is track time, how far into its playing the entry begins, so a reverse entry's is measured back from the animation's end: started at 0.25 of a 1 s animation it shows the animation's 0.75 and plays toward 0, and the keys it has passed are those at 0.75 and after. Rule we chose, so that `start = track_time()` restores an entry whichever way it plays.
  - **Kept calls** (`a_start_set_before_the_data_lands_is_honoured_when_it_lands`): the start is the kept entry's own field, so it is there when the data lands and gives the duration to wrap by.
  - **A crowd** (`two_skeletons_started_half_a_cycle_apart_stay_half_a_cycle_apart`): one looping animation on two skeletons, at starts 0 and half its duration, stays half a cycle apart, each firing its keys in its own phase.
- **Track events** (`track_events_follow_start_interrupt_end_complete_dispose`), sent with the next step when a call causes them: `Start` when an entry becomes current; `Interrupt` when another entry replaces it as current (a call or a queued entry); `End` when it will never be applied again (cleared, replaced with no mix, or its mix-out over); `Dispose` right after `End`, or alone for an entry that never started (queued, or kept before the data landed and dropped); `Complete` each time a current looping entry passes a multiple of its duration (each one, even several in a step, at most 1,000 a step: `complete_tells_at_most_a_thousand_whatever_the_rounding`), and once for a non-looping or zero-length one. An entry mixing out completes no more (`an_entry_mixing_out_completes_no_more`).
- **The empty animation's entries** send `Start`, `Interrupt`, `End` and `Dispose`, and never `Complete` (`set_empty_fades_a_layer_out_then_clears_it`): a game that wants "the fade is over" listens for the `End`. Their events carry the name `<empty>`, which is the public `SkeletonAnimation::EMPTY`; validation keeps any animation in the data from taking it, so the name alone tells an empty entry apart. The constant is for reading events: a track is faded out with `set_empty`, and `set_animation(track, EMPTY, ..)` is an unknown name like any other. No handle to an empty entry is ever given out (`set_empty` returns none and `current` does not report it), so `TrackEntry` has no test for it.
- **Events from calls** wait for the next step in a list of at most 1,024 (`MAX_TOLD`). Past that the oldest are dropped, with one warning per skeleton (`track_events_from_calls_are_bounded_between_steps`): a skeleton that is not stepped while a game keeps calling it cannot grow without bound, and the newest events are the ones it hears when it steps again.
- **Unknown names and data that has not landed** (`an_unknown_name_warns_once_and_does_nothing`, `a_call_before_the_data_lands_is_kept`, `a_later_call_drops_the_calls_kept_for_its_track`): the state learns the data in its first step (the data's shared `AnimationCatalogue`: names, durations, mixes). Until then every call is kept, in order, and returns a handle whose entry can be changed meanwhile; `set_animation`, `set_empty` and `clear` drop the earlier kept calls for their track, but `set_animation` leaves a `set_empty` with nothing but queued entries after it (those it drops), as the empty animation stays on a track for the next entry to mix in from (`an_entry_replaced_before_it_shows_is_dropped`). The first step makes them. A name the data lacks warns once per skeleton per name and does nothing (`None` once the data is known); a state remembers at most 64 such names (`MAX_WARNED`), and past that an unknown name is ignored in silence (`unknown_names_warn_up_to_a_bound`). A kept entry whose mix is still 0 when it is made takes the data's mix, so a 0 set before the data lands counts as unset. At most 256 calls are kept (`MAX_KEPT`: room for a `set_empty`, a `set_animation` and two queued entries on every track); one more is ignored and returns `None`, with one warning per skeleton. A call that replaces kept calls on its own track (`set_animation`, `set_empty`) drops those first and so always fits; a call that is refused has dropped nothing (`kept_calls_are_capped_with_one_warning`). A kept entry that a later call drops, or whose name the data turns out to lack, is disposed: a `Dispose` alone, sent with the first step. So every handle's entry ends with a `Dispose`, unless the bounded list above dropped it.
- **Allocation** (`a_call_after_the_data_lands_takes_the_data_s_name_and_spare_buffers`, `spare_buffers_are_bounded`): a step allocates nothing in steady state, and once the data has landed a call allocates nothing either. The entry's name is the data's own `Arc<str>` (only a call kept before the data lands copies its name), and its per-timeline buffers (cover, fades, turns) are a set that a finished entry left with the state, which keeps at most 16 (`MAX_SPARE`). A steady stream of crossfades so allocates nothing after the first few; the chains, queues and event list keep their capacity.
- **New data** (`new_data_keeps_tracks_by_animation_name`): a step with another catalogue (a reload) finds each entry's animation by name and keeps its times: a started entry goes on from where it was, and its `start` is not applied again. A track whose current animation is gone is cleared whole (the entry it was mixing from does not come back); a gone entry that is mixing out or queued is dropped and the track plays on; each gone name warns once per skeleton. Gone entries end newest first: a cleared track as `clear` ends it (its chain from the newest back, then its queue in order), and on a track that plays on, the gone entries mixing out from the newest back, then the gone queued ones from the last queued. **Limits, development-time only and not pinned by a test:** entries keep their track times across new data, so a looping entry whose animation's duration changed jumps to another phase of it; a non-looping entry already past a shortened duration never sends `Complete` (`is_complete` turns true); and a queued entry's `delay`, worked out from the old duration when it was queued, is not worked out again.
- **Readbacks** (`a_state_says_what_each_track_plays_and_how_far_it_has_mixed`): `animation(track)` is the name of the entry `current(track)` reports, so `None` for an empty track and for the empty animation (fading out, or left by `set_empty`), and a kept call's name before the data lands: a state machine that asks before it sets (`if state.animation(0) != Some("run")`) does not set again every frame while the data loads. `mix_progress(track)` is the newest entry's mix fraction, 0 up to 1, while it has an entry to mix from and its mix is not over; the empty animation's too, so a fade-out reads as a mix while `animation` gives `None`. It is `None` otherwise: a first entry on an empty track, a mix of no length (even between the call and its step), and before the data lands. Both are as of the last step with the calls made since: an animation set reads at once, with a mix of 0.
- **Known differences from Spine's documented behaviour** (its runtimes guide and API reference):
  - Spine documents that a crossfade on a higher track dips toward the tracks below, and offers `holdPrevious` (in the versions that have it) to keep the previous animation at full strength instead. Here the linear-weight rule above, written from first principles, has no dip where the incoming animation keys the property, and `hold_previous` instead holds the previous entry's time.
  - Spine turns `delay <= 0` into the previous duration (the next loop's end for a looping entry) minus the mix plus the delay, so the mix ends at the previous end; here the mix is not taken off, so the mix starts there.
  - Spine makes an entry queued on an empty track current at once, and its delay postpones its time; here it waits in the queue and becomes current (`Start`) when the delay has passed.
  - Spine's `complete` can fire for an entry that is mixing out; here an entry completes only while it is current.
  - Spine's `clearTrack` leaves the skeleton in its current pose; here every step starts from the setup pose, so a cleared track's properties go back to what is under it.
  - Spine applies attachment and draw-order keys of an entry mixing out below its mix thresholds (`mixAttachmentThreshold`, `mixDrawOrderThreshold`, default 0: not while mixing out); here an entry mixing out applies them only until the entry replacing it has weight.
  - Spine applies an entry's attachment timelines when its computed alpha (its alpha and its mix) is above `alphaAttachmentThreshold`, whose default of 0 its reference describes as always applied; here an entry with no weight applies no stepped keys, and the threshold is not offered.
  - Not offered: a track end time (`trackEnd`), a part of an animation to loop (`animationStart`, `animationEnd`: `start` is only where an entry begins, and every loop after is the whole animation), queued empty animations (`addEmptyAnimation`), the attachment and draw-order thresholds above, and the rotation-direction calls (`shortestRotation`, `resetRotationDirections`): the rotation memory here is kept and reset as "Rotation memory" says.

## In the world (`component.rs`, `system.rs`, `socket.rs`, `event.rs`)

`Skeleton` (a component, requiring `Transform` and `Interpolated`) shows a `Handle<SkeletonData>` where its entity stands: it holds the pose, the bones' transforms in its own space as the last two steps left them (`pose.skeleton_space` and `previous_space`, crate-internal, for drawing between steps: Drawing, below), their world transforms as the last step left them (`pose.world`, what `bone_world`, `point` and `bounding_box` answer from) and what the game set on it. `SkeletonAnimation` beside it is optional: without one the skeleton holds its setup pose. `tests/skeleton.rs` drives a headless engine (it skips without a GPU); the tests named `component::`, `socket::` and `system::` below are the files' own.

- **The step** (`update_skeletons`, `FixedSet::Skeletons`: after propagation, so each entity's `GlobalTransform` is this step's, and before particles). Each enabled skeleton whose data is in the store, in `entity_order`, so several skeletons' messages come in one order whatever the world's archetypes (`system::skeletons_send_their_events_in_entity_order`):
  1. If the data's `(AssetId, version)` is not the one its pose was made from, the pose is made anew (New data, below). Mostly the frame's pass has done so already (A pose in the frame it appears, below).
  2. The animation state advances by the fixed timestep and applies (`advance_and_apply`, which starts from the setup pose); with no state the pose is reset to the setup pose.
  3. The game's attachments are written over the slots; the animation state's deform keys move the meshes the slots then show, in the skins worn (`SkeletonAnimation::apply_deforms`: The animation state, Deform keys), and each slot's offsets are kept with its attachment (`Pose::deforms_follow_slots`); then the game's constraint overrides are written over the constraints' values, and the game's bone overrides over the bones, as every bone is placed in the skeleton's space.
  4. The data's constraints are applied, in order (Constraints in a step, below); data without constraints has no such step.
  5. The bones' world transforms are computed under the entity's `GlobalTransform`, mirrored about the entity's origin as its `Renderable` flips it (Flips, under Drawing).
  6. Its sockets, and everything below them, are placed.
  7. Its `SkeletonEvent`s and `SkeletonTrackEvent`s are written.
- **Data still loading** (`a_skeleton_waits_for_its_data_and_keeps_the_animation_set_meanwhile`): a skeleton whose data is not in the store (a `load` on its way, or one that failed) is skipped without a word. It has no bones (`bone_world`, `point` and `bounding_box` find nothing), its sockets stay where their own transforms put them, and nothing is sent. What the game sets meanwhile is kept by name (the animation state keeps its calls, above); all of it shows in the frame the data lands, and the animation starts with the first step after.
- **A pose in the frame it appears** (`pose_new_skeletons`, each frame in `PostUpdate`, after its propagation and before the occluder index, the cameras and the draw; `a_skeleton_has_its_starting_pose_in_the_frame_it_appears_and_says_nothing_until_a_step`, `a_reload_landing_while_the_clock_is_paused_rebuilds_the_pose_that_frame`, `system::a_skeleton_posed_in_a_frame_starts_its_animation_in_its_first_step`). A fixed step is the only thing that advances a skeleton, but a frame may run none (the clock paused, hitstop, slow motion, a display faster than the step), and a skeleton added in such a frame, or whose data landed or reloaded in it, would have nothing to draw, or the old data's pose, with its sockets at its entity's origin, until a step came. So the pass gives every skeleton that has no pose for its data as it now is (`Skeleton::seen` is not the store's `(AssetId, version)`) a pose where it stands, by `Skeleton::appear`: the pose is made anew as in step 1, the animation state poses it without a step (`SkeletonAnimation::apply_unadvanced`: it takes up the data, so kept calls are made and a reload keeps entries by name, then applies the tracks where they stand), the game's attachments and overrides go over that, the bones are placed, the data's constraints applied as a step applies them (`constrain::a_skeleton_posed_in_the_frame_it_appears_has_its_constraints_applied`), and the sockets put on them. The rules:
  - The pose is the animation's, not the setup pose: an entry that has yet to start is shown at its first moment (its `start`, when one is set), so a character does not flash its setup pose before its idle starts. After a reload it is the new data's pose where the tracks stand.
  - Nothing advances and nothing is sent. No event key fires, and the track events the calls caused stay in the state. The entry's first step is still its first: its `Start` and the keys at its time 0 go out then, once, and its time moves on from 0 by one step (from its `start` when one is set, without the keys that start has passed). Nothing is lost and nothing comes twice.
  - A held skeleton gets its pose too; it then waits as it would.
  - The fixed step does the same work for a skeleton it meets first (one added in the step's own `update`), so the pass then finds it posed.
  - **A socket added to a skeleton that has its pose** is put on its bone by the same pass, in that frame, drawn there at once (`a_socket_added_while_the_clock_is_paused_is_on_its_bone_that_frame`, `system::a_socket_added_while_no_step_runs_is_on_its_bone_that_frame`): a frame that looks places the sockets of every skeleton with a pose, on the pose it has. Those already on their bones are found where they stand and left alone.
  - **A steady frame costs one tick compare a skeleton, and looks through none** (`system::a_steady_frame_does_not_look_through_the_skeletons`): the pass looks through the skeletons only when, since its last look, a `Skeleton` or a `BoneSocket` was put on an entity (the store's count, bumped by each component's `on_insert` hook) or the data's store gave out a version (`Assets::versions_given`: a landing, a reload, a change in code). Any other frame it compares the two counts, finds no skeleton's `Renderable` changed (below: the one cost that grows with the skeletons, a change tick compared for each that has a renderable) and returns, writing nothing and allocating nothing; a frame that looks poses only the skeletons lacking a pose. A world with no `SkeletonStore` looks every frame. One more thing makes it look: a skeleton that has a pose and whose `Renderable`, changed since the pass last ran (`Changed<Renderable>`, a tick compared per skeleton entity with a renderable, read through a `ParamSet` beside the skeletons' own query), flips it another way than it is posed (Flips, under Drawing). A renderable changed for anything else (a tint, an alpha) costs the comparison of two flags and no look.
  - Not covered: a skeleton enabled again (`Disabled` taken off) between steps keeps the pose it had until its next step, since nothing counts an enabling; so does its flip, if its `Renderable` was flipped while it was disabled, or was taken off it while no step runs (a change the pass's `Changed<Renderable>` no longer sees, or never does). A socket given to a skeleton by `ChildOf` alone (its `BoneSocket` already on it) while no step runs is not counted either: it is placed, drawn at once, in the next step, or in the next frame that looks. And a `Skeleton` assigned whole through a mutable reference is not an insert: no scene is recorded and no look follows (its rustdoc says to insert).
- **Time** (`a_paused_clock_freezes_animation_and_events`): the step is the fixed timestep, times the state's `time_scale` and each entry's speed. The engine runs no fixed step while the game clock is paused or in hitstop, so nothing advances and nothing is sent then, and what a call made meanwhile causes is told in the first step after. A zero-length step reaches the state only through `time_scale` at 0, where an entry set then gets its `Start` and its keys at time 0 once. Limit: there is no unscaled mode, so a skeleton in a menu stands still while the game clock is paused.
- **Read from a scene, a skeleton is one step behind.** `Scene::update` (`FixedSet::Scene`) and a scene's own systems (`FixedSet::Game`) run before `FixedSet::Skeletons`, so what they read (`bone_world`, `point`, `bounding_box`, `attachment`) is as the last step left it, and a `SkeletonEvent` or `SkeletonTrackEvent` is read one step after it was written. In step: a `BoneSocket`, or a system added with `EngineBuilder::add_systems` and ordered after `FixedSet::Skeletons`, as `tests/skeleton.rs`'s `probe` is; a scene's `add_systems` refuses a call that names an engine set (`SceneScheduleError::EngineSet`).
- **Holds** (`scope.rs`; `a_skeleton_freezes_under_a_scene_that_stops_its_own_and_resumes_with_nothing_lost`, `a_skeleton_under_a_scene_that_keeps_its_own_updating_keeps_animating`): a skeleton animates while its scene updates, exactly as a particle emitter does, by the same mechanism with a store of its own (`SkeletonStore`, a resource). The scene manager runs scene code under `skeleton::in_scene` (beside `audio::in_scene` and `particles::in_scene`), and `Skeleton`'s `on_insert` hook records the scene whose code runs in the component (`scope`; `None` for the engine's own code). The manager tells the store the scenes stopped under another (`hold_scenes`: those below `update_from`, as the stack changes and in `sync_pauses`) and that a scene exited (`release_scene`: its skeletons that outlive it belong to no scene). The step skips a held skeleton whole: nothing advances, nothing is sent (the stopped scene would not read it, and message buffers last two steps), its sockets stay where they are, and what its calls cause waits in its state. In the step its scene updates again it carries on from where it was, and what waited is told once: nothing is lost to the hold. A skeleton of no scene (added outside every scene, or outliving its scene) is held while any scene is. A scene that `updates_below` holds nothing, and a scene transition holds nothing. The scope tests in `scope.rs` mirror the particle store's.
- **Scene transitions** (`what_a_skeleton_sends_during_a_scene_transition_is_heard_by_a_scene_system_and_not_by_update`): a skeleton keeps animating through a scene transition, as particles and physics do. While one runs, `Scene::update` waits (`SceneManager::update` returns at once) and the scenes' own systems keep running; message buffers last two steps. So what a skeleton sends during a transition is never seen by a reader in `update`, and is seen by a reader in a scene system. That is the engine's rule for every message, older than skeletons. What is the skeletons' own to say: "nothing lost" covers the hold and no more. A scene popped with a fade releases the skeletons held under it at the fade's midpoint (the pop), their next step sends what waited, and from then to the fade's end `update` hears none of it; the test pins exactly that, with a real pushed scene popped by `Transition::fade`. A game stays right by reading skeleton messages in a scene system, by queueing what follows with `add_animation`, or by asking the state (`is_complete`, `animation(track)`): the skill gives each. Not taken: holding a skeleton while its scene's `update` waits on a transition. It would freeze characters under every fade and part them from particles (`docs/backlog/skeleton.md`, Later).
- **What the game sets is the skeleton's own, kept by name, and written after the animation every step.** The animation state resets the pose to setup each step, so nothing set on the pose itself would last: the skins, the attachments and the bone overrides live in `Skeleton`, each with its name and its place in the data (found when it is set, and again when new data lands), and step 3 puts the attachments and the overrides over the animation's result, so each of those shows from the skeleton's next step, and what the skeleton answers of them is as its last step left it. **A skin shows at once**: nothing of a skin is written into the pose, which holds only the name each slot shows, and the skins worn are looked up when something asks (`Skeleton::skin_attachment`: the drawer, `point`, `bounding_box`, `attachment`). So a `set_skins` changes the next frame's drawing, with the clock paused too, and those answers at once (`a_skin_shows_in_the_next_frame_and_a_set_attachment_from_the_next_step` in `tests/e2e/skeleton.rs`, `component::a_skeleton_says_what_it_wears_and_what_each_slot_shows`). Skeletons sharing data share nothing else, what an animation keys on a slot's colour and the draw order included (`skeletons_sharing_data_keep_their_own_skin_overrides_and_attachments`, which reads two drawn skeletons' pixels for the last two), and the shapes its deform keys give their meshes (`skeletons_sharing_data_keep_their_own_deform`: one still, one held in a shape, two growing into it half a second apart, and a skin on one of them).
  - **Skins** (`set_skin`, `set_skins`): an attachment name is looked up in the last skin worn that has it in the slot, then in the default skin (`Skeleton::skin_attachment`, the crate's own lookup). A name the data lacks warns once per skeleton per name and is left out; if the data has none of the names given, the call changes nothing. Limit: a skin adds or replaces an attachment and cannot hide one the default skin has (the demo's cap is drawn to cover the hair); a game hides a slot with `set_attachment(slot, None)`.
  - **Attachments** (`set_attachment`, `clear_attachment`; `a_game_set_attachment_beats_animation_until_it_is_handed_back`): a slot the game set shows that attachment whatever animations key there (`None` hides it), for as long as it is set; `clear_attachment` hands the slot back, to the animations' keys or else its setup attachment. The name is looked up in the skins like a key's, so one no skin has in the slot shows nothing, without a warning. A slot the data lacks warns once per skeleton per name and does nothing.
  - **Bone overrides** (`override_bone`, `clear_override`; `a_world_space_override_aims_a_bone_at_a_point` and the `component::` tests): each value given replaces the animated one by `mix` (clamped to 0..=1): `value += (given - value) × mix`, a rotation by the short way round. In `Space::Local` the values are the bone's own (what animations set). In `Space::World` a rotation is the way the bone's x axis points in the world and a translation is where its origin is; each is converted to the bone's parent's space every step, so it holds while the entity and the bones above move. `scale` is always the bone's own. Rules we chose:
    - Overrides are applied in the pass that places the bones, parents first (`Skeleton::place`): a world-space one is converted through its parent as that step left it, the parent's own override included (`component::a_world_translation_puts_a_bone_at_a_point_and_its_children_follow`).
    - A world translation goes back through the parent's world transform (translation is inherited in every mode). A world rotation goes back through the map that carries the bone's own direction to the world, `root × Pose::direction_space`: the parent's linear part under normal inheritance and the no-scale modes, the parent's stretch when the bone inherits no rotation, and nothing but the root when it inherits only translation; a bone with a negative x scale is turned to point its axis, not its back (`component::a_world_rotation_points_the_bone_under_every_inherit_mode`, `..._whose_own_x_scale_is_negative`). One that cannot be converted, because a zero scale above the bone flattens the map, is left out for that step (`component::a_world_override_through_a_flat_parent_is_left_out`).
    - Within one override the translation is applied first, then the scale, then the rotation.
    - A value that is not finite is taken out when the override is set, the rest applying; a mix that is not finite counts as 0. One warning per skeleton (`component::values_that_are_not_finite_are_taken_out_with_one_warning`).
    - A bone the data lacks warns once per skeleton per name and does nothing.
  - **Constraint overrides** (`override_constraint(name, ConstraintOverride)`, `clear_constraint_override`; the `component::` tests below and two in `tests/skeleton.rs`): `ConstraintOverride::Ik(IkOverride)` sets any of an IK constraint's keyed values, `ConstraintOverride::Transform(TransformMixOverride)` any of a transform constraint's six mixes and `ConstraintOverride::Spring(SpringOverride)` any of a spring's `mix`, `wind` and `gravity`, each an `Option` (`Default`: all `None`). Rules we chose, each with its test:
    - Each value given replaces what the animations left (or the setup's), a switch included; each left `None` is the animated one. They are written in `Skeleton::finish` after the tracks (and the clamp) and before the bones are placed, so they show from the next step, and `clear_constraint_override` gives the constraint back to the animation from the next step. Set again, an override replaces the one before (`component::an_override_beats_the_animation_and_clearing_it_gives_the_animation_back`, set before the data lands, so kept until it does). An override of a switch forces it whatever the animations key (`component::an_override_forces_a_switch`). A placing again between steps (a socket, a flip) puts back the values the animations left, kept as the step ended (`Constrained::values`), and writes the overrides over them afresh, so one set or cleared since shows at once, as a bone override does (`constrain::a_constraint_override_set_or_cleared_between_steps_is_taken_up_when_placed_again`: a limb turned off between steps is freed by the placing again, to the bit as a step with that override places it, and cleared, reaches again; the kept list allocates nothing).
    - An IK `mix` given is taken within 0 to 1 and a `softness` below 0 as 0; a transform mix outside 0 to 1 is kept. A `bend` the constraint cannot have (`Pose` on one bone or two, `Negative` on one) is passed over in silence, the animated one kept, and one it can have bends it (`loaders::skeleton::the_bend_setting_loads_keys_and_overrides`: a knee bent `Negative` turned `Positive`, then an override of `Pose` passed over, the knee `Negative` again; a chain sent to either side). A value that is not finite is dropped when the override is set, the rest applying, with one warning per skeleton for all constraint overrides (`component::an_override_value_not_finite_is_dropped_with_one_warning`). A `max_stretch` given is the limit, replacing the setup's; one below 1 is dropped as a value not finite is, with the same warning, and on a chain it is passed over, the values left untouched (`component::an_override_sets_the_stretch_limit_and_drops_one_below_one`, which runs it over one, two, three and sixteen bones).
    - A name the data lacks, or a constraint of another kind, warns once per skeleton per name (the skeleton's warned-names list, its two mistakes told apart) and does nothing: it neither adds an override nor replaces the constraint's own. Before the data lands the call is kept by name, and told of when the data lands if the data lacks it (`component::an_override_for_an_unknown_constraint_or_of_the_other_kind_warns_once`). An override already kept for a name the data lacks, or has as another kind, is updated by a later call all the same (as `override_bone` updates first), and holds the constraint once data that has it of that kind lands (`component::an_override_kept_for_a_constraint_the_data_lacks_takes_a_later_call`, through a reload that removes the constraint and one that brings it back).
    - A spring's override follows the same rules (`component::a_spring_override_beats_its_keys_is_kept_by_name_and_cleared`: set before the data lands, replaced, kept through a reload, cleared, and an override of another kind on a spring, or a spring's on another kind, warning once each; `component::a_spring_override_value_not_finite_is_dropped_with_one_warning`: a `wind` and a `gravity` not finite dropped with one warning, the keyed ones standing, and its `mix` taken within 0 to 1).
    - A mix of 0 turns the constraint off in the same step: an IK constraint at 0, or a transform constraint with every mix 0, is passed over by the pass, and its bones are left to animation and the game's bone overrides: how a game lets a foot go in the air (`turning_foot_ik_off_with_an_override_frees_the_leg_in_the_same_step` in `tests/skeleton.rs`: in one step the game turns a leg's constraint off and swings the leg, and that step has the leg as the game set it; handed back, the next step reaches the target; `constrain::a_transform_constraint_whose_mixes_are_all_0_is_passed_over`, whose last case sets every mix of a transform constraint to 0 by override).
    - Kept by name through new data: each override's place is found again, and a name the new data lacks, or has as another kind, warns once and stays kept, so a later version that has it brings it back (`a_reload_that_removes_or_reorders_constraints_keeps_playing` in `tests/skeleton.rs`: two IK constraints and a transform constraint, one overridden and one keyed, saved reordered, with one retargeted, with the overridden one removed twice and brought back; after each the animation's time goes on, the leg reaches its target, the key moves its constraint at its new place, the override holds its constraint by name, the gone name warns once and nothing else is said; `component::an_override_through_a_reload_that_turns_its_constraint_s_kind_warns_once_and_is_kept`: an IK override through a reload that makes its constraint a transform constraint warns once, leaves it alone, and holds it again when it is IK once more).
  - **Points and bounding boxes** (`point`, `bounding_box`): each finds the first slot, in setup order, that shows an attachment of that name and kind now, and gives it in the world (a point's direction is its rotation carried by its bone's linear part). One a key or the game has hidden is not found, so an animation turns a hit box on and off by keying its slot.
  - **Readbacks** (`component::a_skeleton_says_what_it_wears_and_what_each_slot_shows`, `a_skeleton_says_what_it_plays_wears_and_shows`): `skins()` gives the names of the skins worn, in order, as an iterator over what the component keeps (nothing is allocated). Before the data lands they are the names kept, which cannot be checked; from then on only those the data has, so a kept name it lacks stays kept but does not read as worn. A `set_skins` reads back at once, as the lookup in the skins is made when it is asked (by the draw, `point` and `bounding_box`). `attachment(slot)` gives the name the slot shows as the last step left it (the game's, else the animations' key, else the setup attachment), so a `set_attachment` reads back after the next step. It answers only when a skin worn, or the default, has that name in the slot: a name no skin has shows nothing and reads `None`, as `point` and `bounding_box` do not find it. `None` too for a hidden slot, a slot the data lacks, and before the data lands.
  - **Bounds:** a skeleton remembers at most 64 names it has warned about (`MAX_WARNED`; past that a missing name is ignored in silence), and keeps at most 255 attachments, 255 bone overrides and 255 constraint overrides (one a slot, bone or constraint; before the data lands no name can be checked, so more are ignored with one warning: `component::names_the_data_lacks_warn_once_each_up_to_a_bound`, `component::what_is_set_before_the_data_lands_is_bounded`). Setting again what is already set allocates nothing, and an attachment's name is the data's own string when a skin has it.
- **Constraints in a step** (`constrain.rs`; the tests named are `constrain::`'s own unless a file is given). Every pose's bones are placed by `Skeleton::place`: a step's, a pose in the frame a skeleton appears (`appear`) and a placing again (`place_again`). For data with constraints it runs the constraint pass (`constrain`) once every bone has its place in the skeleton's space, the game's overrides applied, and before the bones are put in the world. So the pass works in the skeleton's own space, and sockets, `bone_world`, the drawer and drawing between steps get the constrained pose as they get any pose. The rules:
  - **Springs** (`spring.rs`, the model; the spring's arm of the pass in `constrain.rs`; the tests named are the files' own unless a file is given). A spring takes its turn in the data's order as the other kinds do: it reads its bone where animation and the constraints before it left it, writes the bone's own values, marks it, and places it and the bones below it again, so a later constraint wins over it as over IK, and the game's override on the bone is the spring's to sway.
    - **The model** (`spring::each_step_of_the_spring_model_matches_the_hand_worked_numbers`, each step and each amount against numbers worked by hand). Each spring follows two points of its bone in the world, its origin and its tip (its `length` along its x axis), each an offset from where animation puts it and the offset's speed, in world units, so the entity's movement and turn count and its flip enters through the world transform. Each step of `h` (the fixed timestep, whatever the animation's speed), for each point: its animated movement since the last step is clamped to `limit × h`; `offset -= moved × inertia`; then, in equal sub-steps, `speed += (-strength × offset + (wind, gravity)) × h_sub`, `speed *= e^(-damping × h_sub)`, `offset += speed × h_sub`, wind along the world's x and gravity along its y (down). Movement that is not finite counts as none, a step that is not above 0 or not finite changes nothing, and a point that comes out not finite is put at rest (`spring::what_is_not_finite_changes_nothing_and_comes_out_at_rest`). The entity's own turn counts as any movement: turned about the bone's origin, the tip is carried round and lags, so the bone turns less far than the entity (`constrain::the_entity_s_own_turn_drags_the_bone`).
    - **Sub-steps and their cap** (`spring::a_stiff_spring_is_stable_at_any_fixed_rate`). A step takes as many sub-steps as keep `h_sub × √strength` below 0.5 (`spring::sub_steps`: one at the defaults at 60 Hz, 34 at a strength of 10⁶ at 60 Hz, 9 at 240 Hz), and at most 64 (`MOST_SUB_STEPS`). Past that the strength saturates: it acts as the most the 64 keep stable (to a thousandth), so a strength of 10³⁰, or the largest float, costs 64 sub-steps, stays bounded and makes no NaN. The test kicks a point at 10⁶ and 10³⁰, at 60 and 240 Hz, and finds it never past 5.5 of the 5 left behind and at rest within 4 s, all four in well under a second. A stiff spring needs damping to match: at the default damping of 5 one of strength 10⁶ buzzes, a fast swing that dies away slowly. Raise damping with strength; critical damping is about `2 × √strength` (2,000 at 10⁶, 20 at the default 100).
    - **Onto the bone, and how mix enters** (the same `spring::` test for the numbers). `mix` scales the offsets, once: the sprung points are the animated ones plus each offset × `mix`. The bone's place moves by the origin's offset × `mix`, times `x` along the world's x and `y` along its y. The sprung bone runs from that place to the sprung tip; the bone turns by the angle from the animated bone (origin to tip) to it, times `rotate`, and its scale along its length is multiplied by `1 + (sprung length / animated length - 1) × scale_x`. So a bone whose place stays (`x` and `y` 0) pivots on its origin toward its lagging tip, and one whose place follows whole turns only as far as its two points lag differently. A bone whose animated length is 0 neither turns nor stretches, its place still following (`spring::a_bone_of_no_animated_length_neither_turns_nor_stretches`). Rule we chose: the turn and the stretch are measured to the line from where the bone's place goes, not from the sprung origin. Both points, given one movement and the same forces, keep equal offsets, so measured from the sprung origin a body's travel, wind and gravity would never turn a bone whose place stays; the two agree when `x` and `y` are 1. The move, turn and stretch are written into the bone's own values as a world-space override is (`spring::onto_bone`): the move back through the parent's world transform, the turn back through `root × Pose::direction_space`, the stretch times its x scale; under a flat parent nothing is written. At a mix of 0, or with both offsets 0, the spring writes, marks and places nothing again (`component::a_spring_at_rest_or_at_a_mix_of_0_poses_as_one_without`: a springs-only rig, which takes the constraint path, at rest and at a mix of 0 while it swings, steps and is drawn between steps as the rig without the spring, to the bit). A keyed `wind` blows it and an overridden `mix` takes it out or halves its offsets in the same step (`constrain::a_keyed_gust_and_an_overridden_mix_reach_a_spring`).
    - **It lags and settles** (`constrain::a_spring_lags_a_moving_parent_and_settles`: a tail carried at 180 units a second trails, swings back past the vertical once stopped and settles; two skeletons stepped alike match to the bit). Deterministic: the same steps give the same spring (`spring::the_same_steps_give_the_same_spring`). A strand lags link by link, each spring listed after its parent's, so each link lags its parent's sprung place and the lag grows down the strand (`constrain::a_strand_lags_link_by_link`, which also sways one bone with two springs).
    - **Where it is kept.** `Constrained` holds each spring's two points, its last animated origin and tip and, for the overlay, its tip's lag, sized when the data is taken up and never grown (`constrain::a_spring_lags_a_moving_parent_and_settles` checks the room). The step gives the pass its `dt`; a pose outside a step (`appear`) and a placing again (`place_again`) give none, so they apply the offsets the springs have and advance nothing (`constrain::pause_holds_a_spring_and_placing_again_does_not_advance_it`: placed again where it stands, three times, the step's pose to the bit and the springs as they were; placed again elsewhere, the springs as they were). The paused clock runs no step, so a spring holds where it swung to and goes on from there (`a_paused_clock_holds_a_swinging_spring_and_a_teleport_puts_it_at_rest` in `tests/skeleton.rs`).
    - **When it forgets** (offsets and speeds 0, the next points taken as its last; `system::a_spring_forgets_on_a_teleport_a_reload_and_an_appear`, each case found at rest after one step where the entity stands still): the data taken up (a new skeleton, a reload a step or the frame's pass takes up: `rebuild` makes the state anew), an appear (the same), a teleport (the step reads the entity's `PreviousGlobalTransform::skip`, which reached only the sockets before, so a jump inside a step and a pool's acquire count; and `skip_interpolation` resets the springs itself, for a jump between steps), a step after one the entity spent disabled, and the game's `Skeleton::reset_springs()`. The test also flings the same jump without the skip as far as the limit lets it.
    - **A hold keeps it, a disabled step does not** (`system::a_held_skeleton_s_springs_go_on_where_they_were`: held five steps, the springs unchanged; let go, one step on from where they were, not from rest). Both miss steps; what tells them apart is what the system sees: a held skeleton is visited and skipped, a disabled one is not visited at all. So the step records the number of each step it holds a skeleton through (`Skeleton::held`, written without marking the component changed) beside those it steps it in and the one it was posed after (`counted`), and a step whose number does not follow that one found the skeleton disabled (`Skeleton::arrive`, before the step). A gap before a hold is still a gap: a skeleton disabled, then enabled while its scene is held, has its springs put at rest at the first step that holds it, as a step would (`system::a_skeleton_disabled_then_enabled_under_a_hold_forgets_its_springs`). `took_step`'s own first placing for the drawer is unchanged: a hold still draws its first step at once.
    - **A flip mirrors it** (`constrain::a_flip_mirrors_a_spring_s_swing`: an entity turned 0.3 and swinging, flipped where it stands, has its tail's tip at the mirror image across the entity's axis and its speeds mirrored, and the step after goes on from the mirrored motion). `place`, finding the skeleton flipped another way than it was last placed (a step or the frame's pass), mirrors each offset and speed by `R × diag(±1) × R⁻¹` (`R` the entity's axes, unflipped) and each last point about the entity's origin, so a turning character's cape swings on round. The mirror is taken about the stand the last points were recorded under (the entity's transform at the last placing), not the new one: a character that turns round in a step it also moves in then takes that step's movement as its mirrored history would have it, and its swing carries on with no twitch (`constrain::a_flip_while_moving_carries_the_swing_on_as_its_mirrored_history_would`: carried right, then flipped and sent back left in one step, it matches a twin flipped all along and carried the mirrored way). An entity whose axes are flat forgets them instead (`constrain::a_flip_on_axes_gone_flat_makes_a_spring_forget`).
    - **Limit: a skeleton on a socket sees its carrier's motion a step late.** It is stepped where the socket stood the step before and placed again where it now stands without advancing, so the carrier's movement reaches its springs in its next step (no test of its own: the placing again is `pause_holds_a_spring_and_placing_again_does_not_advance_it`'s).
  - **Skeletons without constraints do no new work.** `place` branches on `data.constraints().is_empty()` and without constraints runs the code it ran before there were any. Such a skeleton keeps nothing for constraints: `Constrained` holds nothing and has no room, the pose holds no constraint values, and the data's lists are empty (`a_skeleton_without_constraints_keeps_no_constraint_state`, which also takes a skeleton from data with a constraint to data without).
  - **Order** (`constraints_apply_in_their_order`). Each constraint in the data's order changes its bones, and the bones it moved are placed again before the next is read. So of two constraints on one bone the later one's result stands, and a later constraint sees a target that an earlier one moved where it now is; the other way round, it sees it where animation left it. That holds across the kinds (`a_transform_constraint_then_an_ik_constraint_and_the_other_way_round`: an eye that an IK constraint points down and a transform constraint turns to a post's 0.5 ends at whichever is later; a level that copies an arm's turn copies it turned down when the arm's IK constraint comes first, and as animated when it comes after).
  - **An IK constraint** (`a_one_bone_two_bone_and_chain_constraint_each_reach_their_target_in_a_step`). Its target is the target bone's origin in the skeleton's space, brought into the space of its first bone's parent (back through the whole of `Pose::parent_space`, its translation too: `a_limb_under_a_moved_turned_and_unevenly_scaled_parent_reaches_its_target`, whose limb hangs from a parent away from the skeleton's origin, turned and scaled unevenly, and reaches to 0.01). `one_bone`, `two_bones_following` (with the constraint's kept knee) or `chain` is called by its bone count, with the bones' own values as animation and the overrides left them, the last bone's data `length`, the constraint's values in the pose and the setup's `uniform`. Two bones are copied out of the pose and written back; a chain's bones, which need not be next to one another there, are copied into scratch the skeleton keeps, solved, and their rotations copied back.
  - **A transform constraint** (`transform_constraint.rs`, above, for each mode's rules) applies the values the pose holds (`the_pose_s_constraint_values_return_to_setup_each_step_without_allocating`, whose transform constraint with every mix at 1 puts its bone on its target). Its bones are taken in bone order, whatever order it names them in, and each is set, and the bones below it placed again, before the next is read: so a bone and its own descendant can both be in one constraint, and the descendant is read where the bone above it now puts it (`a_bone_and_its_descendant_in_one_constraint_are_both_constrained`: an arm moved to a post and turned half way to it, and its forearm, named first, read under it and turned half way on from there; the tip below follows the forearm).
    - **A world mode** sets the bone's transform in the skeleton's space, whatever its inherit mode, and leaves its own values as they were (`a_world_mode_ignores_the_bone_s_inherit_mode`: in each of the five modes, a child of a turned and scaled parent takes the post's transform exactly, its own values to the bit, and a mark below it is placed under it). The bones below it are placed again; the bone itself is not, nor marked as changed.
    - **A local mode** changes the bone's own values, marks it changed, and places it and the bones below it again. It reads the target's own values as animation, the game's overrides and earlier constraints left them (`an_own_values_mode_reads_the_target_as_animation_overrides_and_earlier_constraints_left_it`: a keyed turn, an override's turn and place, an IK constraint listed before; one listed after turns the target once it has been read). The bones a local mode changes are among those kept as animated and put back before every pass (A pass starts from the animated pose, below).
    - **A later constraint wins.** A world-mode result is lost when a later constraint moves a bone above it: that bone is placed again from its own values, and, being unmarked, has its override applied afresh (`a_world_mode_result_is_lost_when_a_later_constraint_moves_an_ancestor`: a hand copied to a post is put back at the tip of the hip a later IK constraint turns, and one held at 0.2 in the world points at 0.2 again). A local-mode result is kept, as an IK result is, since it is in the bone's own values, and has the last word over the game's override on that bone (`an_own_values_result_is_kept_when_a_later_constraint_moves_an_ancestor`, either order, with the hand overridden).
    - **One whose mixes are all 0 is passed over** (`a_transform_constraint_whose_mixes_are_all_0_is_passed_over`), as an IK constraint at a mix of 0 is: nothing written, marked or placed again, so an override on its bone holds when a later constraint moves a bone above it. So is one with a mix that is not a number. At a rotate mix of 0.01 it applies and has the last word. The mixes are those the pose holds, keyed or overridden too (`a_keyed_mix_of_0_turns_a_constraint_off`, and this test's last case, every mix 0 by override).
    - **The pelvis case** (`a_pelvis_turns_six_tenths_of_its_trunk_s_lean`): a pelvis under the hip with a local rotate mix of 0.6 and a rotation offset of a quarter turn, targeting a torso whose own rotation is a quarter turn back plus a lean, turns six tenths of the lean.
  - **A pose of other data is left alone** (`a_pose_of_other_data_is_left_alone`). The pass's own lookups by a bone's or a constraint's index are made with `get`. The pose's helpers it calls (`Pose::parent_space`, `Pose::place_bone`) index directly, which is sound because the pass first checks the pose's count of bones against the data's and does not touch a pose of another count: a skeleton makes its pose anew before it places it, so this guards a mistake, not a path a game takes.
  - **A flat parent leaves the bones as animated** (`a_flat_parent_leaves_the_bones_as_animated`). When no point can be brought back through the first bone's parent (the determinant of its linear part is within 1e-12 of 0, or not finite: `component::invertible`, as for a world-space override), the constraint is passed over whole: its bones keep their own values as animation, overrides and earlier constraints left them, to the bit, none is marked as changed and nothing is placed again. The test scales the parent by 0, and by 1e-20: a target brought back through that is finite and far off, and only this rule stops the bones turning to it.
  - **Placed again** (`a_constraint_places_the_descendants_of_its_bones_again`). After an IK constraint its bones and every bone below them are placed again from their own values (`Pose::place_bone`), in bone order, from the list the data worked out as it was built: a hand below an IK arm, and what the hand carries, follow the arm in the same step.
  - **Overrides come first, and still hold.** The game's overrides are applied as the bones are first placed, so one on a target bone steers its constraint in the same step (`a_target_moved_by_a_world_override_is_reached_in_the_same_step`). A bone placed again that the game overrides, and that no constraint has changed in this pass, has its override applied afresh: it is put back to its animated own values (`SetOverride::animated`) and `apply_override` is run on them against the pose as it now stands. So a world-space override on a bone below an IK bone still points where the game asked, and an override at a part mix is not applied twice (`a_world_override_below_an_ik_bone_still_holds`: a foot held at an angle in the world under a leg whose target moves is within 0.001 rad of it at two targets, and at half its mix is half way from where the shin carries it; `world_and_local_overrides_below_a_limb_come_out_as_asked_after_it_moves`: a world translation on the hand below a limb that moves puts it at the point asked for, and a local override at half its mix on a bone below the limb gives it its own values half way, once).
    - **An override first set between steps** has no animated values kept until its next step, so a placing again before that step (the skeleton rides a socket, or is flipped) first takes them from the pose as it stands (`Skeleton::place_again`): it is applied afresh below a constraint, as a step applies it (`an_override_first_set_between_steps_holds_when_its_skeleton_is_placed_again`: a foot so held points where the game asked, placed again twice), and at a part mix it is applied once, however often the skeleton is placed again (`an_override_at_a_part_mix_first_set_between_steps_is_applied_once_however_often_it_is_placed_again`). This holds for skeletons without constraints too (`an_override_first_set_between_steps_holds_on_a_skeleton_without_constraints`). It is wrong for an override cleared and set again between steps: the pose as it stands then holds the old override's result, which the new one is applied over, as it was before an override took its animated values here.
  - **The constraint has the last word on its own bones** (`a_constraint_has_the_last_word_over_an_override_on_its_own_bone`). A solve starts from its bones as the overrides left them (an override of 0.5 and an IK mix of a half end half way from 0.5 to the solved turn). The bones of a constraint that has been applied are marked (`Constrained::changed`, cleared as each pass starts), and a marked bone placed again, because a later constraint moved a bone above it, keeps its own values: the override is not put back over the constraint's result.
  - **An IK constraint at a mix of 0 is passed over** (`a_constraint_has_the_last_word_over_an_override_on_its_own_bone`), as one under a flat parent is: no solve, no mark, nothing placed again. So is one whose mix is not a number (the test is `!(mix > 0)`). The mix is the one the pose holds, however it came to be 0: the setup's, a key's (`a_keyed_mix_of_0_turns_a_constraint_off`) or the game's override (`turning_foot_ik_off_with_an_override_frees_the_leg_in_the_same_step` in `tests/skeleton.rs`). Its bones are left to the game: one with a world-space override, placed again because a later constraint moved a bone above it, has its override applied afresh and points where the game asked. A constraint that is applied marks every one of its bones, whatever the solve wrote; whether it wrote anything is not judged by comparing the bones' values before and after, which would flip on rounding whenever the solved pose equals the one passed in.
    - **Limit: the treatment changes as the mix leaves 0.** In that corner (a world-space override on an IK bone, and a later constraint moving a bone above it), at a mix of 0 the override is applied afresh, and above 0 the constraint has the last word and the bone is carried round by the bone above it: an eye held at 0.3 in the world under a hip that a later constraint turns a quarter turn points at 0.3 at a mix of 0 and about 1.6 further round at 0.01 (`a_world_override_on_an_ik_bone_is_the_game_s_at_a_mix_of_0_and_the_constraint_s_above_it`, on an entity turned and moved so that the world and the skeleton's space differ, which also passes a mix that is not a number over).
  - **Bends under a flip and a mirrored ancestor** (`a_flipped_entity_and_a_mirrored_ancestor_keep_the_bend_side`). The pass works before the root transform, which holds the entity's flip, so a flipped entity's pose in the skeleton's space is the unflipped one's to the bit: the joint is on the same side of the limb there, and the limb is drawn mirrored, joint and all. A target the game puts at a point in the world is reached flipped or not, the joint on the positive side in the skeleton's space and so on the other side on screen. Under a mirrored ancestor the bend is judged in the first bone's parent's space, as the solve judges it: the limb is the mirror image of the one an unmirrored parent gives a mirrored target. A character turned round either way keeps its knees.
  - **A pass starts from the animated pose** (`a_skeleton_placed_again_has_its_step_s_pose`). IK constraints and local transform constraints write bones' own values, so the own values of the bones they change are kept as animation left them (`Constrained::animated`, kept in `Skeleton::finish` beside each override's), and `place_again` puts them back, with the overridden bones' and the constraints' values (`Constrained::values`, kept before the constraint overrides are written), writes the constraint overrides again, and then places the bones and runs the pass. So a skeleton placed again where it stands has its step's pose to the bit, in the skeleton's space, in the world and in its bones' own values, however often it is placed; and placed again elsewhere, turned and flipped, it has the pose a step there gives. The test's rig has two IK constraints (the second at half its mix), a world-mode and a local relative transform constraint after them, an animation that turns a constrained bone, a world-space override on the target, one on a bone the second constraint changes and one at half its mix on the bone below the limb.
  - **Sockets** (`a_socket_on_a_constrained_bone_follows_it_in_the_same_step` in `tests/skeleton.rs`): a socket at the tip of an IK arm is at the arm's target inside the step the game moved the target in, while the entity moves and turns.
  - **Drawn as any pose** (`tests/e2e/skeleton_constraints.rs`): a two-bone arm's hand and elbow are drawn where the 3-4-5 triangle puts them, at two targets (`a_two_bone_arm_reaches_a_moved_target`); a constrained skeleton is mirrored whole by a flip, sorts by where it stands and not by where its hand reaches, and is drawn half way between two constrained poses half a step on (`a_constrained_skeleton_flips_sorts_and_draws_between_steps_as_any_other`); a part a world-mode transform constraint holds level is drawn level, every pixel, while the arm carrying it points right, down and left (`a_part_held_level_stays_level_while_its_arm_turns`); and a tail on a spring trails a body carried right and settles straight below it (`a_tail_on_a_spring_swings_behind_a_moving_body_and_settles`).
- **New data** (`a_reload_keeps_the_playing_animation_and_its_time`, `a_reload_that_removes_the_playing_animation_clears_the_track_with_one_warning`; a reload landing in mid-crossfade keeps both entries and how far the mix has run, and the mix ends when it would have, the pose finite throughout: `a_reload_that_removes_a_socket_s_bone_warns_once_and_keeps_playing`): a skeleton follows its data by `(AssetId, version)`, as particles' compiled effects do, with no event handling: a reload keeps the id and moves the version on, and so does a change made in code. The step that sees another version makes the pose anew (`Pose::setup`) before the animation state applies, finds each kept name's place again (a name the new data lacks warns once per skeleton per name and stays kept, so a later version that has it brings it back), and carries each bone's last transform in the skeleton's space to `previous_space` by name; a bone the old data lacked gets its current one, so it draws where it is (`component::the_last_two_steps_transforms_are_kept_by_bone_name_across_new_data`). No deform offsets are carried to the new pose: its meshes may hold other counts of positions, so the first pose of new data draws the shapes its own deform keys give it. What the skeleton keeps to apply constraints is made anew for the new data (`constrain::a_skeleton_without_constraints_keeps_no_constraint_state`), and its constraint overrides are found again by name and kind (Constraint overrides, above; `a_reload_that_removes_or_reorders_constraints_keeps_playing`). A reload that changes a mesh's vertex count, or makes it a region, while its deform plays draws the setup shape (or the region), reads nothing out of range, and is told of once. A save that takes the mesh out of every skin and leaves its deform does not load (Validation rules): the reload fails with an error naming the deform, and the skeleton plays on with the data it had (`a_reload_that_changes_a_mesh_s_vertex_count_skips_its_deform`, which saves the file five times, one of them that save). That is a step that sees new data; a pose made outside a step for new data (`appear`) is drawn as it is, every bone's previous transform its current one, since no step lies between the two. The animation state follows through the data's catalogue (New data, above).
- **Sockets** (`BoneSocket { bone, offset, inherit_scale }`, on a child of the skeleton's entity; `a_socket_is_where_its_bone_is_in_the_same_step`, `a_socket_child_s_children_follow_in_the_same_step`). How they are placed, and why propagation stays right:
  - The socket's local `Transform` is the truth. Step 6 sets it to the bone's transform in skeleton space times the offset (`BoneSocket::local`), through `set_if_neq`, so it is marked changed only when the bone moved it, as any edit would mark it.
  - Its `GlobalTransform` is then written as `skeleton entity's GlobalTransform.mul_transform(local)`, and each descendant's as its parent's times its own `Transform`, walking down as propagation walks (a child without a `Transform`, or disabled, is not placed and nothing below it follows; a subtree under a socket that did not move is not visited).
  - That is the expression propagation uses on the same inputs, so the step and propagation write the same bits. The next propagation runs (the frame's in `PostUpdate`, the next step's) see the socket's `Transform` changed, recompute it and its subtree, find every value equal and write nothing (`system::propagation_after_the_step_writes_what_the_step_wrote`); nothing is hidden from propagation's change tracking, and a descendant's own edit is placed as ever. A still skeleton's socket is not rewritten at all (`system::a_still_socket_is_not_rewritten`).
  - Interpolation and `PreviousGlobalTransform` are propagation's as for any child: under an `Interpolated` skeleton entity a socket draws between where the last step's bone put it and where this step's does. That is right only while it moves with its bone. When it arrives on a bone instead, or its skeleton's entity skips, the placing marks it and everything below it, moved or not, as having jumped (`jumped`): `PreviousGlobalTransform::skip` is set, so an interpolated one is drawn where it stands until the next step, and a `ParticleEmitter` is marked as `skip_interpolation` marks an entity's own (its crate-internal `teleported`), so its next step's particles spawn where it stands and not along the way there. The emitter is told itself because the next step's snapshot clears the skip before the particle step reads it (`system::an_emitter_below_a_socket_is_marked_when_the_socket_jumps`, `an_emitter_on_a_socket_does_not_spread_its_particles_along_a_jump`).
    - **An arrival** is any placing but one where this skeleton's last placing put the socket on this same bone. Each new pose of a skeleton (a step, or a pose in a frame) takes a number no placing in the world has had (`SkeletonStore::next_placing`; the skeleton keeps its last two), and a socket keeps the skeleton it stands on and the number of the placing that last put it there (`SocketState::on`). A socket moves with its bone if it stood in the skeleton's placing before this one, and arrives otherwise. So these are arrivals: a new socket; its skeleton's first pose (its data landing later); a bone found again; another bone named (`system::a_socket_renamed_to_another_bone_is_drawn_on_it_at_once`); another skeleton, or a new `Skeleton` on the same entity; and any placing it missed, detached, disabled or not a socket for a while (`system::a_socket_dropped_and_picked_up_again_does_not_slide_back`). A `BoneSocket` put on an entity starts its state afresh (its `on_insert` hook), so one taken off and put back is a new socket whenever that happens (`system::a_socket_taken_off_and_put_back_does_not_slide_back`). A held or disabled skeleton has no placings, so its sockets are still moving with their bones when it carries on. Without the mark an arrival is drawn sliding: a propagation run has given the socket a previous transform where it waited, and the step's snapshot has taken that as where it was (`system::a_socket_first_placed_does_not_slide_from_where_it_waited`, `system::a_socket_whose_skeleton_s_data_lands_later_does_not_slide`).
    - **A skeleton whose entity skips** takes its sockets with it, by two ways that each cover what the other cannot. The placing reads the entity's `PreviousGlobalTransform::skip` and carries it: that is a jump made inside a step, after its snapshot, as for any entity, and an entity a pool hands out (`system::a_skeleton_whose_entity_skips_in_a_step_takes_its_sockets_with_it`, where the mark is set by hand so that only the placing can carry it). And `skip_interpolation` itself marks the sockets and what is below them as it is called (`skip_sockets`): that is a jump made outside a step, the clock paused, which no placing follows (`system::a_jump_made_outside_a_step_reaches_a_skeleton_s_sockets`).
  - Each socket has a crate-internal `SocketState` beside it (a required component, so `BoneSocket` stays a plain public struct): the bone last looked up, where it stands, and the missing name it has been told about. The bone is kept with the name asked for and the data's `(AssetId, version)` it was looked up in, and it is looked up again when either is another. Both are compared as they are at every placing (a string compare, no hash), never by change detection: a system's "changed since I last ran" is used up by a run that does not visit the socket, so a rename made while the skeleton was held or disabled, or the socket disabled or detached, would never be seen, and the socket would stay on its old bone for good (`system::a_socket_renamed_while_its_skeleton_is_held_takes_its_new_bone`, `..._is_disabled_takes_its_new_bone`, `system::a_socket_given_a_real_bone_while_held_attaches_and_is_not_told_it_is_missing`, `system::a_socket_put_over_another_while_held_takes_the_new_one_s_bone`, `system::a_socket_follows_its_bone_s_name_and_its_data_as_they_change`).
  - Not taken: writing the bone's exact matrix into `GlobalTransform`. Propagation re-places a child from its `Transform` whenever its parent moves, so a matrix the `Transform` cannot hold would be overwritten in every frame the skeleton's entity moved, unless propagation learned to skip sockets.
  - **Limits.** A `Transform` has no shear, so a skew in the bone's skeleton-space transform (a sheared bone, or a turned bone under an unevenly scaled one) is dropped: the socket points along the bone's x axis, as long as it, and as tall as the bone is across it, mirrored when the bone is (`socket::a_skewed_bone_s_socket_keeps_its_direction_and_drops_the_skew`). The skeleton entity's own transform is applied whole, as to any child, so an unevenly scaled and turned entity skews its sockets exactly as it skews its bones. Without `inherit_scale` the socket takes the bone's place and direction only (`socket::without_scale_a_socket_takes_its_bone_s_place_and_direction_only`). Colliders on a socket follow the bone a physics step late, since physics runs before propagation. A sprite on a socket is an entity of its own in the draw order, so it draws wholly in front of the skeleton or wholly behind it by its z: an item the hand must wrap is a region attachment in a slot between the hand's slots, not a socket's sprite. An ordinary child of the skeleton's entity (not a socket, nor below one) is not reached by the entity's `skip_interpolation`, as under any parent.
  - **A bone the skeleton lacks** warns once for that name and leaves the entity where it is on the skeleton, however many steps and versions of the data pass. Naming another bone the skeleton lacks is another mistake and warns again; a bone that is found clears what was told, so the same name lost again later warns again (`system::a_missing_bone_is_told_once_a_name_and_a_bone_found_again_is_not_slid_to`). If a later version of the data has the bone, the socket follows again, drawn on it at once (`a_reload_that_removes_a_socket_s_bone_warns_once_and_keeps_playing`). A socket whose parent is not a skeleton, or whose skeleton's data has not landed, does nothing and says nothing.
  - **A skeleton on a socket** (a rider on a mount): skeletons step in entity order, so one may be posed before the socket that carries it moves. Each skeleton a socket's placing moves is noted and, after every skeleton has stepped, placed again where it now stands (`Skeleton::place_again`: its overrides and its constraints afresh from the animated pose, its world transforms, its sockets, which may move another in turn). Nothing advances or is sent twice (`system::a_skeleton_on_a_socket_is_posed_where_the_socket_puts_it`). A constrained skeleton so carried has, after the frame's placing again, the pose a skeleton stepped where it now stands has, to the bit (`system::a_constrained_skeleton_on_a_socket_has_the_pose_a_step_gives_it_where_it_lands`: a limb at half its mix reaching for a target held at a point in the world).
- **Messages** (`events_reach_the_game_as_messages_in_time_order`, `track_events_reach_the_game`; both registered with the engine's messages): a `SkeletonEvent` carries the skeleton's entity, the track and animation, the event's name, the key's values (the event's where the key gives none) with its audio path, volume and balance (played by the engine for a skeleton with `SkeletonSounds`, below; a game may still play them itself, turning the linear volume into `PlayOptions::volume_db` and the balance into `PlayOptions::pan`), and the key's time; a step's events are in the order they happened, across tracks. A `SkeletonTrackEvent` carries the entity, track, the entry's `TrackEntryId`, its animation's name and the kind; the empty animation's carry `SkeletonAnimation::EMPTY` and never `Complete`. A despawned skeleton sends nothing more, and what its last calls caused goes untold (`a_despawned_skeleton_sends_nothing_more`).
- **Allocation:** the step keeps its buffers in a `Local` (the order, what a skeleton fired, the walk below a socket, the skeletons moved), and a skeleton's step swaps its two buffers of the bones' transforms in its own space (`pose.skeleton_space` and `previous_space`; the world transforms are computed over in place) and each slot's two lists of deform offsets, so a steady step allocates nothing, with deforms playing or not (`component::a_steady_step_takes_no_more_room_for_its_deforms`). A skeleton's constraints are applied in what it keeps for them (`Constrained`: the animated values of the bones they change, the scratch its longest chain is solved in, and each spring's motion; a transform constraint needs none), made when the data is taken up (`constrain::a_one_bone_two_bone_and_chain_constraint_each_reach_their_target_in_a_step`, by the scratch's pointers and capacities over a pass and over steps; and a steady frame of fifty constrained skeletons, keys, overrides and moved targets included, allocates nothing the same entities as sprites do not: `fifty_skeletons_with_constraints_allocate_nothing_per_frame` in `tests/tilemap_alloc.rs`). What does allocate: new data (the pose, and what the constraints keep); a first warning; the first deform key to move a mesh of the skeleton, which makes the list of every slot's offsets; each slot's first deform, which sizes that slot's two lists (this step's as the key is blended in; the other in the next step, when the swap brings it round, or at once where the slot's attachment has just changed or the pose is settled); and a later mesh in the slot that holds more positions, which grows them the same way.

## Event sounds (`sounds.rs`)

- **`SkeletonSounds`** (a component on the skeleton's entity, in the prelude) has the engine play each event's sound once as its event fires (`a_footstep_event_plays_its_sound_once`): `new()` (also `default()`) at the skeleton's place in the world, heard from there as the sound's spatial settings say; `flat()` with no place; `at_bone(name)` at the bone's world place, and at the skeleton's for a name it lacks, warned once per name per skeleton data (kept with the store, so a reload tells again), as a socket's missing bone is (`skeleton_sounds_play_at_the_skeleton_flat_and_at_a_bone`, which also turns the skeleton a quarter to check the bone's place is its world one; `a_missing_bone_is_warned_once_a_name_and_its_sounds_play_at_the_skeleton`). `SkeletonEvent` is still sent (`a_footstep_event_plays_its_sound_once`).
- **The system** (`play_skeleton_sounds`, exclusive) is chained after `update_skeletons` in `FixedSet::Skeletons`, so it reads the events of the step that sent them. With no `SkeletonSounds` in the world it only moves its readers on: a skeleton without one takes the path it took before, and loads no audio (`the_sounds_are_loaded_once`: its paths are never read).
- **The store** (kept by the system): per `AssetId<SkeletonData>`, a handle to each distinct audio path its events name, with the data's version it was taken at. A path ending `.sound.ron` is loaded as a `Sound`, any other as an `AudioClip`; the path stays a string in the data, not a dependency. It is filled in the first step that finds a `SkeletonSounds` skeleton showing data whose version it has not seen (the data landing, a reload, a change in code), the new handles taken before the old drop so a path kept through a reload is not read again; it is emptied on `AssetEvent::Removed`, which frees the sounds (`the_sounds_are_loaded_once`: two skeletons sharing data read each sound file once, a change in code reads nothing again, and the sounds leave their store once the data goes). A reload that names another path plays the new sound once it loads (`skeleton_sounds_follow_their_scene_and_a_reload`).
- **Loading is asynchronous**: an event whose sound is not in its store yet plays nothing, with a debug log (never the audio engine's one-shot-while-loading warning, nor a refusal in the audio log), and the events skipped are not made up once it lands (`a_sound_still_loading_is_skipped_without_a_warning`).
- **Playing**: volume is `20 × log10(max(volume, 0.001))` dB (`an_event_s_volume_is_its_level_in_db`; a volume that is not finite is 0 dB), balance becomes `PlayOptions::pan` (a balance that is not finite is 0), and a place that is not finite plays flat (`a_place_or_balance_that_is_not_finite_plays_flat_and_centred`, through `SkeletonEvent`s a game wrote: valid data holds no non-finite value, and the system plays every `SkeletonEvent` of a `SkeletonSounds` skeleton, whoever wrote it). Balance pans a flat play and a play at a place whose sound has no spatial settings; a sound heard from its place takes its pan from there (`balance_pans_a_flat_play`, `skeleton_sounds_play_at_the_skeleton_flat_and_at_a_bone`). A key's own volume and balance win over its event's (`balance_pans_a_flat_play`).
- **Scenes**: each play runs with the audio engine's scope set to the skeleton's scene (`Skeleton::scope`), so its voice is that scene's: a long one stops with the scene's exit, and a held scene's skeleton fires no events, so plays nothing (`skeleton_sounds_follow_their_scene_and_a_reload`). A skeleton of no scene plays the engine's voices, which a scene's exit leaves playing (`a_skeleton_of_no_scene_plays_the_engine_s_voices`).
- **A looping sound** named by an event is played as its definition says: each firing starts another loop, and only its scene's exit stops them (the system keeps no voice). It is warned about once per sound (`a_looping_sound_named_by_an_event_is_warned_once_and_stacks`).
- **Limits**: the sounds play from the fixed step, so up to a step late, as every skeleton event is. A voice is not tied to its skeleton: despawning it does not stop a sound already playing. A sound played at a place stays where it started; it does not follow the skeleton or the bone (a long sound on a moving skeleton is left behind). In a crowd sharing one sound, the sound's `per_frame` limit (one start a frame by default) keeps the first skeleton in entity order whose event fired that frame, not the nearest to the listener.

## Drawing (`draw.rs`, `render/entities.rs`, `debug.rs`)

A skeleton is drawn when its entity has a `Renderable::skeleton()` (`RenderStyle::Skeleton`; its `image`, `anchor` and `target` are not used). The entity drawer treats it as one drawable among the entities (render module notes, Draw Order and Placement), so it takes its z, its `YSort` order, its floor and its `RenderLayers` exactly as a sprite does. `tests/e2e/skeleton.rs` holds the pixel tests named below, `tests/e2e/skeleton_mesh.rs` those of meshes and `tests/e2e/skeleton_constraints.rs` those of constrained skeletons (both with the rigs and helpers of the first); `draw::`, `debug::` and `component::` tests are the files' own, and `testing::gpu_world::a_skeleton_s_regions_are_entity_runs_split_where_the_image_or_blend_changes` and `testing::gpu_world::a_skeleton_s_mesh_is_an_item_between_the_runs_of_its_regions` read the draw list.

- **Where it draws** (`Skeleton::drawn`). Under its entity's transform as the frame draws a sprite's: `render_global` (between the entity's last two steps when it is interpolated), plus its `DrawLift`, times its `YSort` depth scale, then mirrored by its flip. The bones are placed under that from their transforms in the skeleton's own space, never from `pose.world`, which is only as fresh as the last step: an entity moved while no step runs (a teleport outside a step, a pooled acquire, a paused clock) draws its skeleton where the frame draws the entity: at its new place at once when nothing is blended, which is a plain entity (`a_skeleton_moved_between_steps_draws_at_its_new_place_that_frame`, with `Interpolated` taken off) or an interpolated one that skips (`a_skeleton_spawned_while_the_clock_is_paused_draws_that_frame`, moved with `skip_interpolation`). All of the entity's transform carries the bones, a skew or a mirroring included, where a sprite takes only its position, turn and axis lengths.
- **Between steps.** A bone draws `alpha` of the way from `previous_space` to `pose.skeleton_space` (`draw::blend`) when its entity is drawn between its own steps (it has a `PreviousGlobalTransform` that does not skip) and the skeleton took the last fixed step; else where it is. So bones, sockets and the entity are interpolated together or not at all: on a plain entity the bones draw where the last step put them, as its sockets and the entity do, and `skip_interpolation` on the entity skips the bones with it (`skeleton_bones_are_interpolated_between_steps`, bones and a socket together). **A skeleton's entity is interpolated without asking**: `Skeleton` requires `Interpolated` (a required component, beside `Transform`), so putting a skeleton on an entity adds it, and a game takes it off for a skeleton drawn at the step rate (`system::a_skeleton_s_entity_is_interpolated_until_the_component_is_taken_off`; the pixel test's second skeleton has it taken off, and `a_skeleton_moved_between_steps_draws_at_its_new_place_that_frame` runs on such a plain entity). Why: without it a skeleton animates at the step rate in silence, the wrong default for a character. What follows from it:
  - The entity's own place is drawn between steps too, as any interpolated entity's is: moved by hand outside a step, it is drawn there a step later unless it skips (`skip_interpolation`), which is why the pixel tests that place a skeleton between frames read the second step's frame (`settled`).
  - A bundle cannot leave a required component out: it is removed after the spawn (`world.entity_mut(entity).remove::<Interpolated>()`), and it stays off as the skeleton steps.
  - Inserting a `Skeleton` on the entity again adds it again.
  - One rule for the whole entity stays: smooth bones on a root and sockets that step would come apart.
  - `blend` is `GlobalTransform::lerp`'s rule carried to a bone's transform: its place along a line, the direction of its x axis the short way round, its lengths evenly; and its y axis blended in the frame its x axis points along, which keeps a skew and a mirroring as it turns (an entity's transform holds neither). What did not change stays exact. Where the x axis has no length, or the bone is mirrored in one and not in the other (a scale keyed through zero), each number is blended on its own, so the bone flattens and opens again with no turn (`draw::` tests).
  - Rule we chose: the blend is of each bone's transform in the skeleton's space, not of the local poses. A child of a bone turning fast draws a chord's sagitta off its parent's tip mid-step (a 60 px bone turning 10 degrees a step: about 0.2 px).
  - **Deform offsets are drawn between steps as bones are** (`Skeleton::drawn_deform`): a mesh's offsets are that same fraction of the way from the step before's to the last step's, and the last step's wherever the bones draw where they are. A list with nothing in it is the setup shape, drawn from and drawn to, so a deform that starts or ends is drawn growing from, or shrinking to, the setup shape (`draw::a_deform_draws_between_its_last_two_steps_when_both_fit_the_mesh`, `a_deform_is_interpolated_between_steps`, an unweighted and a weighted mesh on bones that stand still).
  - **Every read of the offsets is checked against the mesh the frame finds in the slot.** The lists were filled by a step for the mesh the slot showed then, and a skin shows at once, so a frame can find a mesh of another count there. A list is used only if it holds an x and a y for each position of that mesh: this step's of another length moves nothing (the setup shape), and the step before's of another length is not drawn from, this step's being drawn as it is. No read goes past a list (the same `draw::` test, with lists too short and too long; `a_skin_swap_to_another_shape_skips_the_deform_without_repeating_the_warning`, where the frame after a skin is put on draws its mesh while the pose holds the other's offsets).
  - **A step number** tells a skeleton that stood still from one drawn between steps. `SkeletonStore::next_step` numbers each run of `update_skeletons`, and a skeleton keeps the number of its last step (`took_step`). The drawer blends only a skeleton whose number is the store's latest: one held, disabled or waiting for its data while the last step ran draws where it is, or it would sway with the frame's fraction. And a step that follows one the skeleton missed is a first placing, each bone's previous transform its current one, so a skeleton enabled again does not blend from the pose it had (`a_skeleton_enabled_again_does_not_blend_from_its_old_pose`, `component::a_step_after_a_missed_one_and_a_pose_outside_a_step_are_first_placings`). A hold is a missed step too: a skeleton released from one draws its first step's pose at once. A pose made outside a step takes the latest number (`posed_after`), so the step after it follows on.
- **What it draws** (`Skeleton::parts`, into the drawer's `Parts`). One walk of the pose's draw order, back to front, gives the skeleton's parts: a slot that shows a region attachment is a quad on its bone (the attachment's offset, turn, scale and size, placed by its pivot: Data, above), and one that shows a mesh attachment is its vertices where the skinning puts them (Meshes, below). A part's colour is its slot's (clamped to 0..1 here, where the pose keeps it unclamped; not a number counts as 0) times its attachment's. A slot showing nothing, a point or a bounding box, or a name no skin has, draws nothing. A skeleton with no pose, or whose pose was made from another version of its data than the store holds, draws no part: its slots and bones are not that data's (`draw::a_skeleton_with_no_pose_for_the_data_draws_nothing`, `draw::a_mesh_that_cannot_be_placed_is_left_out_and_nothing_panics`; after `pose_new_skeletons` the drawer finds a pose in every frame, `a_skeleton_spawned_while_the_clock_is_paused_draws_that_frame`). Its shadows draw nothing then either: the entity drawer leaves a skeleton with no pose for its data out whole (`a_skeleton_whose_pose_is_stale_draws_nothing_not_even_its_shadows`, and for a body that is a mesh `a_mesh_whose_pose_is_stale_draws_nothing_not_even_its_shadow`). That state is reached only by the case the frame's pass does not cover, a skeleton enabled again between steps after its data changed.
- **Meshes** (`Parts::skin`). A mesh attachment draws as a region of its image would: triangles of the region's rectangle, a UV for each vertex running 0 to 1 across it, in its slot's place in the draw order.
  - **Skinning is on the CPU, in each frame the mesh is drawn**, from the bones as the frame draws them (`Skeleton::drawn_bone`: between their last two steps when the skeleton is) under the root the regions take (the entity's `render_global`, its lift, its depth scale and its flip). An unweighted vertex is a place in the space of its slot's bone (`an_unweighted_mesh_draws_where_its_bone_puts_it`). A weighted vertex is the sum, over its influences, of where each one's bone puts the place given in that bone's space, times the influence's weight; the slot's own bone plays no part (`a_weighted_mesh_bends_between_two_bones`, where a middle vertex half one bone's and half another's goes half as far as the bone that moves, and folds when it turns; `draw::meshes_and_regions_come_in_the_draw_order_each_mesh_skinned_to_its_bones` has the sums).
  - **Deform.** Each position's offset is added to its place before its bone carries it: an unweighted vertex's in the space of the slot's bone, and each influence's in that influence's bone's space, before the influences are weighted and summed. So an offset turns and scales with the bone it is given in, and a vertex moves by an influence's offset times that influence's weight (`draw::a_deform_moves_each_position_in_its_bone_s_space_before_it_is_weighted`; `a_deform_key_moves_a_mesh_s_pixels`, a flag stretched, stretched at half alpha and slanted, and one on a turned pole). A mesh no deform moves is skinned with no offset looked up. The body, the drop shadow and the overlay all read the skinned positions, so each follows the deform (the same pixel test for the shadow; `debug::the_overlay_draws_a_mesh_s_triangles_and_its_hull_where_it_is_skinned` for the triangles and the hull). So does an effect on the entity: a hit flash whitens the mesh where the deform puts it, every pixel of the deformed shape and none the setup shape alone had (`an_effect_on_a_deformed_mesh_shows_where_the_deform_puts_it`).
  - **Each bone a skeleton's meshes use is placed once a frame, not once a vertex** (`Parts::placed`: blended between its steps, then put under the root, and kept for the rest of that skeleton's meshes; `draw::each_bone_a_mesh_uses_is_placed_once_a_skeleton`, which counts the calls). The regions' own lookups are as they were, one a slot, so a skeleton of regions alone never touches it.
  - **Scratch.** The entity drawer keeps one `Parts` from frame to frame (`EntityDrawer::parts`, recycled as its list of drawables is): the list of parts, every mesh's skinned vertices and the placed bones, filled again for each skeleton in turn, so a steady frame allocates nothing (`draw::walking_a_skeleton_again_takes_no_more_room`). The vertices are then read once into the batch's mesh stream, which keeps its room too. `fifty_skeletons_with_a_deforming_mesh_allocate_nothing_per_frame` (`tests/tilemap_alloc.rs`) holds it in the engine: fifty skeletons, each with a weighted mesh of 60 vertices among its regions and a deform playing on it, make no allocation of the engine's own in a frame that the same entities drawn as sprites do not make, and none from the skeletons' path, which counts the drawer's mesh functions and the mesh stream; and no call the engine makes into the GPU API allocates more for them than for the sprites but for a small room, so a buffer or a bind group made for each mesh would be told wherever it was made (the encoder's finish apart, where wgpu's own four allocations a mesh are: `docs/backlog/skeleton.md`, Allocations).
  - **In the draw order, across images** (`meshes_and_regions_keep_the_draw_order_across_images`: a mesh between two regions, a region between two meshes, a skeleton that is all meshes, a mesh first and last, on one image and on three, and under a draw-order key; `testing::gpu_world::a_skeleton_s_mesh_is_an_item_between_the_runs_of_its_regions`).
  - **Exactly a region's pixels.** A two-triangle mesh of a region draws what a region attachment of it draws, pixel for pixel: tinted, faded, flipped, shadowed, lit or unlit, additive, emissive, flashing, with its slot's and attachment's colours, in a slot with a blend of its own, upright, doubled, turned and mirrored, and on a bone of its own that is turned a quarter turn and twice as long, under an entity upright and turned (`a_mesh_draws_as_the_region_of_its_image`). On whole pixels: see the snap, below.
  - **Sorted, lit and lifted with its entity** (`a_mesh_follows_ysort_light_and_floor_as_its_regions_do`), **mirrored with it** by a flip, a negative scale on the entity or a mirrored bone, its triangles then wound the other way and drawn all the same (`a_mesh_mirrors_with_its_skeleton_and_under_a_mirrored_bone`), and **drawn between steps** when its skeleton is, a weighted vertex following the bones that hold it and not its slot's (`a_mesh_is_interpolated_between_steps`).
  - **Culled by its skinned vertices' box**, not by where its entity stands or its slot's bone is: a skeleton out of view whose bone carries a mesh into view draws it, and a mesh carried out of view costs no draw (`a_mesh_off_the_screen_is_culled_by_its_skinned_bounds`, by the frame's draw calls).
  - **What draws no mesh.** A vertex skinned to a place that is not finite (an entity scaled past what a float carries, or by no number) draws no mesh, as a quad that is not finite draws no region, and nothing panics (`a_mesh_skinned_to_no_finite_place_draws_nothing`, `draw::a_mesh_that_cannot_be_placed_is_left_out_and_nothing_panics`). A mesh whose image is not loaded draws what a region of it draws: nothing in a release build, the checker over the whole part in a debug build (`a_mesh_whose_image_is_missing_draws_what_its_region_draws`).
  - **What the skeleton answers** is the same with a mesh shown: `attachment(slot)` gives its name, and `point` and `bounding_box` pass over a mesh of the name they are asked for (`component::a_slot_showing_a_mesh_answers_as_any_slot_and_is_no_point_or_box`).
  - **Limits.** Each mesh is a draw of its own (two with a drop shadow, the shadow's and the body's), and a pipeline change before it and after it in the pass (render module notes, Meshes): a character is made of a few meshes, and the tests' rigs keep to a few. Measured, a weighted mesh of 60 vertices between two runs of quads adds about 1.6 µs to a frame's render step and 0.55 µs to a step's update while a deform plays on it (`docs/backlog/skeleton.md`, Meshes), and wgpu's Metal backend allocates four times a frame for its two pipeline changes. Pixel-art snapping does not apply to a mesh's vertices, so a mesh agrees with a region to the pixel only where both stand on whole pixels (a limit with no test of its own: the pixel tests of meshes stand on whole pixels, on the smooth and the pixel-art target, so they hold either way). An effect that grows its sprite's quad does not reach a mesh (Colour, blend and look, below).
- **Runs and meshes** (`Emitter::draw_parts`). The quads join the draw list as runs pushed with the entity kind (`DrawList::push_run_as(DrawKind::Entity, ..)`), at the entity's place in the order the entity drawer pushes: each stretch of consecutive quads of one image and one blend is one item, so a skeleton of regions drawn from one atlas is one item whatever its slot count. A mesh is an item of its own where it comes (`Emitter::draw_skinned`, over the batch's `Emitter::draw_mesh`): the run before it ends there, and the quads after it start another, though they share the image and blend of those before (`testing::gpu_world::a_skeleton_s_mesh_is_an_item_between_the_runs_of_its_regions`). A run is culled whole, by the box round its quads, per camera; one no camera on the entity's layers sees is not pushed and its image is never uploaded. Why runs and not a sprite item a quad: a sprite item is a sort key, a per-camera visibility test, a texture slot lookup and a pipeline key each, and a run pays those once. Not measured against each other; the skeleton bench (`benches/skeleton.rs`) measures the drawing work as a whole. `draw_parts` takes the walk's own list and two functions, how a region and how a mesh of it is drawn (the body's blend and tint, or the drop shadow's), and turns each part into what is drawn where it uses it: handed an iterator of ready-made parts of either kind, it builds every quad behind a call the compiler does not inline, which measured a third more of the loop's time for a skeleton of regions alone; and the mesh's skinning and its drawing (`Parts::skin`, `Emitter::draw_skinned`) are never inlined. With the attribute the shadow case of regions read 2.5 points nearer the build before meshes and the crowd 2, which is within what the same engine code moves between two builds, so the gain is not shown to be the attribute's. It stays because it cannot hurt and does two things for certain: it is one call a mesh and none for a skeleton of regions alone, it keeps code that only a mesh runs out of the functions the regions' loops are in, and it keeps both functions named on a stack, which is how the allocation test finds them (`docs/backlog/skeleton.md`, Meshes, Against the build before meshes). With a game shader on the entity each quad is a sprite item of its own instead (`Emitter::draw_shaded`, still an entity item in the same place): a run is shaded plain or by a built-in effect, and the sprite path already draws through a game shader. A mesh keeps its place among those sprite items, between two regions, first or last (`a_shaded_skeleton_keeps_its_mesh_in_the_draw_order`, three overlapping parts under a game's shader, on three images and on one).
- **Colour, blend and look.** A part's colour is its slot's times its attachment's times the renderable's `tint`, its alpha those three's times the renderable's `alpha` (`a_region_s_colour_is_its_slot_s_times_its_attachment_s_times_the_tint`; a mesh's the same, `a_mesh_draws_as_the_region_of_its_image`). Its blend is its slot's (`SlotBlend` maps onto the batch's `Additive`, `Multiply` and `Screen`), a mesh's as a region's (the same test's slot that adds light); a slot that blends normally takes the renderable's `blend`. `lighting`, `filter` and `emission` are the renderable's, for every part. The entity's sprite shader (`Renderable::shader`, or its `Material`'s first, as for a sprite) applies to each part on its own, a built-in effect or a game's own: a flash whitens every part, a per-pixel effect works part by part, and an outline or a glow outlines each region, not the figure (`a_skeleton_s_parts_take_their_entity_s_sprite_shader`). A game shader's `in.uv` runs over the part's own rectangle of its image.
  - **A mesh takes the effect or the game's shader as the regions beside it do**: a hit flash, a silhouette, grey, inverted colours and a colour shift change a region and a mesh of one image to one colour (`a_built_in_effect_changes_a_skeleton_s_region_and_mesh_alike`), and so does a game's own sprite shader, on the renderable or in a `Material` (`a_game_s_sprite_shader_changes_a_skeleton_s_region_and_mesh_alike`). A game shader is checked once for quads and once for meshes, so a mesh may draw plain for the frames its own check takes after the regions have turned (a limit with no test of its own: the pixel test of a game's shader steps until both parts show it).
  - **Limit: an effect that grows its sprite's quad (an outline, a glow, a directional outline) leaves a mesh plain.** The regions take it and the meshes draw as with no effect: a quad is enlarged to hold what such an effect draws round the sprite, and a mesh has its own shape. The log is told once a run, naming the first such skeleton's entity (`an_effect_that_grows_a_quad_reaches_the_regions_and_leaves_the_mesh_plain`).
- **Shadows.** The drop shadow is each part's own silhouette, a mesh's its own shape and not its box, drawn before every part, moved by the shadow's offset. Its `scale` shrinks the silhouettes toward where the skeleton stands, in the entity's turned axes: a sprite's shrinks toward its box's top-left corner, and a skeleton has no box (its pose's bounds move with every step, and a shadow anchored on them would jitter). A part faded by its slot or attachment casts less. The blob and contact shadows are a sprite's own code (`ground_shadows`), under where the entity draws: once a skeleton, not following the pose's bounds, for the same reason (`a_skeleton_casts_a_drop_shadow`, `a_one_region_skeleton_draws_as_the_sprite_of_its_image`; for a mesh `a_mesh_casts_the_drop_shadow_of_its_silhouette`, where a mesh's shadow falls on a region and a region's on the mesh, and both parts show over them). **Limit: where parts overlap, a skeleton's drop shadow is darker than a sprite's uniform one**, since each part's shadow is laid over the last (a half black shadow leaves 187 of a white floor once and 136 where two parts overlap: `overlapping_parts_cast_a_darker_drop_shadow_than_one_part` pins the two). A uniform silhouette needs the skeleton drawn offscreen first, which is not built; a character should use a blob or contact shadow. A part faded by its slot casts as much less (`a_part_faded_by_its_slot_casts_less_shadow`).
- **A skeleton no camera can see is not walked** (`bounds.rs`; the drawer's side in `render/entities.rs`, `skeleton_reach`). The entity drawer tests one box per skeleton against the cameras before it builds any part, so an off-screen skeleton costs a few transforms of a frame's drawing, not its walk.
  - **Each bone's reach** (`bone_reaches`, worked out once as the data is built and kept in its index): the furthest any attachment of any skin in a slot of that bone reaches from its origin, in its own units: a region's corners (placed by its pivot), a point, a bounding box's corners, an unweighted mesh's vertices; each influence of a weighted vertex counts toward its own bone, with its place in that bone's space. Every skin counts, as a skin or an attachment set by the game shows at once, before the next step. A bone also keeps whether a mesh vertex is given in its space (which a deform moves) and how far a region's corner on it moves for each texel an effect grows the quad by (a texel's diagonal, counted in the region's pixels or the checker's 16, whichever are fewer, as the checker stands in for an image that is not loaded). Only the bones that carry something are kept (`BoneReaches`), with whether any bone carries a mesh vertex. The padding assumes an image draws at its own size in its slot, its region's rectangle inside it: an image reloaded smaller than a region's rectangle is a data error, and an outline on that region can then reach past the box.
  - **The box is kept in the skeleton's own space** (`Skeleton::bounds`, `pose_bounds`), found after every posing (a step, `appear` and `place_again`, each calling `find_bounds`): for each bone that carries something, the box between its place at this pose and at the pose before (`previous_space`), widened by its reach, plus the largest deform offset of either step where a mesh vertex is in its space, times its largest stretch at either pose. The stretch is the length of its two axes' longest lengths taken together, which no turn, scale or shear passes, and which the blend between steps (`draw::blend`: a place along the line between the two, axes of lengths between theirs) keeps within; a weighted vertex is a mean, by weights of 0 or more, of points inside its influences' boxes. So drawing anywhere between the two steps stays inside. It allocates nothing, and keeps its cost in a step small: it visits only the bones that carry something, takes one square root a bone (the stretch as the square root of the larger squared lengths), and reads the deform offsets only when some bone carries a mesh vertex. Rule we chose: the deform widening is read from the pose's own offsets, not worked out from the keys, since an added layer, a mix and a curve that overshoots can each carry a vertex past every key.
  - **At draw** (`skeleton_reach`) the box's corners go through the transform the parts take (`Skeleton::drawn`: the entity between steps, its lift, its depth scale, its flip), grown first by an effect's padding (an outline, a glow or a directional outline) times that texel figure; the drop shadow adds the box cast as the parts are (its offset, its scale about where the skeleton stands); the blob and contact shadows add their quads as `ground_shadows` places them. A camera on one of the entity's render layers, a texture camera as much as a window's, must see that box; else nothing more is done for the skeleton that frame (no parts, no shadows). Each run and mesh is still culled by its own box after that.
  - **Never culled while any part shows** (`a_skeleton_showing_any_part_is_never_culled`, `render/entities/cull_tests.rs`: cameras walked over a grid past a skeleton at many poses, drawn where it is and between steps, under an entity moved, turned and stretched between steps, lifted, depth-scaled, flipped, outlined, glowing, with a directional outline, a drop shadow offset and scaled, a blob and a contact shadow; on a rig of pivoted and mirrored regions, weighted and unweighted meshes deformed past their keys, bones turned, scaled, sheared and moved, and two skins; and each rule alone on a rig where nothing else in the box hides it; a bone held at a place in the world, posed again with no step after the entity is carried (`place_again`) and posed outside a step there (`appear`), so each path's box is checked). Not walked when out of every view, walked when its shadow alone shows (`an_off_screen_skeleton_is_not_walked`, `testing/gpu_world.rs`, counting the bones the drawer places); seen by a texture camera, and seen only by cameras on its layers (`a_skeleton_is_walked_for_the_cameras_on_its_layers_texture_cameras_too`).
  - **Only the drawing is skipped.** The step (animation, events, IK, transform constraints, springs, sockets) runs in `FixedSet::Skeletons`, which never reads what the drawer does, so a culled skeleton keeps animating and nothing pops when it comes back into view (`a_culled_skeleton_is_still_stepped`, `testing/gpu_world.rs`: never walked, yet its keys fire and its spring swings while it is out of view). A socket's child is its own entity, drawn or culled on its own.
  - **Cost.** `skeletons_extract_offscreen` (`benches/skeleton.rs`) is held to a tenth of the on-screen crowd's extract measured in the same run.
- **Sorting by its feet.** A y-sorted skeleton's key is its entity's own y plus `YSort::sort_offset`: where it stands, the origin, where a rig's feet are. `foot_offset` measures a sprite's image and plays no part. The key does not move with the pose or a flip (`a_y_sorted_skeleton_sorts_among_ysort_sprites_by_its_feet`, `a_y_sorted_skeleton_sorts_within_a_y_sorted_tile_layer`).
- **Flips.** `Renderable::flip_x` and `flip_y` mirror the skeleton about its entity's origin, in the entity's own axes (`Flip`, `Stand`): the root the bones are placed under is `entity × mirror`, so `pose.world`, `bone_world`, `point`, `bounding_box` and a world-space bone override all follow, and a socket's `Transform` is the mirrored bone's (a negative scale, which a `Transform` holds), so its `GlobalTransform` is mirrored too. A sprite on a socket mirrors with it, as any sprite under a mirrored transform does (`an_item_on_a_flipped_skeleton_s_socket_mirrors_with_it`), and so does a skeleton riding one (`a_skeleton_riding_a_flipped_skeleton_s_socket_keeps_its_mirror_between_steps`). The flip is read in the step and kept (`Skeleton::flip`); the drawer mirrors by the kept one, so regions and sockets always agree. A flip made while no step runs is seen by the frame's pass (above), which places the bones and sockets the new way round in that frame. A socket lands across a flip: it is marked as a jump, not drawn sliding through the body. The bones land with it: the pose a skeleton is flipped in is a first placing (`Skeleton::settle`, in the step and in the frame's pass), since the pose before was made for the other side, and a bone aimed in the world would otherwise swing for a step from where that side's answer points on this one (`a_flipped_skeleton_mirrors_its_regions_and_sockets`, `a_flipped_skeleton_s_bones_and_sockets_jump_together`, `system::a_flipped_skeleton_s_bones_jump_with_its_sockets`, `component::a_flipped_skeleton_s_bones_mirror_about_its_entity_s_origin`).
  - **A negative scale on the entity** mirrors the skeleton the same way, between steps too: `GlobalTransform::lerp` keeps a mirror, and in the step a scale changes sign it closes the skeleton to nothing and opens it the other way, never turning it (`an_interpolated_mirrored_skeleton_keeps_its_mirror_between_steps`; ECS module notes, Transform). Unlike a flip, a scale's change of sign is an ordinary move: bones and sockets are drawn through it.
- **`debug:skeletons`** (`debug.rs`; `DebugConfig::show_skeletons`, a console command in a build with the tools, drawn through `overlay_shapes`): for every posed skeleton, each bone as a line its length along it with a ring at its origin, the outline of each region drawn, each mesh drawn as its triangles (a darker blue) with its hull over them in the regions' blue (its first `hull` vertices in order and back to the first; a mesh with no hull shows its triangles alone: `debug::the_overlay_draws_a_mesh_s_triangles_and_its_hull_where_it_is_skinned`), each bounding box its slots show, and each point as a cross with a line the way it faces. Each constraint is marked in rose (`colors::SKELETON_CONSTRAINT`, clear of the bones' amber and the kinematic bodies' orange), at the bones as drawn, between steps too (`debug::the_overlay_marks_constraints_where_a_frame_between_steps_draws_the_bones`: a target and a bone moved between two steps, drawn half way, are marked half way, not where the last step left them): an IK constraint with a cross at its target and a line from its last bone's tip (its `length` along its x axis) to it (`debug::the_overlay_marks_an_ik_constraint_s_target_and_reach`), a transform constraint with a line from each of its bones' origins to its target (`debug::the_overlay_links_a_transform_constraint_s_bones_to_its_target`), and a spring with a line from where animation puts its bone's tip to where the spring has put it, its lag kept in the skeleton's space once the whole pass is done (so a later constraint that moves the bone again is counted: the same test, an IK constraint after the spring on its bone) and laid beside the tip as drawn, and nothing at rest (`debug::the_overlay_draws_a_line_from_a_spring_s_animated_tip_to_its_sprung_tip`); every index is looked up, so a bone a reload removed marks nothing and panics nowhere (`debug::the_overlay_marks_nothing_for_bones_or_constraints_that_are_gone`: new data without a bone and a constraint draws nothing until its step and then only what it has, and a pose shorter than its data marks no constraint whose target or bone it lacks), and a skeleton without constraints draws no more (`the_overlay_draws_its_marks_at_the_target_and_the_tip`, `tests/e2e/skeleton_constraints.rs`, in pixels, with nothing of the colour while the overlay is off). Unlit, on top, for the cameras on the entity's layers. It is drawn where the frame draws the skeleton (interpolated, lifted, flipped), so the lines lie on the art; the engine's other overlays show where things stand (`debug_skeletons_draws_bones_bounding_boxes_and_points_on_top`, `debug::` tests).
